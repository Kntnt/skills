````markdown
# Ten minutes on a Friday

For four years, Priya Nand kept her receipts in a biscuit tin. She tutors maths in Leeds, and every January the tin came out. "The last weekend of January was always gone," she says. The Self Assessment return is due on 31 January, and most of us spend the months before it the way Priya did, with receipts collecting in a drawer or an inbox until there is no more room for later.

The tin has two costs. The first is the weekend. The second is quieter. Till receipts are printed on thermal paper, and they often fade within months, faster in a warm kitchen. When Priya finally opened the tin one January, two receipts from the spring had faded to blank, and she could not claim them.

In March 2026, Priya moved to a session every Friday: "About ten minutes, with a cup of tea." She photographs each receipt when she gets it, and on Friday she matches the week's photographs to her bank lines and notes anything odd.

In a survey this summer, 412 of our users told us how long their last return took. Those who said they matched receipts at least once a week reported a median of 6 hours of their own time. Those who said they left receipts until January reported 19. But the 57 whose return was prepared by an accountant reported a median of just 3 hours. The figures are what people told us rather than anything we measured, and they do not show what caused the difference.

Priya's habit also answers the tin's quieter cost. As our support team notes, a photograph taken while the receipt is new stays readable.
````
