# Brief: fixing it together at the Repair Café

Invented evaluation material. The Hatherley Repair Café and every person named are fictional and describe no real organisation or client.

## 1. What is the assignment?

- Working title: Bring it, fix it, keep it
- Topic: what happens when you bring something broken to the Repair Café.
- Genre: general, a short piece for the community newsletter.
- Channel: the Hatherley community newsletter, printed and posted online.
- Language: en_GB, one version only.
- Length: roughly 300 to 450 words.
- Search phrase: none, deliberately.

## 2. Which brand is the sender?

The Hatherley Repair Café, a volunteer group that holds repair sessions in the village. Promise: broken things get a second life, and people learn to mend them. Image: liked by those who have been, but many think it is a free repair shop. Mandate: its own sessions and the repairs its volunteers do; it says nothing about product safety standards. Tone of voice: warm, practical, neighbourly. Tone in this text: the same, and the newsletter's conventions pull the same way.

## 3. What does the sender want to convey with this text?

The Repair Café is where neighbours learn to fix things together, not a free repair service.

## 4. Who is the reader?

Households in Hatherley who read the community newsletter, many of them retired, some young families. They have heard of the Repair Café but have not been, and many assume they would drop an item off and collect it later. They care about not throwing things away and about saving money. Why now: the autumn clear-out, when broken lamps and bikes turn up in sheds.

## 5. What is the text's angle?

What a first visit is like: you sit down with a volunteer and fix the thing yourself.

## 6. What is the hook?

The lamp at the back of the shed that you were about to throw away, and the surprise that you will be the one holding the screwdriver.

## 7. How should the text be structured?

Free structure: the piece follows a first visit in the order it happens.

The whole text in one sentence: a broken lamp you were about to throw away can come back to life at the Repair Café, where you sit down with a volunteer and fix it yourself, and leave knowing how to do it next time.

Sketch:

1. Opening: the lamp at the back of the shed.
2. Arriving: you bring the item and stay with it; a volunteer sits down with you. Most items brought in are fixed on the day (the share is to be found out; see sources).
3. Fixing: Jo Carr and the lamp she rewired with a volunteer (source 2). Members say the café has saved them money on replacements.
4. What is not repaired: microwaves and anything with a gas supply (source 1).
5. The ending: back to the lamp, now on, and the next session; bring something broken.

## 8. What should be left out?

No safety advice about electrical repairs at home. No criticism of manufacturers. No mention of donations.

## 9. What should the reader have understood afterwards?

At the Repair Café you fix your broken item yourself, with a volunteer beside you, and leave able to do it again.

## 10. What should the reader feel, think or do afterwards?

- Main effect: the reader brings a broken item to the next session.
- The way there: they first need to understand that they stay and take part.
- Call to action and measure: direct, "Bring something broken to the next session." Measure: the number of first-time visitors at the December session.

## 11. What makes the text RIV for the reader, and does the brief hold together?

- Relevant: the autumn clear-out, in the opening.
- Interesting: the hook, that you hold the screwdriver yourself.
- Valuable: knowing what happens and what is not repaired, in steps 2 to 4.

Chain check:

- Does the reader follow from the sender and the channel? Yes.
- Does the angle lie close to the reader, and does the hook carry the angle? Yes.
- Does the structure resolve the hook's tension, and does it fit the length? Yes.
- Is the conclusion what the structure actually lands in, does it carry the message, and does it lie within the brand's mandate? Yes.
- Does what the reader understands lead to the effect you have described, and does that effect serve the brand's goals? Yes.
- Does anything in the structure conflict with what should be left out? No.
