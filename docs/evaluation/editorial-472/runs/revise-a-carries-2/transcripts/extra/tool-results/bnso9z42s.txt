# Reviewing against a Writing Brief

Use this branch only when `## Resolution` selects a brief. The brief is a standard for the Text Artifact, never a second Text Artifact. Read it without editing, proofreading or delivering it. If its path or URL cannot be read, report that obstacle and stop before review or writing; never silently substitute a contract-only review. Resolve relative material references from the brief's location.

A brief is a direction for the writer, not a finished dossier. It is often written before the research is done, so its sketch may make claims it does not yet support and its sources may list what is still to be found out. The requirement that every claim is supported binds the text, and Source Fidelity below is where it is checked.

## Read and map

Read `$LIBRARY/references/editorial/writing-brief.md`, the authoritative English template. Its numbered questions, their order and their headings are the ones this review reports by, however many there are. Map the selected brief onto every numbered question by meaning, in any language or layout. Retain the answer and its provenance for each question; preserve gaps instead of supplying answers. The template's guidance under each question explains what the question means; it is not an instruction to review or repair the brief.

An answer consisting only of `[MISSING: …]` is unanswered: name it in the report, and never count it as a shortfall of the text. Review an answer marked `[SUGGESTED: …]` as written and label its assessment **unconfirmed**. Review an answer marked `[WEAK: …]` as written and label its assessment **weak**; the current template no longer produces that marker, but a brief written earlier may carry it. When a question has both supplied content and missing parts, assess only the supplied content and name the missing parts. An unmarked brief has the same mapping: an absent answer remains unanswered.

An open research question carries no marker. Recognise one by meaning: something the answer on sources says still has to be found out, or an answer elsewhere that defers to such a finding, such as a sketch step waiting on a figure. Record it as open. It is not a requirement the text can fall short of.

A brief written to the earlier template had one *message* question where the current template has two: the sender's message, and the text's conclusion. Map such a brief by meaning onto the current questions like any other. Its *message* answer becomes the conclusion, and the sender's message counts as unanswered, unless the brief itself keeps the two apart. Report it by the current template's numbers and headings.

Read the material the brief points at where it bears on an answered requirement, including the sources it lists, source evidence and a referenced brand platform. Record what it actually supports; the brief's demand for a fact or a quotation is not evidence for that fact or quotation. An inaccessible or missing source is a reported limitation, not permission to fill its contents from memory or external research. Review what is available and leave shortfalls needing the unavailable material for the writer.

## Assess and correct

At step 6 and each re-review, assess each answered question against the current text: **fulfilled**, **partly fulfilled** or **not fulfilled**. Give a passage that demonstrates the judgement, or explicitly say which needed passage is absent. For either shortfall status, state what the reader loses. Cover what each answer actually gives, requirement by requirement:

- **The assignment:** the text is about the topic. The genre is resolved as `## Resolution` says, and the text suits the channel. The text is in the brief's source language; other language versions the brief plans are not assessed. The length is judged against the range the brief gives, where it gives one, a provisional range counting as a range. A working title identifies the assignment; it does not require a verbatim final headline. Where a search phrase is given, the text can be found on it; a search phrase deliberately left out is fulfilled.
- **The sender and the tone in this text:** the text speaks as the named brand, within its mandate, in the tone the brief sets for this text.
- **The sender's message:** fulfilled when the text carries it, either by saying it or by where it leads the reader. That the message is never stated outright is never a shortfall. The text falls short when it leads the reader somewhere else or contradicts the message.
- **The reader, and why now:** the text meets the reader the brief describes, with their knowledge and concerns, and what makes the topic pressing now shows in the text.
- **The angle:** the text keeps to the one angle.
- **The hook:** the text sets up the tension the hook names, and the reader knows what is at stake before it arrives.
- **The structure:** judged on whether the text realises the brief's one sentence for the whole text, resolves the hook, and lands in the conclusion. It is fulfilled only where all three hold, and its evidence names each of them. The step-by-step sketch is guidance for the writer, so the text is not judged section by section against it, and a text that departs from its steps while doing those three things has no shortfall there. An ending that does not tie back to the hook is a shortfall.
- **What is left out:** the text carries nothing the brief excludes, and keeps to the line the brief draws where a boundary has exceptions.
- **The conclusion:** judged on whether the text lands in it.
- **The effect and the call to action:** an intended effect is judged by how the text works towards it, not by claiming to know real reader reactions or measured results. A direct call to action is judged by whether the text makes it. An indirect one is judged by what the text leaves the reader with, not by whether the text explicitly asks the reader to do something. A call to action deliberately left out is fulfilled, and a measure is not assessed against the text.
- **RIV:** relevance, interest and value are judged over the whole text. The chain check belongs to the brief, not the text: it is not assessed against the text, and it is reported only as answered or unanswered.
- **The use of sources:** judged against the sources the brief lists; a list that is not yet complete is no shortfall. The text is never faulted for leaving out a claim of the sketch that nothing supports, nor for lacking the answer to an open research question.

Record every shortfall as an ordinary finding with its question number, supplied requirement, passage or absence, and reader loss. Mark findings requiring missing facts, sources or quotations as left for the writer. Keep the original metadata conflicts as findings in their own right, naming both values and the resolved value, even after step 8 synchronizes the text's map. Formal options still settle configuration: report the mismatch with the brief without correcting the text against an overridden genre, technique or language.

Repairable brief findings join the same list, fresh correction rounds, Correction Budget, difference comparison and rejection rules as editorial findings. There is no separate budget or parent-authored substantive repair. With each repairable brief finding, include the answer it is measured against and the exact supporting passages from the text or the material read through the brief, identified by path or URL. This support travels inside the complete finding sent to the correction agent; do not send that agent an extra interpretation of the brief. A shortfall on the sender's message is repaired by where the text leads, never by stating the message outright.

Source Fidelity binds these repairs: every fact, source, quotation, attribution, uncertainty, scope, chronology and causal relation must stay supported by the text or the material the brief points at. The available material may supply a missing fact, but an inference, demand, sketch claim or gap in the brief cannot. A claim the brief makes and the material does not support is never repaired into the text. A claim the text makes and the material does not support is a finding, left for the writer. Where neither the text nor the material contains what fulfilment would require, report the shortfall and leave it for the writer. Check source support as part of every candidate's re-review; invented support is a repair-created defect, so the existing rejection rule applies. Keep the normal preservation rules for claims beside a defect, quotation meaning and code samples.

## Report

Deliver a **Brief fulfilment** section in the artifact's language, separate from the Text Artifact and present even when no correction was needed. Name the selected brief and any suppressed selection of a different brief. Report question by question, under the template's number and its heading word for word as the template has it: never shortened, paraphrased or spelled out, so RIV stays RIV, and translated whole only where the section is not in English. List each answered question with its status, evidence in the delivered text (or explicit absence), and reader loss where it falls short. Flag every status measured against an unconfirmed or weak answer. Name all unanswered questions by number and heading, including MISSING-only answers, and any missing parts of otherwise answered questions. Name each open research question as open, never as a shortfall.

The section assesses what is delivered: refresh its evidence after accepted corrections and the final mechanical pass without making a further edit. Where an accepted correction changed a question's status, give the status the text arrived with beside the delivered one, so a shortfall the run repaired stays visible as one. Keep the normal finding and claim account so the writer can distinguish repaired shortfalls, unresolved ones and rejected attempts; report an original map conflict as such, never as a conflict still present after synchronization. Reporting the section does not authorize returning the brief itself or rewriting an unchanged artifact.

# The correction brief

Give this to a subagent started fresh for one correction, filled in from the run as it now stands. Everything in angle brackets is replaced; nothing else is rewritten.

`<text>` is the current Text Artifact pasted whole — the text as it stands after every correction so far, never the text as it arrived and never an extract of it. `<findings>` is every finding of the most recent review, in the order it was recorded, each pasted whole under the same rule. `<genre>`, `<technique>` and `<language>` are the values the run resolved, written as the resources are named; write `none` for `<technique>` where no technique was resolved, and drop the sentence naming its resource with it. `<library>` is the Collection Library path this run resolved.

A brief-shortfall finding also carries its numbered requirement and exact, attributed supporting passages supplied by the parent review. Those are part of the finding, not an extra interpretation.

Tell the subagent nothing else. Not what an earlier round found, not what an earlier correction attempted, not which findings you think matter most, and not your reading of what the text is trying to do — that reading is the thing a fresh subagent is dispatched to be without.

Hand the filled-in brief to the subagent directly, as the whole of its instruction, and hand it over no other way. Never write it to a file and send a path in its place: this brief holds the user's text entire and every finding standing against it, and a file is a name some other process can read, replace, or delete for as long as the round lasts. A long text makes a long brief, and a long brief is passed whole anyway — trimming it, summarising it, and pointing at it are the three things this brief exists to rule out.

---

You are making one correction to one text. You have not seen this text before, and there is nothing you are expected to remember: the text below is its current state, and the findings below are the current review's, complete and as they were written. Repair them.

**The text.** This is the complete Text Artifact, exactly as it now stands:

`<text>`

**The findings.** This is every finding the review recorded against that text, complete and in the order it recorded them:

`<findings>`

**The contract.** The text is a `<genre>` in `<language>`, and it is held to `<technique>`. Read what it is held to before you change anything: `<library>/references/editorial/base.md` and `<library>/references/editorial/base.review.md`; `<library>/references/editorial/genres/<genre>.md` and the `.review.md` file beside it where one exists; `<library>/references/editorial/techniques/<technique>.md` and its `.review.md` the same way; `<library>/references/editorial/anti-slop.md`; and the `composition`, `review` and `anti-slop` scopes of the resolved language, which `uv run "<library>/scripts/languages.py" resolve --scope=composition --scope=review --scope=anti-slop "<language>"` returns. For `article`, `casestudy`, `column` and `opinion`, and for `webcopy`, also read `<library>/references/editorial/web-craft.md` and `<library>/references/editorial/web-craft.review.md`; for `article`, `casestudy`, `column` and `opinion` alone, also read `<library>/references/editorial/article-anatomy.md` and `<library>/references/editorial/article-anatomy.review.md`, which fix the parts, order and dimensions of such a text; and for those four and for `pressrelease`, also read `<library>/references/editorial/headlines.md` and `<library>/references/editorial/headlines.review.md`, which say how a headline and its subheadings are written. These are the only additional genre support files: follow no other genre links and load no unselected genre or technique. A review extension that is not there is not a defect. Run the resolver with `UV_NO_CACHE=1 UV_NO_PROJECT=1` and a fresh private `TMPDIR`, removing that directory afterwards, including on failure. Load no mechanics guidance and correct no spelling, punctuation, or grammar: a mechanical pass runs after you, and work you do there is work done twice.

**What you change, and what you preserve.** Repair what the findings name and nothing else. Every difference between the text you return and the text above is traced to a finding, or to what repairing a finding required, before this round is accepted, and a difference that traces to no finding is restored from the text you received — so a phrase you improved outside a finding is work that will be undone. Preserve everything the findings do not concern: every sentence, every fact, every quotation, every code sample, every heading, every piece of formatting and every line of frontmatter among them comes back exactly as you received it — a repair that improves a paragraph nobody complained about is a change nobody asked for and nobody will review. A code sample — a fenced block, an indented block, or an inline code span — is quoted material rather than the text's own prose: its contents are read past rather than read against the rules, so nothing inside one is a finding and nothing inside one is changed, the docstrings, comments, and string literals among them. Prose about code is ordinary prose and is read like every other sentence. Where a finding can be repaired several ways, make the smallest change that removes it. Where a finding names something you cannot repair without inventing a fact, changing what the text claims, or altering a quotation's meaning, stance, certainty or distinctive wording, leave that passage alone and say so: in an ordinary review this text is answerable to material you do not have, and a plausible invention is worse than an unrepaired finding. For a brief-shortfall finding carrying exact source support, use only that support or the text to supply what is missing. A repair never states the sender's message outright, since the message may always be conveyed indirectly. Source Fidelity binds the repair: retain attribution, uncertainty, scope, chronology, causality and quotation meaning. A requirement in the brief is not source evidence; where the finding has no support for a needed fact, source or quotation, leave it for the writer and report why.

**A defect of your own costs the whole round.** The review that follows you establishes whether this text has a defect it did not have when you received it — a headline or a heading claiming more than the text claims, a heading repeating the passage under it, a limit your repair dropped. Where it establishes one, the whole round is rejected: the text you received is restored entire, nothing you did survives however much else you repaired, and no fresh attempt follows. So a finding you cannot repair without creating another one is a finding you leave and say so, exactly as you would one you cannot repair without inventing a fact — an unrepaired finding a person can settle costs less than a repair that has to be thrown away with everything beside it.

**What a repair may take with it, and what it may not.** A finding names a defect, and what a repair removes is that defect. Where the defect a finding names is the whole of the passage — an opening that would sit in front of any text, a closing that restates what the reader has just read, a claim credited to nobody that the text cannot source — removing it may be the repair, and any claim removed with it must be named in your return. That stops at a sentence whose work is to bound what the text asserts — that something is not measured, not claimed, not witnessed or not general, or that the text is an observation of a document rather than a scene from an occasion. Remove or weaken one only where the finding names a defect inside the sentence, and only where a sentence you keep still states that limit in full, so the text bounds what it claims exactly as far as it did, or where the limit contradicts another passage of the same text. Otherwise leave it exactly as it stands and say so. Where the passage carries a claim beside the defect — a fact, an observation, the point the passage exists to make — the smallest change is the one that leaves that claim standing, and deleting the passage entire is not that change. And where you can see no way to remove the defect that does not take the claim with it, leave the passage alone and say so, exactly as you would a finding you cannot repair without inventing a fact: a person can give that claim what it needs or let it go, and a round that deletes it leaves them nothing to weigh. That judgement is about the passage in front of you and never about how much of the text is left — a short text is no licence to leave a finding standing, and a long one is no licence to go on cutting.

A repair at a punctuation joint can be the smallest change even when the finding names the implication created there rather than punctuation itself. In *We finished in March, the finance team got a new reporting tool the same month.*, the defect is the implication created by joining unrelated claims, not either claim. Repair it as *We finished in March. The finance team got a new reporting tool the same month.*: splitting at the joint removes the implication and keeps the reporting-tool fact. Do the same wherever the named defect is confined to a joint; do not delete either side or supply a relationship the text does not establish. This example does not admit a wider mechanics pass: it identifies the smallest repair for the finding already in hand.

Before you return the text, compare it before and after, claim by claim. Every claim you received must still appear with its scope, certainty, attribution, chronology, causality and meaning intact unless the finding named the whole claim as the defect; that exception does not reach a sentence whose work is to bound what the text asserts, which goes only on the two conditions above, and the number or concentration of findings changes nothing about that check. The concentrated anti-slop case makes the boundary visible: removing its patterns must not discard its claims about meeting load, documentation, decisions, satisfaction, distributed work, adoption conditions, or the synchronous/asynchronous trade-off. Repair the patterns around those claims. If one cannot be separated from its pattern, keep it and report the finding unresolved; never compress several content-bearing passages into a cleaner summary.

Where the text is an `article`, a `casestudy`, a `column` or an `opinion`, measure it before you return it: run `uv run "<library>/scripts/article_anatomy.py" -` with the complete corrected text on stdin, under the same `UV_NO_CACHE=1 UV_NO_PROJECT=1` and fresh private `TMPDIR` terms as the resolver above. Exit 0 settles the counted requirements only: it says they hold and nothing about the anatomy requirements the script does not count, so your return reports the text as conforming to the anatomy only where you examined every one of those too, and otherwise names the ones you did not examine. Exit 1 names each counted requirement of the article anatomy that fails, with the part, the rule and the figure measured: repair the ones a finding named and the ones your own repair introduced, then measure again, except a part a finding names as absent and a failure caused only by an absent part, which you leave unwritten and name in your return as left for a person to supply; a requirement the text was already failing when it reached you, and that no finding names, is left exactly as it stands like everything else the findings do not concern. Exit 2 says the script could not measure the text, and your return says so. Read every `heading_pairs` entry against the complete candidate and the loaded headline contract before returning, whatever the exit status: judge whether each part adds different information in different wording. Necessary repeated names and topic words can be legitimate, and a paraphrased echo can share no words; `shared_words` alone never authorizes a change or creates a finding. Verify absent or ambiguous sentence estimates against the text; on exit 2 inspect the pairs by hand. Repair echoes named by the findings or introduced by your own correction by wording the heading from its whole text or section without strengthening its claims and without pre-spending the judgement, figure or concession of a quotation standing under a subheading, then measure again. An unrelated pre-existing echo stays as received and is reported in your return, with the pair and reader loss; the evidence does not expand your correction authority. Report any echo you cannot repair within that authority the same way.

Where the text is a `pressrelease`, measure it before you return it the same way, with `--genre=pressrelease` before the `-`, which measures the two counted limits the genre states and nothing else of it. Exit 1 names the headline or the summary that is past its limit, with the rule and the figure measured: repair the ones a finding named and the ones your own repair introduced, then measure again, except a summary a finding names as absent, which you leave unwritten and name in your return as left for a person to supply; a limit the text was already failing when it reached you, and that no finding names, is left exactly as it stands. Exit 2 says the script found no level-1 heading or could not read the text, and your return says so. The output carries no heading pairs: read the headline and the summary against each other as the genre's review half says before returning.

**Your own working files.** Any file you write for your own working purposes — a candidate you measure, a draft you compare, anything you keep while you work — goes in a directory you created for this one round, under a name made unique when you created it, such as the one `mktemp -d` returns. Remove that directory before you return, including on failure. A fixed name under a shared temporary directory is not that directory, whether the directory is the system's own or a scratchpad your session was given: another run may be working at the same time, in the same place and on this same text, and write that same name. The file you come back to is then that run's work and not yours, and when you take it for your own stale copy and delete or replace it, you destroy work that is not yours, and nothing tells the run it came from.

**What you return.** The complete corrected text and a short note of which findings you repaired and which you left, with the reason for each you left. Where any repair removed a passage carrying a claim, name every removed claim and say which finding made the claim itself the defect, and name every changed claim the same way — one you left standing with its scope, certainty, attribution, chronology, causality or meaning moved, by what it now asserts rather than how far its wording moved, and the finding the repair that moved it answered. Where a repair took a limit, a connective or a bounding clause out of the text — a sentence or a heading saying what is not measured, not claimed or not known, a connective giving the text's own reason for what follows, a clause saying what a claim is not about — say what a reader of the text you return no longer has, however little of the text went with it. Name every added claim the same way, whether or not anything was removed: one the text you return asserts and the text you received has no counterpart for, an inference drawn from what that text already said among them where it asserts something that text did not say, and the finding the repair that wrote it answered. A claim with a counterpart in the text you received is changed rather than added. Reporting a removal never makes an impermissible one acceptable. The complete text: not a diff, not the changed passages, not a description of what you did. Return no commentary inside the text itself.

# The reply check

Give this to a subagent started fresh once step 9 has produced the final Text Artifact and the reply is drafted, before anything is delivered. It runs on every run whose reply says anything about a text, which is every run except one that returns only the short no-change status. Everything in angle brackets is replaced; nothing else is rewritten.

`<received>` is the Text Artifact as it arrived, pasted whole, frontmatter included. `<delivered>` is the final Text Artifact step 9 produced, pasted whole. `<reply>` is the reply as drafted, pasted whole: every finding, the claim account, the closing paragraph and every other sentence it will deliver, leaving out only the delivered text where the reply carries it. `<measurement>` is, for `article`, `casestudy`, `column`, `opinion` and `pressrelease`, the full output of step 6's command run on the delivered text, as step 11 says, pasted whole; for any other genre, drop the paragraph headed **The measurement of the delivered text** and the placeholder under it. `<library>` is the Collection Library path this run resolved.

Tell the subagent nothing else. Not the review's reasoning, not the rounds or what any of them attempted, not why any change was made, and not this Skill's own instructions: the checker is a reader who knows what the reader of the reply will know, the two texts and what the reply says of them, and that is what lets it see what a change did beyond what it was meant to do. Hand the filled-in brief to the subagent directly, as the whole of its instruction, and never as a file whose path you send in its place: it holds the user's text twice, and a file is a name another process can read, replace or delete while the check runs.

**What you do with what it returns.** The subagent returns two lists. Act on every item of both, and correct only the reply:

- A statement on the first list is rewritten so that it is true of the text it names, or left out, as *The truth of a report about the text* in `$LIBRARY/references/delivery.md` says. A count or length about the delivered text takes its figure from the measurement you handed over.
- A difference on the second list is added to the claim account, or its entry completed, the way step 11 reports a removed, changed or added claim. Anything the reply says about the claims as a whole that the new or completed entry contradicts is narrowed to what holds, or left out.

The list is a check of the reply, not a note of what a correction did, so the closing paragraph is still built from your own comparison of the two texts, and is corrected from the list like every other statement. The text is not touched: the Text Artifact step 9 produced is delivered as it is, and step 10 holds. The check is made once, and the corrected reply is delivered without being checked again.

---

You are checking one reply against the two texts it describes. You did not write the reply and you did not make the changes it reports; you know only what is below, which is what the reply's own reader will know. Find what the reply gets wrong about the texts, and only that: whether a change was a good one, whether a finding was worth making and how the text reads are questions for somebody else.

**The text as it arrived.** This is the complete text the run received:

`<received>`

**The text as delivered.** This is the complete text the run delivers:

`<delivered>`

**The reply.** This is everything the run is about to say about the two texts:

`<reply>`

**The measurement of the delivered text.** This is the full output of the collection's measuring script, run on the delivered text. A count or length the reply states about the delivered text is read against it:

`<measurement>`

**The duties the reply is held to.** Before you read the reply, read two passages and apply them as written: the section *The truth of a report about the text* in `<library>/references/delivery.md`, and the sentences of `<library>/references/editorial/base.review.md` that say what the claim account holds, from *A claim removed because the finding named that claim itself* to the end of that paragraph. Read nothing else of either file, and no other file.

**First compare the two texts yourself**, passage by passage, frontmatter and headings included, and note every difference, before you read what the reply says about them. The reply's own account is what you are checking, so it is never how you find a difference.

**Then return two lists.**

1. **False statements.** Every statement of the reply that the text it names contradicts, read as *The truth of a report about the text* reads it: a statement about the text as it arrived against that text, and every other statement against the delivered text. A finding's description of a passage, a count, a length, a grammatical label, where a passage stands, what a round did to a passage, a statement that something holds everywhere or nowhere, and every entry and sentence of the claim account are statements the text can contradict. A judgement of quality — that a heading was vague, that a passage read as filler — is not. For each: quote the statement, name the text it is about, quote the passage that contradicts it, and say what is true.
2. **Claim changes the account misses.** Every difference between the two texts that moves a claim and that the claim account leaves out, or reports short of what it did: scope narrowed or widened; a condition, a qualification, a limit or a connective lost; chronology, causality or attribution moved; or the meaning moved, a sense or a double sense lost and an actor, an ordering or a framing added among it. A headline, a standfirst and a subheading carry claims like any other passage. For each: quote the passage before and after, say what the difference did to the claim, and quote what the account says of it, or say that it says nothing.

Write `none` under a list with nothing on it. Return the two lists and nothing else, and write no file.
