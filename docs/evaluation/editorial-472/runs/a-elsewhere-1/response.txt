```markdown
# Ten minutes on a Friday

For four years, Priya Nand kept her receipts in a biscuit tin. She tutors maths in Leeds, and every January the tin came down from the shelf. "The last weekend of January was always gone," she says. The Self Assessment return is due on 31 January, and most of us spend the months before it the way Priya did, with receipts collecting in a drawer or an inbox until there is no more room for later.

The tin has two costs. The first is the weekend. The second is quieter. Till receipts are printed on thermal paper, and they often fade within months, faster in a warm kitchen. When Priya finally opened the tin one January, two receipts from the spring had faded to blank, and she could not claim them.

The usual advice is a weekly habit: photograph each receipt, match the week's photographs to your bank lines every Friday, and note anything odd. It is good advice, and it asks for fifty-two Fridays a year from people who already work most of them.

In March 2026, Priya moved to a session every Friday. "About ten minutes, with a cup of tea," she says. She photographs each receipt when she gets it, and a photograph taken while the receipt is new stays readable.

In a survey this summer, 412 of our users told us how long their last return took. Those who matched receipts every week reported a median of 6 hours of their own time. Those who left everything until January reported 19. The figures are what people told us rather than anything we measured, and they do not show what caused the difference.

This January, the tin could stay on the shelf.
```
