# A quiet table on Saturdays

It is Sunday night, the maths homework is due in the morning, and the kitchen table is covered in books, plates and a younger brother's noise. For many families in Pellbrook, that is where homework gets done.

"At home my little brothers are always shouting," says Amira, in year 9. Since March she has spent her Saturday mornings somewhere else: the homework club at Pellbrook Library. Her mother drops her off on the way to the market. "Here I get my maths done before lunch."

The club meets every Saturday in term time, from 10:00 to 12:00, in the first-floor study room. It is for pupils in years 7 to 11, it is free, and nobody needs to book: pupils sign in at the door and bring their own homework. There are laptops and a printer, and two volunteer tutors are there each week, one for maths and science and one for English and humanities.

Colin Parry, who tutors maths, is a retired teacher. "Most of them just need someone to ask when they're stuck," he says. On the Saturday we visited, 14 pupils signed in.

So if your Sunday nights look like the one above, the Pellbrook homework club gives your child a quiet table and a volunteer tutor every Saturday, free of charge. Mention it at dinner this week. Saturday morning is when the table is waiting.

## Findings

1. **Unresolved, left for the writer.** First paragraph: "For many families in Pellbrook, that is where homework gets done."
   - **Problem:** neither the leaflet nor the visit notes in `material.md` say where or how Pellbrook families do homework, so nothing supports this claim (brief question 12; base contract, Claims).
   - **Reader loss:** the reader takes a statement about "many families" as something the council knows.
   - **Needs:** a source, or the writer deciding how to frame the opening.
2. **Repaired.** Second paragraph, "For Amira, in year 9, it used to be.", and final paragraph, "if your Sunday nights look like Amira's used to".
   - **Problem:** together these turned the imagined Sunday-night opening into Amira's past. The visit notes record only her words "At home my little brothers are always shouting. Here I get my maths done before lunch." They mention no kitchen table and no Sunday nights, and the shouting is in the present tense (brief question 12; base contract, Claims).
   - **Reader loss:** the reader couldn't tell what Amira said from what the text built around her.
3. **Unresolved, left for the writer.** Missing: a reason to act this term.
   - **Problem:** the brief's relevance answer names the first mock exams in November and homework piling up in the autumn term. `material.md` mentions neither, so this couldn't be written in without a source (brief questions 4 and 11).
   - **Reader loss:** a parent has no reason to act now rather than later.
