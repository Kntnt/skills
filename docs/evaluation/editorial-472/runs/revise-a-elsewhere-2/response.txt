```markdown
# Ten minutes on a Friday

For four years, Priya Nand kept her receipts in a biscuit tin. She tutors maths in Leeds, and every January the tin came out. "The last weekend of January was always gone," she says. The Self Assessment return is due on 31 January, and most of us spend the months before it the way Priya did, with receipts collecting in a drawer or an inbox until there is no more room for later.

The tin has two costs. The first is the weekend. The second is quieter. Till receipts printed on thermal paper often fade within months, faster when kept somewhere warm. When Priya finally opened the tin one January, two receipts from the spring had faded to blank, and she could not claim them.

In March 2026, Priya moved to a session every Friday. "About ten minutes, with a cup of tea," she says. She photographs each receipt when she gets it, and on Friday she matches the week's photographs to her bank lines and notes anything odd.

In a survey this summer, 412 of our users told us how long their last return took. Those who matched receipts at least once a week reported a median of 6 hours of their own time. Those who left their receipts until January reported 19. The figures are what people told us rather than anything we measured, and they do not show what caused the difference.

A receipt in a tin can still fade. A photograph taken while the receipt is new stays readable.
```

## Fixed

The second half now follows Priya's move to a Friday session and her steps, and quotes her on how long it takes.

3. **The fading problem had no answer.** Handing the tin over in January does nothing for receipts that have already faded. The ending now answers it: "A photograph taken while the receipt is new stays readable."

5. **Unsupported details.** "came down from the shelf" and "a warm kitchen" were reworded. "from people who already work most of them" and "A tutor's time is worth more at the whiteboard…" were removed.
6. **Advice credited to nobody.** "The usual advice is a weekly habit" had no source. The steps are now Priya's own.
7. **Claims wider or narrower than the material.** "Till receipts are printed on thermal paper" (all of them), "every week" (the survey says "at least once a week") and "left everything" (the survey says "left receipts") now match the material.
8. **The title's promise.** The body now delivers the "ten minutes"; the title's wording is unchanged.
