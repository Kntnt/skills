# Bring it, fix it, keep it

Somewhere at the back of a Hatherley shed there is a lamp that stopped working, waiting for the autumn clear-out and the tip. Before it goes, bring it to the Repair Café.

You will not be leaving it with us. At the Repair Café, the person who brings a broken thing stays with it. You sit down at a table, a volunteer sits down beside you, and between you, you work out what is wrong. Then, as far as you can, you mend it yourself. The volunteers cover small electrical items, bikes, clothes and small furniture, and the tea is on the table.

In September, Jo Carr brought in a standard lamp that had stopped working. With Dev, one of our volunteers, she opened the plug, found a loose wire and fitted it again herself. "I've never opened a plug in my life," she said. "Now I'd do it on my own." That was one of 31 items that came in that morning, among them a toaster, four bikes and a pair of jeans.

Not everything can come back. We do not repair microwaves or anything with a gas supply. But since the café opened, the volunteers have kept more than a tonne of electrical goods out of landfill, one plug and one hinge at a time.

There is no charge. Two new volunteers joined in September, a retired electrician and a tailor, so there are more hands at the tables.

The lamp in the shed does not need a new owner or a skip. It needs a Saturday morning, and the person who owns it. Bring something broken to the next session.
