## 2. F1 — source support: **pass**

Every assertion in the delivered draft pairs with a supplied passage at the same subject, scope, date, modality and certainty. The items worth naming, and why none of them fails:

The headline renders a decision as the practice. The limiting scope "for new jobs" is carried, the backlog is not swept in, and the body restores the approval frame. Ordinary headline compression; not a defect.

- **"from the scheduling software supplier Benchline"** — the material never says in one sentence that the schedule is Benchline's product, but Benchline was checked for the capability, configured the choices and trained the staff, and the brief names it the scheduling software supplier. The contrary case cannot be built without reading the package against itself. Supported.

"Produced" still ties the approval loosely to the trial; the material presents all three as the trial's outcomes and conditions the postponement on the status definitions the trial exposed. Thin but inside the material. No date is asserted for the decision.
- **"Collection arrangements stayed on the telephone."** Source: "The team continued to use telephone calls to discuss collection arrangements with customers." The counterparty is dropped without a different one being asserted; "stayed" carries "continued". Supported.

"The trial recorded visibility, not speed" claims a record, not an effect. The material's own caveat is carried in the draft's own words: "That is the workshop's own account of six weeks, not a measured improvement in repair speed." - **Unknown is kept apart from absent.** The draft states the three things the summary does not measure and claims nothing about them. It adds no scene, no spoken exchange, no expression and no emotional reaction — all of which the material explicitly excludes — and states the medium ("by email", "replied", "wrote") every time it approaches the interview. It makes no recommendation for other workshops.
- **No personal attribute is invented.** After repair the draft carries no third-person pronoun for Vale at all; name and role only, which is all the material gives.
- **Both-directions term check.** "repair time" for "repair duration" — same measured quantity here, nothing falls under one and not the other. "the two statuses" for "these statuses" — the material names exactly two and no more. "the workshop" for "the team" — the material uses "the workshop team", "The workshop" and "The team" of one body. "brass and woodwind workshop" from "services brass and woodwind instruments" — neither asserts exclusivity. "separate paper sheets ... one set ... another" no longer fixes a count. I record it as a configuration artefact, openly flagged, not as an F1 failure — but it is the one line in the artefact a reader would take as fact and the source cannot support.

## 4. Bridges into quotations

Two quotations, in order.

**Q1.** Bridge: *"Benchline's case writer asked workshop manager Priya Vale by email what the team had to settle before the schedule was useful."* Quotation: *"We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench."*
**Class (b).** The words that put it there: "Benchline's case writer asked … by email" (attribution and occasion) and "what the team had to settle before the schedule was useful" (the subject, in the form of the question actually put). The bridge delivers the *question*; the quotation delivers the *answer* — that the contested term was "ready for work", and why. Nothing in the bridge tells the reader that a part could be in the building without being at the right bench. **Second reading considered:** "what the team had to settle" names the very thing the quotation supplies, which invites a (a) reading. I reject it, because class (a) requires the bridge to have already delivered what the quotation is there to deliver, and naming the subject a quotation goes on to speak about is expressly not enough. The bridge poses; the quotation answers.

**Q2.** Bridge: *"Asked whether the same choice would be made again, Vale wrote:"* Quotation: *"I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog."*
**Class (b).** The words: "Asked whether the same choice would be made again" (the occasion, again the question as put) and "Vale wrote" (attribution plus medium). **Second reading considered:** if the bridge is stretched back to the preceding sentence — "The manager approved continued use for new jobs, but postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions" — it overlaps the quotation's territory, since the quotation also speaks of new jobs and the backlog. I reject that reading on two grounds: the brief defines the bridge as the sentence or clause standing *immediately* before the quotation, and the overlap is not a restatement in any case — the recorded decision is what happened, the quotation is Vale's counterfactual ("I would use it again") and a fresh, unrecorded prescription ("another week to check the status names"). The reader gains something at the quotation marks.

- **Neither quotation stands without a bridge.**
- **Counts: (a) 0, (b) 2, (c) 0.**
- **Interviewer's mouth:** the draft puts nothing in the interviewer's mouth that the material does not carry — Q1's bridge renders the supplied question "What did the team have to settle before the schedule was useful?" indirectly and near-verbatim, and Q2's renders the first half of "Would you make the same choice again, and what would you change?"; the dropped half understates the question without misstating it, and the answer quoted covers both halves.
- **Unsupported bridge assertion:** none; every element of both bridges — the parties, their roles, the medium, the questions — is in the material, and "replied"/"wrote" match "The interview took place by email."

## 8. L1 — language: **pass**

Idiomatic British journalistic English throughout, with no imported syntax. "trialling", "telephone", "23 February 2026", curly quotation marks and headline article-dropping ("Fenwick keeps shared bench schedule") are all native to the register. The prose has real control: the lead's colon construction, "Collection arrangements stayed on the telephone" as a four-beat close to a dense paragraph, and headings that carry argument rather than labels ("The trial recorded visibility, not speed").

Two blemishes, neither disqualifying. **"Asked whether the same choice would be made again, Vale wrote"** is an agentless passive inside a participial opener; the natural English is "Asked whether the workshop would make the same choice again". It reads as the pronoun repair showing through, and check 1's own suggested wording was the better one. **"Vale replied" / "Vale wrote"** repeats the surname where a pronoun would ordinarily fall; acceptable in British practice, but the reader feels the avoidance over two paragraphs. Locale mechanics are separately clean and are not what this verdict rests on.
