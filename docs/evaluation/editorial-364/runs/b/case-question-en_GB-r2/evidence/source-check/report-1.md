## 2. Findings

### Finding 1 — "she"/"her" asserts a natural gender the material never supplies (CONFIRMED)

**Draft:** line 10 "what **she** would change"; line 32 "**Her** verdict"; line 36 "**her** order of work".
**Source:** "workshop manager Priya Vale"; "Publication of the extract was approved by Vale"; "Vale: 'I would use it again …'". The material never uses a pronoun for Vale, never says woman, man, Ms, Mr, or anything else that carries natural gender, and the interview was conducted by email, so no spoken or visual cue is recorded either.
**Missing support:** the feminine feature of the pronoun. Identifying that "she" means Priya Vale, which the context does settle, establishes the referent only; it does not establish person, number or gender, and gender is the one of the three the material leaves open.
**Case in which the draft fails and the source holds:** Priya Vale is a man, or does not use feminine pronouns. Every supplied statement — the title "workshop manager", the approval line, both answers in the first person — remains true word for word, while "what she would change" and "her verdict" are false. No supplied statement excludes this case.
**Smallest supported repair:** drop the pronoun in all three places: "and what Vale would change"; "Vale's verdict comes with a condition and a delay"; "What travels is Vale's order of work". This changes no claim and keeps the voice.

### Finding 2 — "no repair time was measured" turns the summary's silence into a known absence (CONFIRMED)

**Draft:** heading, line 24: "Staff could see the waiting jobs; **no repair time was measured**".
**Source:** "**It** does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction" — a statement about one document, the trial summary dated 23 February 2026. The closing note likewise limits itself to the material: "The summary and interview establish the team's reported experience, **not a measured improvement in repair speed**", which says the material does not establish such a measurement, not that none was taken.
**Changed support:** the subject and the scope. The draft's passive drops the document and asserts that no measurement of repair time occurred at all, at Fenwick or anywhere in the trial.
**Case in which the draft fails and the source holds:** the workshop timed the 38 repairs on its own bench cards, found the spread too wide to be worth reporting in a six-week trial, and left the figures out of the summary. "It does not measure repair duration" is then still exactly true and so is "not a measured improvement in repair speed", while "no repair time was measured" is false. Nothing supplied excludes it; the material states only what the summary contains.
**Smallest supported repair:** "Staff could see the waiting jobs; the summary measured no repair time." The body paragraph beneath it already attributes correctly, so only the heading changes.

### Finding 3 — the heading states as the draft's own prospect what Vale said she would do (CONFIRMED)

**Draft:** heading, line 30: "One more week on the wording, then the backlog can move".
**Source:** Vale: "I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog." And: "The manager approved continued use for new jobs but postponed adding the backlog **until the team had reviewed its status definitions**."
**Changed support:** whose assertion, and modality. Vale's answer to "what would you change?" is a statement of her own approach in the conditional; the manager's postponement names a condition — a completed review — with no duration and no stated outcome. The heading converts both into the draft's own forward account in which a further week of wording work is what stands between the workshop and the backlog, and after it the move is permitted ("can move").
**Case in which the draft fails and the source holds:** the review of the status definitions runs four weeks because two of the four trained staff are away in March, and it ends with the team splitting "ready for work" into two statuses and the manager deciding the backlog will not be entered this year. Vale's sentence and the postponement sentence are both still exactly true; "One more week on the wording, then the backlog can move" is false in both halves. Nothing supplied fixes the review at a week or promises the move follows it.
**Smallest supported repair:** attribute the week and drop the permission — for example "Vale would give the status names another week before the backlog moves", or "Another week on the status names, Vale says, before the backlog is added".

### Finding 4 — the title states the summary's bounded past report as an unattributed present fact (PLAUSIBLE)

**Draft:** title, line 8: "Fenwick sees which instruments are waiting for parts".
**Source:** "The workshop's trial summary, dated 23 February 2026, records 38 jobs entered in the schedule. **It says** staff **could see** which instruments **were waiting** for parts." Plus the two limits the material sets: "postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions", and "The summary and interview establish the team's **reported experience**".
**Changed support:** whose assertion (the summary's report becomes the publication's own), time (a past state during a six-week trial becomes a standing present one) and scope (the 38 jobs entered in the schedule become Fenwick's instruments without restriction).
**Case in which the draft fails and the source holds:** a cornet in the backlog is awaiting a spring. Because the backlog was never entered, it appears in no status in the schedule, and nobody at Fenwick can see from the schedule that it is waiting for parts. The summary's statement about the 38 entered jobs remains exactly as supplied; the title, read in the present and without restriction, does not hold. No supplied statement says the backlog was entered or that the schedule covers every instrument.
**Smallest supported repair:** bound the claim to the trial and keep the summary's tense — "In the trial, Fenwick saw which instruments were waiting for parts", or "Fenwick's trial summary: staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts".

## 3. Editorial questions (not findings)

These are places where the reading is settled enough to be defensible as written, or where the line is genuinely unsettled. No repair is proposed.

1. **"Asked … what had to be settled first" (line 20)** compresses the supplied question "What did the team have to settle before the schedule was useful?". The draft's "first" keeps the priority relation but drops the endpoint (usefulness) and could be read as asking for the first item in a sequence rather than for everything that had to precede. Vale's answer names one thing, and the article's own last line supplies the sequence, so the reading holds; whether to restore "before the schedule was useful" is an editorial call.
2. **"Her verdict comes with a condition and a delay" (line 32)** names a condition. Vale imposes no condition on using the schedule again; the "condition" available to an ordinary reader is the scope limit "for new jobs", which is hers, and the delay is her "another week". Defensible as written, though "a limit and a delay" would track the quotation more closely.
3. **"Collection arrangements stayed on the telephone" (line 18)** may be read as exclusivity. The material's "The team continued to use telephone calls to discuss collection arrangements with customers" supports the unchanged channel and the draft adds no claim that the schedule was kept out of the subject, so no case stands; the dropped "with customers" changes nothing.
4. **"The workshop wrote the definitions" (line 16)** for "The workshop defined the statuses". The material supplies the definitions as an object of its own ("its status definitions"), so authorship is supported; only the medium (written or not) is unstated, and the heading does not turn on it.
5. **"replaced two paper sheets" (line 10)** asserts the sheets stopped. The supplied "Previously, jobs awaiting parts and jobs ready for work appeared on separate paper sheets" places them before the schedule and excludes the parallel-running case, so this is not reported.
