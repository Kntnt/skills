## Findings

### Finding 1 — source support: "replaced" overstates what the schedule took over (deck, line 10)

- **Draft:** "A six-week trial at Fenwick Instrument Repairs replaced two paper sheets with one shared bench schedule."
- **Source:** "Previously, jobs awaiting parts and jobs ready for work appeared on separate paper sheets." / "The workshop's trial summary, dated 23 February 2026, records 38 jobs entered in the schedule." / "The manager approved continued use for new jobs but postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions."
- **What is missing or changed:** the material supports that the sheets held the jobs *previously* and that 38 jobs were entered in the schedule, with the backlog deliberately not added. It nowhere says the sheets stopped being used. "Replaced ... with" asserts that the schedule took the sheets' place.
- **Case in which the draft fails and the source holds:** the workshop enters its 38 new jobs in the schedule during the six weeks while its backlog — the jobs whose addition the manager postponed — continues to sit on the same two paper sheets, which are still in daily use at the end of the trial. Every supplied statement remains true: the sheets are where the jobs appeared previously, 38 jobs were entered, and the backlog was not added. The deck's claim that the trial replaced the sheets is false in that case.
- **Case in the other direction:** a trial could replace the two sheets entirely — every job, backlog included, moved across — and then the supplied sentence "postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions" would be false. So the two statements are not interchangeable in either direction.
- **Set-aside test:** no supplied statement excludes the first case; "postponed adding the backlog" points towards it rather than away from it.
- **Smallest supported repair:** "A six-week trial at Fenwick Instrument Repairs moved jobs from two paper sheets onto one shared bench schedule." (Keeps the duration, the workshop, the two sheets and the one schedule; drops only the claim that the sheets were superseded.)

### Finding 2 — source support: the heading states the summary's say-so as established fact (line 24)

- **Draft:** "## Staff could see the waiting jobs; the summary measures no repair time"
- **Source:** "It says staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts." / "The summary and interview establish the team's reported experience, not a measured improvement in repair speed or a recommendation for every workshop."
- **What is missing or changed:** whose assertion it is. The material reports this as something the workshop's own trial summary states, and says expressly that the summary establishes reported experience. The heading's first clause drops that attribution and asserts the visibility directly, while the second clause in the same heading names the summary — so the reader takes the first clause as the article's own statement rather than the summary's. The body sentence attributes correctly ("says staff could see"), which makes the heading the only place the qualification is lost; a qualification must be preserved wherever the claim appears.
- **Case in which the draft fails and the source holds:** the workshop's summary records that staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts, but two of the four trained staff never opened the schedule during the six weeks and went on asking at the bench. The supplied statements all remain true — the summary does say it, and it is the team's reported experience. The heading's flat assertion that staff could see the waiting jobs is false for those staff.
- **Case in the other direction:** staff could in fact all see the waiting jobs while the summary said nothing about visibility; then the supplied "It says staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts" would be false.
- **Set-aside test:** no supplied statement verifies the visibility independently of the summary; the closing paragraph explicitly limits the summary and interview to "the team's reported experience".
- **Smallest supported repair:** "## The summary: staff could see the waiting jobs, and no repair time" — or, keeping the draft's two-clause shape, "## The summary says staff could see the waiting jobs; it measures no repair time".
