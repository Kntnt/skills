# Fenwick keeps its shared schedule for new jobs

Jobs split across separate paper sheets went onto one shared bench schedule at Fenwick Instrument Repairs, in a six-week trial with the supplier Benchline. What had to be settled first was what the statuses meant — and the backlog was still waiting on that.

By Thomas Barregren

Fenwick Instrument Repairs services brass and woodwind instruments. In January 2026 its team began a six-week trial of a shared bench schedule from Benchline, a scheduling software supplier, chosen after checking that it could distinguish jobs awaiting parts from jobs ready for work.

## The team agreed the terms before Benchline configured them

The workshop defined the statuses itself and decided who would update them. Benchline configured those choices and trained four staff in one online session. Collection arrangements stayed on the telephone.

Benchline's case writer asked workshop manager Priya Vale by email what the team had to settle before the schedule was useful. Vale answered: ‘We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench.’

## Neither document measures repair speed

A workshop summary dated 23 February 2026 records 38 jobs entered in the schedule, and says staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts.

It does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction. The summary and the interview carry the team's reported experience, not a measured improvement in repair speed.

## Vale would allow an extra week for the status names

The manager approved continued use for new jobs, and postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions. Asked whether the choice would be made again, and what would change, Vale answered: ‘I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog.’
