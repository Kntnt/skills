## Person, number and natural gender

Accounted separately from referent identification.

- `its team`, `its status definitions`, `it could distinguish`, `It says`, `It does not measure` — third person singular inanimate, referring to the workshop, the schedule and the summary. The material uses the same person and number for the same referents ("The team chose Benchline after checking that **it** could distinguish these statuses"; "**It** says staff could see"). ## Considered and not reported

Recorded so the absence of a finding is legible, with the concrete case tested in each direction.

**A. Title in the present tense (row 1).** Case tested: the manager approved continued use on or before 23 February 2026, and the workshop dropped the schedule in April, so *Fenwick keeps its shared schedule* fails while the approval still holds. The material is silent after 2 March 2026, so nothing excludes that case in the abstract. It is not a defect: the claim is reported as of the material, by the party with authority over it, and Vale's "I would use it again for new jobs" on 2 March is the later of the two data points and points the same way. Rendering an approved continuation in the present is defensible as written.

**B. Standfirst and the backlog (row 2).** Case tested: all 38 entered jobs were new arrivals, every job already on the paper sheets is the backlog, and none of the backlog was migrated — so *jobs split across separate paper sheets went onto one shared bench schedule* fails of those particular jobs. Set aside: the supplied statement "It says staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts" establishes that both of the two statuses that had been split across sheets are held in the one schedule, which is what the sentence claims; and the same standfirst says the backlog was still waiting, so no reader takes it as a migration claim.

**C. The second interview question (row 23).** Case tested: *whether the choice would be made again* could be answered for the workshop while Vale personally would not repeat it, so the impersonal rendering holds where "Would you make the same choice again" fails. Set aside: the supplied answer is "I would use it again for new jobs", first person, and the draft prints it immediately after the frame *Vale answered*, so the questioned party and the answering party are settled in the draft as they are in the material. The smallest alternative repair — naming Vale in the indirect question — is available but is a preference, not a correction, and the agentless form avoids asserting a natural gender the material does not supply.

**D. Omissions.** The interview date of 2 March 2026, Vale's approval of publication, the word *trial* in the summary's name, "with customers" at row 13, and "or a recommendation for every workshop" at row 20 are all dropped. None of them changes a claim the draft makes; what is unclaimed is not thereby denied, and the quotation guidance's point about approval — that a draft may not treat absent approval as a solved problem — does not require a draft to state approval that exists.
