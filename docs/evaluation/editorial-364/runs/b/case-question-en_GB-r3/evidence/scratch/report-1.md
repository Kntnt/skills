## C. Findings (source support)

### Finding 1 — "The six weeks produced no performance figures" makes a document's silence into a fact about the trial

- **Draft** (heading, line 15): "The six weeks produced no performance figures"
- **Material**: "It does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction." — where "It" is "The workshop's trial summary, dated 23 February 2026". The material also says: "The summary and interview establish the team's reported experience, not a measured improvement in repair speed or a recommendation for every workshop."
- **What differs**: whose knowledge is at stake, and scope. The material reports that one document does not measure three named things. The heading reports that the six-week period itself yielded no performance figures of any kind.
- **Case where the material holds and the draft fails**: during the six weeks Fenwick's till and job-card system recorded revenue and average repair duration for the period, and the workshop simply did not put them in the trial summary. Nothing in the material excludes this — its only statement about measurement is about what the summary does and does not contain, and the closing note is likewise about what "the summary and interview establish", not about what was measured anywhere. Under that case, "It does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction" is true and "The six weeks produced no performance figures" is false.
- **Case where the draft holds and the material's statement fails**: none needed; the direction above is enough.
- **Missing support**: any statement that no performance figures exist for the trial period.
- **Smallest repair**: "The summary produced no performance figures" — or, staying closer still to the material, "The summary records no performance figures". Either keeps the section's point and the draft's plain register while returning the absence to the document that the material speaks about.

### Finding 2 — "two paper sheets" puts a number on the old arrangement that the material does not give

- **Draft** (standfirst, line 3): "Jobs split across two paper sheets went onto one shared bench schedule..."
- **Material**: "Previously, jobs awaiting parts and jobs ready for work appeared on separate paper sheets."
- **What differs**: the thing counted. "separate paper sheets" states that the two statuses were kept apart on paper; it states no total.
- **Case where the material holds and the draft fails**: the workshop kept one sheet for jobs awaiting parts and a sheet per bench for jobs ready for work — three sheets in all, with the two statuses still on separate sheets. This is compatible with every supplied statement; no supplied sentence gives a sheet count or says there were exactly two. Under that case, "jobs awaiting parts and jobs ready for work appeared on separate paper sheets" is true and "two paper sheets" is false.
- **Missing support**: a sheet count.
- **Smallest repair**: "Jobs split across separate paper sheets..." — one word changed, and the contrast with "one shared bench schedule" survives intact.

### Finding 3 — "the backlog is still waiting" asserts a present state past the end of the material

- **Draft** (standfirst, line 3): "...and the backlog is still waiting on that."
- **Material**: "The manager approved continued use for new jobs but postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions."; Vale, in an interview "on 2 March 2026": "I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog."
- **What differs**: time. The material's latest word on the backlog is 2 March 2026, and it is Vale describing a check she would give another week. The draft asserts the wait as continuing at the time of reading.
- **Case where the material holds and the draft fails**: the team spent the week Vale described on the status names, settled them, and entered the backlog on 9 March 2026. Nothing in the material excludes this — the postponement is explicitly conditional ("until the team had reviewed its status definitions"), and Vale's answer treats the review as about a week's work. Under that case, every supplied sentence remains true and "the backlog is still waiting on that" is false.
- **Note on the title**: "Fenwick keeps its shared schedule for new jobs" is not reported alongside this. An approval of continued use is a standing arrangement that persists unless something changes it, so the present tense there names the state the material records; the standfirst instead asserts the non-occurrence of a specific, imminent step that the material's own last speaker puts about a week out.
- **Smallest repair**: "...and the backlog was still waiting on that." One word, and the sentence then reports the position the material actually fixes.

## E. Considered and not reported

These were tested and found defensible as written; they are recorded so the same ground is not walked twice.

1. **"Jobs split across ... paper sheets went onto one shared bench schedule"** (standfirst). A reader could take this as saying the previously-split jobs were migrated, which sits awkwardly beside the postponed backlog. But the sentence reads equally as a generic statement of what the trial changed for the jobs in question, and the very next clause tells the reader the backlog is outstanding, so the reading that would be a defect is not the one the standfirst's own context supports. Reported here rather than as a finding with a repair. (The sheet count within the same sentence is Finding 2.)
2. **"What had to be settled first was what the statuses meant"** — a plural where Vale named one status ("what ready for work meant"). The plural is independently carried by "The workshop defined the statuses and decided who would update them" and by "until the team had reviewed its status definitions", so no widening is left unsupported.
3. **"Collection arrangements stayed on the telephone"** — tested for implied exclusivity (that nothing about collections moved into the schedule). "The team continued to use telephone calls to discuss collection arrangements with customers" states exactly that continuation, and the draft asserts no more than continuation.
4. **"The workshop defined the statuses itself"** — the emphatic adds no claim beyond the division of labour the material states between workshop and supplier.
5. **"and" for the material's "but"** in A14 — a lost contrast marker, not a lost qualification; the condition on the backlog is carried in full.
6. **Dropped from A12: "or a recommendation for every workshop"** — an exclusion the draft nowhere breaches, so its omission changes no claim.
7. **Omitted interview date (2 March 2026) and quote approval** — omissions assert nothing, and the draft neither claims nor denies approval, which the quotation guidance places outside the text's reach.
8. **Byline "By Thomas Barregren"** — supplied by the invocation; its absence from the material is no finding, per the task.
