## 2. F1 — source support

**Pass**, with three cautions named below, none of which I count as an unsupported assertion.

Claim by claim, everything load-bearing is carried by the material:

- "services brass and woodwind instruments" — verbatim.
- "In January 2026 its team began a six-week trial" — date, duration and actor verbatim ("the workshop team").
- "a shared bench schedule from Benchline, a scheduling software supplier" — "from Benchline" is carried by "The team chose Benchline" plus "Benchline configured those choices"; the descriptor is the brief's own words.
- "chosen after checking that it could distinguish jobs awaiting parts from jobs ready for work" — the anaphor "these statuses" is expanded into the material's own two terms, in the material's own order; nothing could fall under one term and not the other here.
- "The workshop defined the statuses itself and decided who would update them. Benchline configured those choices and trained four staff in one online session." — verbatim but for the emphatic "itself", which asserts no more than the division of labour the next clause states. Counts (four, one) are given bare, with no evaluative characterisation — correct, since the material supplies no comparison.
- "38 jobs entered in the schedule" — bare count, no characterisation. Correct.
- "A workshop summary dated 23 February 2026 **records** … and **says** staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts" — the reporting verbs are kept, so the visibility claim stays the summary's and is not upgraded into the article's own voice. This is the single most important thing the draft gets right.
- "It does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction." — verbatim, all three items.
- "Neither document measures repair speed" (heading) — carried jointly by the summary's non-measurement and by "The summary and interview establish the team's reported experience, not a measured improvement in repair speed". An absence stated about the documents, not about the world.
- "The manager approved continued use for new jobs, and postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions." — scope ("for new jobs"), actor and condition intact; only "but" → "and", which loses a contrast marker and no qualification.
- No causal verb and no causal hedge appears anywhere; no sequence in the piece is turned into a cause. Chronology ("before Benchline configured them", "after checking") is entailed by the material's own ordering. Vale's conditional "would" survives in both the heading and the quotation. No scene, spoken exchange, expression or emotional reaction appears — the material expressly excludes all four, and the draft stays documentary throughout. No recommendation for workshops generally appears.
- Unknown is kept apart from absent: the draft says the documents do not measure these things; it nowhere says the trial produced no result, and nowhere says the backlog was never added.

**Caution 1 — the byline.** "By Thomas Barregren" is an attribution the material does not carry; the brief says in terms "No author name or call to action is supplied." G2 requires a byline, so the draft had to put something there, and `response.md` discloses the substitution plainly and tells the user to change it before publication. Both checkers ruled it out of scope as coming from the invocation. I record it as a disclosed addition rather than a defect, but it is the one line in the piece a reader could not trace to the package.

**Caution 2 — the standfirst's migration reading.** "Jobs split across separate paper sheets went onto one shared bench schedule at Fenwick Instrument Repairs" can be read as saying the jobs that had been on those sheets were moved into the schedule. On the material, those jobs are largely the backlog, and the backlog was explicitly *not* added. The generic reading — this is the kind of work that now sits on one schedule — is supported (the summary has 38 jobs entered and staff seeing which instruments awaited parts, so both statuses do live in the one schedule), and the second half of the same sentence tells the reader the backlog was still waiting, which closes the wrong reading before it sets. Both checkers tested this and set it aside; I agree it is loose rather than false, but it is the draft's weakest sentence.

**Caution 3 — a dropped exclusion.** The material's "not a measured improvement in repair speed **or a recommendation for every workshop**" becomes "not a measured improvement in repair speed". The dropped half is a caveat, and dropping a caveat is normally an F1 matter; here the draft breaches it nowhere — it generalises to no other workshop at any point — so nothing the reader takes away is wrong. Worth restoring, not worth failing.

Nothing else: no changed subject, no changed scope, date, modality or certainty, no invented event, no invented personal attribute (notably, the draft uses **no third-person pronoun for Vale anywhere**, so it asserts no gender the material withholds).

## 3. G2 — required parts

**Pass**, with one structural weakness.

- **Headline** "Fenwick keeps its shared schedule for new jobs" — states the outcome with its scope attached. Does its own job.
- **Standfirst** — carries the change, the trial and the unresolved backlog; does not merely repeat the headline.
- **Byline** — present (see Caution 1).
- **Lead** — the opening paragraph places the workshop, the date, the duration and the reason for choosing; distinct from the standfirst.
- **Three sections**, each with a statement heading doing a different job: how the work was divided, what the documents do not show, what the manager would change.
- **Order** is headline → standfirst → byline → lead → sections → close, as required.

Customer-account elements: **situation** (two statuses on separate paper sheets), **action** (six-week trial, the workshop defining statuses and deciding who updates them, four staff trained in one session), **results** (38 jobs entered, staff able to see what awaited parts — and, stated outright, no measured repair, revenue or satisfaction figure), **appraisal** (Vale would use it again for new jobs; would allow another week first). The **publisher stance is truthful**: Benchline is named as the supplier, and the interview is attributed to "Benchline's case writer", so the reader is told who is asking. The **customer is left an acting party** throughout — Fenwick chose, checked, defined, decided and approved; Benchline configured and trained. This is the sharpest thing the draft does, and it is the failure mode this genre usually shows.

**Weakness — the ending.** There is no close doing a job of its own: the final section ends on Vale's quotation, so the last section and the ending are the same paragraph. And there is **no call to action**, because the brief supplies no offer, link or contact route to build one from. That is a material gap, not an invention, and `response.md` names it explicitly and asks for the step and the link. Inventing a CTA here would have been the worse failure. I mark G2 a pass on that basis, with the missing distinct ending noted.

## 4. L1 — language

**Pass.** The prose reads as British general-interest journalism, not as a translation or as American copy in British spelling.

- Register is understated in the British way, with the claim left to carry itself: "Neither document measures repair speed"; "Collection arrangements stayed on the telephone." No intensifier appears anywhere, which is right for the resolved language.
- Idiom is native: "on the telephone" (not "on the phone with"), "four staff" as a count of people, "went onto one shared bench schedule", "allow another week".
- Syntax is English-native throughout — no fronted adverbial chains, no nominalised constructions, no imported phrasing.
- Headings are plain statement-form and each earns its section; the standfirst's spaced em dash carries the turn cleanly.
- Quotation marks are single as the outer mark, consistently, which is the majority British house style (a mechanic, listed here only to note it is not an L1 issue).

Reader effect: a workshop manager reads this as a trade piece written by somebody who knows the trade and is not selling to them.

## 5. Bridges into quotations

Two quotations, in order.

**Quotation 1.**
Bridge: *"Benchline's case writer asked workshop manager Priya Vale by email what the team had to settle before the schedule was useful. Vale answered:"*
Quotation: *"We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench."*
**Class (b).** The words that put it there: "Benchline's case writer asked … by email" (attribution and occasion) and "what the team had to settle before the schedule was useful" (the question, not its answer). The bridge poses; the quotation answers. Nothing the quotation delivers — that the term at issue was *ready for work*, and that a part in the building need not be at the right bench — is pre-delivered here.
*Second reading:* it could be read towards (a), because the standfirst has already said "What had to be settled first was what the statuses meant". I chose (b): the standfirst is not the bridge, and even taken together the pre-delivery stops at the topic, whereas the quotation supplies the specific term and the bench distinction. The speech tag "Vale answered" carries attribution only.

**Quotation 2.**
Bridge: *"Asked whether the choice would be made again, and what would change, Vale answered:"*
Quotation: *"I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog."*
**Class (b).** The words that put it there: "Asked whether … and what would change" (the question as put) and "Vale answered" (attribution, nothing more).
*Second reading, and it is a real one:* if the bridge is taken to include the sentence immediately before it — "The manager approved continued use for new jobs, and postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions." — the class becomes **(c)**, because that sentence carries the postponement condition, which the quotation does not carry. I chose (b) on the brief's own definition: the clause standing immediately before the quotation and leading into it is the "Asked whether…" clause. What deserves recording separately is that the **section heading** "Vale would allow an extra week for the status names" states the quotation's second sentence in advance, and the preceding sentence states its first in documentary form — so the reader arrives at the quotation with both halves already in hand. That is the class-(a) hazard sitting one layer above the bridge, and it is the draft's main quotation weakness even though no bridge is class (a).

No quotation stands with no bridge.

**Counts:** two bridges, both class (b); none class (a) and none class (c) on the immediate-clause reading.
**Interviewer utterances:** the draft attributes to the interviewer only the two questions the material itself carries, rendered indirectly and without content added ("what the team had to settle before the schedule was useful"; "whether the choice would be made again, and what would change") — no question or remark is put in the interviewer's mouth that the package does not supply.
**Unsupported bridge assertions:** none — "by email" rests on "an email interview", and "Benchline's case writer" on "between Benchline's case writer and workshop manager Priya Vale".

## 6. Quoted speech

Both quotations are **word for word**, and the whole of what the material supplies is used.

- Quotation 1 keeps the hedge "did **not always** mean", which is the entire force of the sentence; removing it would have manufactured a rule out of a tendency. The distinctive image — a part in the building that has not reached the right bench — is untouched, not smoothed into the surrounding register.
- Quotation 2 keeps both conditionals ("I **would** use it again", "I **would** allow another week"), so a stated intention is not hardened into a decision taken. The implicit referent of "it" is left implicit inside the marks and settled by the surrounding text, which is what the guidance asks.
- Neither quotation is welded from separate remarks; neither is truncated; no fluent interpretation is supplied where the speaker was plain.
- Meaning, stance (measured approval with a reservation), degree of certainty and speaker's voice are all where the source left them.
- **Nothing is missing.** The material offers exactly two Vale answers, publication of the extract was approved, and both appear in full.
