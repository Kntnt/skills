## 3. Source-support findings

### S1 — The heading drops the scope of the median that was measured

- **Draft (line 23):** "## Mediantiden var lägre under testet – orsaken är öppen"
- **Source:** "Median time **from report to assignment** was two working days during the trial and three in the preceding eight-week period."
- **What differs:** the thing measured. The source measures one interval only — report to assignment. The heading says "Mediantiden" with a definite article and no qualifier, and it is the draft's first mention of any median, so the definite has no earlier antecedent in the text to attach to. Read as an ordinary reader reads a section heading, it says that the median time of the repair process was lower during the trial.
- **Concrete case, compatible with the material, in which the source statement holds and the draft's fails:** during the trial the team assigned jobs faster but, with a heavier or differently composed workload, took longer on average to finish them — median completion time rose from six to nine working days. The sourced claim (report→assignment fell from three days to two) is true in this case; "mediantiden var lägre under testet" is false in it.
- **Tested in the other direction too:** median completion time could have fallen while assignment time did not, so neither statement entails the other.
- **Can the case be set aside by a supplied statement?** No. The nearest candidate is "There are no cost, resident-satisfaction or **completion-time** measurements", which does the opposite of excluding the case: it establishes that the material knows nothing about completion times and therefore cannot rule the case out. The material's judging note names this exact drift — "Do not turn assignment time into completion time."
- **Missing support:** a source passage licensing an unqualified "the median time". There is none; the only median in the material is qualified.
- **Smallest supported repair:** restore the qualifier in the heading, leaving everything else as written —
  `## Mediantiden till tilldelning var lägre under testet – orsaken är öppen`
  (or `## Mediantiden från anmälan till tilldelning var lägre under testet – orsaken är öppen`). The body sentence D23 already carries the qualifier and needs no change.

No other source-support finding. The remaining draft passages either rest on a source passage with the thing named, the subject, scope, time, modality, certainty and asserting party preserved, or are addressed below.

## 4. Translation findings (quotations)

Checked separately from source support: same meaning, stance, certainty and distinctive voice, expressed in idiomatic Swedish speech rather than English syntax.

**Q1 (line 19)** — "Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var våra – Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen."
Against: "We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log."
Meaning, stance and certainty carried whole; the past perfect "redan hade gjort" matches "had already done"; the possessive emphasis of "Kategorierna var våra" matches "were ours"; the semicolon becomes a dash, which is how Swedish sets this juxtaposition in reported speech. Compounds written solid (*kvällsskiftet*, *morgonskiftet*), V2 respected. Nothing additive. **No finding.**

**Q2 (line 21)** — "Vi lade mer tid på att komma överens om kategorierna än på att registrera de första anmälningarna. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan nästa hus drar i gång."
Against: "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts."
The comparison, the conditional ("skulle"), and the forward-looking advice are intact. Fronting "Den tiden" with the subject behind the verb is idiomatic Swedish emphasis, not English word order; "drar i gång" is ordinary spoken Swedish for a building's start and does not add meaning. Double definiteness ("de första anmälningarna") correct. **No finding.**

**Q3 (line 33)** — "Jag skulle välja att göra testet igen. Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser."
Against: "I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation."
The three moves of the original — endorsement, a present-tense benefit, and the "but" that qualifies it — are all present and in order, so the qualified assessment the material insists on ("Her final quotation is her actual, qualified assessment") survives. "En samlad bild av anmälningarna" was tested against "one view of the reports": in this context, where the log exists so that "the evening shift [can] see what the morning shift had already done", a consolidated view of the reports in one place is what "one view" names, and I can identify no concrete obstruction to that reading — only alternative wordings one might prefer. The draft also uses two distinct verbs, *avsätta* in Q2 and *lägga in* here, matching the source's own *set aside* and *leave*. **No finding.**

**Attribution verb** — *skriver* rather than *säger* is not merely permissible but the accurate signal, since "All interviews occurred by email". It neither adds nor removes certainty. **No finding.**

No quotation is welded together from separate remarks, no hedge is removed or added, no filler-free sentence is sharpened, and each of the three quotations carries experience the surrounding narrative does not (the shift-handover motive, the category-agreement cost, and the qualified would-do-it-again). **No translation findings.**

## 5. Considered and set aside

Each of these was tested for a concrete case in which the draft's statement fails while the sourced one holds, and none produced one; they are recorded so the reasoning is visible, not as defects.

- **"Hösten 2025" for "In September 2025"** (D3) — the trial cannot fall outside autumn 2025 given an eight-week trial, a preceding eight-week period, and a note dated 4 December 2025 reporting both.
- **"Syftet var" for "The team wanted"** (D8) — the team is the deciding party in the immediately preceding sentence, and the material asserts the want in its own voice.
- **"Elm Quay valde" for "It [the team] chose"** (D9) — the brief requires Elm Quay to remain the acting party, and the team is "its own maintenance team".
- **"Akuta ärenden" for "emergencies"** (D22) — the established Swedish term in this field; no case in this material falls under one and not the other.
- **"Mättes inte" for "There are no … measurements"** (D25) — the material states this absolutely, in contrast to "No comparison with another supplier **is available**", and the draft mirrors both phrasings distinctly.
- **"Beslutet … dröjer"** (D26) — carries "still to come", which "has not yet expanded" plus a precondition ("will decide after checking") supports; it asserts no missed deadline that the material contradicts.
- **Heading "ett eget test" beside the eight-week "testet"** — a reader could in principle read the heading as saying the supplier was chosen after the eight-week trial, which would invert the chronology; the indefinite "ett eget test" and the sentence directly beneath it ("Efter den prövningen valde teamet Svale Systems") settle it before the reader can act on the misreading. Defensible as written.
- **Median figures stated in the draft's own voice** (D23) — the source states them in its own voice too, one sentence after naming the note, and the draft re-anchors to "noteringen" in the next sentence.

## 6. Editorial questions (reported as such, not as defects)

**E1 — Whose checklist it is.** The draft writes "Svale Systems implementeringschecklista"; the brief writes only "the supplied implementation checklist at https://example.invalid/svale/checklist" and never names its author. Ownership by Svale is the natural reading of supplier-published material pointing at a `/svale/` URL, and the genitive is formed correctly for a name ending in *s*. Whether that inference should be spelled out in the draft's own voice is an editorial call for the commissioning party, not a defect with a repair; the alternative ("Implementeringschecklistan är ett dokument att läsa igenom") loses nothing but also gains nothing the material settles.
