# Judgement — case-study-en_US-r2

## 1. Outcome

**Delivered.** Two source comparisons ran (`report-1.md` against `draft-under-check.md`, `report-2.md` against `draft-under-check-2.md`). The delivered prose is **identical** to the last prose a checker saw: `delivered.md` below its frontmatter matches `evidence/source-check/draft-under-check-2.md` exactly, to the character. The run delivered with one checker finding open and disclosed in `response.md`, and declined its repair on the stated ground that the prose must be exactly what the final comparison read. Length 449 words against the brief's "approximately 400", disclosed.

## 2. F1 — **FAIL**

The body's factual spine is accurate and careful: the count and its exclusions, the two-versus-three working days with the workload confounder and the note's explicit non-attribution, the three absences, the non-expansion and the pending decision, the no-comparison statement, and the "Assignment is not completion" guard are all faithful and in the right voices. No causal claim is made or hedged anywhere. The failure is not in the body's figures; it is in an attribution the run exempted from checking, and in one scope slip.

**Unsupported additions and changed attributions:**

1. **The byline: "By Thomas Barregren."** The brief states, in as many words, **"No author name supplied."** A byline is an attribution, and this one asserts that a named individual wrote the case. The material names exactly two people-shaped parties — Maya Lind and the corporate actors — and no author. This is an invented personal attribution placed in the text's most load-bearing attribution slot. It is aggravated by the material's own statement that **"The supplier approved publication of this case as supplier-published material; it is not independent journalism"**: a personal byline standing alone, with no publisher line anywhere in the draft, carries the implication of independently authored journalism, which is the one thing the material says this is not. Both checkers ruled it out of assessment by fiat ("comes from the invocation, not the material, and is not assessed"; "its absence from the material is not a finding"). That exemption is not available on the source: the brief made the absence of an author name an explicit supplied fact, not a silence. `response.md` does disclose the byline and tells the reader to correct it, which mitigates but does not repair the delivered text.

2. **"The team's internal note on the eight-week trial counts 31 reports"** (lead). The material names the note's owner once, as **"Elm Quay's internal trial note, 4 December 2025"**, and says nothing about who wrote or kept it. "The team's" narrows ownership to the maintenance team. The material is compatible with the company's administration having compiled a note about a trial the team ran, in which case the draft's possessive is false and the material's is true. Unknown has been turned into known. The draft uses the supported form nine lines later ("Elm Quay's internal trial note of 4 December 2025"), so the two forms sit in one text. This is checker report-2's F1, correctly found and rejected.

3. **"What it has so far is one shared view of its repairs"** (ending). "its" refers to Elm Quay Housing, a company managing 640 flats; the shared log ran in two buildings and **"has not yet expanded"**. Read as written, the clause hands the reader one shared view of Elm Quay's repairs. The preceding half-sentence states the non-expansion, and the deck and lead both fix two buildings, so context pulls against the over-broad reading — but the sentence is the article's summary verdict, the place a scanning operations manager stops, and it is the one sentence in the piece where the two-building scope is dropped from a statement of what the customer now has. Both checkers raised this and both declined to call it a defect (report-1 §3; report-2 EQ1). I call it a real if second-order scope widening, and note that report-2's own proposed wording — "one shared view of the repairs in those two buildings" — would have closed it at no cost.

**Weaker points examined and passed:**

- "The trial produced no cost figures, no resident-satisfaction measurements and no completion times" against **"There are no cost, resident-satisfaction or completion-time measurements."** The draft moves from an existential absence to an absence of production. In this material the two coincide, and the draft states absence as absence rather than as a negative result. Passed, narrowly.
- "The team designed the categories itself" against "The maintenance team designed its categories" plus Lind's "The categories were ours". The emphatic "itself" is carried by the material's own division of labour. Passed.
- "The next document to read is Svale Systems' implementation checklist" against the brief's **"The optional next step is the supplied implementation checklist ... it is a document to read, not a consultation booking or a product trial."** The destination is described exactly right — a document to read, no booking, no trial. The word **"optional"** is dropped, and the section heading is an imperative ("Read the implementation checklist before you start"). This is a softening of the reader's freedom rather than a claim about the world, and Svale's ownership of the checklist follows from the brief's publisher statement and the URL path. Passed as an editorial matter, flagged.
- Chronology, the September 2025 date, the 4 December 2025 note date, 640 flats, two buildings, eight weeks, 31, two/three working days, six staff, two sessions and the URL all match the material exactly.
- Translation: none. Source and target are both English; no term crosses a language boundary in either direction.

## 3. G2 — **FAIL**

Present and in order: headline, standfirst, byline, lead, four sections, ending. The customer account's substance is mostly there — situation (640 flats, telephone reports and emails stored separately), action (chose the supplier, designed the categories, kept telephone reporting open, ran eight weeks in two buildings), results (31 reports with exclusions, median assignment times with the confounder and the non-attribution, three named absences), appraisal (Lind's qualified verdict with the reservation intact). **The customer is unmistakably the acting party**: Elm Quay decides, chooses, designs, keeps and runs; Svale configures and trains, in the third person throughout, with no first-person supplier voice anywhere. The call to action is built from the one supplied link and describes it as the brief describes it. Those parts work.

Two required jobs are not done:

1. **No truthful publisher stance.** The material states plainly that the supplier approved publication **"as supplier-published material; it is not independent journalism"**, and the brief states **"The publisher is the supplier, Svale Systems."** Nothing in the delivered text tells the reader this. Svale Systems appears three times — as the supplier chosen, as the party that configured and trained, and as the owner of a checklist — and never as the publisher of what the reader is reading. The slot where that stance would live is instead occupied by a personal byline. Reader effect: an operations manager finishes a piece that reads as an independent trade write-up which happens to end on a supplier's checklist, and is never told whose publication it is. This is the run's largest single miss, and no checker looked for it.

2. **The standfirst, lead and first section do not perform distinct jobs.** The standfirst runs "Elm Quay Housing manages 640 flats and stored telephone repair reports and emails separately. For eight weeks its maintenance team ran a shared log in two buildings instead. Here is what it set up, what its trial note counted and what its supervisor would change." The lead then runs "In September 2025, Elm Quay Housing's maintenance team decided to test a shared repair log in two of its buildings. ... The team's internal note on the eight-week trial counts 31 reports, and its supervisor's verdict is qualified." The standfirst promises the note's count and the supervisor's verdict; the lead delivers the same two items one paragraph later, adding only the month. Then section 1 opens "Elm Quay Housing manages 640 flats, and telephone reports and emails had been stored separately" — the standfirst's first sentence again, near word for word. In a 449-word piece the reader is told the flat count twice, the separate storage twice, and the two-building trial three times before reaching a single new fact. The standfirst's job (tell me why to read on) and the lead's job (start the account) have collapsed into one job done twice.

## 4. L1 — **PASS**

The prose is idiomatic American English with no imported syntax; source and target are both English, so no cross-language phrasing could arise, and none did. Collective-noun agreement is handled correctly throughout for the resolved locale — "The team designed the categories itself", "It wanted staff on different shifts" — with no British plural agreement anywhere. Sentence rhythm varies, the active voice dominates, and the hard passages ("Median time from report to assignment was two working days during the trial and three in the preceding eight-week period") read cleanly rather than as a data dump. "Assignment is not completion" is a genuinely good four-word paragraph opener: it does a precision job in a register a reader will accept.

Two blemishes, neither enough to fail: **"puts the aim beside the division of work"** is a strained construction that leaves the reader briefly parsing what has been put beside what, and **"Lind's account returns to the categories"** reads as a stage direction about the article rather than a sentence about the person. Both are the article talking about its own construction. "Flats" and "4 December 2025" are the material's own term and a date form, i.e. source fidelity and locale mechanics respectively, and are outside L1 by the brief's own instruction.

## 5. Bridges into quotations

**Quotation 1.**
- Bridge: "Maya Lind, the maintenance supervisor, puts the aim beside the division of work:"
- Quotation: "We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log."
- **Class (b).** The words that put it there: "Maya Lind, the maintenance supervisor" (attribution) and "the aim beside the division of work" (the two topics, named abstractly). *Two readings.* It could be read as (a), since "the aim" and "the division of work" announce the quotation's two halves in order, so a reader arriving at the quotation finds a structure already sketched. I choose (b) because the bridge delivers neither content: it never says what the aim was (seeing what the morning shift had done) nor how the work divided (the team's categories, Svale's implementation). The reader learns the quotation's topics, not its substance, and meets both facts for the first time inside the quotation marks. Naming the topics a quotation goes on to speak about is not class (a).

**Quotation 2.**
- Bridge: "Lind's account returns to the categories."
- Quotation: "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts."
- **Class (b).** The words that put it there: "Lind's account" (attribution) and "returns to the categories" (subject, and nothing else). The bridge does not say that agreeing the categories cost more time than entering the first reports, and does not say she would set that time aside next time. Both are the quotation's own work. No second reading available.

**Quotation 3.**
- Bridge: "Her verdict is qualified, and she does not extend it to every housing company."
- Quotation: "I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation."
- **Class (c).** The words that put it there: "she does not extend it to every housing company" — a fact the quotation does not carry. The quotation says "helps **us**" and stops; the limit on whom the recommendation reaches comes from the material's separate statement "Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company", not from her words. *Two readings.* The first clause, "Her verdict is qualified", leans (a): the quotation's "but I would leave an extra week for preparation" **is** the qualification, so the reader is told the shape of the verdict before meeting it. I choose (c) over (a) because the second clause supplies genuinely new information, and a bridge carrying a fact its quotation lacks is (c) whatever else it also does. Read as a whole, this is the draft's weakest bridge: it pre-announces the reservation and then the quotation arrives to confirm it.

**No quotation stands without a bridge.** All three are led into.

- **Per-class counts:** (a) 0, (b) 2, (c) 1.
- **Interviewer voice:** the draft attributes nothing to an interviewer and puts no question or utterance in an interviewer's mouth. There is no interviewer in the text at all — consistent with the material's statement that all interviews occurred by email.
- **Unsupported bridge assertions:** none. "puts the aim beside the division of work" is borne out by the quotation; "Lind's account returns to the categories" is borne out by quotations 1 and 2; "she does not extend it to every housing company" is carried by the material's "Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company", and the shift from past report to present standing position is licensed by the material's own "Her final quotation is her actual, qualified assessment."

## 6. Quoted speech

All three quotations are reproduced **character for character** from the material, verified by exact string match: word order, the semicolon in quotation 1, the comparative "more time ... than" in quotation 2, and the pivot "but" and present-tense "helps us" in quotation 3. Nothing was repaired, smoothed, welded, split or translated (source and target are both English).

- **Meaning:** unchanged in all three.
- **Stance:** unchanged. Lind's willingness ("I would choose to do the trial again") and her reservation ("but I would leave an extra week") both arrive intact and in the same sentence, so the reader cannot take the first without the second.
- **Certainty:** the conditional "would" survives in all three places it appears, and the surrounding narrative mirrors it rather than hardening it — the heading reads "Lind would do it again, with an extra week first", not "Lind will expand".
- **Reservation:** survives four times over — in the standfirst ("what its supervisor would change"), in the section heading, in the bridge ("Her verdict is qualified"), and in the quotation itself.
- **Voice:** preserved; nothing is lifted into the article's register.
- **Missing quotations:** none. All three quotations the material offers and permits are used, each in a distinct position, and each carries experience the narrative around it does not — the shift-handover motive in her own terms, the unanticipated cost of agreeing categories, and the qualified verdict with its condition.

One note on the narrative around them: the material states **"Her final quotation is her actual, qualified assessment. It is not an inference from the figures."** The draft respects this — the verdict is introduced by her standing position, not derived from the two-versus-three days — and the verdict paragraph is kept in its own section, away from the figures.

## 8. Stop or delivery

**A delivery that should not have happened.**

The reasoning that produced it is not careless — the run ran both comparisons, repaired both of the first report's findings, disclosed the second report's open finding with its repair, disclosed the byline with an instruction to correct it, disclosed the 449-against-400 overrun, and named what further material would close the case's gaps. On its own terms the delivery is orderly and honest.

But the text that went out is missing a required part. The material says in plain words that this is supplier-published material and not independent journalism, and the delivered case tells the reader neither. In its place stands a byline naming an author the brief says was not supplied — so the one attribution the reader is given is both unsupported and pointed the wrong way. Disclosing that in the covering reply does not fix a text that will be read without the reply.

The declined repair compounds it. The run's stated rule — deliver only prose a checker has read — is defensible in the abstract, but here it preserved a defect the run agreed was a defect, whose fix was one word, and whose correct form already appears elsewhere in the same draft. A rule that turns a free correction into a delivered fault is doing the opposite of its job; the right move was a third comparison, or delivery of the repaired line with the change named.

What should have gone out instead: the same text with "Elm Quay's internal note" in the lead, "the repairs in those two buildings" in the ending, a publisher line identifying Svale Systems as publisher of a supplier-published case, no personal byline, and the standfirst or the opening of section 1 rewritten so the two are not the same sentence. That is four small edits and one removal on a draft whose facts, quotations, reservations and non-causality are already sound.
