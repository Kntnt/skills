## 4. Findings

### F1 — The trial note is attributed to the maintenance team rather than to Elm Quay (source support)

**Draft (line 7):** "The team's internal note on the eight-week trial counts 31 reports, and its supervisor's verdict is qualified."

**Source:** "Elm Quay's internal trial note, 4 December 2025, says that 31 repair reports were entered."

**What differs:** whose document it is. The material names the note's owner once, as *Elm Quay's*, and never says who wrote or kept it. The draft's possessive makes it the maintenance team's own note. The draft itself uses the supported form nine lines later: "Elm Quay's internal trial note of 4 December 2025 records 31 repair reports."

**Test, both directions.** A case compatible with the supplied material: Elm Quay Housing's own administration compiled and issued the trial note of 4 December 2025, summarising a trial its maintenance team had run, and the team neither authored nor owned it. Under that case, "Elm Quay's internal trial note" holds and "the team's internal note" fails. In the converse direction, nothing supplied is falsified by the team having written it either — which is exactly the point: the material does not settle authorship, so the draft's narrower attribution is unsupported. No supplied statement excludes the case; the material is silent on who produced the note.

Note that this is the passage that carries the trial's one headline figure, so the attribution decides whose count the reader is being given. The Claims section requires exact attribution and requires that what is unknown stay unknown.

**Smallest supported repair (line 7):** replace "The team's internal note on the eight-week trial counts 31 reports" with "Elm Quay's internal note on the eight-week trial counts 31 reports". Nothing else in the sentence changes.

A second, softer instance of the same possessive stands in the deck (line 3): "what its trial note counted". There, "its" most nearly picks up "Elm Quay Housing" from the same sentence's opening and is defensible as written; no repair is proposed for it, but if F1 is applied, matching the deck to "Elm Quay's trial note" would cost nothing.

## 5. Editorial question (reported as a question, not as a defect)

### EQ1 — the scope of "one shared view of its repairs"

**Draft (line 33):** "Elm Quay's trial has not expanded yet; the team will decide after checking how the categories work for larger repairs. What it has so far is one shared view of its repairs and a supervisor who would run the trial again with more preparation."

**Material:** the shared log ran "in two buildings" of a company that "manages 640 flats"; "The trial has not yet expanded"; Lind: "Having one view of the reports helps us".

Read strictly, "one shared view of *its* repairs" could be taken as a view covering Elm Quay's repairs as a whole, and a case compatible with the material stands against that reading: repairs in the buildings outside the trial continue to arrive as telephone reports and emails stored separately, since the trial has not expanded. Read as an ordinary reader would in context, the clause is governed by the preceding half-sentence, which states the non-expansion outright, and by the deck and lead, which both state "two buildings"; on that reading the claim is the trial's yield and is supported by Lind's own presupposition. The line between an over-broad factual claim and a conclusion correctly scoped by its context is genuinely unsettled here, so it is reported as an editorial question rather than as a defect with a repair. Should the writer wish to close it, "one shared view of the repairs in those two buildings" would settle the scope without changing anything else.
