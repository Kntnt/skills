## 2. Findings

### Finding 1 — the lede drops the exclusions that define the count of 31, and states the filtered figure as the trial's output

- **Draft (line 7):** "The eight-week trial produced 31 logged reports and a qualified verdict from its supervisor."
- **Material:** "Elm Quay's internal trial note, 4 December 2025, says that 31 repair reports were entered. **It excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial.**"
- **What is changed:** 31 is a count after two exclusions. The draft's lede presents it without them as what the eight-week trial produced, which an ordinary reader takes as the number of reports the shared log received during the trial.
- **Concrete case, compatible with the material, in which the source statement holds and the draft's fails:** over the eight weeks, residents also phone in six emergency repairs — telephone reporting stayed open throughout — and these are entered in the shared log like any other. The log then holds thirty-seven entries for the trial, of which thirty-one remain once emergencies and pre-trial work are excluded. The note's statement holds exactly; "the eight-week trial produced 31 logged reports" is false. Nothing in the material excludes this case; the material's own sentence "It excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial" is what makes it available.
- **Reverse direction:** a case where the draft holds and the material fails is harder to construct, which is the asymmetry — the draft's sentence is the stronger claim.
- **Why it is a defect and not a preference:** the Claims section makes circumstantial detail a claim and requires a count to be given with its exclusions, and states that a length limit "cannot justify … loss of a qualification that changes a claim". The body carries the exclusions correctly at line 19, so the repair costs the draft nothing it has not already said.
- **Smallest supported repair:** "The eight-week trial produced 31 logged reports, excluding emergencies, and a qualified verdict from its supervisor."

### Finding 2 — a statement the material makes in its own voice is attributed to the trial note

- **Draft (line 21):** "**The note says** the two periods carried different workloads and explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software."
- **Material:** "**The periods had different workloads**, and **the note** explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software."
- **What is changed:** whose assertion it is. The material states the workload difference flatly, as a fact of the case, and attributes to the note only the abstention from a causal claim. The draft places both inside the note's assertion, making the document the source of a statement the material never puts there.
- **Concrete case, compatible with the material, in which the source statement holds and the draft's fails:** the differing workloads are established by the maintenance team's own scheduling records, and the trial note confines itself to the figures and to one sentence declining to attribute the difference to the software — it says nothing about workloads. The material's two clauses are then both true; "the note says the two periods carried different workloads" is false. No supplied statement excludes this case: the material never says what else the note contains.
- **Reverse direction:** if the note does say it, both statements hold — so the draft's version is true only on a reading the material does not settle, while the material's own wording is true either way.
- **Why it is a defect and not a preference:** the Claims section requires exact attribution and names the actual source of an attributed claim; the change here is not a hedge the draft is entitled to add but a relocation of a fact into a named document's mouth.
- **Smallest supported repair:** "The two periods carried different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software."

## 3. Considered and not reported

Recorded because each was tested and set aside, so that the absence of a finding is deliberate rather than an oversight.

- **"one shared view of its repairs" (line 33).** Read alone, it could be taken to cover all 640 flats, which the trial does not. It is set aside because it stands in the same sentence as "Elm Quay's trial has not expanded yet", and because the two-building scope is fixed in the title, the deck and the lede; in that context an ordinary reader takes "its repairs" as the trial's repairs. Defensible as written.
- **"The next document to read" (line 33) against the brief's "optional next step".** The brief's "optional" governs whether the reader takes the step; the draft describes the destination exactly as the brief does, as a document to read rather than a booking or a trial, and adds no obligation about Elm Quay or Svale. An editorial matter about call-to-action strength, not a source-support defect.
- **"puts the aim", "Lind's account"** where all interviews occurred by email. The material supplies these as "complete usable quotations" and permits their use; the draft never claims spoken delivery, a scene, an emotion or remembered dialogue. No finding under the quotation guidance.
- **"telephone repair reports" (line 3) for the material's "telephone reports".** The context settles the referent; nothing in this material could be a telephone report of repairs and not a telephone report, or the reverse.
