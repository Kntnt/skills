## 3. Findings (source support)

### F1 — The supplier heading turns a sequence into a cause and asserts a single check
Draft (line 9): "## One check decided which supplier Elm Quay picked"
Source: "It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair." — "No comparison with another supplier is available."
What is changed: "after testing" is a sequence; "decided which supplier … picked" is a causal claim about what determined the choice, and "One check" additionally asserts that the check was the whole of the team's evaluation. The base contract holds this boundary in headings as in the body.
Concrete case, compatible with the material: the team ran the status-display test, which Svale passed, and then chose Svale because it was the only supplier its existing property-management contract covered. The source statement ("It chose Svale Systems after testing whether …") holds; the heading fails, because the check settled nothing. In the other direction: a check that decided the choice would also have to be shown to be the reason, which no supplied statement gives. Nothing in the material excludes the case — the material says only that no comparison is available, not that no other consideration existed.
Smallest supported repair: "Elm Quay picked its supplier after one check" — or, to keep the ordering claim out entirely, "Elm Quay checked one thing before choosing a supplier".

### F2 — Training delivered is reported as learning achieved
Draft (line 15): "## Six staff members learned the log in two sessions"
Source: "Svale configured the log and trained six staff during two sessions."
What is changed: the thing measured. The material records training given over two sessions; the heading records that the six acquired working command of the log within those two sessions — an outcome the material never measures. The draft's own body keeps the supported verb ("trained six of Elm Quay's staff members over two sessions"), so the heading is the only place the claim is strengthened.
Concrete case, compatible with the material: the two sessions were held, and afterwards three of the six still needed a colleague to enter reports for them for the first fortnight. "Svale … trained six staff during two sessions" holds; "Six staff members learned the log in two sessions" fails. No supplied statement excludes it; the material reports no competence assessment of any kind, and Lind's quotations mention none.
Smallest supported repair: "Six staff members took two training sessions" (keeps the customer as the acting party the brief requires), or "Svale trained six staff members in two sessions".

### F3 — "nothing else" converts three named gaps into a closed measurement set
Draft (line 21): "## The figures cover counts and assignment time, nothing else"
Source: "There are no cost, resident-satisfaction or completion-time measurements."
What is changed: scope. The material excludes three named kinds of measurement; the heading asserts that nothing beyond counts and assignment time was measured at all. The contract's "what is unknown or unclaimed is not thereby known not to have happened" is the rule at issue: the material's silence about other measures is turned into their asserted absence.
Concrete case, compatible with the material: the 4 December note also records how the 31 reports were split between telephone and email, and how many fell into each category the team designed. The supplied statement ("There are no cost, resident-satisfaction or completion-time measurements") holds in full; "nothing else" fails. No supplied statement excludes it — the material lists what is absent by kind and never claims the list is exhaustive.
Smallest supported repair: drop the two words — "## The figures cover counts and assignment time" — or name the gaps the material names: "## No cost, satisfaction or completion figures were taken". The body sentence (line 25) is already exact and needs no change.

### F4 — The workload difference is moved into the note's voice
Draft (line 25): "The note states that the two periods carried different workloads, and it does not attribute the difference to the software."
Source: "The periods had different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software."
What is changed: whose assertion it is. The material asserts the workload difference itself and attributes only the non-attribution to the note. The draft makes the note the asserting party for both clauses.
Concrete case, compatible with the material: the note records the two medians and adds one line declining to credit the software for the difference, while the fact that the two eight-week periods carried different workloads is something the team told the writer and the note never mentions. The material's sentence holds; "The note states that the two periods carried different workloads" fails. No supplied statement excludes it — that the note declines to attribute the difference says nothing about whether it discusses workloads.
Smallest supported repair: "The two periods carried different workloads, and the note does not attribute the difference to the software." (This also lets the draft restore the material's "explicitly", which it currently drops; see §5.3.)

### F5 — The internal note is assigned to the maintenance team
Draft (line 23): "The maintenance team's internal note, dated December 4, 2025 …"; deck (line 3): "what its own trial note measures", where "its" is the maintenance team.
Source: "Elm Quay's internal trial note, 4 December 2025, says that 31 repair reports were entered."
What is changed: the authoring party, narrowed from the company to one unit inside it.
Concrete case, compatible with the material: the trial note was compiled by Elm Quay's property-administration office from the log's own export, with the maintenance team as its subject rather than its author. "Elm Quay's internal trial note" holds; "The maintenance team's internal note" fails. No supplied statement excludes it — the material names the document as Elm Quay's and never names who wrote it.
Smallest supported repair: "Elm Quay's internal trial note, dated December 4, 2025 …", and in the deck "what the company's own trial note measures".
Severity: minor. The narrowing stays inside the same organisation and strengthens no measured claim, but it is circumstantial detail the material does not carry.

## 5. Considered and not reported

**5.1 The deck's unattributed count (A3).** The material carries the figure as the note's statement ("the note … says that 31 repair reports were entered"), and the deck states it directly. The next deck sentence names the trial note as the source of the measures, and the body attributes the figure and both exclusions exactly. Reported here rather than as a finding: a deck that states a figure its body sources is defensible as written, and the attribution is not lost to the reader.

**5.2 "reached its maintenance team" (A6).** The material does not name who received telephone and email reports. The whole material frames repair reports as the maintenance team's work and has the team keep "telephone reporting open for residents", so no rival recipient is available on an ordinary reading. Defensible; not a defect.

**5.3 The dropped "explicitly" (A23).** The material says the note "explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software"; the draft says it "does not attribute". The draft's weaker form remains true wherever the material's holds, so this is not a source-support defect — but the material's word is a deliberate disclaimer by the note, and the repair proposed in F4 can carry it back at no cost. Editorial question, not a finding.

**5.4 Forbidden inferences.** None is committed: no software causation for the shorter assignment time (line 25 states both figures and the non-attribution), no cost or satisfaction claim, no rescued-customer framing, no conversion of assignment time into completion time, and the customer's reservation survives at lines 26 and 29 as her own qualified assessment. Supplier narration stays third person throughout, and the link is described as a checklist to read, matching the brief's destination.
