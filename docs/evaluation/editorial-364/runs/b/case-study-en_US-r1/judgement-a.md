# Judgement — case-study-en_US-r1

## 1. Outcome

**Delivered.** The run produced a draft, ran **two** source comparisons (`evidence/source-check/report-1.md`, `report-2.md`), repaired the five findings of the first, and delivered in the reply.

**Prose identity.** `delivered.md` minus its four-line `kntnt` frontmatter block is byte-identical to `evidence/source-check/draft-prose.md`, the prose the second checker read. The prose in the reply, the prose in `delivered.md` and the prose the last checker saw are one text. Nothing was touched after the final check except the addition of the frontmatter map, which carries no prose claim.

Only one `draft-prose.md` survives in the evidence, and it is the repaired text; the pre-repair wording is recoverable only through report 1's quotations of it. Length is 458 words against a brief asking for "approximately 400 words, as material allows" — 14 percent over, and the surplus is not invention (see F1).

## 2. F1 — source support

**Pass**, with two reservations recorded below, one of which a publisher must settle before the piece runs.

Everything in the body holds. I checked each claim against the material myself rather than against either checker's accounting.

- Chronology holds: September 2025 decision, eight-week trial, note dated 4 December 2025, and the preceding eight-week comparison period all sit where the material puts them.
- The causal boundary holds everywhere, including in the headings, which is where the pre-repair draft broke it. "Elm Quay picked Svale **after** a status test" is sequence, not cause. "The note measures assignment time, **not the software's effect**" carries the material's own disclaimer rather than hedging a causal claim into existence. The two medians stand side by side with "The two periods carried different workloads, and the note **explicitly** does not attribute the difference to the software" — the material's "explicitly" is present, and the non-attribution is attached to the note, which is where the material puts it.
- Assignment time is never converted into completion time. "Median time from report to assignment" names both endpoints, and completion time appears only among the unmeasured items.
- The exclusions survive in the body: "The count excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial began," with "ordered before the trial" correctly attached to *work* alone and not spread over *emergencies*.
- Unknown is kept apart from absent. "No comparison with another supplier is available" is carried verbatim — availability, not non-existence. The draft nowhere says the team evaluated no other supplier.
- The reservation survives twice over: "Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company" (not-to-every, not to-none) and her quoted "but I would leave an extra week for preparation."
- No count-size judgement anywhere. The 31 reports and the two- and three-day medians are given bare, with no "only", "just", "impressive" or equivalent. This is the correct handling: the material supplies no comparison, target or capacity against which 31 could be called anything.
- The future stays open: "Whether the log moves beyond the two buildings is still open: the team will decide once it has checked how the categories hold up for larger repairs." "Hold up for" and "work for" name the same test here; nothing could satisfy one and not the other in this context.
- Publisher stance and link are exact: "Svale Systems publishes and approved this account, which is not independent journalism," and the checklist is described as something to **read**, not a booking or a trial, matching the brief's explicit warning.
- Terms checked in both directions: *flats* → *apartments* (nothing in this material is one and not the other); *4 December 2025* → *December 4, 2025* (same date, en_US form); *trial* → *try/trial* (modality unchanged); *"trained six staff"* → *"trained six of Elm Quay's staff members"* (the added possessive is what a supplier training staff at a customer during that customer's trial means; no role, department or seniority added).

### Reservation A — the byline (the one addition external to the material)

Draft line: **"By Thomas Barregren"**. The brief says, in terms, **"No author name supplied."** The delivered text therefore asserts an authorship the material does not carry, and does so in supplier-published material whose stated publisher is Svale Systems. Both checkers excluded this line from their comparison by instruction ("comes from the invocation, not the material"), so neither weighed it.

I considered failing F1 on it and decided not to, for two reasons: the name is not a fabricated person or a fabricated source — it is the commissioning user's own name, filling an anatomy slot the genre requires; and the reply names the substitution unprompted and tells the publisher to change it ("If the piece is to run unsigned, under Svale Systems' own name or under a staff writer's, change that line before publication"). That disclosure is what keeps it out of the fail column. It remains the single line in the delivered text that no supplied passage supports, and it must not survive publication as written.

### Reservation B — the standfirst states the note's filtered count in the draft's own voice

Draft: **"Thirty-one repair reports went into it over eight weeks."** The material carries 31 only as what "Elm Quay's internal trial note, 4 December 2025, says", and immediately qualifies it: "It excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial." The standfirst drops both the attribution and the exclusions, so a reader meets 31 as the number of reports the log received, when the supplied figure is a filtered count from a document. The body repairs both within four paragraphs ("Elm Quay's internal trial note, dated December 4, 2025, **records** 31 repair reports entered … The count excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial began"), and the same standfirst sentence that follows names the note as the source of the measures. Minor, and not a fail — but it is the one place in the draft where a caveat the material attaches to a figure is not travelling with it.

Two further tightenings I tested and set aside: "Repair reports **reached its maintenance team** by telephone and by email" names a recipient the material never names, though the material's whole frame is the team's handling of repair reports and offers no rival recipient; and "Cost, resident satisfaction, and completion time **were not measured**" converts "There are no … measurements" into an act that did not happen, which asserts no more than the material's denial does in this context.

## 3. G2 — required parts

**Pass.**

The order is exactly headline → standfirst → byline → lead → four sections → ending, and each part does its own work:

- **Headline**: "Elm Quay Housing tested one shared repair log" — the customer as subject and actor, the act, no claimed effect.
- **Standfirst**: supplies the when, the where (two buildings), the volume, and — its own job, not the headline's — a declared limit: "what Elm Quay's **own** trial note measures". A reader is warned before the first section that the figures are the customer's own. Weakness: the third sentence is a signpost ("Read what … what … and how …"), which reads faintly like a table of contents; it earns its place only because of the "own trial note" flag it carries.
- **Byline**: present, and discussed above.
- **Lead**: does not repeat the standfirst. It supplies scale (640 apartments), the situation (telephone and email, stored separately), the motive (shifts seeing the same information), and the agency ("the decision to try a shared log was its own") — none of which is in the standfirst.
- **Sections**: selection and categories; setup and training; measurement and its limits; appraisal and open future. Four distinct jobs, no overlap.
- **Ending**: the disclosure and the call to action in one paragraph.

**Customer-account requirements**, each met:
- *Situation*: separate stores, shifts blind to each other.
- *Action*: the team decided, tested, chose, designed its own categories, kept telephone reporting open.
- *Results*: 31 reports with exclusions; two medians with the workload caveat and the non-attribution; the three unmeasured dimensions stated as unmeasured.
- *Appraisal*: Lind's third quotation, hers and conditional.
- *Publisher stance, truthful*: "Svale Systems publishes and approved this account, which is not independent journalism." Third person throughout; the supplier never narrates. "Svale" is the grammatical subject only where the material makes it one ("Svale configured the log and trained six").
- *Customer as acting party*: Elm Quay or its team is the subject of every decision sentence, and of the pending one.
- *Call to action from a supplied route*: the checklist link, optional ("can"), described as a document to read.

The one place the acting party slips is the heading "**Svale** trained six staff members in two sessions" — the only heading with the supplier as subject. It is accurate, and the body restores the customer immediately, but a heading is the most quoted line of a section and this one hands a section to the supplier. Report 1's alternative repair, "Six staff members took two training sessions", would have kept the customer in the chair.

## 4. L1 — language

**Pass.**

This reads as American prose written by someone who writes for a living, not as an import. Collective nouns take singular agreement throughout, held consistently: "the team wanted", "the team designed the categories **itself**", "the team will decide once **it has** checked". The serial comma is used and held ("Cost, resident satisfaction, and completion time"; "what the team set up, what Elm Quay's own trial note measures, and how its supervisor judges"). Date form, *apartments*, *staff members*, *two working days* are all en_US. No Britishism survives outside the quoted material, and no British syntax is smuggled in under American vocabulary.

Sentence rhythm is varied and the paragraphs are properly weighted: the measurement section builds from the count, through the medians, to the flat statement of what was not measured, and the final short sentence lands. "Whether the log moves beyond the two buildings is still open" is a genuinely good sentence — it converts an absence of news into information.

One blemish, a loop rather than a locale error:

> "Before choosing Svale Systems, the team tested whether the log could show the status of each repair. **It chose the supplier after that test.**"

The second sentence restates the first's subordinate clause and adds nothing. A professional editor would strike it. It is the only place the prose marks time.

Minor: "Repair reports reached its maintenance team by telephone and by email, and **the two** were stored separately" — "the two" reaches back to channels to mean stores; it survives a first reading but asks the reader to do a small piece of work for nothing.

## 5. Bridges into quotations

Three quotations, in draft order.

**Q1.**
- *Bridge*: "The team designed the categories itself and kept telephone reporting open for residents." Speech tag: "writes Maya Lind, Elm Quay's maintenance supervisor, who answered questions by email."
- *Quotation*: "We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log,"
- *Class*: **(c)**. The words that put it there: "**and kept telephone reporting open for residents**" — a fact the quotation nowhere carries, and the tag's "**who answered questions by email**" likewise. *Second reading*: the bridge's first clause, "designed the categories itself", pre-states the quotation's "The categories were ours", which on its own would be class (a) material. I chose (c) because the bridge's own load-bearing content — the open telephone channel for residents, and the medium of the interview — is content the quotation does not deliver, and because the quotation's first sentence (the shift-handover motive in the team's own words) is untouched by the bridge. The overlap is real and could have been avoided by moving "designed the categories itself" out of the bridge.

**Q2.**
- *Bridge*: "Lind would plan the next building differently."
- *Quotation*: "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts,"
- *Class*: **(a)**, on balance. The words that put it there: "**would plan the next building differently**" — every element of that clause is drawn from the quotation's second sentence and nothing else, so the bridge contributes no understanding of its own. *Second reading*: it could be argued as (b)-plus, on the grounds that the quotation's real payload is the comparison — more time on agreeing categories than on entering the first reports — which the bridge does not pre-state, so the reader does learn something new at the quotation. I chose (a) because the test is whether the bridge has already delivered what the quotation is there to deliver, and this bridge delivers the quotation's conclusion in advance while adding no fact of its own; the surviving comparison is the quotation rescuing the bridge, not the bridge doing work. This is the draft's weakest lead-in.

**Q3.**
- *Bridge*: "Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company, and her own appraisal carries a condition."
- *Quotation*: "I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation,"
- *Class*: **(c)**. The words that put it there: "**did not recommend Svale to every housing company**" — a fact from the material that the quotation does not carry and could not carry, since it is a statement about the scope of her recommending, not about the trial. *Second reading*: the trailing clause "her own appraisal carries a condition" announces the quotation's "but" without delivering it, which is signposting of the (a) kind; it flags the shape of what follows rather than its content, so it does not by itself reclassify the bridge. I chose (c) on the first clause, which is the sentence's weight.

- *No quotation stands without a bridge.* All three are led into.

**Counts**: one bridge of class (a), none of class (b), two of class (c).

**Interviewer attributions**: the draft attributes no question or utterance to an interviewer and quotes none; the only reference to the exchange is "who answered questions by email", which reports the medium the material supplies ("Customer interview with maintenance supervisor Maya Lind"; "All interviews occurred by email") and puts no words in an interviewer's mouth.

**Unsupported bridge assertions**: none. "Kept telephone reporting open for residents" is the material's own clause; "did not recommend Svale to every housing company" is the material's own sentence; and "Lind would plan the next building differently", with its presupposition of a next building, takes that presupposition from her own words ("before the next building starts") while the draft's closing paragraph keeps expansion explicitly open.

## 6. Quoted speech

All three supplied quotations appear **word for word**, both sentences of each, with no compression, no welding of remarks, no added referent and no repair of any kind. Meaning, stance, certainty and reservation are therefore untouched by construction:

- Q1's stance — the categories are the customer's, the supplier helped — survives, and is the reason the piece does not read as supplier-led.
- Q2's comparative ("more time … than") and conditional ("I would set that time aside") survive, including the implication that the preparation cost surprised her.
- Q3's concessive survives intact: the approval ("I would choose to do the trial again"), the present-tense benefit ("helps us"), and the condition ("but I would leave an extra week"). The draft neither promotes the approval nor buries the condition; the section heading carries both halves ("would repeat the trial, **with more preparation**").

The material states that no physical scene, emotion or remembered dialogue is available, and the draft invents none: no room, no gesture, no remembered exchange, no attributed feeling. The attribution verb is "writes" throughout, which matches the email medium and does not pretend to speech.

**No supplied quotation is missing.** The material offers exactly three complete usable quotations and the draft uses all three, one per section from the second onward. Each contributes beyond the surrounding narrative: Q1 the handover motive in the team's own terms, Q2 the time-allocation lesson that appears nowhere else in the piece, Q3 the qualified verdict. None restates its own paragraph — though Q2's bridge comes close to restating Q2 (see point 4).

## 8. Stop or delivery

**A valid delivery.**

The first comparison found five real defects, every one of them a claim strengthened beyond its supplied passage, and three of the five sat in headings whose body sentences were already exact — the classic shape of a draft whose prose is honest and whose headings are not. All five were repaired at the smallest scope that fixed them, the second comparison read the repaired prose in full and returned nothing, and the prose delivered is the prose that checker read. The delivered text commits none of the material's four forbidden inferences, keeps the customer as the acting party, keeps the supplier in the third person, keeps the customer's reservation in both a heading and a quotation, and describes the link as what the brief says it is.

The one line that should not go to a publisher untouched — the byline — was named in the reply with an instruction to change it before publication, so it leaves the run disclosed rather than hidden. Delivering was the right call.
