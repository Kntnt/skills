## 2. Bridges into quotations (every quotation in the input, in order)

### Q1 — "— Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var våra – Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen…"

- **Bridge, input:** the preceding narrative paragraph, "Kategorierna i loggen tog underhållsteamet fram själv, och de boende kunde fortsätta anmäla fel per telefon. Svale konfigurerade loggen och utbildade sex medarbetare vid två tillfällen. Testet pågick i åtta veckor." Immediately preceding sentence: "Testet pågick i åtta veckor."
- **Bridge, delivered:** "De boende kunde fortsätta anmäla fel per telefon. Svale utbildade sex medarbetare vid två tillfällen. Testet pågick i åtta veckor." Immediately preceding sentence unchanged.
- **Class before: (a)** — mixed, but decisively (a) through "Kategorierna i loggen tog underhållsteamet fram själv", which says in advance exactly what the quotation is there to deliver ("Kategorierna var våra"), and "Svale konfigurerade loggen", which anticipates "Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen". A reader who has read the bridge meets the quotation as the bridge said again. (The strict last sentence alone, "Testet pågick i åtta veckor", is (c).)
- **Class after: (c)** — the anticipating clauses are gone; what remains carries facts the quotation does not carry: telephone reporting, six trained staff on two occasions, eight weeks.
- **Reported?** Yes. The reply's finding 2 and its "Påståenden som rundan tog bort" list name both deletions by name and quote them.
- **Does the change supply anything the rest of the text does not carry?** No. Nothing was added; only two clauses were removed.
- **Was anything lost that the quotation does not carry?** Yes, one thing: *"Svale konfigurerade loggen"*. The quotation only says Svale helped enter the categories into the log; the configuration of the log itself now appears nowhere in the text. "Kategorierna … tog underhållsteamet fram själv" survives inside the quotation ("Kategorierna var våra"), so that one is not a loss. The reply states both of these correctly, including that the configuration fact has no remaining equivalent.
- **Speech tag:** "skriver Maya Lind, arbetsledare för underhållet på Elm Quay Housing" — class **(b)** before and after, unchanged; it names the speaker and her role and nothing more.

## 4. R1 — **FAIL**

**What the review got right.** Two of its repairs answer real, visible defects. The heading "Elm Quay valde efter ett eget test" and the clause "Efter den prövningen" pointed back at an evaluation the text never describes; both are gone, and the reply explains the repair and then states plainly, as finding 5, that the text still gives no basis for the vendor choice and that this cannot be fixed from the text alone — an irreparable finding, correctly classed as one for a human. The bridge that said the quotation in advance was a genuine defect and is genuinely repaired: Q1's lead-in moves from class (a) to class (c). All three quotations came through verbatim. The body paragraphs, all figures, the date, the disclosure line and the byline are untouched. The accounting it does give is candid and, item by item, accurate — it volunteers that its own fix created four of the five outstanding findings.

**Why it nonetheless fails.**

*Removals and claim changes, and whether the reply reports them accurately:*

- **"Svale konfigurerade loggen" (removed).** Reported, and reported accurately: the reply says outright that it has no remaining equivalent and that the quotation's "Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen" is narrower. But reporting an avoidable loss does not repair it. The defect was that the narrative pre-stated the quotation about *categories*; deleting the *configuration* fact was collateral, and the text was delivered with the hole in it. The reply itself calls the missing step something the genre asks for.
- **"i två av husen" (removed from the lead).** Reported accurately as finding 1 and under "Flyttade". The reply's own reasoning is correct and damning: a lead must stand alone, and this one now says the team tried a shared log with no scope at all, immediately after a sentence about 640 apartments. The scope survives in the H1 and the opening paragraph, so the fact is not lost from the text — but the lead is worse than it came in, and it was shipped that way.
- **"Efter den prövningen" (chronology removed).** Reported accurately, and here the removal is legitimate: the ordering referred to an event the text does not contain.
- **"Under åtta veckor" added to the lead, "åtta veckor" added to a heading.** Reported as finding 4. The duration is carried by the text ("Testet pågick i åtta veckor"), so nothing was invented — but the review added a third and a second statement of it while removing the scope, which is a net loss on both counts.
- **Heading: "Mediantiden" → "Mediantiden till tilldelning".** **Not reported.** This narrows the subject of a claim. It is supported by the body and is an improvement, but it is a claim change that the reply does not name — and the reply closes with "Inga andra påståenden ändrades: varje siffra, datum, citat och sakuppgift kom igenom som den kom in." That sentence is inaccurate.
- **CTA: "är ett dokument att läsa igenom" → "Läs igenom".** **Not reported.** A change of taste that also shifts modality, turning a flat statement in a disclosure-bearing promotional close into an imperative. Nothing in the text demanded it; it is exactly the kind of rewrite-to-taste on working prose that R1 forbids, and it is covered by the same blanket denial.
- **Talstreck em dash → en dash, three times.** Not reported. Harmless and locale-correct, but again unnamed under a blanket claim of no other change.

R1 asks the review to address concrete visible defects *while preserving* voice, arguments, quotations and claims outside its findings, and to report removals and changed claims accurately. This run cleared the quotations and the figures, and it was honest about the damage it chose to name. But it shipped a lead stripped of the test's scope and a section stripped of what the vendor actually did, and it made two changes to claims — one to a heading's subject, one to the closing sentence's modality — that it does not report and then expressly denies having made. A review whose closing assurance is false about its own delivered text fails the criterion, however good the rest of its accounting is.
