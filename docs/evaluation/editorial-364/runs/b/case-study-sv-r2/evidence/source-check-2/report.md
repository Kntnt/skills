## 1. Claim accounting

Each entry gives the draft passage, the source passage it rests on in the source's own words and language, and what differs.

### Title

**1.1 — "Elm Quay fick en gemensam bild av felanmälningarna"**
Source: "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings. Previously telephone reports and emails were stored separately." / "The team wanted staff on different shifts to see the same information." / Lind: "Having one view of the reports helps us".
Thing named: a shared view of the repair reports — the definitional effect of a shared log replacing separately stored telephone reports and emails, and a state Lind's quotation presupposes when she says having one view helps. Subject: Elm Quay, the acting party in the material. Time: past, matching the completed eight-week trial. Modality: flat assertion; Lind supplies the speaker assessment that the state obtained.
Scope is the one point of tension: the log ran in two buildings of a 640-flat portfolio and has not expanded. The title carries no scope marker; the deck on the next line does ("i två av husen"), and headline plus deck is read as one unit. No causal claim about assignment time, cost, resident satisfaction or a rescued customer is made or implied. Assessed as supported; the scope question is recorded in section 4.1 rather than reported as a defect.

### Deck

Number, subject and thing measured identical. *Sköter* covers both running and maintaining; both readings hold of a housing company with its own maintenance team. No difference.

**1.3 — "prövade hösten 2025 en gemensam felanmälningslogg i två av husen"**

Time: a September 2025 decision and an eight-week trial closed by a 4 December 2025 note place the trial inside autumn 2025. *Hösten 2025* is entailed, not added. Scope (two buildings) and the thing named (a shared repair log) carried over. Subject: the deck names Elm Quay Housing where the material names its own maintenance team — a part-for-whole the material licenses ("its own maintenance team"). No difference.

**1.4 — "Här får du veta vad underhållsteamet gjorde, vilka siffror den interna testnoteringen stöder och vad arbetsledaren skulle lägga mer tid på nästa gång."**

Reader address *du* asserts nothing about the reader; *Väger du samma beslut* (1.22) is conditional. Person and number of *du* are a composition choice, not a claim.

### Opening paragraph

Two channels, stored separately, in a period ending at the decision. *Dittills* fixes the same anterior reference the pluperfect carries. No difference.

Duration, the existence of the note and the existence of an assessment held by the supervisor. Chronology holds: the note is dated after the eight weeks. Authorial framing over supported material, and the facts it presents as given are each supported. No difference.

Subject, two acts, and both counts carried. *Vid två tillfällen* and "during two sessions" name the same two occasions. No difference.

**1.15 — quotation, Maya Lind (second), and "säger hon"**

Draft: "— Vi lade mer tid på att enas om kategorierna än på att registrera de första anmälningarna. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan nästa hus drar i gång"
The comparison, its two terms, the conditional *skulle*, and the forward reference to the next building all carried. Kept as a separate remark from 1.13, as the material's separate bullets have it.

### Section "Tilldelningstiden mättes, inte kostnad eller nöjdhet"

**1.16 — heading**
Source: "Median time from report to assignment was two working days during the trial" / "There are no cost, resident-satisfaction or completion-time measurements."
States what was measured and two of the three things that were not. The third, completion time, is given in 1.19. No claim of effect. No difference.

**1.17 — "Elm Quays interna testnotering från den 4 december 2025 redovisar 31 registrerade anmälningar. Akuta ärenden och arbeten som beställts före testet ingår inte."**
Source: "Elm Quay's internal trial note, 4 December 2025, says that 31 repair reports were entered. It excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial."
Whose assertion: held to the note, named as internal and dated, exactly as the material holds it. Figure and both exclusions carried, and the exclusions attach to the same count. The count is given with its exclusions and without any evaluative characterisation — no *bara*, *hela* or *så många som*. No difference.

**1.18 — "Mediantiden från anmälan till tilldelning var två arbetsdagar under testet och tre under de åtta veckorna dessförinnan. Perioderna hade olika arbetsbelastning, och noteringen tillskriver uttryckligen inte programvaran skillnaden."**
Source: "Median time from report to assignment was two working days during the trial and three in the preceding eight-week period. The periods had different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software."
Statistic named (median, not mean), endpoints named (report to assignment, not completion), both figures, both periods and the comparison period's length carried. The elided *tre [arbetsdagar]* takes its unit from the first clause.
Modality is the load-bearing point. *Uttryckligen inte* is "explicitly does not", the material's reading; the alternative order *inte uttryckligen* would have said "does not explicitly", a weaker and different claim, and the draft does not use it. The draft states the two figures and the workload caveat without any causal verb or causal hedge in its own voice — no *kortade*, *tyder på att*, *tack vare*. Whose assertion is kept with the note. No difference.

**1.19 — "Kostnader, de boendes nöjdhet och tiden fram till avslutat arbete har inte mätts."**
Source: "There are no cost, resident-satisfaction or completion-time measurements."
All three items carried, including completion time, which is stated as unmeasured rather than conflated with assignment time. See 4.4 on "there are no measurements" against *har inte mätts*.

### Section "Arbetsledaren skulle göra om testet med mer förberedelse"

Whose assertion: the heading names *arbetsledaren* as its subject, so the conditional stays hers and is not turned into the article's recommendation. Modality: *skulle*, conditional, matching "would". The qualification about preparation is carried into the heading rather than left behind in the body. No difference.

Both conditionals, the *men* qualification and the present-tense "helps us" carried. Nothing additive. Translation assessed in section 3.

## 2. Findings — source support

### Finding 1 — the party that kept telephone reporting open is dropped, next to a Svale subject

Draft, line 18: "Svale Systems konfigurerade loggen och utbildade sex medarbetare vid två tillfällen. **Telefonanmälan behölls för de boende.**"

Material: "**The maintenance team** designed its categories and **kept telephone reporting open for residents**. Svale configured the log and trained six staff during two sessions."

What is missing: the material states the agent explicitly, and the draft's agentless s-passive removes it. The material orders the two sentences customer-first; the draft reverses them, so the agentless clause now follows a sentence whose subject is Svale Systems and inherits that subject on the default reading.

Concrete case, compatible with the material, on which the draft's sentence and the material's come apart: Svale's standard configuration closes the telephone channel and routes all reporting through the log, and Elm Quay's maintenance team overrode that to keep the phone line open for its residents. Nothing in the material excludes this — the material says only that the maintenance team kept telephone reporting open, and says nothing about Svale's default or preference. In that case the material's sentence holds and the draft's sentence, read with the subject the preceding sentence supplies, attributes to Svale a decision that was the customer's. Read the other way: the draft's clause is also satisfied by nobody having decided anything and the line simply remaining open, which the material's active verb excludes.

This is a source-support finding: an attribution the material makes explicit is not present in the draft, and the placement invites a reading the material does not support. It is also the one place where the customer stops being the acting party.

Smallest supported repair: give the clause back its subject and nothing else.

> Teamet behöll telefonanmälan för de boende.

(*Underhållsteamet* is equally supported if the fuller noun reads better after the Svale sentence.)

No other change is needed; the sentence order may stay as it is.

## 3. Findings — translation of the quotations

The three quotations are translated from English into Swedish. Each was checked separately for meaning, stance, certainty, distinctive voice and idiomatic target-language speech, against the quotation guidance.

**Quotation 1.** "We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log." → "Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var våra; Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen."
*Kvällsskiftet* and *morgonskiftet* are the ordinary Swedish shift nouns and are written as single compounds. *Skulle se* is the natural Swedish complement of *ville att*, not English syntax carried across. *Kategorierna var våra* keeps the flat possessive assertion and its emphasis, and *Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen* keeps the supplier in the helping role rather than the deciding one. Nothing narrowed, widened or sharpened; no hedge added or removed; no filler removed that carried anything; no referent made explicit that the material does not settle (*dem* refers to *kategorierna* in her own sentence). No obstruction to an ordinary Swedish reading. No finding.

**Quotation 2.** "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts." → "Vi lade mer tid på att enas om kategorierna än på att registrera de första anmälningarna. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan nästa hus drar i gång."
*Lade mer tid på ... än på att* is idiomatic and keeps both terms of her comparison parallel. *Enas om* is "agreeing on", a reaching of agreement, not merely discussing. *Avsätta* is the exact Swedish of "set aside" in this sense. Fronting *den tiden* puts her emphasis where the English "that time" puts it, with the finite verb second as Swedish requires. *Drar i gång* is spoken-register Swedish for "starts" and keeps her plain voice rather than lifting it into the article's register. Certainty unchanged: the conditional *skulle* renders "would" without adding or removing a hedge. No finding.

**Quotation 3.** "I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation." → "Jag skulle välja att göra testet igen. Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser."
*Jag skulle välja att göra* keeps her slightly deliberate construction — she says she would choose to do it, not simply that she would do it — rather than smoothing it to *jag skulle göra om testet*. *Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss* keeps the present tense and the plain "helps us" without upgrading it to a stronger endorsement. *Men* carries the pivot, and the reservation after it is not softened or dropped. *Lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser* renders "leave an extra week for preparation" as scheduling that week in; it neither strengthens her into a demand nor weakens her into a wish, and it does not simply repeat *avsätta* from quotation 2, where the English also used a different verb. No obstruction to an ordinary Swedish reading. No finding.

All three are set with a speech dash, the ordinary Swedish marking for reported speech, rather than quotation marks — consistent with the resolved composition guidance and not a claim about the phrasing itself being disputed or new.

**No translation findings.**

## 4. Considered and not reported

Recorded so the reasoning is visible, each with the test applied.

**4.1 — Scope of the title (1.1).** A case can be described in which the title's unqualified reading fails: the two trial buildings use the shared log while the remaining buildings of the 640-flat portfolio still store telephone reports and emails separately, so Elm Quay as a company has no shared view of its repair reports. That is not merely compatible with the material, it is what the material describes. It is not reported as a defect because the claim is not read alone: the deck on the immediately following line states *i två av husen*, and a headline with its deck is read as one unit by an ordinary reader. The title also makes no claim the material forbids — no effect on time, cost or satisfaction — and the state it asserts is one Lind's own quotation presupposes. Reported here rather than as a finding; if a reviewer wants the scope in the headline itself, that is a preference about the draft, not a repair of a defect.

**4.2 — *ärende* against "repair" (1.7).** *Ärende* is in general broader than "repair"; a resident enquiry that needs no work is an *ärende* and not a repair. The draft itself uses *akuta ärenden* for the material's "emergencies". But the phrase is *status för varje ärende* inside a *felanmälningslogg*, and in this context the items that log holds are the repair reports the material counts — the material's own "each repair" is the same shorthand it uses in "31 repair reports were entered". The two expressions pick out the same set here, so nothing in this context falls under one and not the other. Not a finding.

**4.3 — *säger* for an email interview (1.14, 1.15, 1.21).** The material states "All interviews occurred by email". Swedish journalistic usage applies *säger* as a general attribution verb to written statements as readily as to spoken ones (*säger X i ett pressmeddelande*), so *säger* does not assert an oral utterance and no case can be built in which it fails while the material holds. The quotation guidance's instruction to signal what the material is bites where the material is notes or a summary rather than the person's words; here the material supplies "complete usable quotations", which are her own words. *Skriver hon* would be marginally more precise and is available, but the draft is defensible as written. Not a finding.

**4.4 — "There are no ... measurements" against *har inte mätts* (1.19).** The material's formulation is about the existence of measurements and the draft's about the act of measuring. Attempts to separate them collapse: any case in which measuring occurred leaves measurements, and any case in which no measurements exist for this trial is one an ordinary reader takes the material itself to assert. No case stands in either direction within what the material supplies. Not a finding.

**4.5 — Past to present in 1.22.** "Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company" is reported in the past; the draft states her position in the present. A case can be imagined in which she changed her mind after the interview, but the sentence sits among reports of what she gave in that interview and is read as her position as the material gives it — the ordinary journalistic present for an interviewee's standpoint. The load-bearing content, the scope of the negation and the survival of the reservation, is unchanged. Not a finding.

**4.6 — Absence of a supplier-published disclosure.** The material states "The supplier approved publication of this case as supplier-published material; it is not independent journalism", and the draft carries no such disclosure. This is an omission, not an unsupported or altered claim, and the draft makes no claim of independence anywhere: it names the internal note as the source of every figure, keeps every evaluation with Lind, and keeps Svale in the third person. Whether a disclosure line is wanted is an editorial decision outside a source comparison. Not a finding.

**4.7 — *programvaran* on first mention (1.18).** The definite *programvaran* appears without the log having earlier been called software. The equation of the log with software is the material's own, in the same sentence ("does not attribute the difference to the software"), so no unsupported claim is made. Cohesion is a mechanical matter outside this comparison. Not a finding.

**4.8 — Material present in the source but absent from the draft.** "The team wanted staff on different shifts to see the same information" appears only inside Lind's first quotation rather than also in the article's voice. Omission is not a source-support defect, and the fact is in fact carried.

General editorial and mechanical review was not performed and is outside this comparison. Composition guidance was consulted only where a Swedish structure could change meaning — the negation order in 1.18, the s-passive in 1.12, the genitives in 1.9 and 1.17, and the speech dashes in section 3.
