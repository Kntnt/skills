## 4. R1 — **PASS**

Removals and changed claims, one by one:

- **Difference 1 (lead).** The rewrite is a genuine repair: the standfirst already carried the six weeks, the two paper sheets and the two statuses, and the input lead spent both its sentences restating them before reaching anything new. Finding 1 reports the change and reports its reason accurately. One thing it does not report: the input asserted plainly that the paper sheets "used to carry the work **at Fenwick Instrument Repairs**", the firm; the delivered sentence buries that in a relative clause, "the brass and woodwind workshop at Fenwick Instrument Repairs, **which** had kept...", where *which* attaches by proximity to Fenwick Instrument Repairs but reads just as easily as the workshop. Under the nearest-antecedent reading the original claim is preserved exactly; under the other reading its subject narrows from firm to workshop. So this is an ambiguity introduced, not a claim demonstrably altered — but the reply's Claims section says flatly that "every claim comes back with its scope, certainty, attribution, chronology, causality and meaning as it arrived", which is stated more strongly than the sentence it produced supports. A reply that had named the new clause would have been accurate; this one is over-confident on one point. It is a reporting shortfall, not a fabrication, and the underlying fact is unchanged under the natural reading.
- **Difference 2.** Reported, accurately, in finding 2. Choosing *the schedule* as the referent is the only reading the text supports — a supplier does not distinguish statuses — so no fact was invented to make the repair.
- **Difference 3.** Reported, accurately, in finding 2. *The workshop* is the subject of the preceding sentence; the substitution is mechanical.
- **Difference 4.** Reported in finding 3, accurately as to what was wrong with the old heading. The cost described in §2 above goes unreported.
- **Removals.** None. No sentence, claim, figure, date, name or quotation left the text. 38 jobs, 23 February 2026, January 2026, six weeks, four staff, one online session, the telephone, and both limiting sentences ("It does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction"; "That is the workshop's own account of six weeks, not a measured improvement in repair speed") all stand untouched, as the reply says.
- **Irreparable finding.** Finding 4 declares the missing call to action unresolved and explains why it cannot be resolved from the text alone: no offer, link or contact route appears anywhere, so any ending written to it would invent a fact. That is the correct disposition — reporting the defect rather than inventing material — and it is stated plainly enough for the author to act on.
- **Clean text left alone.** Three of the four sections came back untouched. The four changes are all answerable to a defect a reader of this text can see; none is a preference edit applied to working prose. Voice, argument, quotations and every claim outside the findings are preserved.

Verdict: **pass**, with the two unreported items above — the relative-clause ambiguity in the new lead, covered by a blanket "no claim was changed", and the new heading pre-stating the quotation beneath it — recorded as accuracy and judgement costs that do not reach the level of a failed criterion.
