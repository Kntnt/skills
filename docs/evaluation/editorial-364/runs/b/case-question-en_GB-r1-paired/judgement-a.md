## 2. Bridges into quotations

The input carries two quotations. Neither bridge was touched.

**Quotation 1** — "We had to agree what ready for work meant. …"

- Bridge, input: "Benchline's case writer asked workshop manager Priya Vale by email what the team had to settle before the schedule was useful. Vale replied:"
- Bridge, delivered: identical, word for word.
- Class before: **(b)**. It names the attribution ("Benchline's case writer asked workshop manager Priya Vale"), the occasion ("by email"), and the subject put to her ("what the team had to settle before the schedule was useful"). It does not say what the answer was — that the sticking point was the meaning of *ready for work*, or that a part in the building was not a part at the bench. A reader who has read the bridge still learns both of those from the quotation.
- Class after: **(b)**, same words, same reasons.
- Changed? No. Nothing to report, nothing supplied, nothing lost.

**Quotation 2** — "I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog."

- Bridge, input: "Asked whether the same choice would be made again, Vale wrote:"
- Bridge, delivered: identical, word for word.
- Class before: **(b)**. It names the occasion (she was asked) and the attribution (Vale wrote), and frames the question without answering it: the bridge does not say she would choose it again, does not say *for new jobs*, and does not mention the extra week or the backlog.
- Class after: **(b)**, same words, same reasons.
- Changed? No. Nothing supplied, nothing lost.
- Adjacent, not the bridge: the section heading two lines above changed (difference #4), and the preceding sentence ("The manager approved continued use for new jobs, but postponed adding the backlog…") is unchanged. The heading is not the bridge under the definition given — a full sentence stands between it and the quotation — but it now announces the quotation's second sentence ("another week", "the status names"), which the old heading did not. The immediate lead-in a reader meets before the quotation marks is unchanged.

## 4. R1

**Pass.**

The three repairs all answer defects a reader can see in the text alone, with no appeal to absent material: a lead that restated the standfirst before advancing; two adjacent pronouns pointing at two different parties, in the one section whose content is *which* party did the defining; and a heading whose every content word was repeated by the sentence under it. Working voice, the argument's shape, the two limiting sentences that fence what the piece asserts ("It does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction"; "That is the workshop's own account of six weeks, not a measured improvement in repair speed"), both quotations and both bridges are untouched. The clean two-thirds of the text was not rewritten to taste; no figure, date, name or attribution was added, dropped or moved; nothing the text lacked was invented, and the one finding that would have required inventing a fact — an ending that calls the reader to action with no offer, link or contact route anywhere in the text — was left unresolved and reported as such rather than filled in. The pronoun resolutions are the only places where the Skill decided something the input left open, and both decisions follow from wording the text already carries.

Removals and changed claims, against what the reply reports:

- Lead compression (#1): **reported**, accurately, as finding 1, including that the old arrangement is now named "in passing".
- Scope trim inside it, "the work" → the two named job categories (#1a): **not reported**, and positively contradicted by the Claims section's "every claim comes back with its scope … as it arrived". Minor — the narrower claim is the standfirst's own and is entailed by the wider one — but it is a scope change asserted not to exist.
- Relative-clause antecedent made indeterminate (#1b): **not reported**. No claim is falsified; the cost is a fresh, small referent ambiguity in the sentence the Skill rewrote to fix clarity elsewhere.
- "it" → "the schedule" (#2): **reported**, accurately, as finding 2, with the reason the nearest antecedent was wrong.
- "It" → "The workshop" (#3): **reported**, accurately, in the same finding ("Both referents are now named").
- Heading replacement (#4): **reported**, accurately, as finding 3, including that the paragraphs under it are untouched. The reply does not note that the new heading anticipates the quotation; that is a trade the reader may fairly dispute, not a misreport.
- Unresolved finding 4 (no call to action): reported as unresolved with the reason — the required material is not in the text — which is the correct handling under R1's no-absent-source rule.

The failure of accuracy is confined to the Claims section's blanket "no claim was removed and none was changed", which overstates: #1a is a scope narrowing and #1b blurs a subject. Neither loses a fact, neither adds one, and both sit inside a change the reply does report, so R1 holds — but the blanket sentence should have been qualified.
