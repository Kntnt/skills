## 4. R1 — **fail**

What the review got right is substantial. The two defects it repaired outright are real and visible in the text alone: the standfirst claimed the trial "replaced two paper sheets" while the body records the backlog as postponed (D1), and the body's "The manager" leaned on the standfirst to name someone it never named (D6). Both quotations came back verbatim. No claim was removed; no argument, no reservation and no piece of Vale's voice was lost; the closing limitation paragraph, the one place a reviewer is most tempted to tidy, is untouched. The reply's account of both repairs is accurate, and its account of the two regressions is unusually candid and correct on the detail — including that "no outcomes" stands directly above the sentence reporting what the summary does say, and that "Lets … continue" reads as permission where the paragraph records a decision.

It fails on what it delivered. The input's subheading "the summary measures no repair time" was accurate; the delivered "The summary measures no outcomes" is not, and it is contradicted by the sentence immediately beneath it, which reports an outcome the summary does record (staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts). A text left self-contradicting on the page is worse off than the echo that was repaired, and an echo is the milder fault. The companion clause drops the "for new jobs" limit that the headline, the standfirst, the paragraph beneath and the quotation all carry — the condition the whole account turns on. Disclosing a regression is not repairing it, and the run delivered the text with both in place.

Reporting, removal by removal and change by change:

- D1 — reported, accurately (scope, and the finding it answered).
- D5 — reported, accurately, and in more detail than needed; correctly marked unresolved.
- D6 — reported, accurately.
- D7 — **not reported**. A subheading that reported Vale's verdict became an unattributed prescription, dropping the attribution and the "for new jobs" scope from the heading line. The reply's "Claims" section says only two claims were moved; this is a third.
- D2, D3, D8 — **not reported**. Taste changes and one attribution expansion on prose that carried no visible defect.
- D4, D9 — **not reported**. Mechanical and defensible, but the reply's "five things to repair" ledger does not admit them.
- No removals: nothing in the input is absent from the delivered text. The reply's "No claim was removed" is correct.

So the reply is accurate on everything it reports and silent on four further changes, one of which moves a claim. Combined with the unsupported heading it shipped, R1 does not pass.
