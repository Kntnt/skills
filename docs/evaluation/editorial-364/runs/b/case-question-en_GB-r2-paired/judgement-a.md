## 2. Bridges into quotations (input order)

**Quotation 1** — "We had to agree what ready for work meant…"

- Input bridge: "Asked by email by Benchline's case writer what had to be settled first, Vale answered:"
- Delivered bridge: "Asked by email by Benchline's case writer what had to be settled first, manager Priya Vale answered:"
- Class before: **(c)** — the words "by email" and "by Benchline's case writer" are facts the quotation does not carry; the speech tag carries the channel and the questioner, not only attribution. Naming the topic ("what had to be settled first") does not deliver the answer, so it is not (a).
- Class after: **(c)** — unchanged; the added "manager Priya" is attribution only.
- Changed: yes. The reply's reported findings name the *effect* obliquely — the claims list reports the naming repair only at "The manager approved continued use" → "Vale approved continued use", and states the finding as "the body never names the manager it refers to". The bridge sentence's own change is **not** separately named. It supplies no event, fact, name or figure the rest of the text lacks (the standfirst already says "Manager Priya Vale"). Nothing the input bridge carried was lost.

**Quotation 2** — "I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week…"

- Input bridge: "Vale's verdict comes with a condition and a delay:"
- Delivered bridge: "Vale's verdict on the bench schedule comes with a condition and a delay:"
- Class before: **(b)** — it names the speaker and the occasion and labels the shape of what follows ("a condition and a delay") without saying which condition or which delay, so the quotation still delivers its content; no fact outside the text.
- Class after: **(b)** — unchanged; "on the bench schedule" names the subject and no more.
- Changed: yes, and **not named** by the reply. It supplies no event, fact, name or figure the rest of the text does not carry. Nothing the input bridge carried was lost.
- Adjacent note (not a bridge by the definition given): the heading above this quotation was class-(a)-like in the input — "Vale would use the schedule again for new jobs" said in advance exactly what the quotation's first sentence says — and the delivered heading no longer does. That is difference #7, and the reply does not report it.

## 4. R1 — **PASS** (qualified)

The defects it went after are concrete and visible in the text alone: two subheadings that restated the prose or the quotation directly beneath them, a standfirst whose unscoped "replaced" outran a body that records the backlog as postponed, and a body sentence leaning on the standfirst to name its manager. No source material was invoked to justify anything, and no fact enters the text that the text did not already hold. Both quotations came back verbatim; the argument, the figures (38 jobs, 23 February 2026, four staff, six weeks), the "no repair time / revenue / satisfaction" limit and the closing refusal to generalise are all preserved.

Removals and changed claims, one by one:

- **Standfirst scope (#1)** — reported, and reported accurately: the reply names the finding, the before and after, and the reason.
- **Attribution "the manager" → "Vale" (#6)** — reported accurately.
- **Bridge naming (#3), bridge subject (#8), quotation-mark removals (#4, #9), sentence reorder (#2)** — all **unreported**. None damages meaning; #2 is a taste change to a sentence that had nothing wrong with it, which is the kind of rewriting a clean text should not attract.
- **Subheading 2 (#5)** — the two claim moves inside it are reported, fully and accurately, as unresolved findings; the reply even names the character count against the limit and explains that the budget of one correction round was spent. But the degraded line was still delivered: the final text now asserts "The summary measures no outcomes", which the paragraph beneath it does not support, and drops the "for new jobs" limit the same review had just restored to the standfirst. Disclosed, not repaired.
- **Subheading 3 (#7)** — a claim change (attributed verdict → unattributed maxim) that the reply does **not** report at all. The claims section's "No claim was removed. Two were left standing with something moved" is therefore an undercount: three claim-level moves were delivered beyond the two it lists (#5's two and #7).

The verdict is a pass because the repairs answer real defects, the text's voice, arguments, quotations and untouched claims survive, and the single place where the delivered text is worse than the input is declared as an open finding rather than hidden. It is a qualified pass because the claims account is incomplete: it states a total ("two") that the diff contradicts, and it leaves four smaller changes — one of them a claim-level rewrite of a subheading — out of the account entirely.
