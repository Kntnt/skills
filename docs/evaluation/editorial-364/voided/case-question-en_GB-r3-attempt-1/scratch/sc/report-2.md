## Findings

### Finding 1 — the closing sentence widens the order Vale's account actually supports (source support)

**Draft (line 23):** "For a workshop already weighing the same move, that is the order Vale's account supports: agree what each status means before any job goes on a shared schedule."

**Material:**
- Vale: "We had to agree what ready for work meant."
- Vale: "I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog."
- "The manager approved continued use for new jobs but postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions."

**What differs — scope, and whose assertion.** The sentence states explicitly what Vale's account supports, so it is an attributed claim about the source and not only the author's inference from it. The order Vale gives is: settle the status names *before adding the backlog*. The draft's colon expands that to *before any job goes on a shared schedule* — a strictly wider rule, covering new jobs as well as the backlog.

**Concrete case, compatible with the material, where one holds and the other fails.** Fenwick itself is the case. The manager approved continued use *for new jobs* while the status definitions were still to be reviewed, and Vale's first answer shows the team worked out what "ready for work" meant during the trial, with jobs already on the schedule. So a workshop that does exactly what Vale's account endorses — new jobs on the schedule now, status names settled before the backlog follows — satisfies Vale's order and violates the draft's. In the other direction, a workshop that holds every job off the schedule until each status is agreed satisfies the draft's rule while doing something Vale neither did nor recommended. No supplied statement excludes either case; the material affirmatively describes the first.

The draft's own heading two lines above states the supported version ("before moving the backlog"), so the body sentence overshoots the section it closes.

**Smallest supported repair.** Narrow the colon clause to the backlog, leaving the rest of the sentence, its scoping and its attribution untouched:

> For a workshop already weighing the same move, that is the order Vale's account supports: agree what each status means before the backlog goes on a shared schedule.
