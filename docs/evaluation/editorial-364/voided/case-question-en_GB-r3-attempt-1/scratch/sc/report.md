## Findings

### Finding 1 — the deck credits Vale with the trial's results, which came from the summary (source support)

- Draft, line 3: "Manager Priya Vale sets out what the team had to agree first, **what the trial established**, and what would get another week next time."
- Material: "The following is a **complete extract** from an email interview on 2 March 2026 ..." — and that complete extract contains only "We had to agree what ready for work meant ..." and "I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week ...".
- Material, for what the trial established: "The workshop's trial summary, dated 23 February 2026, records 38 jobs entered in the schedule. It says staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts. It does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction."

What differs is whose assertion it is. The deck presents a three-item list under one subject, Vale, and an ordinary reader takes all three as hers. The first and third are; the second is not. The material states that the extract is complete, so it excludes Vale having said anything about what the six weeks showed, and the draft's own middle section attributes that content to the summary ("according to the workshop's summary of 23 February 2026"), not to her.

Concrete case in which the material holds and the draft's statement fails: the trial summary is a workshop document of 23 February, written a week before the 2 March email exchange and covering the counts and the non-measurements; Vale is asked two questions, about what had to be settled and about what she would change, and answers only those. Then "Vale sets out ... what the trial established" is false while every supplied statement remains true. No supplied statement excludes that case; it is what the material describes.

Smallest supported repair — drop the item that is not hers:

> Manager Priya Vale sets out what the team had to agree first and what would get another week next time.

If the trial's outcome is to stay in the deck, attribute it to its actual source, e.g. "The workshop's trial summary records what the six weeks showed; manager Priya Vale sets out what the team had to agree first and what would get another week next time."

### Finding 2 — "arranged by telephone" widens the source's "discuss ... arrangements" (source support)

- Draft, line 11: "Collection was still arranged by telephone."
- Material: "The team continued to use telephone calls to **discuss** collection arrangements with customers."

What differs is the act named. The source says the telephone remained the channel for discussing collection arrangements; the draft says the telephone is where collection was arranged, i.e. where the arrangement was made and settled.

Concrete case compatible with the material: staff ring the customer to talk through when the instrument can be collected, and the collection slot is then confirmed and recorded by email or at the counter when the customer calls in. The material's sentence holds — telephone calls continued to be used to discuss collection arrangements with customers — while "collection was still arranged by telephone" fails, because the arranging is not concluded there. Nothing supplied excludes that case; the material says only that the calls were used to discuss.

Smallest supported repair:

> Collection arrangements were still discussed with customers by telephone.

## Editorial question (reported, not a finding)

Draft, line 23: "Any workshop weighing the same move can take that order: agree what each status means, then put jobs on a shared schedule."

The material closes with an explicit limit: "The summary and interview establish the team's reported experience, not a measured improvement in repair speed or a recommendation for every workshop." The draft's closing sentence generalises the ordering beyond this workshop, and the publisher is the supplier, Benchline, so the second half ("then put jobs on a shared schedule") also reads as an invitation to adopt the product category.

It is not reported as a defect with a repair because the line between authorial advocacy and a factual assertion is genuinely unsettled here. The sentence asserts no fact about any other workshop, claims no measured effect, is addressed only to workshops "weighing the same move", is modal ("can take that order"), and the order it recommends is the one Vale herself gives and the manager's postponement enacts — supported material on which the author's own advocacy may rest. A reader who takes "not a recommendation for every workshop" strictly would still want the generalisation trimmed to the case, e.g. "That is the order Vale would take next time." Left to the editor.
