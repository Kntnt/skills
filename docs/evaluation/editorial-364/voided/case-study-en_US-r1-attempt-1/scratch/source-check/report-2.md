## 2. Findings

Four findings, most severe first. Each is a defect against the material, not a preference; the smallest supported repair is given for each.

### F1 — Title states the shared log unqualified, where the material has a two-building, eight-week trial that has not expanded (CONFIRMED)

- Draft (line 1): "Elm Quay gave its shifts one repair log"
- Material: "its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings"; "The trial lasted eight weeks."; "The trial has not yet expanded; the team will decide after checking how the categories work for larger repairs."
- What differs: scope and permanence. The draft's title asserts of Elm Quay, without qualification, a completed change covering "its shifts"; the material supports only a time-boxed trial in two of its buildings, with no decision yet to keep or extend it.
- Case in which the material's statement holds and the draft's fails: Elm Quay's 640 flats are spread over a dozen buildings; two took part for eight weeks; at the note's date the team has not decided, and shifts working the other ten buildings have never shared a log. The material is fully satisfied and the title is false of Elm Quay's shifts. Nothing in the material excludes this case — it states the trial covered two buildings and has not expanded.
- The boundary the base contract draws holds "in the title, summary, headings and body alike", so the deck's later qualification does not repair the title.
- Smallest supported repair: "Elm Quay gave two buildings' shifts one repair log" (or "In two buildings, Elm Quay gave its shifts one repair log").

### F2 — A purpose is attributed to the decision that the material does not supply (CONFIRMED)

- Draft (line 7): "the team at Elm Quay Housing decided to try a shared repair log in two of its buildings, and to see what it would take"
- Material: "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings." The only motive given is "The team wanted staff on different shifts to see the same information."
- What differs: motive, which the contract counts as a claim needing support. The draft makes discovering the effort a second aim of the September decision.
- Case in which the material holds and the draft fails: the team decided in September purely to end the split between phone and email reporting and let shifts see the same information, intending to adopt the log whatever the preparation cost; the preparation effort Lind describes was learned incidentally, not sought. This is compatible with every supplied statement, including Lind's "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports", which reports what happened, not what was intended.
- Smallest supported repair: delete ", and to see what it would take", or replace it with the supplied motive: "so that staff on different shifts would see the same information".

### F3 — The heading names a feature and tests it, where the material tests whether the capability existed (CONFIRMED)

- Draft (line 9): "Elm Quay tested the status view before choosing Svale"
- Material: "It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair."
- What differs: the thing named, and modality. "The status view" is a definite description of a named product feature, and testing it presupposes it exists; the material's test is whether the log *could* show the status of each repair, a question whose answer was open when the test began.
- Case in which the material holds and the draft fails: the log has no "status view" — status appears as a column in the ordinary report list — and the team's test was to enter a report and check whether any status was visible at all. The material is satisfied; the heading names a feature that does not exist and describes a test of it. No supplied statement excludes this case; the material never names a view.
- The body sentence beneath the heading is exact ("It tested whether the log could show the status of each repair"), so the defect is confined to the heading.
- Smallest supported repair: "Elm Quay tested whether the log showed status before choosing Svale".

### F4 — The deck attributes the trial note's counting to the maintenance team (PLAUSIBLE)

- Draft (line 3): "What the team counted afterwards is worth reading before you run a trial of your own."
- Material: "Elm Quay's internal trial note, 4 December 2025, says that 31 repair reports were entered."
- What differs: whose act it was. The material attributes the note and its figures to Elm Quay; the draft attributes the counting to the maintenance team specifically.
- Case in which the material holds and the draft fails: the note was compiled by Elm Quay's administration from the log's records, and the maintenance team counted nothing. The material names only "Elm Quay's internal trial note" and never says who produced it, so this case is not excluded.
- The body's own attribution (line 23, "The numbers come from Elm Quay's internal trial note") is correct; only the deck narrows it.
- Smallest supported repair: "What Elm Quay counted afterwards" (or "What the trial note records").
