# Judgement b

## 1. Differences

I compared `work/input.md` with the fenced text in `response.md`, lines 6–27. The fence lines are the reply's wrapping and are not part of the text. There are two differences.

1. Headline. Before: `# Rutan som inte finns`. After: `# Bibliotekets mötesmall frågar inte varför vi ses`. Class: **change to what a claim says, by meaning**. The old headline was an image, "the box that does not exist". The new one is a flat assertion that the library's meeting template does not ask why we meet. The body backs it: "Vad tiden ska leda till frågar den inte om" and "Den säger inte varför vi behöver göra det tillsammans". So the headline adds nothing that goes beyond the body in scope, certainty, attribution, chronology or causality. But it changes which claim the text leads with, and it replaces the author's figurative title with a summary of the thesis. The reply presents this as the repair of a visible defect. I class the motive as a **change of taste**: a figurative headline on a column is normal for the genre and is not a concrete defect you can see in the text.
2. Paragraph 3. Before: `Det som retar mig är något annat — att tid` (spaced em dash). After: `Det som retar mig är något annat – att tid` (spaced en dash). Class: **mechanical correction, a locale form**. Swedish uses the spaced en dash as its tankstreck.

Nothing else changed: the frontmatter (`genre: column`, `technique: none`, `language: sv`), the byline, the other five paragraphs and all formatting are the same byte for byte. Neither difference follows from the other.

## 4. R1

**Fail.** The passage that decides it is finding 1 and the headline it rewrites. The input is a clean column: the only mechanical defect in it is one dash. The Skill kept every argument, quotation, hedge and limiting sentence, and fixed the dash correctly. But it also replaced the author's headline with a thesis-statement headline. Its reasons were search and list legibility and a character count ("20 tecken" against "48 tecken, 7 ord"), not a concrete defect you can see in the text. Findings 2 and 3 judge the same column against an article anatomy it does not claim to follow (a lead, subheadings, "6 stycken"). They were left unapplied, but they show the same measure at work. R1 forbids rewriting a clean text to satisfy taste or numerical guidelines. Here the author's title was lost to exactly that, even though the Skill reported the change accurately and it creates no untrue claim.
