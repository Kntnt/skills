# Judgement a

## 1. Differences

`work/input.md` and `work/output.md` differ in exactly one line. The standfirst, byline, body, subheadings and formatting are byte-identical. Neither file has frontmatter.

- Headline. Before: `# Mötesmallen har en ruta för allt utom varför` (44 characters). After: `# Mötesmallen frågar när men inte varför` (38 characters). Class: **a change to what a claim says (meaning, also scope)**. The run presents it as the repair of a visible defect, but the input headline is not defective (see heading 4). The old headline says, as ironic hyperbole, that the template has a box for everything except the reason for the meeting. The new one says, literally, that the template asks when but not why. The new headline also drops the word "ruta". That word sets up the text's box motif, which returns in "Det är inget fel på rutorna" and in the subheading "Ännu en ruta, och ändå vill jag prova".

No other difference exists, mechanical or otherwise.

## 2. The account

- The headline change is reported twice: under "Åtgärdat fynd (rubriken)" and under "Påståenden / Ändrat". The reply states the before and after wording and the new length (38 characters, six words) correctly. It also correctly says the change is the only one ("Rubriken är den enda ändringen. Brödtexten, underrubrikerna, ingressen och bylinen är orörda") and that the proofreading pass changed nothing.
- The account is not accurate in one respect. It calls the headline a defect: "En läsare som bara såg rubriken fick alltså motsatt bild av textens poäng." That misreads the text. "En ruta för allt utom varför" and "tid är det enda de räknar" make the same point: the template records the logistics and leaves out the purpose. The headline puts that point as hyperbole, and the body puts it plainly. They agree. The reply turns a stylistic choice that works into a reported contradiction.

## 4. R1

**Fail.**

The deciding passage is the change from "# Mötesmallen har en ruta för allt utom varför" to "# Mötesmallen frågar när men inte varför". The reply justifies it as a contradiction ("fick alltså motsatt bild av textens poäng"). R1 says: "Clean texts may not be rewritten to satisfy taste." This control conforms to the anatomy, and its headline is hyperbole that works and agrees with the body. It is not a visible defect. The run rewrote the headline anyway, which changed what the headline claims (meaning) and cut the headline's link to the box motif. The change is not mechanical, so the proofreading exemption does not apply. Everything else, including the 83-word paragraph, the doubt, the recurrence in the body and the closing invitation, was preserved, and the run avoided every rejection the expectation lists. The single false finding on a clean text still decides R1 against the run.
