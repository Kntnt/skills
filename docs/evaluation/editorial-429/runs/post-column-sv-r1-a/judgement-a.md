# Judgement a

## 1. Differences

I compared the text in the fenced block of `response.md` with `work/input.md`, line by line. The frontmatter, the byline, the paragraph breaks and all other formatting are the same. There are two differences.

1. Headline. Before: `# Mallen har en ruta för allt utom poängen`. After: `# Mötesmallen har en ruta för allt utom poängen`. Class: **a change to what a claim says, in its scope.** The scope is narrowed: "Mallen" becomes "the meeting template". This is not hardening, because the headline asserts no more than before; it asserts it about a narrower thing. It is also not the repair of a visible defect. The input's first sentence, "Jag granskar bibliotekets mötesmall", supplies the referent at once, and a column headline with a bare definite noun that the opening resolves is a normal form, not an error. The unqualified "Mallen" also carried a generality that the text itself plays on later ("bibliotekarien som svarar på en mall med mer mall"). The new headline narrows that generality. At best this is a change of taste that also changes scope.
2. Paragraph "Där börjar tvivlet …". Before: `utöka formuläret — bibliotekarien`, with a spaced em dash. After: `utöka formuläret – bibliotekarien`, with a spaced en dash. Class: **a mechanical correction (locale form).** Swedish uses the spaced en dash as its tankstreck. This difference does not follow from the other one.

## 2. The account

1. Headline: **reported, and reported accurately as to what changed.** The reply says: "Ordet "Mallen" har bytts mot "Mötesmallen" (45 tecken, 8 ord)". I checked both counts and they are correct. That matches my classification. What the reply gives as the reason, that the headline "hade ingen referent för den som bara ser rubriken", is a standalone-headline guideline. It is not a defect in the text, because the text supplies the referent in its first sentence.
2. Dash: **reported, and reported accurately.** The reply says: "Den mekaniska slutkorrekturen bytte ett engelskt tankstreck (—) mot ett svenskt tankstreck (–) i stycket som börjar "Där börjar tvivlet"."

The reply also says the metadata is unchanged, and that is true. Its statement "Utöver rubriken står alla påståenden kvar oförändrade" is also true. Findings 2–4 (the missing lead, missing sections and missing call to action) changed nothing in the text; the reply reports them as not written.

## 4. R1

**Fail.** The decision rests on the headline. `# Mallen har en ruta för allt utom poängen` became `# Mötesmallen har en ruta för allt utom poängen`.

The input is a clean text. Its only concrete visible defect is the em dash, and the Skill corrected it properly. The Skill spent its one correction on the headline, applying a guideline that a headline must stand alone. That is not a defect you can see in the text, because the first sentence names "bibliotekets mötesmall". The edit narrows the scope of the headline and loses the generality that the column's closing self-irony plays on. R1 says clean texts may not be rewritten to satisfy taste or guidelines.

In every other respect the run preserved the text. The voice, the arguments and all body claims survived, and every limiting sentence is intact. The account is complete and accurate, and it did not try to verify anything against a source it did not have. Findings 2–4 apply article anatomy to a reflective column, but they were only reported and left the text untouched.
