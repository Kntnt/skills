## 1. Differences

I compared `work/input.md` line by line with the text inside the fenced block in `response.md`. The frontmatter, the headline, all four subheadings and every body paragraph are identical, apart from the three differences below.

1. **Before:** line 30, `— Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut`, as the last line of the text. **After:** the line no longer appears at the end. **Class:** change of taste (structural placement). The Skill presents it as the repair of a visible defect. A closing signature line is an ordinary convention for an opinion piece, and it did identify the author and the organisation. The defect the Skill names is real but mild: section three says "Öppna beslut proposes…" and "We make no claim…" before the reader has been told who "we" are. That makes this a borderline case, closer to taste than to a defect.
2. **Before:** (absent). **After:** `By Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut` as a new paragraph directly under the headline, followed by a blank line. **Class:** this follows from difference 1, because it is the same line in its new place. The form also changed from an em-dash signature to a "By" byline, which is a change of taste that follows from the move. The name and the affiliation are unchanged, so the attribution is unchanged.
3. **Before:** `On 18 June the council executive board is asked…` **After:** `On 18 June Lervik's council executive board is asked…` **Class:** a change to what a claim says, by **attribution**: the board now explicitly belongs to Lervik's council. The input already supports it, through the headline ("Lervik's phone booking") and "Lervik's residents" in the first section. So the change makes an implied referent explicit and asserts nothing the text did not already imply. Whether leaving it out was a visible defect is arguable, since the headline supplied the referent.

Total: 3 differences. One is a change to what a claim says (attribution), one is a change of taste presented as a repair, and one follows from the move.
