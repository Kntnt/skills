## 1. Differences

Frontmatter, headline, all four subheadings and every body paragraph except the lead are identical to the input, and so is the formatting (italics on *Bokning av föreningslokaler*). There are two differences.

1. **Author line moved and reworded.**
   - Before: final line, after the last paragraph: `— Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut`
   - After: new paragraph directly under the headline: `By Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut`
   - Class: **repair of a visible defect**, and a borderline one. In the input the body names "Öppna beslut" and says "We make no claim…" in the third section. Nothing before the closing sign-off says that Öppna beslut is the author's organisation. That is visible from the input alone. But an end-of-piece sign-off is also a recognised form for opinion pieces, so the move is close to a change of taste. The switch from the dash to "By" follows from the move to a heading-side byline. The name, the role and the organisation are unchanged, so no claim changes. Authorship stays with the same person in the same role.

2. **Lead: whose board.**
   - Before: `On 18 June the council executive board is asked to remove phone booking…`
   - After: `On 18 June Lervik's council executive board is asked to remove phone booking…`
   - Class: **change to what a claim says: attribution.** The acting body is now explicitly Lervik's. The headline ("Lervik's phone booking") and section 1 ("Lervik's residents") already imply it, so the change adds nothing the text did not already support. It changes no scope, certainty, chronology, causality or meaning beyond naming the referent. The Skill presents it as the repair of a visible defect, an unanchored referent in the lead. That is defensible, but it is a mild one, because the headline anchors the referent.
