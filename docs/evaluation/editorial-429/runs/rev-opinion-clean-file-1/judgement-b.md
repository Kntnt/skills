It also makes the section three paragraphs long.

## 4. R1

**Fail.**

The deciding passage is the added sentence: "Att avskaffa telefonbokningen i september skulle vila på ett antagande som underlaget inte belägger: att de som bokar per telefon klarar sig utan den och att två bokningsvägar kostar mer i arbete än de ger användarna."

R1 says a clean text may not be rewritten to satisfy taste. The expectation treats this text as clean. The finding it answers is not a concrete visible defect. The standfirst and the first section already show the reader what the unsupported premise is: "de siffrorna räknar bokningar, inte personer", and "Siffrorna visar varken hur många som saknar digital vana eller hur många som skulle klara sig utan telefonbokning". So "antagande" has a readable referent. The final line and the heading work as rhetoric, not as dangling references.

The repair then puts a claim into the text that it never made: a two-part assumption, the second half of which recasts an open question of cost as something the decision assumes. The reply itself concedes this ("Det sa den inte tidigare"). Reporting the change honestly does not make it legitimate. Adding a claim that shifts meaning to a clean control fails R1's test of preservation. That holds even though every named passage the expectation protects survives verbatim, and no rejected finding was made.
