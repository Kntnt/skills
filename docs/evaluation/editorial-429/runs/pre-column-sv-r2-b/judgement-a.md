# Judgement a

## 1. Differences

I compared the text inside the fenced block of `response.md` line by line with `work/input.md`. The frontmatter, the byline, paragraph breaks and all other formatting are identical. There are two differences.

1. Headline.
   - Before: `# Rutan som inte finns`
   - After: `# Bibliotekets mötesmall frågar när och vem, inte varför`
   - Class: **a change to what a claim says, by meaning.** The old headline named an absence ("the box that does not exist") and left the reader to find out which box. The new one asserts a thesis: the template asks *when* and *who* but not *why*. The body supports that reading ("Mallen vill veta när vi ses och vilka som kommer. Vad tiden ska leda till frågar den inte om."; "Den säger inte varför vi behöver göra det tillsammans."). Still, the headline now says something different. The missing thing is no longer the box for a decision (paragraph 1). It is now the question "why", which is the writer's later proposal and not the observation. This is not the repair of a visible defect. The old headline is grammatical and makes sense, and the first paragraph answers it at once. The Skill made the change to meet a genre guideline: a standalone-intelligible headline within character and word limits. That motive is a matter of taste or a numerical guideline. The change also removes the headline's echo of "ruta", which the closing sentence picks up ("tvivlet på ännu en ruta"), so it costs the author's voice. No verb or later sentence depends on it.
2. Paragraph 3 (line 16), dash.
   - Before: `något annat — att tid`
   - After: `något annat – att tid`
   - Class: **mechanical correction (locale form).** A spaced em dash became the spaced en dash that Swedish uses as its tankstreck. The meaning does not change.

## 4. R1

**Fail.**

The deciding passage is the headline rewrite and the reason the reply gives for it: "”Rutan som inte finns” (20 tecken, 4 ord) gick inte att förstå utan att läsa texten. Den angav inte heller krönikans vinkel."

The input is a clean column. Its only visible defect is the dash, and the Skill corrected that properly. The Skill left every argument, quotation and limiting sentence in the body intact. It also correctly declined to write the missing parts it flagged (lead, sections, reader call to action) and handed them back to the writer. But it rewrote the author's headline to meet a guideline on intelligibility and length, not to repair a concrete defect. In doing so it changed what the headline says (meaning) and lost a voice element the closing line echoes. R1 forbids rewriting a clean text to satisfy taste or numerical guidelines. The honest reporting of the change does not cure that.
