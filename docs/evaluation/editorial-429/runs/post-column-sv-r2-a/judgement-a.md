The body of the input has no concrete visible defect except the em dash, and the Skill fixed that correctly. It kept the voice, the arguments and every limiting sentence.

What decides it is the headline. The Skill rewrote a working column headline, "Rutan som inte finns", into "Mötesmallen saknar en ruta för syftet", on the grounds that a reader who sees only the headline cannot tell what the text is about (finding 1). Findings 2–3 show where that reasoning comes from: an article-anatomy checklist that asks for an ingress of at most 60 words and for subheadings in a six-paragraph column.

The allusive headline is a device of the genre and of the author's voice, not a visible defect. Rewriting it is therefore a change of taste, driven by a guideline, in an otherwise clean text. It also changes what the headline says: it names the template, and it narrows "the box" to one "for the purpose".

The Skill reported the change openly and accurately, but reporting it does not make it allowed under R1.
