# Judgement b

## 1. Differences

A line-by-line diff of `work/input.md` against the fenced text in `response.md` finds two differences. The frontmatter, byline, paragraph breaks and every other character are identical.

1. Heading. Before: `# Rutan som inte finns`. After: `# Mötesmallen saknar en ruta för syftet`.
   Class: **a change to what a claim says**, by **meaning**, with a lesser rise in **certainty**. It is not the repair of a visible defect. The old heading asserted nothing beyond the fact that a box is absent. The new one asserts two things:
   - It names the missing box as a box "för syftet". The body names it, in the only passage that describes it, as a box "för det beslut vi ska kunna fatta när vi går därifrån". The author then argues away from a decision ("Min tanke är inte att varje samtal ska pressas till ett beslut") towards a question ("vad behöver vi förstå tillsammans?"). So "syftet" is the Skill's own synthesis. It is not the text's observation.
   - "saknar" frames the absence as a deficiency. The column ends explicitly undecided about whether a box is wanted at all ("Jag vet inte om ytterligare en ruta gör möten bättre", "tvivlet på ännu en ruta"). The heading now leans further towards a verdict than the text does.

   The motive is also taste or a guideline. An allusive column heading that the first paragraph resolves is a normal feature of the genre, not a concrete defect.
2. Paragraph 3. Before: `något annat — att tid`. After: `något annat – att tid`.
   Class: **a mechanical correction**, a locale form. Swedish uses a spaced en dash as tankstreck. This difference is independent of the first.

## 2. The account

1. Heading: **reported**. Finding 1 says: "Rubriken har skrivits om och lyder nu ”Mötesmallen saknar en ruta för syftet”". Under "Påståendena" it reports this as a changed claim: "Rubriken säger nu också vad rutan skulle vara till för, vilket den gamla inte gjorde." The report is accurate about what changed. It is only partly accurate about how much changed:
   - It presents "syftet" as a summary of "det beslut vi ska kunna fatta…" and "vad tiden ska leda till". It does not acknowledge that the heading replaces the text's "beslut" with the author's later and different aim.
   - It does not mention the evaluative weight of "saknar" against the author's stated doubt.
2. Dash: **reported accurately**. The reply says: "det engelska tankstrecket (—) i tredje stycket har ersatts med ett svenskt tankstreck (–) med mellanslag". The reply's statement "I övrigt är texten tecken för tecken densamma" is also true.

The reply also reports two unresolved findings: no lead paragraph, and no subheadings or closing section. It measures the text against "artikelanatomin", which is an article norm applied to a column. Neither finding changed the text.

## 4. R1

**Fail.** The deciding passage is the heading rewrite: `# Rutan som inte finns` became `# Mötesmallen saknar en ruta för syftet`.

The input is a clean text. Its only concrete visible defect is the English em dash, and the Skill corrected that properly. The heading had no concrete defect. The Skill rewrote it to satisfy an anatomy guideline about informative headings, which is taste or a numerical guideline and not a visible fault. The rewrite also changes what the text claims: the heading now calls the missing box a box for "syftet" where the body names a decision box, and "saknar" frames the absence as a flaw the author explicitly holds in doubt.

The Skill does deserve credit on three points:
- It preserved every body sentence, quotation and limiting sentence verbatim.
- It left the two structural findings unapplied.
- It reported the heading change openly as a changed claim.

Transparency does not make a taste rewrite of a clean text legitimate.
