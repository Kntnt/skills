Five passages I tested individually, each of which holds:

1. **Headline and standfirst name understanding where the notes' explicit absence is of a decision box.** "Mötesmallen saknar en ruta för förståelse" is not stated in the notes. It is entailed by them: "vill jag prova en enkel fråga bredvid tiden: vad behöver vi förstå tillsammans" and "ytterligare en ruta" both presuppose that no such field exists today. The case that would break it — a purpose or understanding field already in the template — is excluded by the notes' own "ytterligare". Supported.
2. **"Tiden, däremot, har mallen två rutor för."** A count not stated in the notes, derived from their enumeration, which names exactly two time fields (starttid, sluttid) in what the notes treat as the template's inventory. The criterion asks a count-size judgement to rest on a supplied comparison; the comparison here — two boxes for time against none for the decision — is the notes' own "men ingen ruta för det beslut". Supported, and no evaluative size word is attached to the number.
3. **The caveat's scope.** The notes attach "Det här är min reflektion, inte något jag har mätt hos andra" to a paragraph holding both the appreciation and the irritation; the draft attaches it to the irritation paragraph only, and the appreciation follows in the next paragraph. The caveat is not dropped, and what it no longer covers asserts no measurement: "Jag uppskattar" is an avowed preference and "kan vara viktigare" is a possibility. No empirical claim is upgraded. Supported.
4. **"Också när syftet är att undersöka något tillsammans går det att säga vad vi vill undersöka."** The notes carry the concessive clause but not the feasibility. Read in the paragraph it sits in — after "borde få formulera" and before "Det är ingen hög tröskel" — it is the columnist arguing for her own proposal, not a claim about what people generally manage; a "borde få formulera" of this kind presupposes the formulating can be done. Supported as her argument. The delivery account reports it anyway (§9).
5. **"Pröva den gärna i din egen mall, och behåll tvivlet på rutan medan du gör det."** No second person appears anywhere in the notes, whose closing is first person: "Jag vill behålla både hoppet om den frågan och tvivlet på ännu en ruta." The hope half is carried by the preceding "Ändå vill jag prova". Its only presupposition about the reader — that they have a template — is the brief's own audience, "yrkesverksamma som planerar möten". Supported.

The boundaries hold: no meeting, journey, childhood memory, line of dialogue or bodily sensation is narrated; no statistic, study, expert or general trend appears; the stance is curious and self-ironic, not meeting-hostile, and the concession that decisionless conversations have value is explicit ("Ett samtal utan beslut kan också ha ett syfte", "Samtal kan skapa förtroende"). No unsupported addition, changed term, changed subject, changed scope, changed modality, dropped caveat, invented event or invented personal attribute. **Pass.**

## 4. G1 — genre does its job: **pass**

The named reader is "yrkesverksamma som planerar möten". The angle is recognisable and held from the headline to the last line: a form that has two fields for time and none for what the time is for. The reader sees the observation ("Där finns rutor för starttid, sluttid, deltagare och dagordning"), considers a proposition they can test against their own practice ("Att gå därifrån med en skarpare bild av var vi skiljer oss är också något att ha åstadkommit, men det finns ingen ruta att skriva in det i"), and leaves with one thing they can do: put "vad behöver vi förstå tillsammans?" beside the time field.

The craft the brief asks for is present. The author's eye is visible in "Tiden, däremot, har mallen två rutor för" — a fronted topic that makes the contrast itself the joke. The låg mälda humour the brief asks for lands on the notes' own irony: "Det finns en komik i att försöka lösa ett problem med formulär genom att utöka formuläret." Nothing reads as a debate campaign or a purchase call, both of which the brief forbids; the strongest exhortation is "Pröva den gärna", immediately qualified by "och behåll tvivlet på rutan medan du gör det".

## 5. G2 — required parts, distinct jobs: **fail**

Almost everything G2 asks for is here, and the column-specific requirements are met unusually well. The failure is narrow and I state it first.

**The failure.** G2 requires subheadings that are understood on their own *without repeating the first sentence under them*. The third subheading is "Jag vet inte om rutan skulle hjälpa"; the sentence directly under it is "Jag vet inte om ytterligare en ruta gör möten bättre." Five opening words are identical, the noun is the same, and "skulle hjälpa" only paraphrases "gör möten bättre". The section's pivot — the turn into the honest doubt that the brief explicitly wants the ending to keep — is spent twice in a row, so the reader's first line of the section tells them nothing they did not have from the heading. Following the requirement was plainly possible: a heading naming what the section does with the doubt rather than restating it (the comedy of solving a form problem with more form, for instance) would have headed the same section without the stall. This is a contract rejection on a plainly stated requirement, not a wording preference; the repair is one line.

The headline's echo of the standfirst I do **not** count against G2. "Mötesmallen saknar en ruta för förståelse" and the standfirst's "hittar ingen ruta för den frågan i bibliotekets mötesmall" restate one proposition, but the standfirst is separately required to stand alone, which it does — "den frågan" refers back to the standfirst's own opening question, "Vad ska ett möte göra möjligt?", not to the headline — and it adds the author, the proposal and the doubt, none of which is in the headline. Some overlap is the price of a standfirst that does not lean on the headline.

**Everything else in G2 passes:**

- **Order and presence.** The script's `parts` holds `headline`, `standfirst`, `byline`, `lead` and three `sections`, with `other: []`, so every required part is present in the anatomy's order and nothing foreign sits among them.
- **Standfirst stands alone.** 47 words, `sentences_estimate` 2, `paragraphs` 1 (script). It names the author and role, the observation, the proposal and the uncertainty, and needs no other part to be read.
- **Lead begins the work and precedes the first H2.** The script parses "Jag granskar bibliotekets mötesmall…" as `lead` (42 words, 1 paragraph), and it stands between the byline and `## Jag värderar det mallen inte frågar efter`. It opens the work rather than summarising it, on the document observation.
- **Explanatory body.** Three sections, each doing its own job: what the template asks for against what she values; that a decisionless conversation still has a purpose; the doubt.
- **Ending shows the lead's expectation met.** The lead sets up a template with no field for what should come out of the meeting; the close returns to that field, declines to be sure it would help, and hands over the question.
- **Byline.** "Av Nora Vik" (script: `byline`, 3 words) names the author the brief names — "Författaren är den fiktiva bibliotekarien Nora Vik" — and the delivery account states it: "The byline carries the author the brief names."

## 8. L1 — professional Swedish: **pass**

The prose reads as written in Swedish, not rendered into it. The resolved locale is `sv` (the frontmatter's `language: sv`, and the brief's source language). Swedish mechanics govern throughout: sentence-case headings rather than English title case ("Mötesmallen saknar en ruta för förståelse", "Ett samtal utan beslut kan också ha ett syfte"); correct solid compounds with no särskrivning ("mötesmall", "starttid", "sluttid", "dagordning", "formulär"); "kolleger", the material's own accepted plural; the direct question inside the sentence at "en enkel fråga bredvid tiden: vad behöver vi förstå tillsammans?" punctuated Swedish-fashion, colon then lower case. One observation. The standfirst's only dash is U+2014 EM DASH with spaces — "…varandras tid — utan att hon vet…" — where a Swedish house style following Svenska skrivregler sets the tankstreck as a spaced en dash. It is the sole non-ASCII punctuation mark in the text, it functions correctly as a tankstreck, and the spaced em dash is established variation in Swedish web publishing, so I record it rather than fail on it. It is the one thing a final proofread in this locale would change.
