## Pronoun, person, number and natural-gender accounting

- **“det” in “när det prövade”** is third-person singular referring to the collective maintenance team. Swedish neuter grammatical gender follows “team”; it makes no assertion about team members' natural gender. The source likewise uses singular “team” and collective “It.”
- **“det egna underhållsteamet”** contains the definite article and an ownership adjective, not a human neuter-gender assertion. “Own” is explicitly supplied in **“its own maintenance team”**; the company is the owner/affiliated organization.
- **“Vi”** in the first and second quotations is first-person plural. **“We”** supplies both person and number. Its group is the customer's maintenance group in context; the draft does not invent a named membership list or a gender composition.
- **“våra”** is first-person plural possession of plural categories, supported by **“ours”**. Its form agrees with the possessed categories and expresses no human natural gender.
- **“oss”** in the first and last quotations is first-person plural object, supported by **“us”**. The customer's group remains the beneficiary. No singular self-reference, resident group or supplier group is substituted.
- **“dem”** in the first quotation is third-person plural object referring to categories. The original **“them”** refers to the same plural categories. Neither supplies human natural gender.
- **“Jag”** in the second and last quotations is first-person singular referring to Maya Lind. The original **“I”** independently supplies person and number; identifying Lind as referent is not needed to infer those features. The Swedish pronoun itself asserts no natural gender.
- **“den tiden”** refers to the time for agreeing on categories, corresponding to **“that time”**. “Den” is a singular demonstrative with Swedish common grammatical gender from “tid,” not a natural-gender pronoun.
- **“nästa hus”** is singular, matching **“the next building”**. The translation problem concerns the unspecified action with “börjar,” not number or gender.
- **“Den redovisar”** is third-person singular referring to the internal note. “Den” has Swedish common grammatical gender from “anteckning”; its nonhuman referent remains the note, as the source's **“It”** does in **“It excludes emergencies”**. No female human referent is introduced.
- **“de boende”** is the plural class of residents. **“residents”** and **“resident-satisfaction”** establish that population in context. “De” here forms a nominal expression and makes no assertion that a particular subset, number or natural gender participated.
- **“de uppmätta tiderna,” “de första anmälningarna” and “de föregående åtta veckorna”** use plural definite articles, not human-gender pronouns. They agree with the measured intervals, first reports and preceding weeks already established.
- **“Det saknas”** uses impersonal/expletive “det.” It does not identify a person, team or company, and is the Swedish construction for the source's **“There are no ... measurements.”**
- **“dessa delar”** is a plural demonstrative referring to the immediately named cost, resident-satisfaction and completion-time dimensions. Those three dimensions are source-supported; the writer's evidential conclusion is accounted for separately above.
- **“Du,” “dig,” “ditt” and “eget”** address one generic reader/user, respectively in the optional-reading invitation and visible byline placeholder. They add no natural-gender information and assert no actual reader behavior. The address is editorial rather than an attributed source quotation.
- **“morgonpasset” and “kvällspasset”** are singular collective shift expressions; both languages use that collectivity. They do not turn an entire shift into one individual worker or assert any worker's gender.
- **“arbetsledaren,” “medarbetare,” “personal” and the proper name “Maya Lind”** convey no additional natural gender in the Swedish draft. The source itself uses **“Her final quotation”**, but the draft supplies no “hon,” “hennes” or other human-gender claim. There is therefore no unsupported natural-gender inference from the name or job title.

## Findings and smallest supported repairs

### S1. Source support: the note is attributed specifically to the team

Draft passage: **“teamets interna anteckning”** in the introduction.

Source passage: **“Elm Quay's internal trial note, 4 December 2025, says that 31 repair reports were entered.”** The workload and causation qualifications attach to that note: **“The periods had different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software.”**

What differs: the institution to which the source note is attributed. The material identifies the company's internal trial note. It does not establish an internal maintenance-team note or that the company note belongs specifically to that team. The body correctly uses **“Bolagets interna anteckning”**, but that does not remove the introductory narrowing.

Concrete test, source true and draft false: Elm Quay's central operations analyst creates and retains an internal company trial note from the log records, independently of the maintenance team's own records. The note has the supplied date, count, exclusions and caveat. It is Elm Quay's internal trial note, but it is not the team's internal note. No supplied sentence excludes this arrangement. The source's **“its own maintenance team decided to trial”** establishes who initiated the trial, not ownership or authorship of the later note.

Reverse test: a team-internal trial note within Elm Quay can also be the company's internal trial note. That direction has no necessary conflict; the defect is the unsupported narrower attribution, not denial of company affiliation.

Smallest repair: **“bolagets interna anteckning”** in place of **“teamets interna anteckning”**. Keep the rest of the sentence and its justified limit on what the figures establish.

### S2. Source support: an exact team-leadership relationship is added

Draft passage: **“underhållsteamets arbetsledare Maya Lind”** in the first quotation's attribution.

Source passage: **“Customer interview with maintenance supervisor Maya Lind, complete usable quotations:”**. The first quotation is **“We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log.”**

What differs: the title **“maintenance supervisor”** is tied in the draft to leadership of the particular team that ran the trial. The source gives the maintenance supervisory role, and establishes Lind's customer's-group voice, but does not specify the reporting relationship of this particular team.

Concrete test, source true and draft false: Lind is an Elm Quay maintenance supervisor responsible for contractor work or another maintenance subgroup, while another supervisor directs the own maintenance team that designed the categories and ran this trial. Lind can truthfully use the company/customer-group “we” and “ours” in the supplied quotations. Her supplied job title remains true, but she is not this trial team's arbetsledare. No supplied statement says she leads that team or excludes that organizational arrangement.

Reverse test: if Lind is this maintenance team's supervisor, she is a maintenance supervisor. The narrower relation implies the source role; the unsupported step is the added specific relationship, not the supervisory title itself.

Smallest repair: **“arbetsledaren för underhåll Maya Lind”** in place of **“underhållsteamets arbetsledare Maya Lind”**. Retain **“skriver”** and **“i en mejlintervju”**, which are supported.

### T1. Quotation translation: the building is left “beginning” without an intelligible action

Draft passage: **“Jag skulle avsätta den tiden innan nästa hus börjar.”**

Source passage: **“I would set that time aside before the next building starts.”** Context: **“In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings.”** **“The trial has not yet expanded; the team will decide after checking how the categories work for larger repairs.”**

Meaning and voice: Lind conditionally recommends allowing time to agree on the categories before starting the trial in the next building. The Swedish retains the first person, conditional stance and time allocation. Its obstruction is the bare **“nästa hus börjar”**: in Swedish the physical house has no established action to begin, and the expression can instead suggest beginning construction of the next house. The construction event is not the source's trial rollout. This is more than a preference between synonyms: the omitted action prevents a clear idiomatic reading of the intended activity.

Concrete reading test: a Swedish reader can take **“innan nästa hus börjar”** to mean before work on building the next house begins, even if that house will not yet enter the repair-log trial. That is not the preparation threshold intended by the supplied trial context. Conversely, beginning a repair-log trial in an existing next building requires no house construction. The source's trial context settles the implicit action sufficiently to name it; the repair need not invent a future expansion decision.

Smallest supported repair: **“Jag skulle avsätta den tiden innan försöket börjar i nästa hus.”** This supplies the contextually settled action while keeping the speaker's conditional recommendation and the next-building scope. It does not say that the next building has been selected or that expansion is approved.
