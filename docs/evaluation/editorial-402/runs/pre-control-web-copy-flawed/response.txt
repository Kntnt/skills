I reviewed `input.md` as web copy in American English, made one correction round and ran the closing proofread pass. The review found six problems, and all six are fixed. The proofread pass made no changes, and `input.md` itself is untouched. No writing technique was applied, because the web-copy genre doesn't name one.

````markdown
# A review of shared-room booking for housing associations

Svale offers a review of how one shared room in one housing association is booked. The review costs SEK 4,800 including VAT. It includes a 45-minute video call with two board representatives and a written account of the booking steps, unclear points and two possible simplifications.

## How to express interest and what happens next

[Open the expression-of-interest form](https://example.invalid/svale/intresse).

The link opens an expression-of-interest form asking for a name, association and email address. It places no order, requires no payment details and books no meeting. Svale replies by email within three working days to discuss whether the review is suitable and suggest a meeting time.
````

**What the review found and fixed**
1. **The page never said what it offers.** The words "the review" and "Svale" first appeared in the last sentence, so "the review" referred back to nothing.
2. **Headline.** "Unlock your association's full potential" was corporate filler, and nothing in the text supported its promise.
3. **Opening paragraph.**
   - "Complex reality… growing complexity" is an empty opening that repeats itself.
   - "Take the thumb out" is a word-for-word Swedish idiom that doesn't work in English.
   - Nothing in the text supported "a fantastic journey".
4. **Link label.** "Book and pay now" contradicted the page's own statement that the form places no order, takes no payment and books no meeting.
5. **Headings.** "Start", "It", "More", "Again", "Things" and "Important" didn't say what was under them. Read on its own, each "It…" section had nothing for "It" to refer to.
6. **Choppy structure.** Price, scope and contents were split into four one-sentence sections, each built as "It costs / It covers / It includes / It includes".

**Claims removed**
- "Decision-makers in a complex reality face growing complexity" was removed under finding 3, which counted the whole sentence as the problem.
- "discover a fantastic journey" was removed under finding 3, as an unsupported promise.
- The promise of the association's "full potential" was removed under finding 2.

**Claims changed**
- **Link label:** it no longer says that the reader books and pays (finding 4). It now describes what the link opens, which matches the last paragraph.
- **What the service is:** "It covers one shared room in one housing association" became "Svale offers a review of how one shared room in one housing association is booked" (findings 1 and 6).
  - Svale is now named as the provider. The original text only implied this.
  - The review is now described as being about the booking process. That comes from its written account of "the booking steps", but the original text never said it outright.

  Please confirm that this description is accurate.

**What changed overall:** The filler headline and empty opening are gone. A headline and opening paragraph now say what is offered, by whom and for how much. The four one-sentence sections are merged into that paragraph, and the six headings became one heading that says what the section covers. The link now comes after the offer, and its label matches what it does. The paragraph listing what the form does not do is word for word the same. The proofread pass found no spelling, grammar or punctuation errors to correct.
