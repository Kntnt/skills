## 1. Differences

I compared the text line by line. The frontmatter, the headline, all four subheadings, the formatting and the body paragraphs about the pilot, the proposal and the trial proposal are identical. There are four differences.

1. Byline. Before: `*Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut*`. After: `*By Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut*`. Class: **change of taste**. A byline without "By" is a normal, readable convention. It is not a visible defect.
2. Lead, second sentence. Before: `keep both routes for six months, in all seven venues`. After: `keep both routes, web and telephone, for six months, in all seven venues`. Class: **change of taste** (the Skill calls it a clarification). The input's "both routes" already meant web and telephone: the sentence before it names the telephone route, and the rest of the text names the web as the other. The scope of the proposal is the same, so this is not a change to what a claim says, although the reply reports it as one.
3. "Six months" section, last sentence. Before: `I am against deciding before the cost of the work can be weighed against what each route is worth to the people who use it.` After: `... before the cost of the double administration can be weighed against ...`. Class: **change to what a claim says, by meaning**. It also narrows scope a little: "the work" is now only the double administration. It follows a real finding. "The work" has no stated antecedent, and the nearest cost is the trial's ("what it would cost is for the board to weigh"). The reading the Skill chose fits the text's own term, and the "time figures" the trial is meant to produce.
4. Ending, first sentence. Before: `It can adopt the September removal ...`. After: `The municipal executive board can adopt the September removal ...`. Class: **repair of a visible defect**. The pronoun opens a section, and only the subheading supplies its antecedent. The referent is unchanged.

No difference is consequent on another. There are no mechanical corrections.
