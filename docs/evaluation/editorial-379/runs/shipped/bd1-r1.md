## 4. Findings

### F1 — The denial of what the evaluation measures is broadened from prior knowledge to knowledge

- **Draft (§ "What the evaluation counted"):** "The evaluation does not measure participants' knowledge."
- **Source:** "Den mäter inte deltagarnas förkunskaper."
- **What differs:** the thing measured, and with it the scope of the denial. "Förkunskaper" is prior knowledge — what a participant brought to the course. The draft denies measurement of "knowledge" without restriction, which covers knowledge at any point, including what participants knew or could do when the course ended. The source denies only the baseline.
- **Test, source-holds-and-draft-fails:** the evaluation rests in part on "kursledarnas skriftliga sammanfattningar". Suppose those summaries record, for each round, what participants were able to do by the final week — a course leader's assessment of end-of-course skill — while no baseline was ever taken at intake. That case is compatible with everything supplied: nothing in the material says what the summaries contain, and nothing says the evaluation is silent about knowledge as such. In that case the source's statement holds (no prior knowledge is measured) and the draft's fails (knowledge is measured, at the end). No supplied statement excludes the case; "Den mäter inte deltagarnas förkunskaper" restricts the denial to förkunskaper and says nothing about later measurement, and "Inga effektstudier ... är belagda" concerns effect studies rather than an end-of-course description.
- **Test, the other direction:** none is available, and none is needed. Prior knowledge falls under knowledge, so any case in which the draft's broader denial holds is one in which the source's narrower denial holds too. The divergence runs one way only: the draft asserts strictly more than the material supports.
- **Why it is a defect and not a preference:** the broadened denial is the load-bearing step of the article's central argument. The draft's own next move, 1.13, is that the evaluation cannot say "what those people could do afterward that they could not do before" — a before-and-after claim that needs only the missing baseline. The broader sentence claims the committee has no knowledge measurement at all, which is a stronger charge against the decision basis than the material licenses, in a text whose boundary is that the author may criticise the documents sharply but may not attribute what is not in them.
- **Smallest supported repair:** insert the qualifier the source carries — "The evaluation does not measure participants' prior knowledge." Nothing else in the paragraph or in the argument that follows needs to change; 1.13 reads more tightly against the repaired sentence than against the present one.

### F2 — "Doubling" quantifies the proposed expansion beyond anything the material states

- **Draft (§ "The staff recommendation rests on one number"):** "That is a thin basis for doubling a course."
- **Source, what is proposed:** "Tjänsteutlåtandet ... föreslår att kursen permanentas och byggs ut till sex omgångar per år."
- **Source, what has been run:** "Kommunens utvärdering ... gäller tre kursomgångar vid vuxenutbildningen i Rönnvik under hösten 2025."
- **What differs:** the size of the change. The material gives the proposed level (six rounds per year) and states that this is an expansion ("byggs ut"), but nowhere gives the current annual number of rounds. "Doubling" asserts a specific ratio between the current level and the proposed one — a factual claim about a magnitude — where the material supports only "more than now". The three rounds the draft itself reports are the rounds the evaluation covers in one autumn term, not an annual total.
- **Test, source-holds-and-draft-fails:** suppose the course also ran one round in the spring of 2025, so four rounds ran in the year before the evaluation, and the evaluation simply covers the three autumn rounds within its stated period. That case is compatible with the material: the evaluation is defined as covering three rounds "under hösten 2025" and nowhere claims to cover every round the course has ever run, and no supplied statement gives an annual figure that would exclude it. In that case the source holds (the proposal expands the course to six rounds a year) and the draft fails (six is half again as many as four, not double). A second case runs the other way on the same defect: if the three evaluated rounds were one term's worth and the same cadence ran in the spring, the course already runs six rounds a year and "byggs ut" refers to something the material does not detail — again no doubling. Nothing supplied excludes either case.
- **Test, the other direction:** a case in which the draft holds and the source fails cannot be built, because the draft's claim entails the source's; the defect is one-directional overstatement, as in F1.
- **Why it is a defect and not a preference:** "thin basis" is evaluation and the brief permits it, but "doubling" is a number, and a number in a sentence that judges the decision basis. The boundary requires figures that carry the argument to be attributable to the evaluation; this one is attributable to nothing supplied, and it sits in the paragraph where the article's charge against the utlåtande is made.
- **Smallest supported repair:** drop the quantification and keep the author's judgment and the proposal's own terms — "That is a thin basis for making a course permanent and expanding it." If the expansion's size is wanted in the sentence, the supported form is the proposal's own figure, already given one sentence earlier: "That is a thin basis for a permanent course at six rounds a year."
