## Findings

### Finding 1 — the denial of what the evaluation measures is broader in the draft than in the material

**Draft:** "The evaluation does not measure participants' knowledge."

**Material:** "Den mäter inte deltagarnas förkunskaper."

**What differs:** the thing measured. *Förkunskaper* is prior knowledge — what participants brought with them before the course. The draft denies measurement of participants' knowledge as such, with no temporal restriction, which covers knowledge at any point, including at the end of the four weeks.

**Case in which the material's statement holds and the draft's fails:** the course leaders' written summaries, on which the evaluation is expressly built, record for each round how well participants handled the e-services the course covered by the final week, but nothing is recorded about what any of them could do on arrival. The evaluation then measures participants' knowledge — at the end — while not measuring their prior knowledge. The material's sentence is true of that evaluation; the draft's is false of it.

**Compatibility with the supplied material:** the material states only that the evaluation "bygger på närvarolistor och kursledarnas skriftliga sammanfattningar" and says nothing about what those summaries contain. No supplied statement excludes end-of-course knowledge appearing in them. The case need not be likely, only compatible, and it is.

**Opposite direction:** the draft's statement entails the material's, so no case exists in which the draft holds and the material fails. The change is a one-way broadening.

**Why it matters in context:** the draft's own argument two sentences later — "It does not tell the committee what those people could do afterward that they could not do before" — is precisely the *before*/*after* gap that *förkunskaper* names. The broadened denial claims more than the material gives while making the argument that follows rest on a claim the material does not support.

**Smallest supported repair:** "The evaluation does not measure participants' prior knowledge."

### Finding 2 — "doubling" is a quantitative claim the material does not support

**Draft:** "That is a thin basis for doubling a course."

**Material:** "gäller tre kursomgångar vid vuxenutbildningen i Rönnvik under hösten 2025"; "föreslår att kursen permanentas och byggs ut till sex omgångar per år".

**What differs:** the thing measured, and the time base. The material gives a round count for one period only — three rounds during the autumn of 2025 — and a proposed annual rate of six. It nowhere states how many rounds the course runs in a year now. "Doubling" asserts that the proposal is twice the present volume, which requires a present annual figure the material does not contain. The comparison in the draft sets three-in-one-term against six-in-a-year, two different time bases.

**Case in which the material's statements hold and the draft's fails:** the course has been running four rounds a year — three in the autumn term of 2025 and one in the spring term of 2026. Six rounds a year is then an expansion by half, which is what *byggs ut* says, and is not a doubling. Every supplied statement remains true of that case: the evaluation still covers exactly the three autumn 2025 rounds, and the recommendation still proposes building the course out to six rounds a year.

**Compatibility with the supplied material:** nothing supplied states the current annual number of rounds, or that the autumn 2025 rounds were the whole of a year's provision. No supplied statement excludes the case.

**Opposite direction:** no case exists in which "doubling" holds and a supplied statement fails, because the material makes no claim about the magnitude of the increase at all. The defect is an unsupported addition rather than a contradiction.

**What is not at issue:** "a thin basis" stands. It is the author's assessment in a signed opinion piece, resting on the two absences the material states, and is defensible as written.

**Smallest supported repair:** "That is a thin basis for expanding a course." If the magnitude is wanted, the supported form is the recommendation's own: "That is a thin basis for building the course out to six rounds a year."

## Editorial questions (not findings)

### Q1 — "Lyssna Först has no measurement of the course's effect"

**Draft:** "Lyssna Först has no measurement of the course's effect, and we do not claim one."

**Material:** "Föreningen gör inget anspråk på att ha någon egen mätning av kursens effekt."

The second clause reproduces the material. The first states as fact what the material states only as a claim the association does not make, and it drops *egen* ("own"). A case is nameable in which the material holds and the first clause fails — the association has an informal canvass of former participants and chooses not to put it forward as a measurement — and nothing supplied excludes it.

Against that: the sentence is the sender's first-person concession about the sender's own organisation, in a piece bylined by that organisation's chair, and the brief carries the association's position as its own. Read as an ordinary reader would read it in a debate article, "we have none and claim none" is what a party conceding it has no effect measurement says. Whether this is a factual assertion about an actual body or the commissioning party's own standpoint about its own case is genuinely unsettled here, so it is reported as the brief's own unevidenced statement rather than as a defect with a repair.

If it is to be brought flush with the material, the narrowest form is: "Lyssna Först makes no claim to have its own measurement of the course's effect." That makes the second clause redundant, so one of the two would go.

## Translation check (separate from source support)

The draft contains no quoted speech. Aziz's supplied standpoint is paraphrased into the draft's own first-person prose, which the material permits ("Detta får citeras eller refereras"), and the byline makes Aziz the speaker, so the paraphrase carries no attribution loss.

Judged as the rendering of his distinctive voice: "Forty-eight people finishing a course says the course could be finished. It does not say it gave them something they did not already have." The Swedish is deliberately flat and tautological — *säger att kursen gick att gå klart* — and the English keeps that flatness rather than smoothing it into an idiom. The doubled *säger / säger inte* becomes "says / does not say", preserving the parallel. "Could be finished" is idiomatic target-language English for *gick att gå klart*. No concrete obstruction to the reading is identifiable, and no meaning is changed in crossing languages.

No translation findings. Both findings above are source-support findings.
