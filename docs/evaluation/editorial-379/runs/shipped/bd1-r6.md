## 2. Findings

### F1 — the denial of what the evaluation measures is wider in the draft than in the material

- **Draft:** "The evaluation does not measure participants' knowledge."
- **Source:** "Den mäter inte deltagarnas förkunskaper."
- **What differs:** the thing measured. *Förkunskaper* is prior knowledge — what a participant brought to the course. The draft drops that restriction and denies that the evaluation measures participants' knowledge at all, at any point.
- **Case in which the source statement holds and the draft's fails:** the evaluation rests in part on "kursledarnas skriftliga sammanfattningar". Suppose each course leader's summary records an end-of-course assessment — what the participants could do by the final week — while nothing recorded what any of them knew on arrival. Then "den mäter inte deltagarnas förkunskaper" is true and "the evaluation does not measure participants' knowledge" is false. The material is compatible with this: it names the evaluation's two inputs but never says what the summaries contain, and the only measurement it denies is of förkunskaper. No supplied statement excludes the case.
- **Reverse direction:** none. The draft's denial entails the source's, so the draft is the stronger of the two; that asymmetry is the defect.
- **Why it is load-bearing:** the widened sentence closes the section that the next paragraph builds on. "It does not tell the committee what those people could do afterward that they could not do before" then reads as something the evaluation's own stated limits establish, rather than as the author's inference from a missing baseline.
- **Smallest supported repair:** "The evaluation does not measure participants' prior knowledge."
- The repair does not weaken C12: a missing baseline is exactly what blocks a before-and-after comparison, so the author's next paragraph follows from the narrower denial.

### F2 — "doubling" states a magnitude of expansion the material does not give

- **Draft:** "That is a thin basis for doubling a course."
- **Source:** "Tjänsteutlåtandet ... föreslår att kursen permanentas och byggs ut till sex omgångar per år." Together with: "Kommunens utvärdering ... gäller tre kursomgångar vid vuxenutbildningen i Rönnvik under hösten 2025."
- **What differs:** the thing measured. The material gives the proposed figure (six rounds a year) and that the proposal is an expansion ("byggs ut"). It never states how many rounds the course runs in a year as things stand. "Doubling" asserts that the present rate is three a year, taking the three evaluated autumn rounds for the whole year's programme.
- **Case in which the material holds and the draft's claim fails:** the course ran three rounds in autumn 2025 and two in spring 2025 — five a year. "Byggs ut till sex omgångar per år" still holds, since six is more than five, and "doubling" fails. The material is compatible with this: the sentence about three rounds states which rounds *the evaluation covers*, not which rounds the course has run, and no supplied statement says the autumn 2025 rounds were the year's only ones.
- **Reverse direction:** if the course has run exactly three rounds a year, both statements hold. The two are therefore not equivalent, and the draft's is the stronger.
- **Smallest supported repair:** "That is a thin basis for expanding a course." (If the sentence should keep both limbs of the proposal: "That is a thin basis for making a course permanent and expanding it.")

## 3. Translation check (separate from source support)

Only one passage in the material is supplied as speech: Milad Aziz's standpoint. The draft does not present it as a quotation; it renders it as the author's own prose, which the material permits ("Detta får citeras eller refereras") and which the byline makes coherent.

- "Att fyrtioåtta personer gick klart en kurs säger att kursen gick att gå klart." → "Forty-eight people finishing a course says the course could be finished." The distinctive move of the original — the plain, almost tautological reading back of the figure — survives, and the English is idiomatic. The impersonal "gick att gå klart" becomes "could be finished", which keeps the claim about the course rather than about the people.
- "Det säger inte att den gav dem något de inte redan hade." → "It does not say it gave them something they did not already have." Meaning, negation and the flatness of the voice all carry. Idiomatic.

No obstruction to an ordinary English reading was identified in either sentence, and no implicit referent needed clarifying: "it" resolves to the course in both clauses, as in the Swedish.

No other meaning was found to change in crossing languages outside quoted speech, apart from F1, which is recorded above as a source-support finding rather than a translation finding.

## 4. Person, number and natural gender conveyed by pronouns

Accounted for separately from referent identification.

- "we" / "our" (C19–C22): first person plural, no natural-gender information. Warranted by the byline — the chair writes for "föreningen Lyssna Först" — and the positions taken in the first person plural are the ones the material assigns to the association ("Lyssna Först föreslår ...", "Föreningen gör inget anspråk ..."). One caveat: C21's "We are not asking for the course to close" renders a position the material assigns to Aziz personally ("Han motsätter sig inte kursen"); as the association's chair stating the association's ask in its own article, no case separates the two here.
- "it" for the evaluation (C7), for the recommendation (C14) and for the committee (C22): third person singular, no natural gender asserted, which matches Swedish neuter/common-gender antecedents that carry no natural gender either.
- "they/them/those people" for participants (C11, C12): third person plural, no natural gender. The material likewise conveys none about them.
- Milad Aziz: the draft uses no pronoun for him anywhere. The material's "Han motsätter sig inte kursen" does assert male natural gender; the draft neither carries that assertion nor contradicts it. No finding.
- No purely grammatical gender in the Swedish ("kursen", "den", "utvärderingen") is rendered as natural gender in the draft.
