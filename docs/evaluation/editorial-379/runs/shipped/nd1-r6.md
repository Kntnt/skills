## 3. Findings

### Finding 1 — the denial about the questionnaire is narrower than the material's

- Draft: "The questionnaire does not ask about participants' mental health."
- Source: "Enkäten frågar inte om deltagarnas hälsa."

What differs: **the scope of what is denied.** The supplied statement denies that the questionnaire asks about the participants' health at all. The draft denies only the mental part of it.

Both directions tested.

- A case in which the draft's sentence holds and the source's fails: a questionnaire that asks about the participants' physical health — say, whether they play sports at the center — but never about their mental health. This case is set aside, because a supplied statement excludes it: "Enkäten frågar inte om deltagarnas hälsa." So the draft's sentence is not false on the material.
- A case in which the source's sentence holds and the draft's fails: none; the broader denial entails the narrower one.

Why it is nonetheless a defect rather than a preference. The draft loses a qualification the source states, and in the losing changes what an ordinary reader takes away. Set as it is, immediately after "The documents contain nothing to support that statement", the sentence is read as naming the limit of the instrument. A reader who is told that the questionnaire does not ask about *mental* health infers that health was on the questionnaire in some other form — that the narrower denial is the strongest one available. On the material, the strongest one available is the broader one, and it is stated outright. The draft's own later list of the three questions ("what they did at the center, how they got there, and whether they wanted it open more evenings") is consistent with the broader denial but does not repair the earlier sentence, since a reader takes the explicit denial, not the list, as the authoritative statement of what was excluded.

The narrowing also weakens the passage against its own purpose: the point being made is that the file carries no health evidence of any kind, and the supplied material supports exactly that.

Smallest supported repair: delete the narrowing word.

> The questionnaire does not ask about participants' health.

This is a verbatim rendering of the source sentence and requires no other change to the paragraph.

### Finding 2 — the lead states that the decision falls on March 3

- Draft: "Nyhamn's culture and leisure committee decides on March 3 whether the open youth center on Hamngatan becomes permanent."
- Source: "Tjänsteutlåtandet inför nämndens möte den 3 mars föreslår att gården permanentas."

What differs: **time and modality.** The material establishes that the committee meets on 3 March and that a staff recommendation proposing permanence is before that meeting. It does not state that the committee decides the matter at that meeting, or that it decides it on that date at all. The boundaries ask the ending to name "nämndens möjliga beslut" — the decision open to the committee — which establishes the committee as the deciding body but not the date on which it decides.

Both directions tested.

- A case in which the source's statement holds and the draft's fails: the committee meets on 3 March with the recommendation before it, sends the item back for further preparation, and settles it at a later meeting. Nothing in the supplied material excludes this; no supplied statement says the item is decided on 3 March, and an item being tabled for a meeting does not, on this material, entail a decision at it.
- A case in which the draft's statement holds and the source's fails: none material to the finding; the two are compatible whenever the committee does decide on the day.

Since a case stands in one direction, this is a finding, notwithstanding how natural the framing is.

Smallest supported repair, keeping the lead's shape and the date:

> Nyhamn's culture and leisure committee takes up on March 3 whether the open youth center on Hamngatan becomes permanent.

The closing sentence ("The committee should make the Hamngatan center permanent on March 3…") needs no change: it is a demand addressed to the committee, not a statement that the decision falls that day.

## 4. Translation check — referred and quoted speech

Checked separately from source support: whether the same meaning and distinctive voice are expressed in idiomatic en_US.

**The recommendation's motive.** "motverkar psykisk ohälsa bland unga i Nyhamn" → "counteracts mental ill health among young people in Nyhamn". The meaning is carried and the register — bureaucratic, unspecific — is preserved, which matters here because the sentence is the object of the criticism. "psykisk ohälsa" denotes poor mental health broadly, not diagnosed illness; "mental ill health" keeps that breadth, where "mental illness" would narrow it to "psykisk sjukdom" and would misstate what the recommendation claims. "Mental ill health" is less common in en_US than in British or institutional English, but it is transparent to an American reader and no concrete obstruction to the intended reading arises from it. Not reported as a finding.

## 5. Editorial questions — reported, not repaired

**1. "Unga Röster does not know how the center affects young people's health, and we do not claim to."** The material says "Föreningen gör inget anspråk på att veta hur gården påverkar ungas hälsa." Declining to claim knowledge and lacking it are not the same: a case in which the association privately believes it knows and refrains from saying so is compatible with the material, which does not exclude it. But the passage is a first-person avowal by an organisation's chair about her own organisation's state of knowledge, signed in the byline, and the second clause states the supplied position verbatim. Whether the first clause is the association's standpoint about itself or a factual assertion needing support is genuinely unsettled, so it is recorded here as the brief's own unevidenced statement rather than as a defect with a repair. If the author wants the two sentences to differ by nothing, "Unga Röster makes no claim to know how the center affects young people's health" states the supplied position and nothing beyond it.

**2. "That is a real basis for a decision."** An evaluative characterisation, and one the material supports as the commissioning party's position rather than as an external assessment — the association proposes that the justification rest on exactly these figures and answers. Recorded here so the basis of its support is explicit, not as a defect.
