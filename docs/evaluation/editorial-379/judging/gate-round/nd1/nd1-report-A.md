# Claim accounting and findings — nd1-narrowed-denial

Draft language: en_US. Source language of the supplied material: Swedish.
Fixture: `docs/evaluation/editorial-379/fixtures/nd1-narrowed-denial.md`

Method: every factual and attributed claim in the draft, including the title, byline,
headings and the closing call, is set beside the supplied passage it rests on, quoted in
Swedish. Where draft and source differ, both directions are tested with a concrete case
compatible with the supplied material; a case is set aside only by quoting a supplied
statement that excludes it. Passages that carry the author's own argument, evaluation or
advocacy are tested on the facts and views they present as given, not on the reasoning.
General editorial and mechanical review is out of scope.

## 2. Findings

### F1 — The lead asserts a decision the material places only as a proposal before the meeting

- Draft: "Nyhamn's culture and leisure committee decides on March 3 whether the open youth
  center on Hamngatan becomes permanent."
- Source: "Tjänsteutlåtandet inför nämndens möte den 3 mars föreslår att gården
  permanentas."
- What is missing or changed: the material establishes a meeting on March 3 and a staff
  recommendation prepared ahead of it proposing permanence. It does not say the committee
  settles the question at that meeting. The draft turns "a proposal is before the committee
  on March 3" into "the committee decides on March 3", which is an assertion about the
  meeting's outcome and its timing.
- Case test, direction one: on March 3 the committee takes the item up, debates it, and
  defers it or refers it back for further preparation, deciding nothing that day; the
  question is settled at a later meeting. In that case the source statement holds — the
  recommendation was still prepared ahead of the March 3 meeting and still proposed
  permanence — and the draft statement fails. Nothing in the supplied material excludes this
  case: the material says only what the recommendation does and when the meeting is, and the
  boundaries speak of "nämndens **möjliga** beslut", which leaves the outcome open rather
  than fixing it to that date.
- Case test, direction two: if the committee does decide on March 3, both statements hold,
  so this direction yields nothing.
- The case stands, so this is a finding. It is not cured by the closing sentence, which is
  advocacy about what the committee should do, nor by C7, which renders "inför nämndens
  möte" correctly.
- Smallest supported repair: replace the verb.
  "Nyhamn's culture and leisure committee takes up on March 3 whether the open youth center
  on Hamngatan becomes permanent."
  This keeps the lead's shape, the date and the stake, and asserts only what the material
  carries.

No other finding. No translation finding.

## 3. Items examined and set aside

### S1 — The narrowed denial about the questionnaire (C9)

- Draft: "The questionnaire does not ask about participants' mental health."
- Source: "Enkäten frågar inte om deltagarnas hälsa."
- The denials differ in scope: health, against mental health.
- Case test, direction one: a case in which the draft statement holds and the source
  statement fails would be a questionnaire that asks nothing about mental health but does ask
  about health in some other respect — say a question about whether the respondent feels
  physically well. That case is excluded by a supplied statement: "Enkäten frågar inte om
  deltagarnas hälsa." It is set aside.
- Case test, direction two: wherever the source statement holds, the draft statement holds
  too, since a questionnaire that asks about no health at all asks about no mental health.
  This direction yields no case.
- No case stands, so this is not a finding: the draft asserts nothing the material does not
  carry, and it does not weaken the state of knowledge in the reader's hands. The scalar
  reading an ordinary reader might otherwise draw — that health was asked about in some other
  form — is foreclosed by the draft itself, which later lists the questionnaire's questions
  in full at C15: what they did at the center, how they got there, and whether they wanted it
  open more evenings.
- Editorial note, not a defect: the material supports the wider denial, and the wider denial
  is the stronger sentence for this argument, since the case being made is that the file
  contains nothing about health at all. "The questionnaire does not ask about participants'
  health" would be supported word for word by "Enkäten frågar inte om deltagarnas hälsa."
  Whether to take that scope is the author's choice; the draft as written is defensible.

### S2 — Attribution of the visit figure (C13)

- The boundaries require "Siffror ska tillskrivas uppföljningen där de bär argumentet", and
  3,240 visits does bear the argument at C16.
- The figure sits in the paragraph that opens "The municipality's follow-up of January 9,
  2026, covers a trial..." and continues "It rests on a count at the entrance...", so the
  follow-up is established as what counted before the number appears, and the next sentence
  returns to what the count does and does not measure. The attribution is carried by the
  paragraph in the ordinary journalistic way.
- Not a finding. The sentence "The school year recorded 3,240 visits" makes the school year
  the recorder, where the source has an agentless passive; if the author wants the
  attribution inside the sentence that bears it, "The follow-up recorded 3,240 visits over
  the school year" would do it. That is a preference, not a defect.

### S3 — "does not know" against "gör inget anspråk på att veta" (C18)

- Taken as a claim about the world, the two differ: an association can refrain from claiming
  to know something it in fact knows, and nothing in the material excludes that case.
- But the sentence is the sender speaking in the first person about her own association —
  "Unga Röster does not know ..., and we do not claim to" — under a byline that establishes
  her as its chair. The association's own state of knowledge is precisely what the sender
  stands behind and is the authority on, and the brief carries the association's position as
  theirs: "Föreningen gör inget anspråk på att veta hur gården påverkar ungas hälsa." It is
  not a figure, an event, a technical effect or a claim about an actual population, so it is
  not a factual claim that needs external support.
- Not a finding. The second clause restates the source's modality exactly, and the sentence
  as a whole does what the brief asks: it keeps Unga Röster out of any claim about health
  effects.

### S4 — "Keep" and "the open youth center" (C1, C3)

- "Keep the Hamngatan youth center" presupposes that the center now operates. "permanenta"
  and "permanentas" carry the same presupposition in the source — a trial operation is what
  is made permanent — so the presupposition comes from the material, not from the draft.
- "den öppna fritidsgården" → "the open youth center" keeps the source's own modifier. An
  English reader may take "open" as "currently open" rather than as the drop-in character of
  the facility, but both readings are compatible with the supplied material and neither
  asserts anything the material contradicts.
- Not a finding.

### S5 — "the file" for both "underlaget" and "handlingarna" (C6, C20, C21)

- The draft renders "handlingarna" as "the documents" at C8 and as "the file" in the heading
  at C6, and "underlaget" as "the file" at C20.
- In this material the two expressions point at the same body of paper — the case documents
  before the committee and the supporting material in them — and no statement in the fixture
  distinguishes them in a way the draft's wording collapses.
- Not a finding.

## 4. Translation check (quoted and licensed speech)

### T1 — The recommendation's motive (C7)

- Source: "motverkar psykisk ohälsa bland unga i Nyhamn"
- Draft: "counteracts mental ill health among young people in Nyhamn"
- Same meaning: "motverkar" → "counteracts" (works against, without claiming success),
  "psykisk ohälsa" → "mental ill health", "bland unga" → "among young people", and the
  place is kept. The rendering does not upgrade the document's claim into, say, "improves
  mental health" or "reduces mental illness", which matters, since the draft's whole case is
  about what that sentence claims.
- Voice: "mental ill health" is the flat administrative register of the Swedish original and
  reads as officialese in English too, which is the point being made about it. It is less
  everyday in en_US than "poor mental health" would be, but it is not an obstruction to
  reading, and a more colloquial rendering would soften a phrase the draft is holding up for
  inspection.
- No translation finding.

### T2 — Elin Sandberg's standpoint (C20)

- Source: "Gården behövs. Men en motivering som inte går att belägga håller bara tills någon
  läser underlaget."
- Draft: "The center is needed. But a justification that cannot be supported holds only
  until somebody reads the file."
- Same meaning: "behövs" → "is needed"; "går att belägga" → "can be supported" in the sense
  of being backed by evidence, which is what the passage is about; "håller bara tills" →
  "holds only until"; "någon" → "somebody", which keeps the indefinite person.
- Voice: the original is short, dry and pointed, with the sting in the final clause. The
  English keeps the two-sentence shape, the concession first, and the same late placement of
  the sting. It reads as idiomatic English speech, not as translated Swedish.
- No translation finding.

## 5. Person, number and natural gender

Pronouns are accounted for separately from their referents; identifying a referent does not
establish these features.

- "It should." / "It rests on" / "It says the center is used" — third person singular,
  no natural gender asserted, referring in turn to the center becoming permanent, the
  follow-up and the follow-up's content. All three referents are third person singular in
  the material ("gården", "uppföljningen"). Supported.
- "what they did at the center, how they got there" — third person plural, no natural
  gender, referring to the young people who answered. The source is likewise third person
  plural with no gender: "vad de gjorde på gården, hur de tog sig dit". Supported.
- "whether they wanted it open more evenings" — as above; "it" for the center matches
  "gården". Supported.
- "what the young people using it want next" — third person plural, no gender. Supported.
- "we do not claim to" / "We are asking" — first person plural for Unga Röster, spoken by
  its chair under a byline that says so. The material gives the association's position in
  the third person ("Föreningen gör inget anspråk..."; "Unga Röster föreslår..."); a signed
  op-ed by the chair carries that position in the first person plural without changing whose
  position it is. Supported.
- "somebody" (C20, twice at C10 and C21 as "somebody"/"the next reader") — indefinite, no
  number or gender asserted, matching "någon". Supported.
- Elin Sandberg is never referred to by pronoun anywhere in the draft, so no natural-gender
  assertion about her is made, and none would be supported: the material gives her name and
  office only.
- No grammatical gender in the Swedish source is turned into a natural-gender assertion in
  the English draft. "gården" and "uppföljningen" are common-gender nouns rendered with
  "it".
