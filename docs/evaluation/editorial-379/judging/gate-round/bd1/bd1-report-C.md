## 2. Findings

### Finding 1 — the evaluation's non-measurement is broadened from prior knowledge to knowledge

- **Draft:** "The evaluation does not measure participants' knowledge."
- **Source:** "Den mäter inte deltagarnas förkunskaper."
- **What differs:** the thing measured, and with it the scope of the denial. "Förkunskaper" is prior knowledge — what a participant brought to the course. The draft drops the "för-" and denies measurement of participants' knowledge at large, at any point in the course.
- **Case where the source statement holds and the draft's fails:** the course leaders' written summaries, on which the evaluation rests, record an end-of-course assessment of what each participant can do, while nothing at intake records what they could do beforehand. Then the evaluation does measure participants' knowledge, and does not measure their prior knowledge. Nothing in the material excludes this: it states only that the evaluation builds on "närvarolistor och kursledarnas skriftliga sammanfattningar" and that it does not measure "förkunskaper" — it does not say what the summaries contain, and no supplied statement rules out an exit assessment in them.
- **Why it matters here:** the broadened denial is load-bearing for the argument. The next paragraph concludes that the figure "does not tell the committee what those people could do afterward that they could not do before"; with the wider denial in place, that conclusion appears to follow from the evaluation measuring no knowledge at all, when the material supports only the narrower and sufficient point that there is no baseline to measure against. The boundary that no effect studies are established is not breached by the draft, but the draft overstates what the evaluation omits.
- **Smallest supported repair:** "The evaluation does not measure participants' prior knowledge." The argument is unaffected: with no prior knowledge recorded, a completion figure still cannot show a gain.

### Finding 2 — what completion shows is shifted from the course to the people who started it

- **Draft:** "It tells the committee that people who started the course could finish it."
- **Source:** "Att fyrtioåtta personer gick klart en kurs säger att kursen gick att gå klart."
- **What differs:** the subject of the capability. The source's statement is about the course — that finishing it was possible. The draft's is about the starters — that those who began were able to finish. The draft's own later sentence keeps the source's subject correctly ("Forty-eight people finishing a course says the course could be finished"), so the two formulations sit side by side in one piece.
- **Case where the source statement holds and the draft's fails:** of the sixty-two registrations, fourteen did not complete, and some of those participants could not complete — for instance because they lacked the prior knowledge the four-week course assumed, which the evaluation does not measure and so cannot exclude. The course was still completable, as forty-eight completions show. Then the source's statement holds and the draft's does not. Nothing supplied excludes this: the material reports only the two counts and that the evaluation does not measure prior knowledge; it gives no reason any registration lapsed.
- **Smallest supported repair:** "It tells the committee that the course could be finished." This is the sender's own formulation and costs the paragraph nothing.

### Finding 3 — "doubling" quantifies an expansion the material does not size

- **Draft:** "That is a thin basis for doubling a course."
- **Source:** "byggs ut till sex omgångar per år"; the evaluation "gäller tre kursomgångar vid vuxenutbildningen i Rönnvik under hösten 2025".
- **What differs:** the magnitude of the proposed change. The material states the proposed level (six rounds a year) and that this is an expansion ("byggs ut"), but never states the present number of rounds per year. The only round count supplied — three — is for one autumn, one term, not a year.
- **Case where the material holds and the draft's claim fails:** the course ran three rounds in autumn 2025 and three in spring 2026, six in the year, and the staff report's proposal fixes six rounds a year as a permanent level, an expansion in commitment and duration but not in the number of rounds. Then there is no doubling. Nothing supplied excludes this: the evaluation's scope is limited to three autumn 2025 rounds, and no statement gives the annual number of rounds before or after, or any factor of increase.
- **Smallest supported repair:** "That is a thin basis for expanding a course to six rounds a year." The criticism of the evidence is unchanged, and the figure remains the staff report's own.

## 3. Translation findings

None. The draft presents no passage as a quotation, so no translated quotation requires separate checking. Aziz's supplied statement appears as the author's own prose (row 18) and carries the source's meaning, its two-clause structure and its plain, unadorned register into idiomatic American English; "en kurs" is correctly kept indefinite, and the concessive-then-limiting move of the original survives. Where a Swedish term crosses into English elsewhere — "utbildningsnämnden" → "education committee", "vuxenutbildningen" → "adult education service", "tjänsteutlåtandet" → "the recommendation prepared for the April 14 meeting", "höstens budgetberedning" → "the autumn budget preparation", "arbetssökande" → "job seekers" — nothing in this context could fall under one term and not the other. The course name "Digitalt grundstöd" is left untranslated throughout, as a proper name of the municipal course. The one meaning changed in crossing languages, "förkunskaper" → "knowledge", is a source-support finding (Finding 1), not a translation finding, because it changes what is claimed rather than how it reads.
