# Test rehearsal use before committing Bellwick Hall

*By Amina Reed, chair of Room to Rehearse*

Before Bellwick Hall’s trustees commit its empty former storage wing to a three-year commercial letting, they should investigate evening rehearsal use. Our association’s case is for a feasibility study before that choice is made.

The trustees’ paper of 3 July 2026 says predictable rental income would help meet maintenance costs. That concern deserves serious consideration: any proposal for the wing must face the question of how it would support itself. Commercial letting may prove the better option, although the paper names neither a prospective tenant nor a rental figure.

Our survey of 14 local music groups found that nine would consider using a rehearsal room on weekday evenings. Those responses are expressions of interest, not booking commitments. We did not ask what groups would pay or how often they would attend. The survey is a reason to investigate a rehearsal room, not a promise that it will pay its way.

We must be equally clear about our own gaps. Our treasurer’s signed note confirms that we have not prepared a conversion cost estimate. Our submission makes no claim that we have secured the building owner’s consent for rehearsal use; it does not establish the consent’s actual status.

A feasibility study should examine sound insulation, access, likely running costs and potential income. Publishing a comparison with commercial letting would give residents and trustees a basis for judging the alternatives.

I am asking for an eight-week deferral, though we have no evidence yet that the study can be completed within that period. The trustees should defer the letting decision for eight weeks, commission the study and publish a comparison of the two uses before choosing.
