# Fenwick’s shared schedule shows jobs waiting for parts

Fenwick Instrument Repairs’ six-week trial of a shared bench schedule let staff see which instruments were waiting for parts. The workshop approved continued use for new jobs, while postponing the backlog until it had reviewed its status definitions.

The brass and woodwind repair workshop began the trial in January 2026. Previously, jobs awaiting parts and those ready for work appeared on separate paper sheets. The team chose scheduling software supplier Benchline after checking that it could distinguish those statuses.

The workshop defined the statuses and decided who would update them. Benchline configured those choices and trained four staff in one online session. Telephone calls remained part of the process for discussing collection arrangements with customers.

In an email interview with Benchline’s case writer on 2 March, workshop manager Priya Vale explained what the team had needed to settle:

“We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench.”

## What the trial established

The workshop’s trial summary, dated 23 February 2026, recorded 38 jobs entered in the schedule and reported that staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts. It did not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction.

The findings document the team’s reported experience; they do not establish faster repairs or a recommendation for every workshop.

Vale approved continued use for new jobs but postponed adding the backlog pending the review of status definitions. Asked whether she would make the same choice again, and what she would change, she wrote:

“I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog.”
