# Fenwick sees which repairs are waiting for parts

Fenwick Instrument Repairs recorded visibility of instruments awaiting parts during a six-week trial of a shared bench schedule. Its manager approved continued use for new jobs, while postponing the backlog until the team had reviewed what its status labels meant.

The brass and woodwind repair workshop began the trial in January 2026. Previously, jobs awaiting parts and those ready for work appeared on separate paper sheets. The team chose scheduling supplier Benchline after checking that it could distinguish these statuses.

The workshop defined the statuses and decided who would update them. Benchline configured those choices and trained four staff in one online session. Telephone calls remained part of the work: the team continued to discuss collection arrangements with customers by phone.

In an email interview with Benchline’s case writer on 2 March, workshop manager Priya Vale described what the team had needed to settle.

‘We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench.’

The workshop’s trial summary, dated 23 February 2026, records 38 jobs entered in the schedule and says staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts. It does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction. The findings establish the team’s reported experience, without establishing an improvement in repair speed.

Asked whether she would make the same choice again and what she would change, Vale offered a qualified appraisal:

‘I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog.’
