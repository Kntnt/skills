# Investigate rehearsals before letting Bellwick Hall’s wing

*By Amina Reed, chair of Room to Rehearse*

Before Bellwick Hall’s trustees commit its former storage wing to a three-year commercial letting, they should investigate whether it could accommodate evening rehearsals. The wing is empty, while the hall’s main rooms continue their usual activities. There is an alternative worth examining.

The trustees’ paper of 3 July 2026 makes a serious case for predictable income to help meet maintenance costs. Those costs matter. Commercial letting may prove the better option, and I accept that. But the paper names neither a prospective tenant nor a rental figure.

Our association surveyed 14 local music groups. Nine said they would consider using a rehearsal room on weekday evenings. That is a reason to investigate. It establishes interest, with no booking commitments, and we did not ask what groups would pay or how often they would attend.

We must be equally clear about our own gaps. As our treasurer’s signed note confirms, we have not prepared a conversion cost estimate. Our submission does not claim that we have secured the building owner’s consent for rehearsal use.

A feasibility study should examine sound insulation, access, likely running costs and potential income. It would give the trustees a basis for comparing rehearsal use with commercial letting. Asking for that work means taking the financial question seriously.

Eight weeks is my requested timetable for a deferral, not an established completion date for the study. The trustees should defer the three-year letting decision for eight weeks, commission the feasibility study and publish a comparison of the two uses before choosing between them.
