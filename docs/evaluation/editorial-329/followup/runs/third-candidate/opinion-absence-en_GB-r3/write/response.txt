# Investigate rehearsal use before letting the wing

*By Amina Reed, chair of Room to Rehearse*

Before Bellwick Hall's trustees commit its former storage wing to a three-year commercial letting, they should investigate evening rehearsal use. An empty wing and an expression of interest deserve a closer look before that decision is made.

The trustees' paper of 3 July 2026 makes a serious case for predictable income to help meet maintenance costs. I accept that commercial letting may prove the better option. But the paper names neither a prospective tenant nor a rental figure: residents cannot read it as a settled financial comparison.

Our association surveyed 14 local music groups. Nine said they would consider using a rehearsal room on weekday evenings. We did not ask what they would pay or how often they would attend. The survey is a reason to investigate a rehearsal room, not a promise that it will pay its way.

Our own proposal has gaps. As our treasurer's signed note confirms, we have not prepared a cost estimate for converting the wing. Our submission makes no claim to have secured the building owner's consent for rehearsal use.

A feasibility study should examine sound insulation, access, likely running costs and potential income. Those are questions the trustees need answers to before choosing between uses. The hall's main rooms continue their usual activities; the decision concerns the currently empty wing.

I am asking for an eight-week deferral, a requested timetable rather than an assurance that the study can be completed within it. The trustees should defer the three-year letting decision for those eight weeks, commission the study and publish a comparison of the two uses before choosing.
