# Investigate rehearsal use before letting Bellwick Hall’s wing

*By Amina Reed, chair of Room to Rehearse*

Before Bellwick Hall’s trustees commit its former storage wing to a three-year commercial letting, they should investigate whether it could serve local musicians. The wing is empty, while the main rooms continue their usual activities. We have a reason to examine another use.

The trustees’ paper of 3 July 2026 makes a serious case: predictable income would help meet maintenance costs. Those costs matter. Commercial letting may prove the better option, and I accept that. But the paper names neither a prospective tenant nor a rental figure. A comparison needs more than a preference on either side.

Our association surveyed 14 local music groups. Nine said they would consider a rehearsal room on weekday evenings. That is interest, not committed bookings. We did not ask what they would pay or how often they would attend. The survey is a reason to investigate a rehearsal room, not a promise that it will pay its way.

We must be equally clear about the gaps in our proposal. As our treasurer’s signed note confirms, we have not prepared a conversion cost estimate. Our submission makes no claim that we have secured the building owner’s consent for rehearsal use; it does not establish the consent’s actual status.

A feasibility study should examine sound insulation, access, likely running costs and potential income. It would give the trustees a basis for comparing the options.

I am asking for an eight-week deferral; that is my proposed timetable, not an established completion time for the study. The trustees should defer the three-year letting decision for eight weeks, commission the study and publish a comparison of the two uses before choosing.
