# Lervik needs evidence before ending phone bookings

*Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut*

Lervik’s municipal executive board should postpone a permanent switch to online-only booking for community venues. Our association, Öppna beslut, proposes a six-month trial with both telephone and web booking at all seven venues. Before removing a booking route, the board needs evidence that allows it to weigh staff costs against its value to users.

## The pilot counts bookings, not barriers

The municipality’s pilot report, *Bokning av föreningslokaler*, dated 8 April 2026, records 96 web bookings and 24 telephone bookings during an eight-week trial at two venues. Those figures count bookings, not individual people. The report measures neither age nor disability nor digital skills.

We cannot use the telephone total to estimate how many Lervik residents are unable to book online. A telephone booking does not establish that someone cannot use the web. It gives us a reason to ask why they chose the phone. That distinction matters: an argument for retaining a service should be honest about what its evidence shows.

## Measure the work before making the change

The officers’ report prepared for the board’s 18 June meeting proposes ending telephone bookings at all seven venues from September. Its reason is that staff would no longer have to enter information through two separate workflows.

That is a practical objection which deserves a practical answer. Yet the papers contain neither measurements of staff time nor a calculation of financial savings. The board is being asked to remove a booking route without the figures needed to weigh the administrative burden against the value users place on it. That is an inadequate basis for a permanent change.

I do not oppose digital booking. The question is whether the case for withdrawing telephone booking has been made. The pilot leaves that question unanswered.

## Give the board a basis for its decision

During the six-month trial we propose, staff would record the time spent on each booking route. Users would be invited to explain voluntarily why they chose the telephone or the web.

We make no claim to have costed or funded this trial. The municipal executive board must assess its cost. It should postpone the permanent switch, decide on the cost of running the trial and keep both routes available for those six months. With that evidence, it can then decide whether a booking route should be removed, changed or retained.
