# Lervik should test both booking options before cutting one

By Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut

Lervik’s municipal executive board should postpone a permanent switch to online-only booking for community facilities. Our association, Öppna beslut, proposes six months with both phone and web booking at all seven facilities, so the board can weigh staff time against the value of each option to users.

## The pilot counts bookings, not people

The municipality’s April 8, 2026, pilot report records 96 web bookings and 24 phone bookings during an eight-week trial at two facilities. Those figures describe transactions, not unique individuals. The report does not measure age, functional ability, or digital experience.

We cannot use those phone bookings to establish what share of Lervik’s residents cannot book online. Choosing the phone is no proof that someone cannot use the web. It is a reason to investigate why people still use the phone.

That distinction matters to the decision. A booking count alone cannot tell the board what users would lose if the phone option disappeared. I do not oppose online booking. I want the municipality to understand the choice it proposes to remove.

## Double administration deserves a measured answer

The staff recommendation prepared for the board’s June 18 meeting proposes ending phone booking at all seven facilities starting in September. Its stated reason is to spare staff from entering information through two workflows.

That is the administration’s objection, and it deserves a direct answer. Keeping both channels means retaining the arrangement it wants to end. But the documents contain neither measurements of staff time nor a calculation of financial savings. The board is being asked to remove a booking option without those measures of the benefit to the administration.

## Use six months to establish the tradeoff

Öppna beslut proposes a trial across all seven facilities. Staff would record the time spent on each booking channel, and users would be invited to explain voluntarily why they chose phone or web. This would give the board evidence for weighing the workload against the value to users.

We do not claim to have financed the trial or calculated its cost. The board must assess that cost as part of deciding whether to proceed.

The board should postpone the permanent switch and approve a six-month trial with both channels, taking responsibility for assessing its cost. Afterward, it can decide whether a channel should be removed, changed, or retained. That decision should rest on a measured tradeoff.
