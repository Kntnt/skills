# Fenwick tracks jobs awaiting parts in a shared schedule

Fenwick Instrument Repairs reported that staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts during a six-week trial of Benchline’s shared bench schedule. Manager Priya Vale approved continued use for new jobs, but held back the backlog pending a review of the team’s status definitions.

The brass and woodwind repair workshop began the trial in January 2026. Previously, jobs awaiting parts and jobs ready for work appeared on separate paper sheets. The team chose Benchline after checking that the software could distinguish those statuses.

The workshop defined the statuses and decided who would update them. Benchline configured those choices and trained four staff in one online session. Telephone calls remained part of the work: the team continued to discuss collection arrangements with customers by phone.

In an email interview on 2 March 2026, Vale described what the team had needed to settle before the schedule was useful.

‘We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench.’

The workshop’s trial summary, dated 23 February 2026, recorded 38 jobs entered in the schedule and reported that staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts. It did not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction. The trial therefore documented the team’s reported experience, without establishing whether repairs were faster.

Asked whether the same choice would be made again and what would change, Vale gave a qualified endorsement:

‘I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog.’
