# Investigate rehearsal use before letting Bellwick Hall’s wing

*By Amina Reed, chair of Room to Rehearse*

Bellwick Hall’s trustees should investigate evening rehearsal use before committing its former storage wing to a three-year commercial letting. As chair of Room to Rehearse, I am asking for a feasibility study so that they can compare the options before choosing.

The trustees’ paper of 3 July 2026 makes a serious financial argument: predictable income would help meet the hall’s maintenance costs. That concern deserves weight. Commercial letting may prove the better option, although the paper names neither a prospective tenant nor a rental figure.

There is also a reason to examine rehearsal use. Of the 14 local music groups our association surveyed, nine said they would consider using a room on weekday evenings. The survey is a reason to investigate a rehearsal room, not a promise that it will pay its way. These are expressions of interest, not bookings; we did not ask what groups would pay or how often they would attend.

Our treasurer’s signed note confirms that we have not prepared a conversion cost estimate. I accept that gap. Our submission also makes no claim that we have secured the building owner’s consent for rehearsal use. A feasibility study should cover sound insulation, access, likely running costs and potential income.

The wing is empty, while the main rooms continue their usual activities. Before making a three-year commitment, the trustees should establish whether rehearsal use offers a workable alternative.

I ask them to defer the letting decision for eight weeks, commission the study and publish a comparison of the two uses. Eight weeks is my requested timetable, not an established completion time. That is the decision I want the trustees to take.
