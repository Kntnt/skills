# Fenwick sees jobs awaiting parts in shared schedule

Fenwick Instrument Repairs recorded 38 jobs during a six-week trial of a shared bench schedule. Its trial summary says staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts. The manager approved continued use for new jobs, while postponing the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions.

The brass and woodwind repair workshop began the trial in January 2026. Previously, jobs awaiting parts and jobs ready for work appeared on separate paper sheets. The team chose Benchline after checking that its scheduling software could distinguish these statuses.

Fenwick defined the statuses and decided who would update them. Benchline configured those choices and trained four staff in one online session. Telephone calls remained part of the process for discussing collection arrangements with customers.

In an email interview with Benchline’s case writer on 2 March, workshop manager Priya Vale explained the decisions behind the schedule:

‘We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench.’

The workshop’s trial summary, dated 23 February 2026, records the team’s experience of seeing which instruments were waiting for parts. It does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction, so it provides no measured result on those outcomes.

Asked whether the choice would be the same again and what would change, Vale gave a qualified appraisal:

‘I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog.’
