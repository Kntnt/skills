# Source and translated-quotation comparison

Scope: the complete supplied draft, brief and Swedish reporting material; the Claims contract and quotation guidance supplied for this comparison. No external facts, editorial review or mechanical review were used. Sources and draft have not been changed.

## Method and overall result

Each independently checkable proposition below includes the modifiers and qualifications governing it in the draft. Repeated claims are accounted for where they recur. For each factual proposition I tested whether the supplied material could remain true while the proposition was false. No source-compatible counterexample exposing an unsupported factual addition was found. Instructions and editorial interpretations are identified separately and assessed for their grounding rather than treated as reports of additional events.

## Translated-quotation assessment

Original: “Vi vet när vi behöver titta närmare. Vi vet ännu inte varför det blev kallt just då.”

Draft: “We know when we need to take a closer look. We don’t yet know why it got cold at those particular times.”

- Meaning: “when”, the need for closer examination, the as-yet unknown cause, and the specificity of the relevant times are retained. The surrounding reporting supplies repeated measurements and lesson periods, so “at those particular times” expresses the contextual temporal referent of “just då” without adding a distinct event or claim about all recorded lessons.
- Stance and certainty: the first sentence remains certain about when to investigate; the second remains explicitly uncertain about why, with “yet” retained.
- Voice: the repeated first-person “We” and short, plain two-sentence contrast remain. “Take a closer look” is an idiomatic English counterpart to “titta närmare”; “got cold” preserves the speaker's ordinary register. The contraction reads naturally as speech.
- Attribution and authority: the material labels this an exact interview statement and expressly permits idiomatic translation. Attribution to Rask is correct.
- Concrete obstruction to faithful, idiomatic reading: none found. No translation repair proposed.
