# Lervik should test both booking routes before cutting one

*By Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut*

Lervik’s municipal executive board should postpone a permanent switch to online-only booking for community venues. Our association, Öppna beslut, proposes six months with both telephone and online booking at all seven venues, so the board can weigh staff time against the value of each route to users.

## The pilot counts bookings, not barriers

The council’s pilot report, *Bokning av föreningslokaler*, dated 8 April 2026, records 96 online bookings and 24 telephone bookings during an eight-week trial at two venues. Those are bookings, not individual people. The report does not measure age, functional ability or digital familiarity.

The telephone total therefore cannot tell us what proportion of Lervik’s residents cannot book online. A telephone booking is no proof that someone cannot use the website. It is a reason to find out why they still use the telephone. We should neither inflate these figures into evidence of exclusion nor treat them as an explanation of users’ choices.

## Double administration deserves a measured answer

The officers’ report prepared for the executive board’s meeting on 18 June proposes removing telephone booking at all seven venues from September. Its reason is that staff would no longer have to enter information through two separate workflows.

That is the objection our proposal must address. Maintaining both routes means retaining the double administration the officers want to remove. Yet the papers contain neither measurements of staff time nor calculations of financial savings. They identify an administrative problem without establishing how much work or money the proposed change would save.

I do not oppose online booking. I oppose making this permanent change before the board can weigh the workload against what users gain from having both options.

## Use six months to establish the trade-off

During our proposed trial, officers would record the time spent on each booking route and invite users to explain voluntarily why they choose telephone or online booking. Extending the trial to all seven venues would also broaden it beyond the two covered by the pilot.

Öppna beslut makes no claim to have funded or costed this trial. The executive board must consider its cost; we cannot promise a saving.

The board should postpone the permanent switch, address the cost of a six-month trial and authorise it with both routes available. With that evidence, it can then decide whether a booking route should be removed, changed or retained.
