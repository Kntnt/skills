# Fenwick’s shared schedule shows jobs waiting for parts

Fenwick Instrument Repairs’ six-week trial of a shared bench schedule recorded 38 jobs and, according to its trial summary, let staff see which instruments were waiting for parts. The manager approved continued use for new jobs, while postponing the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions.

The brass and woodwind repair workshop began the trial in January 2026. Previously, jobs awaiting parts and those ready for work appeared on separate paper sheets. The team chose scheduling software supplier Benchline after checking that it could distinguish these statuses.

Fenwick defined the statuses and decided who would update them. Benchline configured those choices and trained four staff in one online session. Telephone calls remained part of discussing collection arrangements with customers.

In an email interview with Benchline’s case writer on 2 March, workshop manager Priya Vale described what the team had needed to settle:

‘We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench.’

## What the trial established

The workshop’s summary, dated 23 February 2026, records the job count and reports visibility of instruments awaiting parts. It does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction. The account establishes the team’s reported experience; it does not establish faster repairs or a recommendation for every workshop.

Asked about making the same choice again and what to change, Vale gave a qualified appraisal:

‘I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog.’
