# Investigate rehearsal use before letting Bellwick Hall’s wing

*By Amina Reed, chair of Room to Rehearse*

Before Bellwick Hall’s trustees commit its former storage wing to a three-year commercial letting, they should investigate whether it could serve as an evening rehearsal room. The wing is empty, while the hall’s main rooms continue their usual activities. We should examine the alternative before choosing.

The financial argument deserves serious attention. The trustees’ paper of 3 July 2026 says predictable rental income would help meet maintenance costs. That matters. But the paper names neither a prospective tenant nor a rental figure, so it cannot yet tell residents what that income would be.

Our evidence also has limits. Of the 14 local music groups Room to Rehearse surveyed, nine said they would consider using a room on weekday evenings. Those are expressions of interest, not bookings. We did not ask what groups would pay or how often they would attend. The survey is a reason to investigate a rehearsal room, not a promise that it will pay its way.

Our treasurer’s signed note confirms that we have not prepared a conversion cost estimate. I accept that gap. Our submission also makes no claim to have secured the building owner’s consent for rehearsal use; it does not establish whether consent exists.

A feasibility study should examine sound insulation, access, likely running costs and potential income. Commercial letting may prove the better option. I want that choice to follow a published comparison.

I propose an eight-week deferral, although the evidence does not establish that the study can be completed within that time. Trustees should defer the letting decision, commission the study and publish the comparison before choosing between the two uses.
