# Source comparison

Read the complete source/brief and complete draft. Target language: en_GB. No quotation translation is involved. This report tests source support, including implications, rather than style or mechanics.

## Source key

- S1 (brief): “Write a British English opinion article for residents of Bellwick, in the voice of Amina Reed, chair of the local music association Room to Rehearse. She wants the trustees of Bellwick Hall to investigate evening rehearsal use before committing the former storage wing to long-term commercial letting.”
- S2: “The trustees' paper of 3 July 2026 proposes letting the former storage wing for three years. It says predictable income would help meet the hall's maintenance costs. No prospective tenant or rental figure is named.”
- S3: “The wing is currently empty; the hall's main rooms continue to host their usual activities.”
- S4: “Room to Rehearse surveyed 14 local music groups. Nine said they would consider using a rehearsal room on weekday evenings. This is an expression of interest, not a booking commitment. The survey did not ask what they would pay or how often they would attend.”
- S5: “The association proposes a feasibility study covering sound insulation, access, likely running costs and potential income.”
- S6: “Its treasurer's signed note explicitly confirms that the association has not prepared a cost estimate for converting the wing. Reed accepts this gap and wants the trustees to commission the study before choosing between uses.”
- S7: “The association's submission makes no representation that it has secured the building owner's consent for rehearsal use. The supplied material does not state the consent's actual status.”
- S8: “Reed's position: ‘The survey is a reason to investigate a rehearsal room, not a promise that it will pay its way.’ She accepts that commercial letting may prove the better option.”
- S9: “Her proposal is that the trustees defer the three-year letting decision for eight weeks, obtain the feasibility study and publish a comparison of the two uses. There is no evidence that a study can be completed within eight weeks; this is her requested timetable.”

In the accounting below, an advocacy proposition means that the author advances the stated recommendation or assessment, not that its desired outcome has happened or is guaranteed. For support entries giving a source key, the quotation above is the supporting passage and, unless expressly qualified, rules out the precise negation within the supplied fictional account.

## Findings

### F1 — source support: current activity is liable to be read as guaranteed future continuity

Draft: “That would give the trustees a basis for comparing rehearsal use with commercial letting, while the hall’s main rooms continue their usual activities.”

The source says the main rooms currently continue their usual activities. Linking that clause with “while” to a future conditional study benefit adds a natural reading that the activities continue during the proposed study/comparison period. Future continuity is not established. A temporary later maintenance closure is a concrete source-compatible counterexample. The issue is temporal scope, not the truth of the source's current-use statement.

Smallest supported repair: end the comparison sentence after “commercial letting” and make the status a separate sentence: “The hall’s main rooms currently continue their usual activities.” This preserves both the supported purpose of the study and the supported current activity, without the prospective attachment. An equally supported placement would attach the present activity statement to the earlier present empty-wing statement, but moving it is unnecessary.

This is a scope ambiguity finding, not a conclusion that the author definitively promised uninterrupted activities. The current-only reading is supported; the natural prospective reading is not.
