## Source-support finding

### F1 — Lead weakens the hypothetical qualification in the preparation preference

Draft: “Lind skulle välja att göra försöket igen, men **vill avsätta mer tid för förberedelser**.”

Source: “I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but **I would leave an extra week for preparation**.”

The first lead clause preserves “would choose”; the second changes the corresponding “would leave” into present “vill”. The exact separately checkable proposition is that Lind presently wants to allocate more preparation time, whereas the source gives her choice of preparation allowance in a hypothetical repeat trial. The source does not rule out its precise negation. A concrete source-compatible counterexample is that Lind has no present wish or intention to allocate any preparation time because there is no expansion decision, while she would allow an extra week if another trial were chosen. That fully fits S17/S19.

This is a small modal precision issue, not an invented endorsement or an assertion that expansion has already occurred. The rest of the lead supplies substantial conditional context, and the full quotation is accurate. The smallest repair is nevertheless available without changing voice or framing:

“Lind skulle välja att göra försöket igen, men **skulle avsätta mer tid för förberedelser**.”

The source supports that exact hypothetical preference. “Mer tid” is a supported generalization of an extra week; the exact extra week remains in the full quotation.

No other source-support findings. The draft keeps customer agency; attributes the subjective benefit; retains the workload limitation and the note's non-attribution; distinguishes assignment from completion; does not invent savings, satisfaction, recommendation to every housing company, competitor comparisons, physical scenes or emotions; and identifies the actual supplied reading destination.
