**F1 (also table row 17): rejected.** The report does not establish that the draft communicates the broader absence-of-evidence claim it alleges.

The actual contextual proposition is that Nora is examining a meeting template and offering a personal reflection, rather than presenting evidence of colleagues’ shortcomings. The complete sentence is:

> “Jag sitter med en mall, inte med bevis för kollegernas tillkortakommanden.”

Grammatically, “inte med bevis” shares “Jag sitter” with “med en mall”: the contrast concerns the material she is working with in this reflection. Its immediate context explicitly establishes that scope:

> “Det är min reflektion, ingen mätning av hur andra använder sina arbetsdagar.”

The opening identifies her activity—“Jag granskar bibliotekets mötesmall”—and the next paragraph continues:

> “Dessutom uppskattar jag möten där kolleger upptäcker att de har förstått en fråga olika.”

Taken together, these passages distinguish her document-based reflection from an evidential assessment of colleagues. They do not ordinarily communicate an inventory of what evidence she possesses, even of evidence potentially relevant to this topic.

The source supports that contextual proposition directly through the combination of:

> “Jag granskar bibliotekets mötesmall.”

> “Det är en iakttagelse av dokumentet, inte en scen från ett visst möte.”

> “Det här är min reflektion, inte något jag har mätt hos andra.”

This support does **not** depend on inferring that absence of measurement entails absence of every other kind of evidence. That inference would be invalid; it is also not what the draft needs to assert.

The report instead reconstructs the sentence as:

> “Nora har i detta sammanhang inga bevis för kollegernas tillkortakommanden.”

Its negation—“åtminstone något bevis”—makes the shift explicit: the report tests whether Nora **has any evidence**, whereas the draft contrasts the template she is examining with evidence of colleagues’ failings. Adding “i detta sammanhang” does not remove that mismatch.

The counterexample likewise fails to contradict the actual claim. A colleague could have reported “ett dokumenterat misslyckande” while Nora’s present examination still concerns the template and her calendar argument remains a personal reflection. The counterexample does not establish that she is examining or using that documented failure here. It therefore defeats the report’s stronger reconstruction, not the draft’s contextual proposition.

A broader possession reading is conceivable in isolation, but the immediate syntax and surrounding text do not establish it as a genuinely competing material claim. F1 consequently does not warrant an unresolved verdict.
