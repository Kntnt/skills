# Elm Quay brings repair reports into one shared view

Elm Quay Housing’s maintenance team tested a shared repair log in two buildings to give staff on different shifts access to the same information. The eight-week trial left its supervisor willing to repeat the exercise—with more preparation time—while questions about larger repairs remain open.

Elm Quay manages 640 apartments. Before the trial, telephone reports and emails were stored separately. In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to test a shared log, choosing Svale Systems after checking whether it could show the status of each repair.

Maintenance supervisor Maya Lind described the aim in an email interview: “We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log.”

The team kept telephone reporting open for residents. Svale configured the log and trained six staff members during two sessions. For Lind, the work involved in preparing the categories was a lesson to carry forward.

“We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts.”

Elm Quay’s internal trial note, dated December 4, 2025, records 31 repair reports entered during the eight weeks. That count excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial.

The median time from report to assignment was two working days during the trial, compared with three in the preceding eight-week period. The periods had different workloads, however, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software. These figures measure assignment, not repair completion; the trial provides no measurements of completion time, costs, or resident satisfaction.

Asked to assess the experience, Lind offered a qualified judgment: “I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation.”

The trial has not yet expanded. Before deciding whether to extend it, the maintenance team will check how its categories work for larger repairs. Its next decision therefore remains tied to how the team organizes repair information and what it learns from that check.
