# Keep phone booking until Lervik has the evidence

By Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut

Lervik’s municipal executive board should postpone a permanent switch to online-only booking for community venues. Our association, Öppna beslut, proposes a six-month trial with both phone and web booking at all seven venues. Before removing a booking option, the board needs a basis for weighing staff time against its value to users.

## The pilot counted bookings, not barriers

The municipality’s April 8, 2026, pilot report, *Bokning av föreningslokaler*, recorded 96 web bookings and 24 phone bookings during eight weeks at two venues. Those figures establish that both options were used. They do not establish why.

The report counts bookings, not individual people, and measures neither age, functional ability, nor digital proficiency. It cannot tell us what proportion of residents cannot book online. A phone booking is no proof that someone cannot use the web. It is a reason to find out why people still use the phone.

I do not oppose online booking. I oppose making a permanent decision before the municipality can weigh the administrative workload against the value of keeping both options.

## Take the workload objection seriously

The staff recommendation prepared for the board’s June 18 meeting proposes ending phone booking at all seven venues from September. Its stated reason is to spare staff from entering information through two separate workflows. That is a legitimate concern: keeping both channels means administration the board must consider.

But the documents contain neither a measurement of staff time nor a calculation of financial savings. The case for removing phone booking therefore rests on an administrative concern that those documents do not quantify in staff time or projected savings. That is too thin a basis for a permanent change across all seven venues.

## Test both options, then make the decision

During our proposed six-month trial, staff would record the time spent on each booking channel and invite users to explain, voluntarily, why they chose phone or web. This would give the board a basis for assessing both the workload and users’ reasons.

Öppna beslut has not claimed to have costed the trial or secured its funding. The board must assess that cost; our proposal does not make it disappear.

The municipal executive board should postpone the permanent switch, address the cost of a six-month trial, and authorize it with both channels available at all seven venues. Afterward, it can decide whether to remove, modify, or retain a booking channel—with evidence about the work involved and why users choose it.
