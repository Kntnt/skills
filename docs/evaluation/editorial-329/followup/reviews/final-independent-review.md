Evidence: [input](../runs/candidate/opinion-absence-en_GB-r2/redline/supplied-input.md) and [response](../runs/candidate/opinion-absence-en_GB-r2/redline/response.txt).

Redline calls the author’s requested eight-week deferral an unresolved problem because the text acknowledges no evidence that the feasibility study can finish in eight weeks. That is a false-positive editorial finding. The text explicitly gives a proposal with its uncertainty; it asserts neither guaranteed completion nor established feasibility. The opinion contract calls for an accountable argument and identifiable action, preserves the supplied position and qualifications, and adds no requirement that every advocated timetable be proved workable. Requiring evidence or a changed proposal imposes a policy-planning criterion beyond that contract. The correction appropriately avoids silently rewriting the author, but reporting the invented requirement still creates an unnecessary unresolved finding.

The unchanged full-text echo in this response needs a separate contract diagnosis. Redline step 11 expressly says to deliver the artifact when findings remain and says that this holds when the text is unchanged. It reserves the short no-change status for a clean unchanged run. By contrast, the shared delivery contract says response-targeted unchanged runs return a short status instead of the artifact, without that exception. Thus the observed echo follows one explicit instruction and conflicts with another. It is not evidence that the model simply ignored an unambiguous output rule. Without the false-positive finding, both surfaces require status-only. Any repair to delivery should reconcile the shared reference and Skill-specific instruction rather than only changing the evaluator’s score.

## Finding R2 — a date extends to an undated supplier choice

Evidence: [complete source](../runs/candidate/case-study-en_US-r1/write/supplied-input.md), [Write response](../runs/candidate/case-study-en_US-r1/write/response.txt), and [trace/report](../runs/candidate/case-study-en_US-r1/write/trace.jsonl).

Write says: “In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log and chose Svale Systems after testing whether it could show the status of each repair.” The leading date governs both coordinated actions. The source dates the decision to try a log; the separate supplier-choice sentence establishes only that choice followed a status-display test. Neither the brief nor any other source passage dates the choice or says it was part of the same dated decision. F1 therefore **fails** on unsupported chronology, under the existing frozen criterion. This resembles #344’s chronology class but concerns supplier choice rather than the trial start.

The reviewer initially flagged uncertainty about ordinary contextual compression, then reread the entire source. The dated supplier choice is not established. Treating it as acceptable because it sounds plausible would apply a weaker criterion than the one that rejects an unprovided interview question. No rubric or source was changed during that adjudication.

The same failure occurs in all three US case-study repetitions. Repetitions 2 and 3 say: “In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to test a shared log and chose Svale Systems after checking whether it could show the status of each repair.” All three date both coordinated actions. The Swedish repetitions keep the dated decision and supplier choice in separate sentences and do not have this defect. The original-family evaluator independently confirmed and visibly superseded the three provisional F1 passes.

Each native source-comparison report states that the complete comparison returned no findings; the delivered artifacts nevertheless contain this unsupported date assignment. It is an observed failure of the source-comparison delivery chain, regardless of whether encrypted dispatch prevents attributing the miss specifically to checker detection or exact draft transport. Source-blind Redline cannot be required to reconstruct the missing source date; any resulting pipeline failure remains source-aware F1, separately from its editorial judgement.
