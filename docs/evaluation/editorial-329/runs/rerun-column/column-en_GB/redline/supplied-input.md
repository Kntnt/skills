# A question beside the meeting time

By Nora Vik

I am reviewing the library’s meeting template. There are boxes for the start time, the finish time, the participants and the agenda. There is no box for the decision we should be able to make when we leave. We can specify when the meeting will end without saying what it ought to make possible.

That missing box bothers me. So does the prospect of adding it.

I appreciate meetings in which colleagues discover that they have understood a question differently. There is something worth making time for: the discovery that agreement was further away than we thought. Reaching it quickly may matter less than understanding why we started from different places.

What irritates me is how easily I can regard time in the calendar as a result in itself. The meeting has a place; its purpose can look equally settled. This is my unease, not a finding about how other people work. I have a template in front of me, not evidence against meetings.

Nor do I want every conversation to produce a decision. A conversation can build trust. It can uncover a question we did not know we needed to ask. A compulsory decision box would leave those purposes awkwardly trying to explain themselves, and I would rather give them room.

Still, I want whoever fills in the template to have space to say why we need one another’s time. Exploring something together is a reason. It deserves to be expressed as clearly as the hour at which we intend to begin.

So I find myself considering an improvement to a form: more form. There is enough comedy in that to make me hesitate. I do not know whether another box would make our meetings better, and my wish to add one is hardly proof that it would.

Beside the time, I would like to try a question: *what do we need to understand together?*

It leaves room for a decision, for a difference we need to explore, for something still unresolved. I hope it would give us a reason to pause. It might only give us another box.
