# A meeting needs more than a time slot

By Nora Vik

I am looking through the library’s meeting template. There are boxes for the start time, the finish time, the participants and the agenda. There is no box for the decision we should be able to make when we leave. We can record when we will stop. What we might have settled by then has no space of its own.

That omission bothers me, though perhaps a decision is too narrow a thing to ask of every meeting. I value the moment when colleagues discover that they have understood a question differently. There is something hopeful in finding the disagreement before tidying it away. A quick agreement would not necessarily be an improvement.

What irritates me is how easily time in the calendar can begin to look like a result in itself. That is my own irritation, not a finding from research into other people’s working days. Still, looking at the template, I want to distinguish between having set aside the time and knowing why we need it.

A conversation can build trust. It can also uncover a question we did not know we needed to ask. I would not want a revised form to make either of those purposes seem inadequate because nobody could promise a decision at the end. There needs to be room for an honest intention to explore something together.

So the missing box may need a different question. Beside the time, I would like to try: what do we need to understand together?

The word ‘together’ matters to me. It asks whoever is filling in the template to consider why we need one another’s time. It leaves room for an answer that is still taking shape.

There is, admittedly, some comedy in looking at a form, finding it wanting and proposing more form. Apparently my answer to the limitations of boxes is another box.

I do not know whether it would make our meetings better. I want to try the question, and I remain doubtful about its little rectangle. For now, both the hope and the doubt will have to fit.
