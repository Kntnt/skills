# A Little More Room on the Meeting Form

By Nora Vik

I’m reviewing the library’s meeting template. There are fields for the start time, end time, participants, and agenda. There is no field for the decision we should be able to make when we leave. The appointment has somewhere to go. What we hope to accomplish has to find its own space.

That missing field bothers me. I’m irritated by how easily time on a calendar can come to count as a result in itself. An hour has been set aside; something has been arranged. But arranging to spend time together leaves the question of why we need one another’s time.

A decision would be one answer. I don’t think it should be the only acceptable one.

I value meetings in which colleagues discover that they have understood a question differently. There is something worth staying with in that discovery, even when agreement would be quicker. Before deciding what to do, we may need to understand what each of us thinks we are deciding. I would hate to design a form so pleased with its demand for an outcome that it left no room for that.

Conversation can build trust. It can also uncover a question we didn’t know we needed to ask. Those possibilities matter to me, and I want a meeting’s purpose to have room for them. Exploring something together is a reason to meet, too. Whoever fills in the template should have a place to say so.

Which brings me, with rather inconvenient logic, to another field on the form.

There is a certain comedy in looking at a template, finding it wanting, and prescribing more template. I can see it perfectly well while still wanting to try. Beside the time, I would put a question: *What do we need to understand together?*

I don’t know whether that would make our meetings better. It might give us a useful pause while planning them. It might become another box to fill in. For now, I want to leave room for the question—and for my doubts about the box.
