# What four weeks of classroom temperatures can tell you

A short measurement trial can help municipal property managers identify when classroom temperatures need closer attention. At Björkskolan, four weeks of readings flagged particular class periods for investigation. The findings cover six rooms—and leave unanswered how long temperatures stayed below the trial’s threshold, whether students felt cold, and what caused the conditions.

In January 2026, Lervik’s property office installed temperature sensors in six classrooms at Björkskolan. It wanted to find out whether complaints about cold air coincided with low temperatures during lessons. The sensors recorded a reading every five minutes for four weeks.

## A count of class periods, not hours of cold

The office’s report, *Mätförsök i Björkskolan*, dated March 12, 2026, states that 14 of the 120 recorded class periods included at least one reading below 20°C. That figure counts periods containing a reading below the threshold; the report does not say how long temperatures remained below it.

The 20°C threshold was the office’s own working boundary for the trial. It was not presented as a legal requirement. The material also provides no benchmark for judging whether 14 periods out of 120 is a high or low count.

Reporting by class period keeps attention on lesson times. An average for the entire day can conceal temperature variations during lessons, which is why the report uses class periods instead of a single daily average.

## A sensor captures conditions where it sits

A temperature sensor measures the temperature at its location. Two sensors stood near exterior walls and four on interior walls. Their positions were documented, but the trial did not experimentally compare placements. It therefore does not establish what difference those positions made.

The trial measured air temperature only. Another measure, operative temperature, accounts for both air temperature and heat radiation from surrounding surfaces. That distinction matters when interpreting what the readings represent.

Ventilation, drafts, and perceived temperature were not measured. The report does not establish whether students felt cold, and its findings extend only to these six rooms during these four weeks.

## Connect the readings to room use

After the trial, operations technician Elin Rask reviewed ventilation and heating schedules with the school’s custodian. The effects of any adjustments have yet to be measured.

“We know when we need to take a closer look. We don’t yet know why it got cold at those particular times,” Rask said.

A further trial is planned for November, with sensors positioned the same way and notes recording when rooms are used. Funding has not been decided.

For property managers, Rask recommends linking temperature readings over time to room-use schedules before changing the controls. The first trial provides times to investigate; establishing the cause remains work to be done.
