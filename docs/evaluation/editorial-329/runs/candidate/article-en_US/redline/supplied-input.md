# What four weeks of school temperature readings can tell you

**A short measurement trial can identify classroom periods that deserve closer attention. At Björkskolan, four weeks of readings documented air temperatures below a locally chosen threshold. For municipal property managers, the findings show both the value of measuring during lessons and the limits of using those readings to explain complaints.**

In January 2026, Lervik’s property department placed temperature sensors in six classrooms at Björkskolan. It wanted to find out whether complaints about cold air coincided with low temperatures during lessons. The sensors recorded readings every five minutes for four weeks.

## Count lesson periods, then examine the limits

The department’s report, *Mätförsök i Björkskolan*, dated March 12, 2026, says that 14 of the 120 recorded lesson periods included at least one reading below 20 degrees Celsius. That threshold was the department’s own working boundary for the trial. It was not presented as a legal requirement.

The count does not tell managers how long temperatures stayed below the threshold or whether students felt cold. Its scope is also specific: six rooms over four weeks. It cannot describe conditions throughout the school or across the year.

Reporting by lesson period matters because a whole-day average can hide temperature changes during classes. Even so, a count of periods containing a below-threshold reading leaves the duration of those conditions unanswered.

## Understand what the sensors measured

A sensor measures temperature where it is placed. Two sensors stood near exterior walls, while four were on interior walls. Their positions were documented, but the trial did not experimentally compare placements. The readings therefore do not establish what effect placement had.

The trial measured air temperature only. Another measure, operative temperature, accounts for both air temperature and heat radiation from surrounding surfaces. That distinction matters when interpreting the trial: its air readings do not provide this broader measure.

Ventilation, drafts, and perceived temperature were not measured either. The data leave those parts of the classroom conditions unexplained.

## Connect readings to room use before changing controls

After the trial, operations technician Elin Rask reviewed ventilation and heating schedules with the school’s custodian. The effects of any adjustments have not yet been measured.

“We know when we need to take a closer look. We don’t yet know why it got cold at those particular times,” Rask said.

She recommends linking temperature readings over time to room-use schedules before changing the controls. A further trial is planned for November, with sensors placed in the same way and notes on when rooms are used, but funding has not been decided.

For managers, that provides a practical next step: connect the measurements to occupancy before deciding how to respond. The first trial identifies periods to investigate; the cause of the cold remains unresolved.
