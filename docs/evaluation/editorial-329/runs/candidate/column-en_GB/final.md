# A meeting needs more than a time slot

By Nora Vik

I am looking through the library’s meeting template. There are boxes for the start time, the finish time, the participants and the agenda. There is no box for the decision we should be able to make when we leave. The document has room for when we stop, but not for what we hope to have settled by then.

That absence bothers me. So does my own first response to it: perhaps what this form needs is another box. There is a modest comedy in finding a problem with a template and proposing a larger template.

Still, I want to linger over what is missing before deciding where to put it.

I value meetings in which colleagues discover that they have understood a question differently. That discovery can matter more than reaching agreement quickly. If I make a decision the only acceptable result, I leave too little room for the conversations I appreciate.

A conversation can build trust. It can also reveal a question we did not know we needed to ask. Neither purpose fits comfortably into a space labelled ‘decision’, and I would not want to squeeze either into one merely to make the paperwork look complete.

What irritates me is how easily I find myself regarding time in the calendar as a result in itself. I have not measured this in anyone else. It is a suspicion I bring to the template: we can specify the beginning and end without saying why we need one another’s time.

Perhaps that is what I want whoever fills it in to consider. What needs to be understood together? The answer could be a decision we need to reach, or something we need to explore before a decision is possible.

I would like to try that question beside the time. I do not know whether it would make our meetings better. Another box could simply become another box to fill.

But I am still looking at the space beside the start time, wondering whether a question belongs there. I have some hope for the question. Less certainty about the box.
