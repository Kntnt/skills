# Elm Quay brings repair reports into one shared view

Elm Quay Housing’s maintenance team trialled a shared repair log in two buildings to help staff on different shifts see the same information. The eight-week trial gave the team experience of organising reports together, while leaving questions about preparation and larger repairs to resolve before any expansion.

Elm Quay manages 640 flats. Before the trial began in September 2025, telephone reports and emails were stored separately. The maintenance team chose Svale Systems after testing whether its log could show the status of each repair.

Maintenance supervisor Maya Lind explained the team’s priorities in an email interview:

“We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log.”

The team designed the categories and kept telephone reporting open for residents. Svale configured the log and trained six staff during two sessions. For Lind, the preparation involved work that another building would also need time to do.

“We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts.”

Elm Quay’s internal trial note, dated 4 December 2025, records 31 repair reports entered during the eight weeks. The figures exclude emergencies and work ordered before the trial.

Median time from report to assignment was two working days during the trial, compared with three working days in the preceding eight-week period. The periods had different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software.

That measure covers assignment, not completion of the repair. There were no measurements of costs, resident satisfaction or completion times, so those outcomes remain outside what the trial can establish.

Lind’s assessment includes a practical reservation:

“I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation.”

The trial has not yet expanded. Before deciding whether to extend it, the maintenance team will check how the categories work for larger repairs. Its next decision therefore rests on a further check of the categories it designed, alongside the experience already gathered in the two buildings.
