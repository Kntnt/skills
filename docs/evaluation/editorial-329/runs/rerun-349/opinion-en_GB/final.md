# Lervik needs evidence before ending phone bookings

*By Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut*

Lervik’s municipal executive board should postpone a permanent move to online-only bookings for community venues. Our association, Öppna beslut, proposes a six-month trial with both telephone and web booking available at all seven venues. Before removing a service, the board needs evidence of its value and the work it requires.

## Booking figures cannot explain users’ needs

The municipality’s pilot report, *Bokning av föreningslokaler*, dated 8 April 2026, records 96 web bookings and 24 telephone bookings during an eight-week trial at two venues. Those are bookings, not individual people. The report measures neither age nor disability nor digital confidence.

We therefore cannot use the telephone bookings to calculate how many residents cannot book online. But neither do the figures explain why people chose the telephone. A phone booking is a reason to investigate that choice, not proof that the caller cannot use the web.

I do not oppose digital booking. I oppose making a permanent decision before the municipality can weigh staff time against the value of the service to its users.

## Double administration deserves a proper assessment

The officers’ report prepared for the board’s 18 June meeting proposes ending telephone bookings at all seven venues from September. Its stated reason is that staff would no longer have to enter information through two booking processes.

That is a real administrative concern. Keeping both routes requires the board to consider the work involved. Yet the papers contain no measurement of staff time and no calculation of financial savings. The proposal identifies a burden without establishing its size.

That leaves the board without the evidence needed to judge the trade-off. An eight-week booking count from two venues is too narrow a basis for permanently removing telephone booking across all seven.

## Run a trial that answers the missing questions

During our proposed six-month trial, staff would record the time spent on each booking route. Users would also be invited to explain, voluntarily, why they chose telephone or web booking. Together, those findings would help the board assess both administration and users’ reasons.

Öppna beslut has neither costed the trial nor claimed to have funded it. The board must consider that unresolved cost.

The board should postpone the permanent change, assess the cost and authorise a six-month trial at all seven venues with both routes retained. It can then decide whether either route should be removed, changed or kept, with evidence about the work and the value involved.
