## Source findings and precedence

The source summaries in #329 accurately describe the originals’ central intent. The later comments materially narrow how that intent is to be implemented. The following distinctions need to survive the working map.

| Source | Preserve or clarify | Superseded or bounded |
|---|---|---|
| Google article instructions | Informative H1; self-contained standfirst; separate lead before the first H2; informative subheadings; coherent paragraphs with varied sentence length; ending that fulfils the opening’s promise. | The literal Thomas Barregren byline is brief-specific. Original 30–70-character headings, 60-word standfirst and 80-word paragraph ceiling become guidance under #329. Original per-section counts and one-sentence permissions describe scale and rhythm, not quotas. Commercial links and final CTA are governed by actual assignment/material. |
| Google pedagogical dramaturgy | Direct, factual explanation; concepts available before later reasoning needs them; concise relevant context; logical progression; technical detail where useful; analogies, questions, scenes and humour only when useful. | Its repeated minimal-dramaturgy warnings addressed older models. They do not prohibit calm ABT, all narrative interest or an explicit chosen technique. Its investor/business perspective applies only when relevant. Direct Swedish “du” is not a multilingual pronoun mandate. |
| Google style and tone | Concrete language; ordinary words when adequate; useful specialist terms with audience-appropriate explanation; real transitions; professional, engaged pedagogy; source-supported assertions; no invented facts or sources. | Historical/theoretical context, why-before-how, models, examples, constructive framing and challenges to assumptions are useful options, not mandatory blocks in every text. Source-link verification belongs to Write’s actual source contract, not a source-blind Redline pass. |
| Historical article | Distinct jobs for headline, ingress, lead, body and ending; writer’s voice mainly in selection and explanation. | Its 60-character absolute limit differs from the Google original and is superseded. Mini-ABT teaser, hidden answer, compulsory sales link, citation quotas and elaborate nested scene recipe do not transfer. |
| Historical customer case | Customer as acting subject; their attributed experience and appraisal; writer supplies context; supplier in third-person narration; justified results and qualification. | No quote-per-section, one/two-pull-quote requirement, forced rescue story, target word count or automatic commercial CTA. A missing customer judgement is a material gap, not a licence to infer satisfaction from a result. |
| Historical column | Named author’s perspective and reflection; distinction from explanatory article and thesis-led opinion; no invented personal anecdotes or conversations. | Personal voice does not require an anecdote, a prescribed pronoun, constant humour, opaque headings, a hidden-until-last-line point or a ban on arguments. |
| Historical opinion | Stated position early; visible authorial responsibility; honest argument, adequate attribution and concrete desired change. | No argument quota or automatic ABT. Polemic may remain sharp; source fidelity is not a demand to neutralise every position. Generic “time to act” is insufficient when no action follows. |
| Historical web-copy | Descriptive navigation, direct-entry comprehension, concrete address and useful information structure. | Its broad “all writing for web” identity does not absorb the four other genres. #337 adds a specific copy/UX task: relevance, supported benefit/information, actual conditions, choice and next-step consequence as applicable. No universal landing-page template or invented functionality. |
| Historical ABT/PAC | ABT relates necessary situation, a genuine obstacle/question and supported outcome. PAC starts from data/context/situation, interprets it and reaches a warranted conclusion. | PAC does **not** require a falsifiable thesis. The historical evidence-before-conclusion absolutism cannot bar an informative ingress, early opinion thesis or report summary. Technique operates at the level the genre needs. |
| Historical shared style | Reader understanding, accurate implications, idiom, meaningful transitions and restrained emphasis. | No universal ban on new rhetorical questions or explanatory imagery; no all-or-nothing quotation mode; no absolute local-repetition ban. These conflict with the clarified autonomous professional role or self-contained web entry points. |

The controlling choice is **none as the default for all five genres**. Existing explicit ABT/PAC selection and recognised metadata keep their priority. An old `technique: abt` is an explicit choice; natural narrative shape alone is not a choice. Nothing authorises a new `--technique=none` flag.

The latest #329 clarification requires a short editorial brief, with the professional role established early, rather than a compressed catalogue of every ticket sentence. Swedish editorial/copy tradition governs craft; the complete language surface belongs to the target language. That distinction applies beyond the three tested locales. No newspaper names need appear in output. A successful deviation from the numerical guidance needs neither permission nor a justification in the delivered text. No new sentence-length quota, layout-independent line count, resource word cap or mandatory approval stage survives precedence.

## Traceability checks for the working map and frozen matrix

1. Give each retained substantive requirement one runtime home: shared five-genre craft/web base, appropriate genre, selected technique, existing Source Fidelity or language resource. Reviews diagnose the same requirement; the source map and this report are never loaded by Write/Redline.
2. Show the old-to-new transition for ingress versus teaser, absolute web dimensions, default technique, PAC premise, quotation policy, local repetition and invented memoir. These are the places where merely adding a new document could leave conflicting active rules intact.
3. Include all 15 genre/locale pipelines, predetermined baseline cases (five Swedish and at least one English), Swedish-source-to-English and English-source-to-Swedish cases, plus actual positive and negative Redline controls.
4. Give every synthetic fixture sufficiently rich attributed facts and explicit limits. Customer appraisal must actually be said; temporal order is not causality; a ghostwriter’s voice is not permission for new memories; link destination and button consequence must be supplied where used.
5. Cover default none for every genre; explicit ABT and PAC; recognised metadata/instruction none; legacy explicit technique metadata; flag/metadata/instruction precedence. Record actual resource reads, not just declared settings.
6. Preserve positive variation beyond numerical guidance. A long but coherent paragraph or short descriptive heading can pass. Negative dense/chopped controls must name the actual reader loss. Controls should also separate calm ABT from false drama and factual PAC from a manufactured dispute.
7. Separate source-aware Write checks from text-only Redline checks. A hidden fabrication cannot fairly be diagnosed by Redline without evidence in its input; it remains a Write/source-fidelity evaluation. Redline must preserve claims, qualifications, authorial voice and customer attribution, report irreparable gaps and perform its one final Proofread pass.
8. Freeze semantic criteria before model runs. Judge genre, perspective, reasoning, pedagogy, web navigation, idiom and preservation from observable passages; do not score exact phrasing, newspaper-name presence or conformity to a single example text.
