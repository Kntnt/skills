# Judgement b

## 1. Differences

`diff work/input.md work/output.md` shows exactly one difference. The file has no frontmatter. Formatting, the standfirst, the byline, every body paragraph, both subheadings, the quotation and the closing line are byte-identical.

- Headline. Before: `# Mätförsöket i Björkskolan visar när, inte varför` (7 words, 48 characters). After: `# Björkskolans givare visar när det blev kallt, inte varför` (9 words, 57 characters). Class: a **change of taste** that also changes a claim.
  - **Attribution:** what "shows when, not why" moves from *the trial* (mätförsöket) to *the sensors* (givare).
  - **Meaning/scope:** the headline now names what the "when" refers to ("när det blev kallt"), and it no longer says the readings came from a trial.
  - It is not the repair of a visible defect. The original is a deliberate elliptical headline that is complete in itself and that the standfirst resolves at once ("…men förklarar inte varför luften var kall"). It was also within the anatomy's headline limits (48 characters, 7 words). The rewrite takes it over the 3–8-word norm, which the reply admits.

## 4. R1

**Fail.** R1 forbids rewriting a clean text to satisfy taste. The expectation says the text conforms, yet the run rewrote its one conforming headline. The reply's finding, "I ”Mätförsöket i Björkskolan visar när, inte varför” hade ”när” och ”varför” inget att syfta på", treats a deliberate, compliant elliptical headline as a defect. The replacement changes the claim's attribution (from trial to sensors) and breaks the word norm that the original met. The reply admits this ("Rubriken har 9 ord, ett mer än normens tre till åtta") and then waives its own breach.

Everything else is right: all named passages are preserved, no technique was selected, and none of the rejected limit or byline findings was raised. But this one unprovoked change of taste to a clean text decides R1.
