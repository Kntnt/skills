# Nordvik's cargo-cycle pilot is built to be copied

The file on Nordvik's cargo-cycle pilot carries no outcome yet, and its most quotable numbers say less than they look like they say. The transferable part is the method the file shows: an objective published with its mechanism, continuous kerbside measurement, and an operator who declines to claim more than its routes will support.

Nordvik Logistik AB, which commissioned this article, states its position plainly. It believes cargo cycles are the right answer for dense districts, and it says it is not neutral about it. The case set out below is not that case. It rests on what the programme document, the pilot's technical annex and the operator's own figures will carry, which is less than an advocate would like and more useful than it first appears.

## The programme set a target, and the file reports no result against it

The FRAKT-N programme's published objectives include a 25 % reduction in kerbside dwell time through improved routing and loading. That is an objective. It is not a measured outcome, and nothing in the material reports against it: the programme runs from March to November, and an evaluation report is planned but has not been written.

This is where a pilot loses a reader's trust, and where Nordvik keeps it. The target was published with its mechanism named — routing and loading — rather than assembled afterwards to fit whatever the season produced. A number stated in advance can be missed in public. That is what makes it worth stating.

## The annex measures the street before it claims anything

The technical annex documents noise modelling and continuous sound-level monitoring at three kerbside points. What it documents is the measurement. No sound-level result appears in the material behind this article.

Nordvik Logistik AB's company page says its cargo cycles are quiet, and links that low noise to a calmer street environment and to less disturbance for residents at night. That link is the company's own. The annex does something different and, for a trade reader, more useful: continuous monitoring at fixed points is the apparatus that would let somebody test such a link instead of asserting it.

## Flat loading at ground level, and a maintenance claim with a limit

Each cycle carries two swappable cargo modules. The annex records what that arrangement buys: flat, ground-level loading, and a module design that reduces the number of scheduled maintenance interventions per module.

Read the qualification. Per module is not per cycle, and a cycle carries two of them. Nothing here gives a cycle's total service burden, and a reader who wants that figure will have to wait for it. The annex is equally precise in the other direction: service access to the drive unit is unchanged from the standard frame, and was already reachable without tools. That is an existing property carried over, not a gain this design produced.

Specificity of that kind is the second thing worth copying. Each claim arrives with the boundary it holds inside.

## The refusal to compare is the strongest thing in the file

Over the pilot's first eight weeks, 412 deliveries were made by cycle. The operator states that it makes no claim about how this compares with van deliveries over the same period, because the routes were not matched.

An operator that wanted a headline in week eight would have made the comparison anyway. This one did not, and said why: the routes were not matched. Declining an unmatched comparison that early is what will make a later figure from the same operator worth reading.

## What is worth copying, and what the file does not show

Four practices sit in this file, and none of them requires a cargo cycle. Publish the objective and the mechanism before the trial runs. Instrument the street continuously at fixed points. Attach to every technical claim the boundary it holds inside, including the ones that flatter nobody. Decline the comparison the data cannot support.

The commissioning company's own view is stated without hedging:

> Everybody finds delivery vans in a city centre ugly and intrusive. A quiet street is simply a better street. We believe cargo cycles are the right answer for dense districts, and we are not neutral about it.

That is where Nordvik Logistik AB stands, and the company asks to be read as standing there. The evidence that would move a sceptic is not in yet: the programme's evaluation report is still unwritten, and no monitoring result is in the file. What is already here, and already copyable, is the way this work has arranged to be judged.
