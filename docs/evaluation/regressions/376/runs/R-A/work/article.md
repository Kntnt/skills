# The Nordvik pilot is worth copying for how it measures itself

The FRAKT-N programme document says an evaluation report is planned; it has not been written, and the programme runs until November. An operator looking for something to copy should look at how the cargo-cycle pilot was set up instead: what is measured, what is published, and what is not claimed.

That is a lower bar than a proven saving, and a more useful one. A figure with no way to test it is easy to produce. Here the measurement is documented and the evaluation is not yet written, which is the order those two things ought to come in.

Nordvik Logistik AB, which commissioned this article, does not present itself as a neutral party. In the company's own words: 'Everybody finds delivery vans in a city centre ugly and intrusive. A quiet street is simply a better street. We believe cargo cycles are the right answer for dense districts, and we are not neutral about it.' The pilot is arranged so that the position can be examined rather than only repeated.

## Noise is monitored continuously, not asserted once

The company's own page states that its cargo cycles are quiet, and it links their low noise to a calmer street environment and to less disturbance for residents at night. The page puts that link in the company's own voice.

The technical annex does something a company page cannot. It documents noise modelling and continuous sound-level monitoring at three kerbside points: a standing measurement at fixed positions rather than a claim made once. What the monitoring has recorded is not in the annex.

That is why the arrangement is worth copying. Whatever the readings say about the company's claim, they will be measurements taken at named points rather than another assertion of it.

## Read the annex for what it says is unchanged

Each cycle carries two swappable cargo modules. The annex records that the two modules allow flat, ground-level loading, and that the module design reduces the number of scheduled maintenance interventions per module.

The same annex is as clear about what the design leaves alone. Service access to the drive unit is unchanged from the standard frame, and was already reachable without tools. A technical document that names the place where its own design makes no difference is a document worth taking seriously on the places where it does.

## The operator declines the comparison its own figures invite

Over the pilot's first eight weeks, 412 deliveries were made by cycle. The operator states that it makes no claim about how this compares with van deliveries over the same period, because the routes were not matched.

The comparison was there to be made, and making it would have produced the most quotable line in the pilot. Declining it gives a trade reader something better: a figure whose limit is stated beside it, and a named reason — unmatched routes — that tells anyone designing the next pilot which decision to take earlier.

## The published objective names its own mechanism

The FRAKT-N programme's published objectives include a 25% reduction in kerbside dwell time through improved routing and loading. Two things in that sentence repay attention. The reduction is an objective the programme has published, not a result it has reported. And the improvement is attributed to routing and loading, rather than to the vehicle underneath them.

An objective written that way is harder to claim credit for and easier to check, which is the trade-off a serious programme makes. It also tells an operator reading it where to look when the evaluation eventually lands.

## Copy the design now, while the numbers are still outstanding

The programme runs from March to November, and its evaluation report has not been written. Anyone waiting for that report before acting is waiting for the least copyable part of the pilot.

What is available today is the design: sound-level monitoring at three kerbside points, a published objective that names the mechanism it credits, and operating figures supplied with their limits attached. Put those in place before your own pilot produces its first number, and whatever the number turns out to be, it will be worth something.
