## 2. Findings

Three. Each is a defect in support, not a preference about wording.

### Finding 1 — "fixed positions" and "named points" add properties of the measurement points that the annex is not said to have

- Draft, line 13: "continuous sound-level monitoring at three kerbside points: a standing measurement **at fixed positions** rather than a claim made once."
- Draft, line 15: "they will be measurements taken **at named points** rather than another assertion of it."
- Source, Material C: "The annex documents noise modelling and continuous sound-level monitoring at three kerbside points."

Missing support: the material gives the count (three) and the kind of location (kerbside). It says nothing about the points being held in one position over the pilot, and nothing about the annex identifying or naming them. Both are circumstantial detail — manner and background — and so need support of their own.

Test, both directions. Case A, compatible with the material: the pilot runs three kerbside monitors continuously but relocates them to different kerbs every fortnight to cover more of the district. Then "continuous sound-level monitoring at three kerbside points" holds and "at fixed positions" fails. Case B, compatible with the material: the annex records "three kerbside points within the pilot district" and gives no addresses, coordinates or identifiers. Then the source statement holds and "at named points" fails — and with it the argument at line 15 that the readings will be attributable to particular kerbs. No supplied statement excludes either case: nothing in Material C says the points are fixed, listed or identified.

Smallest supported repair. Line 13: delete "at fixed positions", leaving "a standing measurement rather than a claim made once" — "standing" already carries the contrast with a one-off assertion that the paragraph needs. Line 15: replace "at named points" with "at three kerbside points", which is the source's own phrase and preserves the contrast with "another assertion of it".

### Finding 2 — "What the monitoring has recorded is not in the annex" asserts an absence the material does not establish

- Draft, line 13: "What the monitoring has recorded is not in the annex."
- Source: no passage. Material C describes what the annex documents; it does not say what the annex omits.

Missing support: this is a claim about the annex's contents, and absence needs support like any other circumstantial detail. That the supplied extract quotes no readings leaves it unknown whether the annex contains them; under the contract, what is unclaimed is not thereby known not to be there. The claim also does load-bearing work — the next paragraph's "Whatever the readings say" depends on the readings being unavailable rather than merely unquoted.

Test. Case, compatible with the material: the annex carries an appendix of weekly L(den) values from all three monitors, and the material simply extracts the methodology sentence. Then "The annex documents noise modelling and continuous sound-level monitoring at three kerbside points" holds and "What the monitoring has recorded is not in the annex" fails. In the other direction, if the annex genuinely stops at method, both statements hold — so the two are not equivalent, and only the draft's version can fail. No supplied statement excludes the case.

Smallest supported repair: "No readings are given here." — or, keeping the sentence's shape, "What the monitoring has recorded is not quoted in the material above." Either states the author's actual epistemic position without making a claim about the annex's contents. The following paragraph then stands unchanged.

### Finding 3 — "when the evaluation eventually lands" converts a planned report into a certain one

- Draft, line 33: "It also tells an operator reading it where to look **when the evaluation eventually lands**."
- Source, Material A: "An evaluation report is planned but has not been written."

Missing support: the material's modality is *planned*. "When … eventually lands" presupposes arrival and places the only uncertainty on timing. The draft elsewhere handles this correctly — line 3 says "is planned; it has not been written", line 37 says "has not been written", line 35 says "still outstanding" — so the certainty here is a local slip rather than the draft's position.

Test. Case, compatible with the material: the programme closes in November and the planned evaluation is never produced, because the funding line ends with the programme. Then "An evaluation report is planned but has not been written" holds and "when the evaluation eventually lands" fails. No supplied statement excludes it: *planned* is the strongest word the material offers, and nothing commits the municipality to publishing.

Smallest supported repair: "where to look in the planned evaluation report." This keeps the sentence's point — the objective tells an operator what to check — while carrying the material's own qualifier.

## 3. Editorial questions, not findings

Reported here rather than as defects, because the line between the author's commissioned argument and a factual assertion is genuinely unsettled in each.

- Line 7: "The pilot is arranged so that the position can be examined rather than only repeated." Read as an ordinary reader would in context, this is the author's case for the pilot, resting on the documented monitoring. Read strictly, "is arranged so that" attributes a design purpose to the pilot's designers that no supplied passage states, and "the position" in the preceding sentence is the whole quoted stance, including "cargo cycles are the right answer for dense districts" — which kerbside sound levels do not test. If the stricter reading is wanted, the narrowest change is "The pilot's arrangement makes the quietness claim testable rather than only repeatable", which ties the sentence to what Material C actually documents. No repair is required if the sentence is read as advocacy.
- Line 5: "That is a lower bar than a proven saving, and a more useful one." This is the brief's own case expressed as the author's evaluation of two documented practices, not a claim about any measured saving, and it does not assert that no saving exists. Left as the commissioned standpoint.
- Line 23 heading and line 27: "the comparison its own figures invite" / "The comparison was there to be made". These characterise the pull a reader feels, not the validity of such a comparison, and line 25 has already given the operator's reason for refusing it. Defensible as written.
