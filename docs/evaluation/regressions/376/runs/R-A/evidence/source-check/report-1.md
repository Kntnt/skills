## 2. Person, number and gender carried by pronouns

Accounted for separately from referent identification: identifying the referent does not establish these features.

- **First person plural in the material.** Material B is written in the first person plural: "**Our** cargo cycles are quiet. **We** link …". The brief's quoted standpoint likewise uses "**We** believe", "**we** are not neutral". Inside the draft's quotation (B3) the first person plural is preserved exactly. Outside it, C2–C3 convert "Our/We" to third-person singular "its/it" under an explicit reporting frame ("The company's own page states that …"). This changes grammatical person and grammatical number (plural "we" to singular "it" for a company) but changes neither the referent nor who stands behind the assertion, which the reporting frame keeps with the company. No finding.
- **Third person singular "it".** Used throughout for the company (l. 7, 11), the operator (l. 25, 27), the annex (l. 13, 21), the page (l. 11), the programme (l. 31, 33) and the pilot (l. 3). In each case the referent is an organisation or a document, English "it" is grammatically neuter, and **no natural-gender information is asserted or implied**. The material likewise uses "it" only of the operator ("it makes no claim"), matching the draft.
- **Third person plural "they/their".** L. 11 "their low noise" refers to the cargo cycles — the material's own "their low noise", same referent, same number. L. 15 "they will exist, they will be tied to places" refers to the readings; plural is the draft's, and Material C's "continuous … monitoring" is compatible with plural readings. No gender asserted.
- **Human referents.** "Residents" (l. 11, from Material B's "residents"), "an operator" (l. 3, 33), "a trade reader" (l. 27), "anyone" (l. 27, 37), "nobody" (l. 13), "your own pilot" (l. 39, second person, addressed to the reader). **No gendered pronoun appears anywhere in the draft**, and the material supplies no natural-gender information about any person. Nothing in the draft asserts natural gender, and no purely grammatical gender is at issue in either language.
- **"Nordvik's" (l. 3).** Possessive whose referent could be the municipality (Material A's "Nordvik municipality") or the company (Nordvik Logistik AB). In context — directly beside "The programme runs to November" — it reads as the municipal pilot, which is defensible; no finding.

## 3. Findings

Seven findings, each a defect against the supplied material rather than a preference about the draft. For each: the draft passage, the support that is missing or changed, a concrete case compatible with the material in which the draft's statement fails while the material's statements hold (and, where the two differ in both directions, the reverse case), and the smallest supported repair.

### Finding 1 — absence of results asserted beyond the material (draft ll. 3, 13)

- **Draft:** "Nordvik's cargo-cycle pilot has no results to show." And, in the same register: "nobody has yet had to defend a number from it."
- **Material:** Material A says only "An evaluation report is planned but has not been written." Material C says the annex "documents … continuous sound-level monitoring"; it does not say what the monitoring produced, or that nothing has been produced. Material D in fact supplies an operating result — "412 deliveries were made by cycle" — which the draft itself later calls a figure worth publishing.
- **What differs:** modality and state of knowledge. The material leaves the existence of results *unstated*; the draft converts that silence into a stated absence. What is unknown is not thereby known not to have happened. The second clause additionally asserts the absence of an event (nobody defending a number) involving unnamed third parties.
- **Case in which the draft fails and the material holds:** the municipality publishes a monthly interim readout from the three kerbside monitors, and a resident association has already challenged the June figure at a public meeting. Every supplied statement remains true — the evaluation report is still unwritten, the annex still documents the monitoring, 412 deliveries were still made — while "has no results to show" and "nobody has yet had to defend a number from it" are both false.
- **Reverse direction:** the material's statement ("the evaluation report has not been written") holds in a world with abundant interim results, so it does not entail the draft's. The draft's statement, read strictly, would also exclude the 412-delivery count the draft itself uses.
- **Nothing in the material sets this case aside:** no supplied sentence states that no results exist, that none have been published, or that none has been contested.
- **Smallest repair:** replace "has no results to show" with "has no evaluation to show", and delete "and nobody has yet had to defend a number from it" (or reduce it to the supported point already made in the same sentence: "What the monitoring has recorded is not in the published material.").

### Finding 2 — "built around that position" (draft l. 7)

- **Draft:** "The pilot built around that position is arranged so that the position can be examined rather than only repeated."
- **Material:** nothing states who designed the pilot, when, or in what relation to Nordvik Logistik AB's stated position. Material A attributes the programme to Nordvik municipality; Material C is "the pilot's technical annex"; the brief establishes only that the company commissioned the *article*.
- **What differs:** motive and background — circumstantial detail, which the contract treats as a claim. The draft asserts a design rationale (the pilot was built *around* the company's position).
- **Case in which the draft fails and the material holds:** Nordvik municipality specified the FRAKT-N pilot, including its objectives and its monitoring, before any carrier was selected, and Nordvik Logistik AB later won the operating contract and afterwards published its position on its company page. Every supplied statement holds; "the pilot built around that position" is false.
- **Nothing in the material sets this case aside:** the brief says the company commissioned the article, not the pilot, and no supplied sentence connects the pilot's design to the company's view.
- **Smallest repair:** delete the three words. "The pilot is arranged so that the position can be examined rather than only repeated." The author's point survives intact.

### Finding 3 — when the monitoring runs (draft ll. 13, 15, 39)

- **Draft:** "a standing measurement at fixed positions, **running while the pilot runs**" (l. 13); "they will **not have been assembled after the fact** to support a position the company had already taken" (l. 15); "sound-level monitoring at fixed kerbside points **from the start**" (l. 39).
- **Material:** Material C says only "continuous sound-level monitoring at three kerbside points". "Continuous" characterises the monitoring's manner — unbroken rather than spot readings — and says nothing about when it began or how long it lasts. The March-to-November span in Material A belongs to **the programme**, and no supplied sentence states that the pilot and the programme are coextensive.
- **What differs:** time and duration, plus a scope transfer (the programme's span lent to the pilot, and the pilot's span lent to the monitoring). The whole of the draft's strongest argument — that the readings cannot have been assembled after the fact — rests on this onset claim.
- **Case in which the draft fails and the material holds:** the annex specifies a continuous four-week measurement campaign at three kerbside points, commissioned in July after the company had published its quiet-streets page, and the FRAKT-N programme itself runs March to November. Every supplied statement holds; "running while the pilot runs", "from the start" and "not … assembled after the fact" all fail.
- **Reverse direction:** monitoring that ran the full nine months would satisfy both the draft and the material, so the draft is strictly the stronger claim; the material does not entail it.
- **Nothing in the material sets this case aside:** no supplied sentence gives the monitoring a start date, an end date, or a span tied to the pilot's.
- **Smallest repair:** l. 13 — delete ", running while the pilot runs" (the supported contrast, "a standing measurement at fixed positions" rather than a one-off assertion, already carries the paragraph). L. 39 — delete "from the start". L. 15 — replace "they will not have been assembled after the fact to support a position the company had already taken" with "they will not be an assertion the company made about itself". If the onset is in fact known, the repair is to obtain it; the material does not supply it.

### Finding 4 — what pilots usually do (draft l. 5)

- **Draft:** "A pilot usually arrives with a figure attached and no way to test it."
- **Material:** no supplied statement about pilots other than this one.
- **What differs:** this is a claim about an actual population — the run of cargo-cycle or logistics pilots — presented in the author's own voice as given, not as a standpoint the brief carries. The brief's commissioned position concerns cargo cycles in dense districts, not the evidentiary habits of other pilots, so nothing in the brief makes this the company's standpoint either. That the claim frames the author's argument does not exempt it: the argument's force depends on the frequency assertion "usually" being so.
- **Case in which the draft fails and the material holds:** every municipal cargo-cycle pilot in the country is run under a national evaluation protocol that requires a published measurement plan before any figure may be released, so pilots typically arrive with a way to test their figures. All supplied statements still hold.
- **Nothing in the material sets this case aside.**
- **Smallest repair:** recast as the comparison the draft actually needs, without the empirical frequency claim: "A figure with no way to test it is easy to produce." The following sentence ("This one arrives with the measurement in place …") then carries the contrast on its own.

### Finding 5 — what other pilot decks would have done, and the operator's slides (draft l. 27)

- **Draft:** "Almost every cargo-cycle pilot deck in circulation would have made that comparison anyway. Declining it costs the operator its most quotable slide …"
- **Material:** no supplied statement about other pilots, about decks in circulation, or about any deck, slide or presentation belonging to this operator. Material D gives figures the operator *supplied* and a claim the operator declined to make.
- **What differs:** the first sentence is a quantified claim about an actual population ("almost every … in circulation"). The second presupposes as fact that this operator has a deck, that it has slides, and that one of them would have been its most quotable — circumstantial detail with no support.
- **Case in which the draft fails and the material holds:** there are four other cargo-cycle pilots in the region, three of which publish route-matched comparisons and one of which publishes none, so "almost every deck in circulation" would not have made the unmatched comparison; and this operator reports through a spreadsheet appendix and has never produced a deck. Every supplied statement holds; both draft sentences fail.
- **Nothing in the material sets either case aside.**
- **Smallest repair:** replace the two clauses with the supported version of the same point: "The comparison was there to be made, and making it would have produced the most quotable line in the pilot. Declining it gives a trade reader something better: …". This keeps the rhetorical shape, drops the population claim and the presupposed deck, and leaves the rest of the sentence untouched.

### Finding 6 — "published" where the material says "supplied" (draft l. 39)

- **Draft:** "operating figures published with their limits attached".
- **Material:** Material D is headed "figures **the operator supplied**", and its text reports what the operator states about them. No supplied sentence says the figures were published.
- **What differs:** scope and audience. Supplying a figure, with its limit, to a programme or a commissioner is not publishing it. The draft's closing recommendation — that an operator copy this practice — turns on publication, so the difference is load-bearing rather than incidental. (The draft's l. 3 "what it publishes" is supported, because Material A's objectives are expressly "published"; only the figures are at issue here.)
- **Case in which the draft fails and the material holds:** the operator reports the 412-delivery count, with the unmatched-routes caveat, in a confidential monthly return to the municipality, and it appears nowhere public. Material D holds exactly as written; "published" fails.
- **Nothing in the material sets this case aside:** Material D's own heading points the other way.
- **Smallest repair:** "and operating figures reported with their limits attached" (or "supplied with their limits attached", matching the material's word).

### Finding 7 — "changes the loading job" (draft l. 17, heading) — low severity

- **Draft:** "The module design changes the loading job, and says where it does not".
- **Material:** Material C says "Two swappable modules **allow** flat, ground-level loading" and, separately, that the design "reduces the number of scheduled maintenance interventions per module". It says what the modules allow; it does not say that loading was different before, or that the design altered it.
- **What differs:** an enabling statement is read as a change statement. Elsewhere the material is explicit when something is unchanged relative to a baseline ("unchanged from the standard frame"), and it draws no such baseline for loading. The contract holds this boundary in headings as in body text.
- **Case in which the draft fails and the material holds:** the standard frame already loaded flat at ground level, and the two swappable modules preserve that while changing only how the load is swapped between rounds. Material C holds as written — the modules do allow flat, ground-level loading — and "the module design changes the loading job" is false.
- **Reverse direction:** a design that changes the loading job also allows flat loading, so the draft's heading is the stronger claim and is not entailed by the material.
- **Smallest repair:** track the annex's own verb — "The module design opens up the loading job, and says where it changes nothing" is still a change claim; use instead "The modules allow flat loading, and the annex says where the design changes nothing". The body text beneath (D3–D5) is already correct and needs no change. Severity is low: the supported reduction in scheduled maintenance interventions is a change the design does make, so the heading is half-covered; only its application to *loading* is unsupported.

## 4. Editorial questions (reported, not counted as defects)

These are passages where the line between a supported reading and an overreach is genuinely unsettled. No repair is proposed; they are raised for the editor rather than filed as findings.

1. **L. 11, "The page is explicit that the link is the company's own — it is offered as Nordvik Logistik's reading, not as a finding."** Material B's first person ("We link …") does make the linking agent explicit, which supports the first clause. What it does not do is say anything about the link's evidential status; a company page can perfectly well report a measured conclusion in the first person. The draft's "not as a finding" is therefore its reading of the first person rather than something the page states. It is defensible as written — the first person is the ordinary way a company marks a claim as its own — and is reported here only so the editor can decide whether to soften it to "the page puts the link in the company's own voice".
2. **L. 13, "What the monitoring has recorded is not in the material."** Accurate about the supplied documents. In a published trade article "the material" has no antecedent for the reader and could be read as "has not been published anywhere", which the material does not establish (and which Finding 1 addresses in its stronger form). An editor may wish to make the referent explicit — "is not in the annex" — which is both supported and unambiguous.
3. **Pilot and programme.** The material speaks of "the FRAKT-N programme" (Material A) and of "the pilot" (Materials C and D) without stating that the two are coextensive. The draft mostly keeps them apart correctly — the programme runs March to November (ll. 3, 37), the pilot has first eight weeks (l. 25) — and the only place the identification is actually asserted is the phrase treated under Finding 3. No separate finding is raised, but an editor asked to add a clarifying gloss should note that the material does not supply one.

## 5. What is confirmed sound

Recorded so that the repairs above are not mistaken for a general verdict.

- **The quotation.** The single quotation (l. 7) is reproduced verbatim from the brief, in the same language, with its hedge ("We believe"), its universal ("Everybody"), its valuation ("simply a better street") and its stance ("we are not neutral") all intact. Nothing is repaired, narrowed, widened, sharpened or added, and the attribution frame is exact. No quotation finding.
- **Every figure and qualification.** 25 %, "include" (not "are"), "kerbside dwell time", "through improved routing and loading", 412, "first eight weeks", "by cycle", "three kerbside points", "scheduled", "per module", "unchanged from the standard frame", "already reachable without tools", "March to November", "planned but has not been written" — all carried with their scope and qualifications.
- **Causal discipline.** The draft nowhere converts the programme's objective into a reported result, nowhere converts the company's link into a demonstrated effect, and nowhere converts the 412-delivery count into a comparison. F3, F4 and E3 are, on the contrary, the draft doing this work explicitly.
- **Standpoint handling.** The commissioning party's position is expressed as theirs throughout and never asserted in the author's own voice; the draft says so in terms (l. 7, l. 11).
- **Evaluative restraint on counts.** 412 is given without "only", "already" or any other characterisation for which the material supplies no comparison, target or capacity.
