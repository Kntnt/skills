# The Nordvik cargo-cycle pilot is worth copying for its method

Nordvik's cargo-cycle pilot has eight weeks of deliveries behind it, and the material around it carries no verdict on cargo cycles against vans. It carries something usable sooner: an objective with a figure and a mechanism in it, a noise claim under continuous kerbside measurement, and a delivery count supplied with its own limit attached.

Start with what the material does not settle. The operator reports 412 deliveries by cycle over the pilot's first eight weeks, and states in the same place that it makes no claim about how that compares with van deliveries over the same period, because the routes were not matched. Nordvik municipality's programme document for FRAKT-N says an evaluation report is planned but has not been written. The case for the pilot therefore cannot rest on results, and it does not need to.

## Restraint about a figure is the first thing to copy

412 is a count. Without matched routes there is nothing to set it against, and the operator says as much itself rather than leaving a reader to supply the comparison.

A figure that arrives with the reason it cannot be compared is a figure the next operator can work with. It fixes what was counted, over what period, and what the operator declines to conclude from it.

## An objective with a number and a mechanism in it

The published objectives of Nordvik municipality's FRAKT-N programme include a 25 per cent reduction in kerbside dwell time through improved routing and loading. Two things are named there: the quantity, and the means by which it is to be reached.

The programme runs from March to November, and its evaluation report is planned rather than written, so nothing in the material reports whether the reduction was achieved. What can be copied now is the drafting rather than the outcome: a target stated precisely enough that missing it would show.

## Noise is being measured, not only asserted

On its company page, Nordvik Logistik AB says its cargo cycles are quiet, and links that low noise to a calmer street environment and to less disturbance for residents at night. The linkage is the company's own, and on a company page it stands as the company's claim.

The pilot's technical annex is a different kind of document. It documents noise modelling and continuous sound-level monitoring at three kerbside points. What the annex establishes is that the quiet claim is being measured, and by what means; it is not a publication of what the measurement found. For a claim that would otherwise rest on marketing copy, that is the difference worth having, and setting up the same measurement is within reach of any operator making the same claim.

## The modules change the loading, and say where they do not

Each cycle carries two swappable cargo modules. The annex sets out what they do: the two modules allow flat, ground-level loading, and the module design reduces the number of scheduled maintenance interventions per module.

That last phrase is the annex's own, and it is worth reading slowly. A reduction per module is not a reduction per cycle, and with two modules on each cycle the annex does not claim the second. It is equally exact about what did not change: service access to the drive unit is unchanged from the standard frame, and was already reachable without tools.

A specification that names the parts its design left alone is one a fleet can plan against.

## A partisan operator can still build a checkable pilot

Nordvik Logistik AB, which commissioned this article, asks that its position be carried as the company's own view:

> Everybody finds delivery vans in a city centre ugly and intrusive. A quiet street is simply a better street. We believe cargo cycles are the right answer for dense districts, and we are not neutral about it.

That is a standpoint, and it is offered as one. The documents around it do not ask to be read on the same terms. The municipality's programme states its objective as a figure and a mechanism. The pilot's annex puts the noise claim under continuous kerbside monitoring at three points. The operator's delivery count arrives with the comparison it cannot support ruled out by the operator itself. A reader who does not share the company's conviction can still check each of those on its own.

That is what to copy, and none of it waits on an evaluation report: state the target so that a miss would show, measure the claim the case depends on, and report the figure with the limit that belongs to it.
