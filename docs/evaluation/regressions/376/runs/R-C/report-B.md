# Source-support comparison — R-C draft against its material

Draft language: `en_GB`. Material source language: English. Because source and target language are the same, no claim in this draft crosses a language boundary; there are therefore **no translation findings** in this comparison, and the sections below are all source-support findings or accounting.

## 2. Findings

Each finding is a defect that must be repaired before delivery.

### Finding 1 — "Residents agree" is an unsupported claim about an actual population

**Draft:** "Residents agree."

**Source support that is missing:** nothing in the material reports any resident view. The sole appearance of residents is Material B, in the company's own voice: "We link their low noise to a calmer street environment and to less disturbance for residents at night." That is the company asserting a link between cycle noise levels and night-time disturbance. It is not a statement that residents hold any opinion, and it is not a statement that any resident has been asked.

**What differs:** whose assertion it is, and the thing asserted. The source gives a company's causal link about *disturbance*; the draft gives a *population's endorsement of a position*. The subject changes (company → residents), the modality changes (a claim the company makes → a fact stated flatly in the draft's own voice), and the content changes (noise disturbance → agreement with the company's view of vans).

**Why this is not the author's own argument on supported material:** "Residents agree" presents a state of affairs about an actual population as given, not as a reflection or a transition. An ordinary reader in this context takes it as reporting what residents think. A claim about an actual population stays a factual claim, and the brief's having established the company's standpoint supports nothing about whether residents share it.

**Both directions tested.** Concrete case compatible with the material: residents of the pilot district are canvassed and are indifferent to delivery vans, or object to the cycles on other grounds (kerb clutter, cycle-lane congestion), while the company's linkage of low cycle noise to less night-time disturbance remains exactly as stated on its company page. In that case Material B holds in full and the draft's sentence fails. Converse: if every resident endorsed the company's position, the draft's sentence would hold while Material B would still say no more than it says — the two are independent. Nothing in the material excludes the first case; no supplied statement reports a resident view at all, so the case stands.

**Smallest supported repair:** delete the sentence. If a resident-facing point is wanted in its place, the supported substitute is the company's own linkage, attributed: "The company links the cycles' low noise to a calmer street environment and to less disturbance for residents at night."

### Finding 2 — "Delivery vans are unpopular wherever they park" is an unattributed universal claim

**Draft:** "Delivery vans are unpopular wherever they park."

**Source support that is missing:** none exists. The nearest material is the commissioner's own starting point, "Everybody finds delivery vans in a city centre ugly and intrusive" — which the draft has *already* used, with attribution, in A2.

**What differs:** whose assertion it is, and the scope. The material's sentence is the company's rhetorical generalisation, carried as the company's view, about vans **in a city centre**. The draft's sentence is asserted in the draft's own voice, with no attribution, and its scope is **wherever they park** — that is, everywhere, city centre or not. Both the attribution and the geographical scope change.

**Both directions tested.** Concrete case compatible with the material: delivery vans parked at an out-of-town depot, an industrial estate or a suburban loading bay attract no objection from anyone, while the company continues to hold and state exactly the city-centre view the brief records. The material holds; the draft's universal claim fails. Converse: the draft's claim could hold in full while the company's view remained merely the company's view — the draft's sentence asserts something the material never puts in anyone's mouth. No supplied statement restricts vans to city centres or reports any measurement of popularity, so the case stands. The sentence is not defensible as a restatement of A2, because it strips the attribution that made A2 admissible and widens the scope past the material's "in a city centre".

**Smallest supported repair:** delete the sentence — its content is already carried, correctly attributed and correctly scoped, in the preceding paragraph. If a second sentence is wanted there, keep the attribution and the scope: "That is the company's starting point, not a neutral one."

### Finding 3 — the objective's stated mechanism is narrowed by a dropped conjunct

**Draft:** "The programme's own objectives put the target at a 25 % reduction in kerbside dwell time through improved routing."

**Source:** Material A: "The FRAKT-N programme's published objectives include a 25 % reduction in kerbside dwell time through improved routing and loading."

**What differs:** scope, specifically the means named in the objective. The material's objective names two mechanisms jointly; the draft names one. A grammatical modifier that applies across both conjuncts in the source is carried over to only one in the draft.

**Both directions tested.** Concrete case compatible with the material: the 25 % reduction is pursued and largely achieved through the loading side — the two swappable modules allowing flat, ground-level loading, as Material C documents — with routing contributing only marginally. In that case Material A's objective is met exactly as published, while the draft's account of what the published objective says is wrong: it attributes the target to a mechanism the programme document does not single out. Converse: if routing alone were the named mechanism, the draft would hold and Material A's quoted text would not. Material A is quoted verbatim and says "routing and loading", so nothing excludes the case; it stands.

**Why this matters beyond wording:** the draft is a persuasive article whose headings are to carry the argument, and loading is precisely where the cargo-cycle design (flat, ground-level loading via two swappable modules) bears on kerbside dwell time. Dropping "and loading" removes the conjunct that connects the programme's target to the pilot's own mechanism.

**Smallest supported repair:** restore the dropped conjunct — "…through improved routing and loading."

### Finding 4 — the 40 % comparison is invented and is excluded by the material

**Draft:** "Over the pilot's first eight weeks, 412 deliveries were made by cycle — 40 % more than the vans managed on the same streets."

**Source:** Material D: "Over the pilot's first eight weeks, 412 deliveries were made by cycle. The operator states that it makes no claim about how this compares with van deliveries over the same period, because the routes were not matched."

**What differs:** everything after the dash. Three distinct defects sit in one clause.

1. **An invented figure.** No 40 % appears anywhere in the material, and no van delivery count is supplied from which any percentage could be computed. A figure attributed to nobody is not thereby the author's inference; there is no arithmetic available that produces it.
2. **A comparison the source expressly declines to make.** The operator "makes no claim about how this compares with van deliveries over the same period". The draft makes exactly that claim, in the draft's own voice, as established fact. This inverts the state of knowledge: what the source marks as unknown and deliberately unclaimed, the draft renders as known and quantified.
3. **A qualification lost that changes the claim.** The reason the operator gives — "because the routes were not matched" — is the very condition that makes a same-streets comparison unavailable. The draft not only drops that qualification, it asserts its negation: "on the same streets" claims the matched-route basis the operator says does not exist.

**Both directions tested.** Concrete case compatible with the material: the cycle routes covered a dense, short-drop core while the van routes over the same eight weeks ran longer, sparser suburban legs, so the vans made, say, 500 deliveries — more, not fewer — and no street-level overlap allows either figure to be set against the other. In that case Material D holds word for word and every part of the draft's clause fails. Converse: for the draft's clause to hold, there would have to be a matched, same-street van baseline of roughly 294 deliveries — which the material not only does not supply but explicitly rules out as a basis. Material D is the only statement bearing on this and it excludes the comparison rather than the case; the case stands. This is the clearest finding in the draft: the supplied statement does not merely fail to support the clause, it forbids it.

**Smallest supported repair:** delete the comparison and carry the operator's exclusion, which is itself supplied material and is what a trade audience needs in order to read the count correctly:

> Over the pilot's first eight weeks, 412 deliveries were made by cycle. The operator makes no claim about how that compares with van deliveries over the same period, because the routes were not matched.

If a comparison is wanted for the argument, the material that would close the gap is a matched-route van delivery count over the same eight weeks. None is supplied.

## 3. Editorial questions, reported rather than treated as defects

These are noted for the commissioning party and are **not** findings; none requires repair before delivery on source-support grounds.

- **The brief's own unevidenced statements.** "Everybody finds delivery vans in a city centre ugly and intrusive" and "A quiet street is simply a better street" are the commissioner's own unevidenced statements, carried as such by the brief and correctly attributed by the draft. They are supported as the company's standpoint. Whether a trade audience will accept an attributed universal ("everybody") as persuasive is an editorial matter for the commissioner, not a source-support defect, and the draft is defensible as written on this point.
- **Length and the material available.** Once Findings 1, 2 and 4 are repaired by deletion, the draft is reduced to the standpoint paragraph, the programme objective and the bare delivery count. No publication limit or target length is supplied, so the contract's rule applies in the direction of delivering the length the material supports. The further material that would close the gap is already in hand and unused: Material C's noise modelling and continuous sound-level monitoring at three kerbside points, the two swappable modules and flat ground-level loading, the reduced number of scheduled maintenance interventions per module, and the unchanged, already tool-free service access to the drive unit. Drawing on these would extend the article without adding a single unsupported claim. Two cautions if that is done: the annex documents that monitoring *took place*, not any result from it, and the drive-unit access is stated as **unchanged**, so it cannot be presented as an improvement.
- **Headings.** The brief asks that headings carry the argument. The draft's only heading is the scaffold "## The draft", which carries no claim and so raises no source-support question here. Whether the finished article's headings meet the brief is a general editorial matter, outside this comparison — but the contract's boundary on causal language holds in headings as it does in the body, so any heading written later must not assert of the pilot what §2 shows the material does not support.
- **Out of scope.** General editorial and mechanical review — style, rhythm, paragraphing, house punctuation — has not been performed and is outside this comparison.

## 4. Completion status

**Complete.** The whole draft was compared against the whole of the supplied material, claim by claim, including the headings and the implications carried by attribution, scope and pronoun reference. Nothing outside the two supplied files was read, and neither file was modified.

**Unresolved findings: 4.** All four are unrepaired in the draft as it stands and must be repaired before delivery.

1. **Finding 1** — "Residents agree." Unsupported claim about an actual population. Repair: delete, or replace with the company's own attributed linkage.
2. **Finding 2** — "Delivery vans are unpopular wherever they park." Unattributed universal claim, wider in scope than the company view it echoes. Repair: delete.
3. **Finding 3** — "through improved routing." Dropped conjunct narrows the published objective's stated mechanism. Repair: restore "and loading".
4. **Finding 4** — "40 % more than the vans managed on the same streets." Invented figure, a comparison the operator expressly declines to make, and the loss of the qualification ("the routes were not matched") that makes the comparison unavailable. Repair: delete the comparison and carry the operator's exclusion.

**Translation findings: none.** Source and draft are both English; no claim crosses a language boundary and the draft contains no translated quotation.

**Findings classified as source-support: 4 (all of the above).**
