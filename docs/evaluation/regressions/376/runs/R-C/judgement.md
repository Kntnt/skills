# Judgement — R-C, reports A and B

Judged against the material alone. Neither report's expected content was known to me.

---

## 1. What each report did with each passage

### Passage 1 — "Everybody finds delivery vans in a city centre ugly and intrusive, the company says, and a quiet street is simply a better street; cargo cycles are, in its view, the right answer"

- **Report A:** recorded it as supported. §§1.2–1.4 give the verdict "supported as the commissioning party's standpoint" for each clause, and §4 lists the whole opening paragraph among the passages "to be preserved unchanged". §4 states expressly that nothing in the draft is raised as an editorial question.
- **Report B:** recorded it as supported (§1.1, rows A2–A4, each "No finding"), **and** separately raised it as the brief's own unevidenced statement — §3's first bullet names "Everybody finds delivery vans in a city centre ugly and intrusive" and "A quiet street is simply a better street" as "the commissioner's own unevidenced statements", noted for the commissioner and explicitly "not findings".

### Passage 2 — "Residents agree. Delivery vans are unpopular wherever they park."

- **Report A:** demanded a repair, twice. §2.1 ("Residents agree.") and §2.2 ("Delivery vans are unpopular wherever they park.") are both findings, each with a repair.
- **Report B:** demanded a repair, twice. Finding 1 and Finding 2, each with a repair.

### Passage 3 — "a 25 % reduction in kerbside dwell time through improved routing"

- **Report A:** demanded a repair. §2.3, on the dropped conjunct "and loading"; the figure, the measured quantity and the target framing are recorded as supported.
- **Report B:** demanded a repair. Finding 3, on the same dropped conjunct; row C1 records the figure, the quantity, the direction and the target status as supported.

### Passage 4 — "412 deliveries were made by cycle — 40 % more than the vans managed on the same streets"

- **Report A:** demanded a repair on the comparison (§2.4), and recorded the bare count as supported (§1.8).
- **Report B:** demanded a repair on the comparison (Finding 4), and recorded the bare count as supported (row D1).

## 2. What the material warrants, and whether each report agrees

### Passage 1 — supported as the company's own view; no external evidence owed

The brief carries this under "Commissioner's own starting points", states that the company "states its own position as follows", and "asks that it be carried as the company's view". The draft carries every clause inside an attribution — "the company says" governs the first two clauses, "in its view" the third. Nothing here is asserted in the draft's own voice. A party's own declared position, marked as that party's, is expressible on the brief's authority alone; "everybody" inside a marked standpoint is the company's rhetoric, not the draft's claim about a population. Demanding evidence for it would be demanding evidence that the company's view is true, which is not what the draft says. The scope the brief supplies ("in a city centre", "dense districts") is preserved exactly.

- **Report A agrees.** It reaches the same verdict for the same reason, and states the principle plainly: "Nothing external is needed for a party's own declared non-neutrality to be reported as that party's."
- **Report B agrees**, and its §3 bullet is a correct further move rather than a disagreement: it flags the persuasive weight of an attributed "everybody" for a trade audience as a matter for the commissioner, while stating that the draft "is defensible as written on this point". That is an editorial note about effect, not a demand for evidence.

### Passage 2 — unsupported; both sentences need repair

Nothing in Materials A–D reports any view held by residents. The only occurrence of the word is Material B's company-page line, "We link their low noise to a calmer street environment and to less disturbance for residents at night" — the company asserting a link about noise and night-time disturbance. It attributes no opinion to residents, records no canvass, and says nothing about vans. "Residents agree" is a new sentence in the draft's own voice asserting what an actual population thinks, and its function is to corroborate the company's stance from outside; the attribution in the preceding paragraph does not reach it.

"Delivery vans are unpopular wherever they park" fails twice: the attribution that made the same sentiment admissible in the standpoint paragraph is gone, and the scope is widened from the brief's "in a city centre" to everywhere. Both defects are repairs, not questions. Deletion is the smallest repair for both; the second sentence is in any case redundant with the attributed paragraph above it.

- **Both reports agree**, on both sentences and on both grounds, and both reach deletion as the smallest repair. Report A's §2.2 and Report B's Finding 2 each separate the attribution defect from the scope defect, which is the right division.

### Passage 3 — repair: restore the dropped conjunct

Material A reads "through improved routing **and loading**". The draft carries one conjunct and drops the other. Both conjuncts modify the same target, so dropping one misdescribes what the published objective says the reduction is to come from, and the reader cannot recover the missing means. Loading is not incidental here: Material C documents flat, ground-level loading via two swappable modules, so the dropped conjunct is the one that connects the programme's target to the pilot's own mechanism. The figure, the measured quantity and the target framing ("put the target at", which keeps it an objective rather than an achieved result) are all sound and should be left alone.

- **Both reports agree**, and both propose the minimal repair — restoring "and loading" — while preserving the target framing. Report B additionally considers the source's "include" against the draft's "put the target at" and judges the shift defensible as naming one objective rather than claiming it is the only one; I agree, and Report A's silence on that narrower point costs it nothing.

### Passage 4 — repair: delete the comparison

The material does not merely omit a van comparison — Material D records the operator declining to make one, and gives the reason: "because the routes were not matched." No van delivery count appears anywhere, so the 40 % has no arithmetic basis of any kind. "On the same streets" asserts the matched-route basis the material says did not hold, so the clause contradicts a supplied statement rather than merely outrunning it. What a source marks as deliberately unclaimed is not thereby established in either direction. The bare count and its eight-week window are verbatim from Material D and are supported. A hedged rewrite would not fix it, since the comparison itself is what is unsupported.

- **Both reports agree**, both identify all three defects in the clause (the invented figure, the declined comparison, the negated qualification), both keep the bare count as supported, and both recommend deleting the clause and carrying the operator's own qualification across. Report A adds, correctly, that a hedged substitute such as "suggests that cycles outperformed vans" would be equally unsupported.

---

## 3. The three closing questions

- **Did either report demand that the draft's account of the commissioning party's own stated position be supported by evidence outside the brief?** No. Neither did. Both recorded the whole attributed standpoint block as supported, and both stated the reason — that the brief carries it as the company's view and the draft marks it as such. Report B's §3 note about how a trade audience will read an attributed "everybody" is raised as an editorial matter for the commissioner and expressly not as a finding, and it says the draft is defensible as written.
- **Did either report treat a scattering of individual statements as establishing a claim about a whole population?** No. Neither did. Both went the other way: each identified "Residents agree" as an unsupported claim about an actual population, and each showed that Material B's single company-voiced line about night-time disturbance attributes no opinion to residents and cannot establish one. Both also caught the population claim smuggled into "unpopular wherever they park" once the attribution was stripped.
- **Did either report miss a factual claim the material does not carry?** No. Neither did. Between them the draft carries four such claims — residents' agreement, vans' unpopularity wherever they park, routing as the objective's sole named means, and the 40 %/same-streets comparison — and both reports caught all four, with the same repairs. Comparing each report's accounting against the draft clause by clause, I found no further claim in the draft that the material does not carry and that both reports passed over.

---

## 4. Overall

Both reports reach the same four findings, with the same repairs, and both keep the standpoint block, the 25 % figure with its target framing, and the bare 412-delivery count as supported. I agree with every one of those calls. The reports differ only in presentation and in one addition: Report B separates an editorial observation about the attributed "everybody" from its findings, which is a legitimate and useful distinction and not a disagreement with Report A, whose §4 simply records that it is leaving no editorial question open. Neither report demanded evidence for the commissioner's own position, neither built a population claim out of scattered statements, and neither missed an unsupported factual claim.
