## 1. Headline: claim, speaker, scope and support

The headline is **“Gemensam ärendebild hjälper, säger Svale om loggförsöket”**. It says that a shared view of cases helps, attributes that appraisal to Svale, and confines the subject to the log trial. It does not claim that the software caused the measured reduction in assignment time or that the benefit applies to every customer.

The body supports the benefit, at the headline’s affirmative strength, as the customer’s assessment of this experience:

> – Jag skulle välja att göra försöket igen. Att ha en gemensam bild av ärendena hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga till en vecka för förberedelser, säger Lind.

The attribution is unsupported. Lind is introduced as **“Maya Lind, arbetsledare på Elm Quay”**; Svale is introduced separately as **“Leverantören Svale”**. The final disclosure, **“Kundcaset publiceras av leverantören Svale”**, establishes publication, not that Svale supplied Lind’s appraisal. The headline changes whose judgement the reader receives.

This conflicts with the base contract’s **“Every claim is supported by the text or its material, with exact attribution and proportionate strength”**, and Headlines’ **“Every figure, name, claim and conclusion in it is in the text, at the text’s own strength”**. The case-study contract also says **“The appraisal must actually be the customer’s”**. The body satisfies that requirement; the headline misattributes its customer appraisal.

What is absent is support for Svale as the speaker of this judgement. The text does not establish that Svale never expressed it. That distinction does not rescue the attribution actually printed.

It then bounds the undertaking and the account:

Leverantören Svale konfigurerade loggen efter dem och utbildade sex medarbetare.”** Lind adds **“Vi lade mer tid på att enas om kategorierna än på att lägga in de första ärendena”** and says she would reserve that time before another house starts.

A more precise or differently angled opening could also work; no concrete reader loss licenses changing this one.

Read after the standfirst, the lead advances by introducing the aim of seeing the same information across shifts. Repeating **“åtta veckor”** and **“två hus”** gives the body its independent setting; it does not make the lead merely the standfirst again.
