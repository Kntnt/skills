# School temperature readings leave causes unresolved

For municipal property managers, Björkskolan’s four-week trial offers a practical lesson in reading measurements: check what was counted, where sensors stood, and what the trial left unmeasured before using the results to guide heating decisions.

By the user

In January 2026, Lervik’s fictional property office installed temperature sensors in six classrooms at Björkskolan. It wanted to find out whether complaints about cold air coincided with low temperatures during lessons. Understanding the results starts with the distinction between a recorded temperature and what that reading can establish.

## A lesson count leaves duration unknown

The report *Mätförsök i Björkskolan*, dated March 12, 2026, records 14 of 120 lesson periods with at least one reading below 20°C. Values were recorded every five minutes over four weeks. The report does not say how long temperatures stayed below that threshold or whether students felt cold.

The 20°C threshold was the office’s own working limit for the trial, rather than a statement of a legal requirement. A whole-day average can conceal temperature changes during lessons, so the report presents lesson periods instead of a single daily average. Read the 14-period count as a count of periods containing a reading below the threshold, rather than a measure of time spent below it.

## Air readings omit part of the thermal picture

A temperature sensor measures the temperature where it sits. Two sensors stood near exterior walls and four on interior walls. Their locations were documented, but the trial did not experimentally compare those placements.

Air temperature is also different from operative temperature, a measure that accounts for both air temperature and heat radiation from surrounding surfaces. The trial measured only air temperature. Ventilation, drafts, and perceived temperature were not measured, and the results cover only these six rooms and these four weeks.

## November plans await a funding decision

After the trial, operations technician Elin Rask reviewed the ventilation and heating schedules with the school’s caretaker. The effects of any adjustments have not yet been measured.

“We know when we need to take a closer look. We don’t yet know why it got cold right then,” Rask said. She recommends linking temperature records to room-use times before changing the controls. A further trial is planned for November, with sensors placed the same way and notes on when rooms are used; funding has not been decided.

## Use the trial to guide further investigation

Before changing heating controls, follow Rask’s recommendation: connect the temperature readings to the times rooms are used. Keep the trial’s limits alongside its lesson count. It identifies periods to examine more closely; it does not establish the cause of the cold or show whether adjustments have worked.
