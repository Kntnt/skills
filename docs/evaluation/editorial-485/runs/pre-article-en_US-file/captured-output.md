# Classroom readings need context before heating changes

A four-week trial at fictional Björkskolan gives municipal property managers a practical lesson in interpreting classroom measurements. The records identify lesson periods with readings below a chosen threshold, but leave unanswered how long those temperatures lasted, what caused them, and whether students felt cold.

By the user

In January 2026, Lervik’s fictional municipal property office placed temperature sensors in six classrooms at Björkskolan. It wanted to find out whether complaints about cold air coincided with low temperatures during lessons. Readings were recorded every five minutes for four weeks. The trial shows how to read a temperature record without treating it as a diagnosis of the heating system.

## A lesson count leaves duration unanswered

The report, *Mätförsök i Björkskolan*, dated March 12, 2026, records at least one reading below 20 degrees Celsius in 14 of 120 recorded lesson periods. It does not say how long temperatures stayed below that threshold. The count therefore cannot tell a property manager whether a dip was brief or persisted through a lesson.

A whole-day average can hide variation during lessons, so the report presents lesson periods instead. The office chose 20 degrees Celsius as its working threshold for this trial; it was not presented as a legal requirement.

## Air temperature is only part of the picture

A temperature sensor measures the temperature where it sits. Two sensors were near exterior walls, and four were on interior walls. Their positions were documented but were not compared experimentally, so the trial does not establish the effect of placement.

Operative temperature is a measure that accounts for both air temperature and heat radiating from surrounding surfaces. This trial measured air temperature alone. It did not measure ventilation, drafts, or perceived temperature, and the report does not say whether students felt cold.

The records cover only these six rooms and these four weeks. They cannot establish conditions throughout the school or at other times.

## The next trial awaits a funding decision

After the trial, operations technician Elin Rask went through the ventilation and heating schedules with the school’s caretaker. Rask said: “We know when we need to take a closer look. We don’t yet know why it got cold just then.”

The effects of any adjustments have not yet been measured. Another trial is planned for November, with sensors placed the same way and notes on when rooms are used. Funding has not been decided.

## Keep the trial’s boundaries in view

Before changing the controls, follow Rask’s recommendation to match temperature records with room-use times. Use the trial to identify when closer investigation is needed, while keeping its unanswered questions about duration, causes, and students’ experience open.
