## 1. Differences

The frontmatter, byline, paragraph breaks and all other formatting are identical. There are three differences, on two lines.

1. Headline, subject. Before: `# Mallen har en ruta för allt utom poängen`. After: `# Bibliotekets mötesmall har rutor för allt utom poängen`. The change is "Mallen" → "Bibliotekets mötesmall". Class: **a change to what a claim says, by scope.** Read on its own, the headline's "Mallen" can be taken as templates in general. The new headline limits it to one document, the library's meeting template. This narrows the claim and does not raise it, and the body supports it ("Jag granskar bibliotekets mötesmall"). The motive was taste, or the anatomy guideline, and not a visible defect. A definite noun that the first paragraph then explains is a normal device for a column headline.
2. Headline, object. Before: `har en ruta för allt`. After: `har rutor för allt`. Class: **change of taste.** Grammar does not require it, because "Bibliotekets mötesmall har en ruta för allt" is correct Swedish, so it does not follow from difference 1. The literal meaning stays the same. It does lose the singular "en ruta", which the column's last words pick up again ("tvivlet på ännu en ruta").
3. Paragraph 7. Before: `utöka formuläret — bibliotekarien`. After: `utöka formuläret – bibliotekarien`. Class: **mechanical correction (a locale form).** The em dash becomes the spaced en dash that Swedish uses. The spaces were already there in the input.
