## 1. Differences

1. **Headline subject.** Before: `# Mallen har en ruta för allt utom poängen`. After: `# Bibliotekets mötesmall har rutor för allt utom poängen`. The change is `Mallen` → `Bibliotekets mötesmall`. **Class: repair of a visible defect.** The definite form *Mallen* has nothing to refer back to when the headline is read on its own, so a reader cannot tell which template is meant. The new subject takes its words from the first body sentence (*Jag granskar bibliotekets mötesmall*), so no claim gains scope, attribution or meaning. It is a borderline call, because a column may use a bare definite headline on purpose, but the defect can be seen from the input alone.
2. **Headline object number.** Before: `har en ruta för allt`. After: `har rutor för allt`. **Class: change of taste.** It does not depend on difference 1, because *Bibliotekets mötesmall har en ruta för allt utom poängen* is grammatical. The idiom *en ruta för allt* (a box for every item) and *rutor för allt* mean the same thing, and the plural matches the body's list of boxes. No claim changes in scope, certainty, attribution, chronology, causality or meaning, but the singular idiom's slight wit is lost.
3. **Dash, paragraph 6.** Before: `utöka formuläret — bibliotekarien`. After: `utöka formuläret – bibliotekarien`. **Class: mechanical correction (locale form).** The spaced em dash becomes the spaced en dash that Swedish typography uses.

Total: 3 differences, none of them a change to what a claim says.
