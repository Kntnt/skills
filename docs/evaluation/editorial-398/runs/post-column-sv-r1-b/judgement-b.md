## 1. Differences

Three differences. Two are in the headline, one is in the body.

1. **Headline, subject.** Before: `# Mallen har en ruta för allt utom poängen`. After: `# Bibliotekets mötesmall har rutor för allt utom poängen`. The subject *Mallen* becomes *Bibliotekets mötesmall*. Class: **repair of a visible defect**, though a contestable one. From the input alone you can see that *Mallen* has no antecedent in the headline, and that the body's first sentence ("Jag granskar bibliotekets mötesmall") identifies it. The new subject names the same referent the body already names. So the claim's scope, certainty, attribution, chronology, causality and meaning stay as they were: it is still the template, not a meeting, that lacks the box. You could also read the unexplained definite form as a deliberate columnist's hook, and on that reading this is a **change of taste**.
2. **Headline, number.** Before: `har en ruta för allt`. After: `har rutor för allt`. Singular *a box for everything* becomes plural *boxes for everything*. Class: **change of taste**. It does not follow from difference 1, because *Bibliotekets mötesmall har en ruta för allt* is grammatical. The distributive meaning, one box for each thing, is the same in both forms, so this is not a change to what a claim says.
3. **Body, paragraph 6.** Before: `utöka formuläret — bibliotekarien`. After: `utöka formuläret – bibliotekarien`. The em dash becomes an en dash, and the spaces on both sides were already there in the input. Class: **mechanical correction** (a Swedish locale form).

Frontmatter: no difference. Formatting (heading level, paragraphing, byline line): no difference.
