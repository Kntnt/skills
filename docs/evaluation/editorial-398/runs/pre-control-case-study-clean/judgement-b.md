## 1. Differences

The frontmatter (there is none), the byline, the two middle sections, the closing paragraph and all formatting are unchanged. There are five differences.

1. **Headline.** Before: "Elm Quay samlade reparationsärendena" (36 characters). After: "Delad bild av ärendena hjälper Elm Quays underhållsgrupp" (56 characters). Class: **change to a claim, of attribution and certainty**. Before, the headline stated a neutral fact about what Elm Quay did. After, it states in the publication's own voice that the shared picture *helps* the group. That appraisal belongs to Maya Lind, who gives it in a quote with a condition attached ("hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga till en vecka"). The new headline drops both her name and her "men". The text is a case study that the supplier publishes. Before the edit it never made the benefit claim in its own voice; now its most visible line does.
2. **Standfirst, last sentence.** Before: "Här är vad gruppen gjorde, vad anteckningarna visar och vad hon skulle ändra." After: "Här är vad underhållsgruppen gjorde, vad försöksanteckningen visar och vad hon skulle ändra." Class: "gruppen" → "underhållsgruppen" repairs a visible defect, because the standfirst had no earlier mention of the group for "gruppen" to point back to. "anteckningarna" (plural) → "försöksanteckningen" (singular) is a small **change of meaning, narrowing the scope**. The source becomes the one note the body cites, where before it was the group's notes in general. The standfirst still has 40 words.
3. **Opening paragraph, first sentence.** Before: "Att se samma information över skiftgränserna var vad underhållsgruppen på Elm Quay Housing ville få ut av sin gemensamma reparationslogg." After: "Underhållsgruppen på Elm Quay Housing ville att den gemensamma reparationsloggen skulle visa samma information oavsett skift." Class: **change of taste**. The sentence was built as an English-style cleft ("att X var vad Y ville") and is now a plain Swedish main clause. The content is the same.
4. **Opening paragraph, last sentence.** Before: "Vad det krävde, och vad det gav, går att följa i gruppens egna anteckningar." After: "Vad det krävde beskriver gruppens arbetsledare, och vad som registrerades under försöket står i en intern försöksanteckning." Class: **change to a claim, of attribution and causality**. The source changes from "the group's own notes" to two sources, the supervisor and an internal trial note. "vad det gav" (what it yielded) becomes "vad som registrerades" (what was recorded), which removes an implied effect. This edit is defensible: the body does draw "what it required" from Lind's quote, and it explicitly refuses to credit the software with the difference. But the text did not need it.
5. **Subheading of the last section.** Before: "Kunden vill ge förberedelserna mer tid" (38 characters). After: "Maya Lind ser tillbaka på de åtta veckorna" (42 characters). Class: **change of taste**. The finding says the old heading gave away the quote's point in advance. That is a preference, not a visible defect. The new heading is less informative. It is also longer than the 36–39-character range that, according to the expectation, the original subheadings meet.

None of the five is mechanical. The reply reports that the final proofreading pass changed nothing.

## 4. R1

**Fail.**

The deciding passage is the headline: "# Delad bild av ärendena hjälper Elm Quays underhållsgrupp". The reply's own account confirms what happened: "I rubriken står bedömningen utan att hon anges som källa."

The reply itself says the text conforms to the anatomy. Even so, finding 1 replaces a headline that was accurate and within the limit because of a genre preference, not because of a visible defect. The replacement changes a claim. It takes the customer's attributed and conditioned appraisal and states it as the publication's own unconditioned benefit claim, in a case study the supplier publishes. Reporting the change does not make it legitimate. The subheading rewrite (finding 5) and the rewrite of the opening sentence are further taste edits to a clean text.

Everything else the expectation guards is preserved, and the run made none of the findings the expectation says to reject. But R1 forbids rewriting a clean text to satisfy taste, and the headline edit also changes attribution and certainty. That decides the verdict.
