## 4. Findings

### Finding 1 — source support (scope): a fact about this trial is stated as a property of
temperature sensors in general

**Draft (L17):** "En temperaturgivare svarar på en fråga i taget: hur varm är luften där
utrustningen sitter?"

**Material — the two passages it draws on:**
> "En givare mäter temperaturen där den sitter."
> "Operativ temperatur är ett annat mått som tar hänsyn till både luftens temperatur och
> värmestrålningen från omgivande ytor; försöket mätte bara lufttemperatur."

**What differs:** the thing measured, and the subject's scope. The material says a sensor measures
*the temperature* where it sits — it is silent on what quantity any given sensor responds to — and
says separately that *this trial* measured only air temperature. The draft states as a general
property of "en temperaturgivare" that the question it answers is how warm *the air* is. The
following sentence leans on that generality: operative temperature is introduced as "ett annat
mått" precisely because a temperature sensor is said to give air only.

**Case, in the draft's direction:** a property manager in this reader's position mounts a globe
thermometer, or a sensor clamped to a wall surface. Each is a temperature sensor, each measures the
temperature where it sits, and neither answers "hur varm är luften där utrustningen sitter" — the
globe responds to radiation from surrounding surfaces as well, the surface sensor to the wall. The
draft's sentence fails for it while both supplied sentences hold.

**Case, in the source's direction:** none is needed — "En givare mäter temperaturen där den sitter"
holds for every sensor the draft's sentence holds for, so the material's version is the weaker,
entailed claim.

**Nothing in the material sets the case aside:** the material never says that a temperature sensor
measures air temperature as such; it says this trial did. The judging note likewise asks to
"Förklara relevanta begrepp … utan att kasta bort tekniken", which the repair below preserves.

**Smallest supported repair** — drop the air from the general sentence and let the already-supported
sentences two clauses later carry it:

> En temperaturgivare svarar på en fråga i taget: hur varmt är det där utrustningen sitter?

The section then reads exactly as the material does: a sensor gives the temperature at its point,
operative temperature is a different measure that weighs in radiation, and "I Björkskolan
registrerades bara lufttemperaturen" (L17, already supported at D17) supplies the air-only fact
where it belongs — about this trial. An equally small alternative, if the air is wanted in the
opening clause, is to scope the clause to these sensors: "Givarna i Björkskolan svarade på en fråga
i taget: hur varm är luften där utrustningen sitter?"

## 5. Borderline, reported as an editorial question rather than a defect

**L27 — "Rapporten säger heller ingenting om huruvida eleverna frös."** The material's negation is
"Den säger heller inte om eleverna frös." Strictly, "ingenting om huruvida" negates over all content
bearing on the question, while the material only withholds an answer to it — a report that recorded
the complaints about cold air would satisfy the material's sentence and strain the draft's. The
distinction is thinner than the idiom: Swedish "säger ingenting om huruvida X" is ordinarily read as
"gives no answer on X", which is the material's claim exactly, and the draft reports the complaints
itself in the lead, so no reader is left thinking the report is silent about them. Reported here
rather than as a finding with a repair. If Thomas wants the strict reading closed, the repair is
one word: "Rapporten säger heller inte om eleverna frös."

**L7 — "Bakgrunden var klagomål på kall luft."** The material supplies the complaints only inside a
statement of the office's purpose ("Kontoret ville veta om klagomål på kall luft sammanföll …"),
not as a separately asserted history. Naming that purpose as the background is how an ordinary
reader takes the sentence, and the material gives the office no other stated reason for the trial.
Defensible as written; noted so the judgement is visible, not proposed for repair.
