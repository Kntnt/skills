## 1. Differences

The returned text differs from `work/input.md` on two lines only. No frontmatter exists in either version. Headline, standfirst, byline, body paragraphs, quotations, numbers, the publication disclosure and the link are byte-identical.

1. Subheading of section 2.
   - Before: `## Två perioder med olika arbetsbelastning` (39 characters)
   - After: `## Mediantiden sjönk till två arbetsdagar, men belastningen skilde sig` (67 characters)
   - Class: **a change of taste**. The original subheading is not defective: it names the section's comparison and its caveat accurately. The rewrite also shifts the heading's **meaning** (emphasis). It now leads with the result ("sjönk till två arbetsdagar") and moves the caveat into a subordinate "men". The body supports the fall from three to two days, and the heading makes no causal claim, so no fact is wrong. But the carefully qualified appraisal now reaches a skimming reader result-first rather than caveat-first. The new heading is also 67 characters, far outside the 36–39-character band that the original subheadings share.

2. Subheading of section 3.
   - Before: `## Kunden vill ge förberedelserna mer tid` (38 characters)
   - After: `## Maya Lind vill ge förberedelserna mer tid` (41 characters)
   - Class: a change to what a claim says, in **attribution**, and with it **scope**. The wish moves from the customer organisation to one named person. The change is defensible: the quotation under the heading is Lind's first-person view, and the standfirst also frames it as hers. But this is not a visible defect in a supplier-published case study. There the customer's spokesperson normally speaks for the customer, and the text says what "kunden" is ("Kundcaset publiceras av leverantören Svale"). This is a taste-level re-attribution.

The Skill used this finding to replace a working, accurate subheading on a clean text with "Mediantiden sjönk till två arbetsdagar, men belastningen skilde sig".
