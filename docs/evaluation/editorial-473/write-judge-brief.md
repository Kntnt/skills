# Write judge brief

You judge one run of a writing Skill that turned a brief and its material into a first draft of a press release. The material is `work/source.md`. The Skill's whole reply is `response.md`, and the draft alone, as the reply carries it, is `delivered.md`. Read exactly those three files, all in the run directory named in the message that sent you here. Read nothing else — no other run, no repository file, no ticket. You are not told how the Skill or the run was configured, which version of it ran, or which model produced the reply, and you must not guess or try to find out. Do not count characters or words: lengths are measured separately and are not yours to judge.

## The standard

A press release meets its reader, an editor or a journalist, in this order: a headline; a summary, which is the first paragraph after the headline, names the organisation sending the release and tells the news in full; a first quotation directly after the summary, short, commenting on the news and taking a view; a body of one or two short paragraphs that complement and deepen the summary and that a journalist could lift straight into their own text; a second quotation after the body, carrying one of the sender's most important messages and no new fact or figure; background; the links and attachments that go with the release; the contact details; and last a short, neutral description of the organisation. A time from which the release may be published, where the material gives one, stands on its own line above the headline. Where the material carries one quotation, the release carries that one, directly after the summary. Where the material carries none, the release carries none, and the reply says that the material gave no quotation. Where the material carries two, each stands in its place. A quotation is only ever one the material has. The contact carries a name, a title, a telephone number and an email address as far as the material gives them, and nothing the material does not give. No part needs a heading or a label to be present.

## What to write

Write `judgement.md` in your own directory, which is the run directory named in the message. Answer every heading.

**1. The parts.** List the parts of `delivered.md` in the order they stand, naming each as one of: publication time, headline, summary, first quotation, body, second quotation, background, links and attachments, contact, description of the organisation, or other (say what). Quote the first words of each.

**2. P1, the order.** Pass or fail: do the parts stand in the order the standard gives, with every part the material supplies present and a publication time, where the material gives one, above the headline? Name the passage that decides it.

**3. Q1, quotations only from the material.** Pass or fail: is every quotation in `delivered.md` one the material carries, attributed as the material attributes it? Where the material carries no quotation, does the reply say so? Quote what decides it.

**4. Q2, where the quotations stand.** Pass or fail, or `not applicable` where the draft carries no quotation: does each quotation stand where the standard puts it for the number the material carries?

**5. Q3, the second quotation.** Pass, fail, or `not applicable` where the draft carries fewer than two: does the second quotation carry a message rather than a fact or a figure the release does not give elsewhere?

**6. B1, the body.** Pass or fail: is the body one or two short paragraphs, and could a journalist lift them as they stand?

**7. K1, the contact.** Pass or fail: does the contact carry every one of name, title, telephone number and email address that the material gives, and nothing it does not give?

**8. F1, support.** Pass or fail: is every claim, number, attribution, certainty, scope, chronology and causal link in `delivered.md` carried by the material, with any caveat the material states kept? A fail names which of these it is and quotes the passage.

Then reply in at most 120 words: the eight verdicts, and the path you wrote.
