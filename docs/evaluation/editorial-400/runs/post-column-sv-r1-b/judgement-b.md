## 1. Differences

Two differences.

1. **Headline.** Before: `# Mallen har en ruta för allt utom poängen`. After: `# Mötesmallen har en ruta för allt utom poängen`. Class: **a change to what a claim says, by scope**. The input headline makes its claim about "Mallen", a template it does not name; the byline and the first sentence ("bibliotekets mötesmall") then settle which one. The returned headline names the kind of template. Read specifically, it now says what the body already says. Read generically, "Mötesmallen" covers a narrower category than "Mallen". Neither reading raises what the text asserts, so this is not hardening. The Skill's stated reason for the change (the headline alone does not say the text is about meetings) is a headline and keyword norm from its anatomy, not a mechanical error. I therefore treat the motive as close to taste, and discuss it under R1.
2. **Dash in paragraph 7.** Before: `utöka formuläret — bibliotekarien` (em dash with spaces). After: `utöka formuläret – bibliotekarien` (en dash with spaces). Class: **mechanical correction, locale form**. In Swedish the spaced en dash is the standard tankstreck. This difference does not follow from the headline change.

No other differences: no word, sentence, punctuation mark or frontmatter key changed apart from these two.

The deciding passage is the body. Paragraphs 1–8 of the returned text match the input word for word, apart from the dash in "utöka formuläret – bibliotekarien". The input headline is not ambiguous in context, since the byline and first sentence settle the referent at once. Under a stricter reading of "clean texts may not be rewritten to satisfy taste", that change could fail the run. I rule it a pass for three reasons:

- The headline's lack of a stated subject can be seen in the input alone.
- The fix uses the body's own word ("bibliotekets mötesmall") and adds no claim the body does not make.
- The change is disclosed as a changed claim.

The reply makes no claim to have checked anything against an unavailable source.
