# Judgement b

I compared `work/input.md` with the fenced text in `response.md` line by line. Frontmatter, byline and the other five paragraphs are byte-identical. There are two differences.

## 1. Differences

1. **Headline (line 8).** Before: `# Rutan som inte finns`. After: `# Bibliotekets mötesmall frågar efter tid men inte syfte`. Class: **a change to what a claim says, by meaning, and by scope too.** The old headline names the motif, a box that does not exist. In the body, that box is one for *the decision* ("det beslut vi ska kunna fatta"). The new headline makes a different, broader assertion: the template asks about time but not about *purpose* ("syfte"). The word "syfte" does not appear in the input. The Skill defends it with "Vad tiden ska leda till frågar den inte om", and that sentence is close. Still, "decision" has become "purpose", so both what the claim says and how far it reaches have changed. The change also loses the "ruta" motif that the last sentence answers ("tvivlet på ännu en ruta"). That loss is voice, not a claim. Nothing else follows from this difference.
2. **Paragraph 3, dash (line 16).** Before: `något annat — att tid`. After: `något annat – att tid`. The spaced em dash became a spaced en dash. Class: **a mechanical correction, a locale form.** Swedish uses the spaced en dash. It is independent of difference 1.

Of the two differences, one changes what a claim says.

1. **Headline:** reported, and accurately. Finding 1: "Förut stod det ”Rutan som inte finns”. … Nu står det ”Bibliotekets mötesmall frågar efter tid men inte syfte” (54 tecken, 8 ord)." I checked the count: 54 characters and 8 words are correct. The reply also files the headline under claims: "**Ändrat påstående (rubriken, reparationen av anmärkning 1):** … Ordet ”syfte” är rubrikens eget. Första stycket talar om en saknad ruta för *beslut*, men texten stöder det bredare ordet …". So the reply itself admits the word is new and the scope is broader. That is accurate. What I dispute is the finding's reason for the change, not the report of it (see section 4).
2. **Dash:** reported, and accurately in substance. "Den avslutande språkgranskningen bytte ett engelskt långt tankstreck mot ett svenskt kort tankstreck med mellanslag." One small imprecision: the input already had spaces around the dash, so "med mellanslag" describes the result, not something the Skill added.

The reply also says "Brödtexten, bylinen och metadata är i övrigt oförändrade" and "Förutom rubriken ovan har inget påstående tagits bort, ändrats eller lagts till". Both are true.

## 4. R1

**Fail.**

The body is left intact, and the only mechanical defect, the em dash, is fixed correctly. The deciding passage is finding 1 and the headline rewrite it carries:

> "Den rubriken gick inte att förstå utan att läsa texten. Den sa inte vilken ruta det gällde eller i vilket sammanhang, och den angav inte krönikans vinkel. Den upprepade också första styckets formulering (”Någon ruta … finns inte”)."

"Rutan som inte finns" is not a concrete visible defect in a column. An allusive headline that the first paragraph unpacks is ordinary for the genre. The echo of the first paragraph is deliberate: it sets up the motif, and "ännu en ruta" in the last sentence closes it. The Skill measures the headline against an article-anatomy requirement: the headline must be understandable on its own and must state the angle. The same anatomy produces unresolved findings 2 and 3 (lead paragraph, subheads) and the verdict "Texten följer alltså inte artikelanatomin". So a clean text was rewritten to satisfy a guideline.

R1 forbids exactly that ("Clean texts may not be rewritten to satisfy taste or numerical guidelines"). The rewrite spent the correction budget on a change of meaning and scope, from *decision* to *purpose*, and it cost the text's own motif.

The reply accounts for the change honestly, and nothing outside the headline changed. That limits the damage, but the change itself still breaks R1.
