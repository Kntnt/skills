## 1. Differences

Three differences, all in two lines.

1. **Headline, subject.** Before: `# Mallen har en ruta för allt utom poängen`. After: `# Vår mötesmall har …`. `Mallen` becomes `Vår mötesmall`.
   Class: the repair of a visible defect, and also a **change to what a claim says** by **attribution** and **meaning**. In the input, the definite `Mallen` has nothing before it to refer to, and the headline does not name the subject (meetings). Only the body shows that. The new headline says which template is meant (`mötes-`) and whose it is (`vår`). Both come from the body: `bibliotekets mötesmall` in paragraph 1, and `våra möten` and `vi` throughout. The scope stays the same: it is still one template, the one the body examines. No claim rises above the limits the body states (see section 3).
2. **Headline, number.** Before: `har en ruta för allt`. After: `har rutor för allt`.
   Class: a **change of taste**. The singular-to-plural change does not follow grammatically from difference 1, because `Vår mötesmall har en ruta för allt utom poängen` is correct Swedish. What the headline says about the template is effectively the same: every item has a box, except the point. So I do not count this as a change to what a claim says. One thing is lost: the singular `en ruta` echoes `ytterligare en ruta` and `ännu en ruta` in the last two paragraphs.
3. **Paragraph 6, dash.** Before: `utöka formuläret — bibliotekarien`. After: `utöka formuläret – bibliotekarien`. The em dash (U+2014) becomes an en dash (U+2013). The spaces on both sides were there in the input and are unchanged.
   Class: a **mechanical correction** (a locale form: Swedish uses a spaced en dash as its dash).

No other differences. The frontmatter (`genre: column`, `technique: none`, `language: sv`), the byline, the paragraph breaks and the Markdown formatting are unchanged.

## 4. R1

**Pass.**

What decides it is the headline, which is the only change beyond a mechanical correction: `Mallen har en ruta för allt utom poängen` → `Vår mötesmall har rutor för allt utom poängen`. The defect can be seen from the input alone: the headline uses a definite noun with nothing to refer to, and it never names the subject. The repair draws only on the body's own words (`bibliotekets mötesmall`, `våra möten`). The reply reports it as a changed claim. Every argument, quotation, limiting sentence and body claim comes through word for word.

The Skill also showed restraint. It left the missing lead, subheadings and reader call to action unwritten, and returned them as findings, rather than rewriting a column to fit an article anatomy or word counts.

The weak point is the plural `rutor`. It is an unforced change of taste, it loses the headline's echo of `ännu en ruta`, and the reply describes it as part of the repair. It changes no claim and the reply discloses it, so it does not tip R1 to fail. The em-dash change is a legitimate locale correction.
