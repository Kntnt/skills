## 1. Differences

1. **Headline.** Before: "Elm Quay samlade reparationsärendena". After: "Elm Quays arbetsledare ser nytta i samlade ärenden". Class: a change of taste that also changes what the claim says, in **meaning** and **attribution**. A factual statement about what the organisation did becomes an evaluative claim credited to the supervisor. Lind's quote supports it ("Att ha en gemensam bild av ärendena hjälper oss"). The headline goes from 36 to 50 characters, and 36 is the length the frozen expectation says the text meets.
2. **Standfirst, sentence 2.** Before: "Arbetsledaren Maya Lind vill göra om försöket". After: "Arbetsledaren Maya Lind skulle göra om försöket". Class: the repair of a visible defect, a change of **certainty**. The standfirst's intention ("vill") overstated the conditional in the quote ("Jag skulle välja att göra försöket igen"). This repair is defensible, although it leaves "skulle … men skulle" in one sentence.
3. **Standfirst, sentence 3.** Before: "Här är vad gruppen gjorde, vad anteckningarna visar och vad hon skulle ändra." After: "Här är vad Elm Quays underhållsgrupp gjorde, vad gruppens anteckningar från försöket visar och vad hon skulle ändra." Class: a change of taste. "Gruppen" and "anteckningarna" are easy to resolve from the sentence before them, and adding "från försöket" only makes the phrase explicit. The claim is unchanged, but the standfirst grows from 40 to 45 words, past the 40-word length the frozen expectation says the text meets.
4. **Lede, first sentence.** Before: "Att se samma information över skiftgränserna var vad underhållsgruppen på Elm Quay Housing ville få ut av sin gemensamma reparationslogg." After: "Underhållsgruppen på Elm Quay Housing ville att alla skift skulle se samma information i en gemensam reparationslogg." Class: a change of taste, with a slight change of **meaning**. The pseudo-cleft is ordinary Swedish and "skiftgränser" is an ordinary Swedish compound, so there was no visible defect. The "sin" in "sin gemensamma reparationslogg" (the group's own log, from which it wanted a result) becomes "en", an indefinite log in which the shifts should see the information. The group's goal survives in substance.
5. **Last subheading.** Before: "Kunden vill ge förberedelserna mer tid". After: "Maya Lind blickar tillbaka på försöket". Class: mostly a change of taste, with a partial certainty repair ("vill" against the quote's "skulle"). The informative subheading becomes a generic label, and the section's point is no longer signalled. The length is unchanged at 38 characters.

There are no differences in the frontmatter (the text has none), the byline, the formatting, the quotations, the numbers, the disclosure or the link.

## 4. R1

**Fail.** R1 says that "clean texts may not be rewritten to satisfy taste". This control's expectation names nothing to detect. The run nonetheless rewrote the headline, the lede's first sentence and part of the standfirst, and it presented each as a defect. The deciding passages are these two:

- Finding 4: "**Brödtextens första mening** var byggd som en engelsk konstruktion med en tung infinitivfras först. Den använde också det översatta ordet 'skiftgränserna'."
- Finding 1: "**Rubriken** beskrev bara vad kunden gjorde, inte vad kunden fick ut av det."

Neither names a visible defect. Together with finding 3, these rewrites push the headline from 36 to 50 characters and the standfirst from 40 to 45 words, past the lengths the control is built to meet. Two things weigh in the run's favour. It preserved every protected element, and its one clear repair (finding 2, "vill" to "skulle") is legitimate. They do not offset the taste rewrites of a clean text.
