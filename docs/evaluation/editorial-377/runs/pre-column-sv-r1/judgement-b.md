3. *"Jag har inte mätt det här hos andra, och jag påstår inget om möten i allmänhet."* (¶3) — not measured, not general. **Kept as it was**, verbatim.

Only number 4 is weakened or deleted.

**Does the reply name a defect inside that sentence, rather than naming the whole sentence as the defect?** Partly, and the reply is candid that it is only partly. Finding 1 does locate a defect inside one clause: *"gjorde det i en form som inte kan vara falsk: en reflektion har den räckvidd en reflektion har"* — that is a charge against the predicate *"med den räckvidd en reflektion har"*, not against the sentence as a whole. Its other charge, *"upprepade avgränsningen i meningen före den"*, is a charge against the whole sentence in its context. The Skill itself classifies the finding the second way: *"Fyndet pekade ut hela meningen som defekten, så borttagningen står."*

**Ignoring what the reply asserts, can the defect be verified from `work/input.md` alone?** Yes, both charges. The clause *"med den räckvidd en reflektion har"* predicates a reflection's reach of a reflection and so states no bound a reader can check — visible in the sentence itself. And the sentence immediately before it, *"Jag har inte mätt det här hos andra, och jag påstår inget om möten i allmänhet."*, already states the substance of the same bound, so the overlap is visible in the input too.

**(a) or (b)?** **(a).** The limit the deleted sentence states is still stated by sentences the returned text retains. Its two components are that the observation is the writer's own and that its reach is limited: the first survives in the paragraph's own grammar (*"Det som retar mig"*, first person throughout) and in the retained *"Det är dokumentet jag tittar på, inte ett bestämt möte."*; the second survives, more concretely than the deleted sentence stated it, in the retained *"Jag har inte mätt det här hos andra, och jag påstår inget om möten i allmänhet."* The returned text therefore bounds what it claims exactly as far as the input did. No surviving claim in that paragraph gains scope, certainty, attribution, chronology, causality or meaning: *"Det som retar mig är något annat: att tid i kalendern så lätt behandlas som ett resultat i sig."* and *"Timmen är bokad, alltså har något hänt."* are untouched, and the sentence that now closes the paragraph is the input's own, unchanged. Not (b): the deleted sentence contradicts no other passage.

## 4. R1

**Fail.**

The deciding passage is the sentence added in paragraph 5:

> Den rymmer bara det ena av mina skäl: ett möte som finns till för att bygga förtroende har inget svar att ge på vad vi behöver förstå tillsammans.

Two things go wrong in it, and both are the failure R1 names rather than the reporting duty it also names.

First, it puts a claim into the writer's mouth that she does not make anywhere in the input. The input asserts that conversations *can* create trust and that this *tål att motiveras*; it never says that a trust-building meeting has nothing to answer to the proposed question. Inserting an authorial claim is the same kind of infidelity as removing one, and R1's requirement to preserve claims is not satisfied by a repair that manufactures one.

Second, it destroys a working passage that no finding had marked as defective. In the input, paragraph 5 is a single sentence — the proposal, standing alone — and paragraph 6 turns on *"Där börjar tvivlet."* In the returned text the doubt has already started one sentence earlier, so the hinge points at a turn that has happened, and the sentence *"Där börjar tvivlet."*, which was outside every finding, no longer does its work. The verbatim echo *"vad behöver vi förstå tillsammans?"* / *"vad vi behöver förstå tillsammans"* a clause apart is a second, independently verifiable blemish the input did not have. The Skill sees both — *"Läsaren förlorar kolumnens tydligaste rörelse"* — and returns the text that way regardless, leaving the reader a version that is worse in two named respects at a place where nothing had been wrong.

What is good here does not reach far enough to carry the verdict. The one deleted limiting sentence is handled correctly: the removal is declared in its own section, the surviving limit is named, and, as §3 finds, the returned text bounds its claims exactly as far as the input did — no hardening. The mechanical fix is right for the locale and nothing clean was rewritten to taste: only two paragraphs were touched, each with a defect visible in the text itself, and no verification against unavailable sources is attempted anywhere. The account of the damage is unusually honest, and it correctly hands the writer the choice between the two repairs. But R1 asks whether the Skill addresses visible defects **while preserving working voice, arguments and claims outside findings**, and this returned text does not: it trades a defect the reader could have lived with for an inserted claim and a broken turn. Reporting an injury accurately is not the same as not inflicting it.
