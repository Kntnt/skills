## 4. R1

**Pass.**

The deciding passage is the closing sentence of *The proposal rests on an inconvenience the papers do not measure*:

- input — "Double administration is the administration's genuine objection to keeping both channels, not any view that telephone users are lazy or expensive, and I do not accuse it of holding one."
- returned — "Double administration is the administration's genuine objection to keeping both routes, not any view that telephone users are lazy or expensive."

This is the only place where the run touches what the text asserts, and it is the place R1 turns on. The defect it acts on is concrete and visible on the page — one sentence refusing the same imputation twice — the removal is openly declared as a removed claim rather than buried in the text, and the limit it carried survives intact in the clause beside it, so nothing outside the finding loses a claim, an argument, a figure or a quotation. The author's voice survives whole: the first person ("I am not against digital booking", "We make no claim to have funded or costed that trial"), the plain municipal register, the sentence rhythms and every one of the other thirteen limiting sentences come back untouched.

Set against that, the run's weak points are real but not disqualifying. The pronoun repair in *What the board can decide on 18 June* mends a genuine referential gap. The three "channel" → "route" substitutions are the run's only exposure to R1's bar on rewriting clean texts to taste: the input's mixed vocabulary is a defensible thing to standardise, but it was not broken, and the reply never names the substitution, so the reader has to diff to find it. Three unreported lexical swaps that move no claim do not amount to rewriting a clean text, and they leave voice and argument exactly as they were. No unavailable source was invoked at any point — the reply reasons only from what the text itself says, and every claim about the pilot report, the officers' report and the June meeting is passed through unaltered.
