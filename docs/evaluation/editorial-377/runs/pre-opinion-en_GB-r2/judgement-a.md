## 4. R1

**Fail.**

The deciding passage is the closing sentence of *The proposal rests on an inconvenience the papers do not measure*:

> **Before:** "Double administration is the administration's genuine objection to keeping both channels, not any view that telephone users are lazy or expensive, and I do not accuse it of holding one."
> **After:** "Double administration is the administration's genuine objection to keeping both routes, not any view that telephone users are lazy or expensive."

Two clauses of R1 are broken here at once.

*Claims outside findings must be preserved.* The clause deleted is a limiting clause, its removal raises what the text asserts by narrowing a two-part disclaimer to one part, and the defect offered to justify it — that the clause restated a negation already made — cannot be verified from the input, because the two negations have different subjects and different work. This is a rejected loss, not a legitimate removal: the text loses the author's express refusal to impute a motive, which is a live asset in an opinion piece whose whole case is that the administration's objection should be taken at face value and answered with figures.

*Clean texts may not be rewritten to satisfy taste.* I find no concrete visible defect anywhere in `work/input.md` — no misspelling, no punctuation error, no agreement fault, no en_GB locale slip, no broken reference, no unsupported figure. Against that, the Skill made three substitutions of "channel(s)" for "route(s)" and one expansion of "It" to "The board", all four of them preferences rather than repairs, and reported none of them while declaring "No findings remain". The input's alternation between "route" and "channel" is not an error; unifying it is taste applied to clean text.

Nothing in the run is irreparable in the sense of lost material: the input file is intact and every removed word can be restored from it. But the returned text as it stands asserts more than the text it was given, and says less about what it did than it changed.
