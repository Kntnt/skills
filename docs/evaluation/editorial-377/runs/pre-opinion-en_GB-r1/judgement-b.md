## 1. Differences

1. **Before:** `## Six months, both routes, and something to measure` — **After:** `## Six months, both routes and something to measure` — **Class: change of taste.** A serial comma is deleted from a heading. It is not an error in en_GB either way (the input's form is equally standard there), so this is preference, not a mechanical correction and not the repair of a visible defect. It could be argued as a locale-form adjustment towards the commoner British style; it corrects nothing.

2. **Before:** ` That is thin ground for making the change permanent.` (final sentence of "A motive on the table, but no figures under it") — **After:** deleted — **Class: change to what a claim says — meaning and scope.** An evaluative claim the text made is no longer made at that point: the section's verdict on its own evidence is gone, so the judgement "thin ground" is no longer predicated on this specific gap in the documents. It also reads as the repair of a visible defect (a near-echo of paragraph 1's *It is not yet a reason to make the change permanent*), and the Skill files it as one. Not hardening: the deleted sentence's work is to assess the administration's case, not to bound what the text asserts, and its loss lowers rather than raises what the text claims.

3. **Before:** `I am not against digital booking.` — **After:** `I support digital booking;` — **Class: change to what a claim says — certainty and meaning.** The input denies opposition; the return asserts positive endorsement. "Not against" and "support" are not the same claim: the first leaves indifference, reservation and conditional acceptance open, the second closes them and commits the author to a positive position the input never takes. This is **hardening, form 1**: a bounded non-claim becomes an assertion. (Fourth-form note, if form 1 is read narrowly as covering only explicit refusals to assert: *a negative bound replaced by its positive converse*, which asserts more than the negation licensed.)

4. **Before:** `I am against settling its shape before the council can set the work…` — **After:** `my objection is to settling its shape before the council can set the work…` — **Class: change of taste.** The proposition survives intact; the first-person predication is nominalised. **Consequent on difference 3**: the clause is recast to hang off the new first half, and the sentence boundary between the two halves becomes a semicolon. The cost is voice, not content — the input's deliberate antithesis (*I am not against X. I am against Y.*), two short parallel sentences in a passage that is otherwise clean, is collapsed into one longer sentence.

No other differences exist. ## 4. R1

**FAIL.**

The deciding passage is the last paragraph of "Six months, both routes and something to measure":

> **Input:** *"I am not against digital booking. I am against settling its shape before the council can set the work each route costs staff against what it is worth to the people who use it."*
>
> **Return:** *"I support digital booking; my objection is to settling its shape before the council can set the work each route costs staff against what it is worth to the people who use it."*

R1 asks whether the Skill addresses concrete visible defects while preserving working voice, arguments and claims outside findings, and holds that clean texts may not be rewritten to satisfy taste. This passage was clean. It carried no visible defect, and the reply names none. It was nonetheless rewritten, and the rewrite did three things R1 forbids at once: it **changed what a claim says** (certainty and meaning) by turning a denial of opposition into an assertion of support — **hardening, form 1**, the failure mode this corpus is built to catch; it **destroyed working voice**, collapsing a deliberate two-sentence antithesis, the rhetorical hinge of the argument, into a nominalised semicolon clause; and it did both **outside any finding**, then went unreported in an account that claims *"One repair"* and *"No findings remain."*

The Skill's genuine credits do not save it. It left every one of the other twelve limiting sentences verbatim, including the two hardest traps in the text — *"We make no claim to have funded or costed that trial."* (a refusal to assert, covering two things, returned covering both) and the full "measures nothing about age, functional ability or how used to digital services people are" list. It preserved all quotations, names, dates and figures. Its one reported repair is defensible and its report of it is honest and complete, including the residue. But R1 is decided by what happens to claims outside findings, and a clean, load-bearing sentence was hardened, its voice flattened, and the change concealed by an account that asserts nothing else was done. Difference 1, an unreported taste edit to a clean heading, is the same fault in miniature and corroborates it.
