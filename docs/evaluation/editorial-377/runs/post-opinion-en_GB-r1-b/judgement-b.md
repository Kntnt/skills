## 4. R1

**Fail.**

The deciding passage is this one, difference #5:

> Before: "A booking made by phone is not proof that somebody cannot use the web. It is a reason to find out why the phone is still being used."
> After: "A booking made by phone is a reason to find out why the phone is still being used, not proof that somebody cannot use the web."

Two short, grammatical, load-bearing sentences in the piece's own plain voice — the rhetorical hinge of the argument, stating the denial first and the alternative second — were fused and inverted. No defect is visible in them, and the reply names none: it reports the change as a repair while describing only what the change did, not what was wrong. That is a clean passage rewritten to taste, which R1 forbids.

Two further differences point the same way and confirm it is not a one-off. Difference #6 swaps a working idiom ("set … against") for "weigh … against" and thereby introduces a word repetition the input did not have, two sentences after "for the board to weigh" — a taste change that leaves the prose slightly worse. And differences #3/#4 move a claim's certainty, which the reply itself concedes ("Its certainty moved"), on a sentence whose present tense is already scoped by the preceding "Öppna beslut **proposes** a six-month trial" and the opening "During it".

Against this, the Skill did address the text's two genuinely concrete defects — the stray serial comma in the third H2 (#2) and the channel/route inconsistency (#1, #8) — and it preserved the argument, the figures, the attributed report, the byline and, importantly, every limiting sentence, including the two-part disclaimer "We make no claim to have funded or costed that trial". Nothing was lost that cannot be recovered; there is no irreparable finding. But R1 asks for defects addressed *while* working prose is preserved, and the merged-and-reversed thesis sentence, the idiom swap and the certainty shift are three rewrites of prose that was working. Compounding it, the account fails to report the two substitutions that were legitimate (#1, #8) and positively misstates #6 as having "moved the wording of the noun phrase alone", so a reader of the reply cannot reconstruct what happened to the text.
