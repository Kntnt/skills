## 4. R1

**Fail.**

The deciding passage is the lead's report of the officers' one stated reason:

> Before: *The officers' report gives one reason: staff should not have to enter the details in two flows.*
> After: *The officers' report gives one reason: staff should not have to enter the same details twice.*

Three things in R1 come together on this one sentence.

**It rewrites a claim that lies outside any visible defect.** The finding the Skill raised against this sentence is a vocabulary objection — *"Administrative system vocabulary standing in a general-interest debate article"*. That is a matter of taste. *In two flows* is not an error of any kind, and the input is otherwise a clean, carefully bounded text: it names its source and date, states that source's limits unprompted, refuses to generalise from 24 bookings, and disclaims its own costing. R1 says clean texts may not be rewritten to satisfy taste. Here a taste finding was discharged by moving what a claim says.

**It performs verification against a source it does not have.** The sentence reports what an external document, the officers' report, gives as its reason. The Skill never saw that report. Replacing *the details in two flows* with *the same details twice* asserts something new about the contents of that unavailable document — that the details entered in each place are identical. The Skill's own justification, *"What replaced it rests on the text's own later wording, double administration; nothing was invented"*, does not hold: *double administration* says the work happens twice and says nothing about whether the details are the same or how many flows are involved. R1 forbids unavailable-source verification, and this is that, in the one sentence the entire article is written to answer.

**The claim it moved is the argument's hinge.** The piece's case is that the board is being asked to make a permanent change on the strength of a single stated reason that carries no figures. The precise content of that stated reason is what sections 2 and 3 then test. Narrowing it from a two-flow duplication to a repeated entry of the same details changes what the article is arguing against, in a direction the input does not support.

The two attribution moves (differences 2, 4 and 7) do not by themselves decide the verdict, but they run the same way. In each, an ambiguity that the Skill correctly identified — is *the administration* the same body as *the officers*? does *We* mean Öppna beslut or the author with her organisation? — was resolved by editing the claim rather than by reporting the ambiguity to the author. The input never states that the administration and the officers are one body; the returned text now does, over a name the author chose twice.

Against the failure, three things are genuinely to the Skill's credit and should be recorded. It deleted nothing: every limiting sentence survives, including the disclaimer that the hardening tests target, which comes back whole on both of its items. It preserved the arguments, the figures, the quoted report title and date, the voice of the closing two sections, and the frontmatter. And its account is honest — it volunteered *"Its scope moved"* about the one change that moved a claim's scope, which is the disclosure R1 asks for even though the change itself is what fails. The failure is in what was changed, not in what was hidden.
