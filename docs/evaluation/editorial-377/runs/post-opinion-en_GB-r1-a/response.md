# Don't switch off Lervik's phone booking before we know what it costs

On 18 June the council executive board is asked to remove phone booking from all seven community venues from September. The officers' report gives one reason: staff should not have to enter the same details twice. That is a fair thing to want. It is not yet a reason to make the change permanent.

## What the pilot actually counted

The pilot report *Bokning av föreningslokaler*, dated 8 April 2026, covers an eight-week trial in two venues. It records 96 bookings made on the web and 24 made by phone. Those figures are the report's, and so are their limits: it counts bookings rather than unique people, and it measures nothing about age, functional ability or how used to digital services people are. Twenty-four phone bookings therefore cannot tell us what share of Lervik's residents are unable to book digitally. Nobody should claim they can.

## A motive on the table, but no figures under it

The officers' objection to keeping both routes is double administration, and I take it at face value: it is not a claim that people who ring are lazy or expensive. But the documents contain no measurement of how long either route takes staff, and no calculation of what removing one would save. The board is being asked to close a channel for good on the strength of an inconvenience the documents put no number on. That is thin ground for making the change permanent.

## Six months, both routes and something to measure

Öppna beslut proposes a six-month trial in all seven venues with both booking routes kept open. During it, the officers record the time each booking route takes and ask users to say, voluntarily, why they chose the phone or the web. Öppna beslut makes no claim to have funded or costed that trial. What it would cost is for the board to weigh.

A booking made by phone is not proof that somebody cannot use the web. It is a reason to find out why the phone is still being used. I am not against digital booking. I am against settling its shape before the council can set the work each route costs staff against what it is worth to the people who use it.

## What the board can decide on 18 June

Postpone the permanent switch and run the six months with both routes open. When the trial has produced figures, decide whether a channel should be removed, changed or kept. That decision is the board's either way, and it will be a better one for being taken with the cost of both routes on the table.

— Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut

## Findings

None remain. The three the review raised were these, and the correction round closed all three.

1. **One body under two names.** The report's authors were *the officers* in the lead and *the administration* in the section examining their reasoning, and in British usage *the administration* reads readily as the ruling political group rather than council officers. A reader could not tell whether the second section was weighing the one stated reason from the lead or a second objection from elsewhere — and that section's argument, that the stated reason has no figures under it, depends on the two being the same.

2. **An unanchored *We* in the disclaimer.** *We make no claim to have funded or costed that trial* arrived two sentences after the same body was named in the third person, and it sat directly after a section about what the council has failed to cost. A reader could take *We* for the council and read the sentence as continuing that critique, which reverses the work it does.

3. **In two flows.** Administrative system vocabulary standing in a general-interest debate article, leaving the duplication unspecified in kind. The argument turns on how much work that duplication is for staff, so the reader met the central fact in its vaguest form.

## What the corrections did to the claims

No claim was removed and no passage deleted. Three claims came through with their wording or attribution moved, and each is named here so the change is visible without a diff.

- **The officers' stated reason** — *enter the details in two flows* became *enter the same details twice*, repairing finding 3. Its scope moved: the text now states the duplication as a count and no longer says it spans two separate flows. What replaced it rests on the text's own later wording, *double administration*; nothing was invented.
- **The objection, and the recording during the proposed trial** — both moved from *the administration* to *the officers*, repairing finding 1. The attribution changed; what is attributed did not.
- **The funding and costing disclaimer** — *We make no claim* became *Öppna beslut makes no claim*, repairing finding 2. The attribution narrowed from an unnamed *we* to the organisation. The limit itself is intact in extent and in strength: it still disclaims both the funding and the costing of that trial, and it was neither shortened nor softened.

The closing mechanical pass made one correction: the text omits the serial comma in its other three-item series, so the third heading lost the one it carried.
