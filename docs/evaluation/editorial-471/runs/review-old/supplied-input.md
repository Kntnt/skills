# 13 questions before you write — Brackwater Bakery case

Invented evaluation material. Brackwater Bakery and Tallow Ovens are fictional companies and describe no real client.

## 1. What is the assignment?

- Topic: a new oven at a bakery.
- Genre: case study.
- Channel: Tallow Ovens' website, customer stories section.

## 2. Who is the reader?

Owners of small bakeries. [WEAK: a defined group with its prior knowledge and reason to read]

## 3. What makes the text RIV for the reader?

- Relevant: small bakeries are replacing ovens as energy prices rise.
- Interesting: a bakery like theirs that changed its night shift.
- Valuable: [MISSING: What value should the text give the reader?]

## 4. Which brand is the sender?

Tallow Ovens, a maker of deck ovens for small bakeries. Mandate: its own ovens and how customers use them. Tone of voice: straightforward and warm.

## 5. What is the text's angle?

How Brackwater Bakery moved its first bake from 03:00 to 05:00 after installing a Tallow deck oven.

## 6. What should the reader have understood afterwards?

Message: a Tallow deck oven lets a small bakery start its day two hours later.

## 7. What should the reader feel, think or do afterwards?

Main effect: the reader books a visit to see the oven at Brackwater. Measure: visit bookings through the form on the page.

## 8. How should the text be structured?

ABT.

## 9. What is the hook?

The bakery's owner, Mira Holt, had started work at 03:00 for eleven years.

## 11. What should be left out?

Prices and competitors' ovens.

## 12. What is the working title?

Two more hours of sleep at Brackwater.

- Language: sv
- Tone: warm, in the customer's voice.
