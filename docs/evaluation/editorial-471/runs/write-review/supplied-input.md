# Brief: the seed swap page

Invented evaluation material. The Hollin Seed Circle is a fictional association and describes no real client.

## 1. What is the assignment?

- Working title: Swap seeds with the Hollin Seed Circle
- Topic: the seed swap.
- Genre: web copy.
- Channel: the Hollin Seed Circle's website, as the page about the swap.
- Language: en_GB, one version only.
- Length: roughly 300 words.
- Search phrase: [MISSING: Which search phrase should the page be visible for, or is it deliberately written without one?]

## 2. Which brand is the sender?

The Hollin Seed Circle, a volunteer association of home gardeners.

- Promise: anyone can grow from seed without buying it.
- Image: friendly but little known outside its members.
- Mandate: its own swap rules and what its members grow.
- Tone of voice: warm and plain.
- Tone in this text: warm and plain, with the short, scannable sentences a web page needs; the two pull the same way.

## 3. What does the sender want to convey with this text?

The Seed Circle is open to anyone who grows something, not only experienced gardeners.

## 4. Who is the reader?

Home gardeners in the area who have grown from shop-bought seed and found the swap through a search or a neighbour. They know how to sow but not how a swap works, care about not wasting seed, and read the page to decide whether to come. Right now the spring swap on 14 March 2027 is being planned, and the page is read in the weeks before it.

## 5. What is the text's angle?

What a first-time visitor brings and takes home from a swap.

## 6. What is the hook?

You can come with a single packet of leftover seed, or with none, and still leave with seed for the season.

## 7. How should the text be structured?

Free structure: the page answers the visitor's questions in the order they arise.

In one sentence: a first-time visitor can bring what little seed they have, or none, and leave with seed for the season, because the swap runs on what members share and asks only that newcomers note what they take.

Sketch:

1. Opening: the hook, that one leftover packet or none is enough.
2. What to bring: leftover seed in a labelled envelope, if you have any.
3. What happens at the swap: tables by kind of plant, and a sheet where you note what you take.
4. How much seed a first-timer usually takes home (the figure is to be found out; see question 12).
5. The ending: the date and place of the spring swap, tying back to the leftover packet, with the invitation to come.

## 8. What should be left out?

No advice on how to save seed; that is another page. No claims about germination rates. Commercial seed brands are not named.

## 9. What should the reader have understood afterwards?

A first-time visitor is welcome at the swap whether or not they bring seed, and leaves with seed for the season.

## 10. What should the reader feel, think or do afterwards?

- Main effect: the reader comes to the spring swap.
- The way there: they first need to believe that they need not bring anything.
- Call to action and measure: direct, "Come to the spring swap on 14 March." Measure: the number of names on the newcomers' sheet on the day.

## 11. What makes the text RIV for the reader, and does the brief hold together?

- Relevant: the spring swap is weeks away, which the ending's date carries.
- Interesting: the hook, that you can come with nothing.
- Valuable: knowing what to bring and what happens, in steps 2 and 3.

Chain check:

- Does the reader follow from the sender and the channel? Yes, local gardeners find the page through search or a neighbour.
- Does the angle lie close to the reader, and does the hook carry the angle? Yes, a first-timer's question is what to bring, and the hook answers it.
- Does the structure resolve the hook's tension, and does it fit the length? Partly: step 4 needs a figure we do not have yet.
- Is the conclusion what the structure lands in, does it carry the message, and does it lie within the brand's mandate? Yes.
- Does what the reader understands lead to the effect, and does that effect serve the brand's goals? Yes.
- Does anything in the structure conflict with what should be left out? No.
