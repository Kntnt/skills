# Writing brief: Why the evening repair sessions run over

## 1. What is the assignment?

- **Working title:** Why the evening repair sessions run over
- **Topic:** The evening repair sessions.
- **Genre:** Report.
- **Channel:** A PDF for the board of the Alder Tool Library.
- **Language:** `en_GB` is the source language. A Swedish version will be translated from it afterwards by the library's volunteer translator.
- **Length:** Provisionally 800 to 1,200 words.
- **Search phrase:** None, deliberately, since the text goes to the board only.

## 2. Which brand is the sender?

The sender is the Alder Tool Library. Its platform is set out in a separate document that was not to hand when this brief was written (see the sources).

- **Promise:** [MISSING: What does the Alder Tool Library promise its members and want to be known for?]
- **Image:** [MISSING: How does the board see the library, and the volunteer coordinators, today? Is there trust to build on or distrust to handle?]
- **Mandate:** The volunteer coordinators may not recommend spending, since the budget is the treasurer's mandate.
- **Tone of voice:** Plain and factual.
- **Tone in this text:** Sober and neutral. The brand's tone of voice and the report's conventions pull the same way.

## 3. What does the sender want to convey with this text?

The volunteer coordinators have the sessions under control and can fix the overruns themselves.

## 4. Who is the reader?

The seven members of the library's board. They know the evening repair sessions exist but not how they run. They care about the volunteers' wellbeing and about the terms on which the library rents its premises. They asked for this report and will read it before the November board meeting, where the session schedule is decided; that meeting is what makes the question pressing now.

## 5. What is the text's angle?

What the September sign-in sheets show about where the overrun time goes.

## 6. What is the hook?

The sessions are booked for two hours, but they ended late on every evening in September, and nobody has known why.

## 7. How should the text be structured?

The report follows the genre's own form, so no separate technique is chosen. The report genre's own form is PAC (Premise, Analysis, Conclusion), with the answer stated at the top before the evidence, and headings that name what each section found.

**The whole text in one sentence:** The evening repair sessions are booked for two hours but ended late on every evening in September without anyone knowing why, and the September sign-in sheets show that most of the overrun comes from repairs started in the last half hour, which means that a cut-off for new repairs at 20:30 would end the sessions on time, and that the volunteer coordinators can fix the overruns themselves, within their mandate and without new spending.

**Sketch** (about 800 to 1,200 words in total):

1. **Opening: premise and answer** (about 100 to 150 words). What the report covers (the evening repair sessions), why it exists (the board asked, ahead of the November meeting where the session schedule is decided) and the period it speaks to (September). The hook: the sessions are booked for two hours, ended late on every evening in September, and nobody has known why. The answer at the top: most of the overrun comes from repairs started in the last half hour, so a cut-off for new repairs at 20:30 would end the sessions on time.
2. **What the sign-in sheets record** (about 100 to 150 words). What the sheets count, how many sessions September had, and each evening's booked end time against its actual end time, with the basis of every figure given.
3. **Where the overrun time goes: the analysis** (about 400 to 600 words, in one or two sections). The overrun broken down by what the sheets separate out, showing how much of it comes from repairs started after 20:30. A table of evenings and overrun minutes if the data allows. Headings that name the finding. Other explanations weighed where the material gives reason to.
4. **The strongest objection, and what the sheets cannot settle** (about 100 to 150 words). Any question the board is likely to raise, put at its strongest and then answered. The limits of the evidence, among them that the sheets cover a single month.
5. **Ending: conclusion and proposal** (about 100 to 150 words). Ties back to the hook by saying why the sessions run over. Proposes, for a board decision, a trial cut-off for new repairs at 20:30 from the first session in December, run by the volunteer coordinators at no cost, with the share of December sessions that end on time as the measure. Notes that the premises must be empty by 21:30.

## 8. What should be left out?

- No recommendation to hire staff or spend money.
- No names of individual volunteers.
- Nothing on the rental terms, except noting that the premises must be empty by 21:30.

## 9. What should the reader have understood afterwards?

Most of the overrun comes from repairs started in the last half hour, so a cut-off for new repairs at 20:30 would end the sessions on time.

## 10. What should the reader feel, think or do afterwards?

- **Main effect:** The board approves a trial cut-off for new repairs from the first session in December.
- **The way there:** The board first needs to see that the overrun has one main cause.
- **Call to action and measure:** Direct: a proposal for a board decision. Measure: the share of December sessions that end on time.

## 11. What makes the text RIV for the reader, and does the brief hold together?

- **Relevant:** The board asked for the report and decides the session schedule at the November meeting. The report also touches both of the board's concerns: volunteers kept late, and premises that must be empty by 21:30. This sits in the opening (step 1) and the ending (step 5).
- **Interesting:** The hook: sessions booked for two hours ended late every evening in September, and nobody has known why. This sits in the opening (step 1).
- **Valuable:** The conclusion: the overrun has one main cause, and the fix costs nothing and is ready for a decision. This sits in the analysis (step 3) and the ending (step 5).

**Chain check:**

- *Does the reader follow from the sender and the channel?* **Yes.** It is a board-only PDF from the library, answering the board's own question.
- *Does the angle lie close to the reader, and does the hook carry the angle?* **Yes.** The sign-in sheets answer the question the board has, and the hook is the unexplained overrun the sheets explain.
- *Does the structure resolve the hook's tension, and does it fit the length?* **Yes.** The analysis shows where the time goes and the ending says why the sessions run over. The step allocations add up to 800 to 1,200 words.
- *Is the conclusion what the structure actually lands in, does it carry the message, and does it lie within the brand's mandate?* **Yes.** The cut-off is where the analysis lands, it shows that the coordinators can fix the overrun themselves, and it involves no spending.
- *Does what the reader understands lead to the effect you have described, and does that effect serve the brand's goals?* **Partly.** One main cause leads naturally to approving a trial, and the message fits the call to action as long as the coordinators propose and run the trial while the board approves it. Whether the effect serves the brand's goals cannot be checked, because the library's promise is missing. The promise under the sender would settle this.
- *Does anything in the structure conflict with what should be left out?* **No.** No spending is recommended, the rental terms appear only as the 21:30 limit, and the proposal refers to the coordinators by role rather than naming volunteers.
