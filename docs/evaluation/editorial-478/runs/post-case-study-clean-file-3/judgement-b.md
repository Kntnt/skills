# Judgement b

## 1. Differences

There is no frontmatter in either file. The formatting is unchanged: the same heading levels, the bold standfirst, the `Text:` byline, the dash quotations, the link and the line breaks. Exactly three lines differ (lines 1, 3 and 7).

1. **Headline (line 1).**
   Before: `# Gemensam ärendebild hjälper, säger Elm Quay om loggförsöket` (59 characters, 8 words)
   After: `# Gemensam ärendebild hjälper, säger Elm Quays arbetsledare om loggen` (67 characters, 9 words)
   Class: a change to what a claim says, in two ways.
   - **Attribution:** the appraisal moves from the organisation to its work supervisor.
   - **Meaning/scope:** "loggförsöket" (the trial of the log) becomes "loggen" (the log). The headline no longer says that this was a trial.

   The new headline also breaks the 60-character and 3–8-word recommendations, which the original headline met.

2. **Standfirst, last sentence (line 3).**
   Before: `Här är vad gruppen gjorde, vad anteckningarna visar och vad hon skulle ändra.`
   After: `Här är vad Elm Quays underhållsgrupp gjorde, vad gruppens anteckningar visar och vad hon skulle ändra.`
   Class: a change of taste, with a minor **attribution** change. The notes are now explicitly the group's. The body already said so ("gruppens egna anteckningar"), so the claim stays consistent with the text. As a side effect, the standfirst grows from 40 to 43 words.

3. **Opening, first sentence (line 7).**
   Before: `Att se samma information över skiftgränserna var vad underhållsgruppen på Elm Quay Housing ville få ut av sin gemensamma reparationslogg.`
   After: `Underhållsgruppen på Elm Quay Housing ville kunna se samma information över skiftgränserna i sin gemensamma reparationslogg.`
   Class: a change of taste (word order and the pseudo-cleft "var vad"), with a slight change of **meaning**. "Ville få ut av" (what the group wanted to get out of the log, its expected benefit) becomes "ville kunna se … i" (wanted to be able to see in it). The construction is idiomatic enough in Swedish to be no visible defect.

Nothing is purely mechanical.

## 4. R1

**Fail.**

The run preserves every protected element and avoids every named rejection. But R1 says clean texts "may not be rewritten to satisfy taste", and the corpus treats this text as conforming.

The decisive passage is the opening rewrite:

> "Att se samma information … var vad underhållsgruppen … ville få ut av" → "Underhållsgruppen … ville kunna se …"

This is a taste preference against a working sentence, and it changes meaning slightly: the reply concedes that the emphasis is gone. The standfirst rewrite is also taste. The headline rewrite is the most defensible of the three (attribution), but it trades a headline that met its guideline for one that breaches it, and it drops "försöket".

Three rewrites of a clean control, two of them for taste, fail R1.
