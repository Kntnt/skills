## 1. Differences

`work/input.md` and `work/output.md` differ on two lines and nowhere else. The frontmatter (none), standfirst, byline, subheadings, quotations, numbers, disclosure and link are identical.

1. Headline (line 1).
   - Before: "Gemensam ärendebild hjälper, säger Elm Quay om loggförsöket"
   - After: "Gemensam ärendebild hjälper, säger arbetsledare på Elm Quay"
   - Class: a change to what a claim says, in two ways. **Attribution:** the appraisal moves from the organisation Elm Quay to an unnamed supervisor there. **Scope/meaning:** "om loggförsöket" is gone, so the headline no longer says what the appraisal is about (a trial of a log). Both lines are 59 characters and 8 words.
2. Last sentence of the first body paragraph (line 7).
   - Before: "Vad det krävde, och vad det gav, går att följa i gruppens egna anteckningar."
   - After: "Vad det krävde berättar arbetsledaren Maya Lind, och vad som registrerades går att följa i en intern försöksanteckning."
   - Class: a change to what a claim says. **Attribution:** "what it required" is now credited to Lind. In the text, though, the categories being the group's own and Svale configuring the log and training six staff are told in the narrator's voice, not by Lind. The source also changes from "the group's own notes" to "an internal trial note". **Meaning/scope:** the promise that the reader can follow "what it gave" is replaced by "what was recorded". The paragraph grows from 43 to 47 words.

The reply says the final proofreading pass changed nothing, and there are no mechanical-only differences.

## 4. R1

**Fail.**

The deciding passage is the repair of finding 2: "Vad det krävde berättar arbetsledaren Maya Lind …". The original sentence is a working signpost. The text does give what the trial required (in Lind's quote and in the narrator's account of Svale's work) and what it gave (the note's counts and median times, with their caveat). That makes finding 2 an over-reading, not a concrete visible defect. Its repair changes attribution: facts the text tells in its own voice are now credited to Lind, which the reply itself concedes.

Finding 1 is at most arguable. Naming an organisation for its staff member's statement is a common headline convention. Its repair also costs the headline its subject ("om loggförsöket"), a loss the reply acknowledges.

A text the corpus treats as conforming was rewritten in two places, and claims changed in attribution and scope. Everything the expectation lists for preservation survives, and every change is reported accurately, but that does not rescue the verdict. R1 forbids rewriting a clean text without a concrete defect, and the second repair introduced a new misattribution.
