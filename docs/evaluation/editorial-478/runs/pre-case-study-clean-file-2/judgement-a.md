# Judgement a

## 1. Differences

The texts differ in exactly two places. The two files have no frontmatter, and the run changed no formatting: Markdown heading levels, the bold standfirst, the byline, the subheadings, the dash quotes and the link are identical.

1. **Headline (line 1).**
   Before: `# Gemensam ärendebild hjälper, säger Elm Quay om loggförsöket` (59 characters, 8 words)
   After: `# Gemensam ärendebild hjälper gruppen, säger arbetsledaren på Elm Quay` (68 characters, 9 words)
   Class: change to what a claim says, in **attribution** (the judgement moves from the organisation "Elm Quay" to "arbetsledaren på Elm Quay") and **meaning** ("hjälper" gets the object "gruppen", an interpretation of "oss" in the quote, and "om loggförsöket" is dropped, so the headline no longer says what the article is about). The change also moves the headline from inside the 60-character norm to above it.

2. **Lede, third sentence (line 7).**
   Before: `Vad det krävde, och vad det gav, går att följa i gruppens egna anteckningar.`
   After: `Det krävde förberedelser, och hur ärendena registrerades och tilldelades går att följa i en intern försöksanteckning.`
   Class: change to what a claim says, in **meaning** (the promise to show "vad det gav" is removed, and the new sentence asserts flatly that the trial "krävde förberedelser"), **scope** (what can be followed in the notes shrinks from what the trial required and gave to how cases were registered and assigned) and **attribution** (the source changes from "gruppens egna anteckningar" to "en intern försöksanteckning").

The run made no mechanical corrections.

Under "Ändrade påståenden" the reply says that the judgement is now attributed to a person, that "gruppen" is the headline's interpretation of "oss", and that the headline no longer says the text is about a log trial. Under "Artikelanatomin" it says the new headline has 68 characters and 9 words, above the norms of 60 characters and eight words, and that the incoming headline had 59 and 8. All of this is correct.
- **Difference 2 (lede):** reported, and reported accurately. Finding 2 quotes the old sentence. "Ändrade påståenden" lists both clauses: the first now states that the trial required preparation without citing notes, and the second now names "en intern försöksanteckning" as the source. The account is complete and honest. The problem lies in whether the changes should have been made at all.

## 4. R1

**Fail.**

The control is a clean text, and R1 says "Clean texts may not be rewritten to satisfy taste." Neither finding names a concrete visible defect:

- **Finding 1** treats "Gemensam ärendebild hjälper, säger Elm Quay om loggförsöket" as a misattribution. A headline that credits a named company employee's statement to the company, followed at once by a standfirst that names "Arbetsledaren Maya Lind", is ordinary headline practice, not an error. "loggförsöket" is ordinary definite headline usage. The rewrite changes the attribution and the meaning, drops the topic from the headline, and pushes it from 59 to 68 characters, past the anatomy norm the input met. The reply admits this and hands the repair back to the author.
- **Finding 2** reads "vad det gav" as an unsupported promise of an effect caused by the software. The body does show what the trial gave (31 cases, a median of two working days against three) together with the reservation. The rewrite removes a working promise in the lede and asserts a new flat claim, "Det krävde förberedelser".

The accounting is exemplary, and every quote, number, disclosure and link survives. Even so, the run rewrote claims in the headline and the lede of a text that conforms, on readings rather than defects, and left the headline worse against the anatomy than it found it.
