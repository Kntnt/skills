## Finding dispositions

### Finding 1 — "digital proficiency" for "digital vana" — **accepted**

Evidence:

- What the draft asserts in context: "The report does not measure age, functional ability, or digital proficiency." In context this is a plain three-item list of what the pilot report leaves unmeasured; the preceding sentences ("Those are bookings, not individual people") fix the subject as the report's measurement scope. There is no weaker contextual reading available — the assertion is that the report measures no digital proficiency.
- No supplied statement carries that claim with its subject and scope intact. The only supplied statement on point is "Den mäter inte ålder, funktionsförmåga eller digital vana." *Digital vana* is habituation — how accustomed and practised in digital use a person is — and is not *proficiency*, which is skill or capability. The draft's first two items ("age", "functional ability") already carry "ålder" and "funktionsförmåga" exactly, so the third item is required to carry "digital vana" and does not. The draft thus asserts a non-measurement the material does not state, and drops the one it does.
- The report's counter-case stands and is not excluded. A report that checks whether participants could complete the online booking form unaided, while recording nothing about how routinely they use digital services, is compatible with the supplied material: then "Den mäter inte … digital vana" is true and the draft's sentence false. The nearest candidate exclusion, "Telefonbokningarna kan därför inte användas för att säga hur stor andel av invånarna som inte kan boka digitalt", limits what the *phone-booking count* can show about the share of *residents*; it does not state that the report contains no ability measure. A counter-case need only be compatible with the material, not likely.
- The opposite direction is likewise handled: a frequency-of-use log would be a *vana* measure, and that case is excluded by "Den mäter inte … digital vana" — correctly recorded as excluded rather than as a second finding.
- The proposed repair ("digital habits", or "familiarity with digital services") is the smallest change that restores the supplied item and touches nothing else.

No translation findings were claimed; the draft contains no quoted speech, so §4 is correct as written and nothing there requires disposition.

FINDING Finding 1: accepted
