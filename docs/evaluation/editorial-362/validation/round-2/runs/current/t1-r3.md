## 2. Finding dispositions

### Finding 1 — "digital proficiency" for "digital vana" — **accepted**

Draft: "The report does not measure age, functional ability, or digital proficiency."
Source: "Den mäter inte ålder, funktionsförmåga eller digital vana."

What the draft actually asserts, in context, is a list of three things the pilot report leaves unmeasured; the third item is the dimension *proficiency* — skill at using digital services. The supplied material states the report leaves unmeasured the dimension *vana* — habituation, how accustomed and practised a person is. These are distinct dimensions, not two names for one: a person can be practised but unskilled, or skilled but unaccustomed. So the draft asserts a non-measurement the material does not state, and it is the checker's reading, not a merely conceivable stronger one, that the two dimensions come apart.

To reject the finding I would need exact source text either supporting "proficiency" or excluding the checker's case (a report that checks whether participants could complete the online form unaided while recording nothing about how routinely they use digital services). The material contains neither. "Rapporten räknar bokningar, inte unika personer" governs what the report counts, not what it may otherwise record. "Telefonbokningarna kan därför inte användas för att säga hur stor andel av invånarna som inte kan boka digitalt" governs what the phone-booking count can show about residents, not whether the report holds any ability measure. "Funktionsförmåga" is functional capacity in the disability sense, not task-specific digital skill, so it does not exclude the case either. With no excluding statement to quote, the case stands, and the draft's third list item is unsupported as written.

The checker also tested the converse direction (a frequency-of-use log, excluded by "Den mäter inte … digital vana"), so both directions were tested as required. Its proposed repair — "digital habits" — is the smallest supported change and restores the dimension the material names, which is also the dimension the proposed trial sets out to investigate ("be användarna frivilligt ange varför de väljer telefon eller webb"). The neighbouring sentence "cannot tell us what share of Lervik's residents cannot book online" already carries the ability point, so nothing in the paragraph depends on reading the third item as proficiency.

Not a translation finding: the passage is not quoted speech, so the checker's classification of it as a source-support finding (meaning changed in crossing languages, outside quoted speech) is correct.
