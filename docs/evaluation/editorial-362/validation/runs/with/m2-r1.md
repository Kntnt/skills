## 2. Dispositions

### Source-support findings: **none** — upheld

The report proposes no repair, so there is no finding to reject. I re-tested the passages where the report names a difference and sets it aside, and verified in each case that the quoted statement excludes the candidate case.

- **C9, "But the paper names neither a prospective tenant nor a rental figure."** Set aside at Case A on the ground that the material's "No prospective tenant or rental figure is named." belongs to its account of the paper. Verified in the fixture: that sentence stands third in the Material paragraph, between "It says predictable income would help meet the hall's maintenance costs." and "The wing is currently empty; the hall's main rooms continue to host their usual activities." The header also declares "This is the complete source package." The attribution to the paper is the only reading the run of sentences leaves. The draft asserts only that none is named, not that none exists. Upheld.
- **C16, "The survey cannot promise that a rehearsal room would pay its way."** Set aside at Case B against Reed's "not a promise that it will pay its way." The capability form would add unsupported strength only if this survey could have yielded such a promise; the material forecloses that: "The survey did not ask what they would pay or how often they would attend." An instrument that asked neither cannot yield a paying-its-way promise. Upheld.
- **C20, "it does not establish whether that consent exists."** Set aside at Case C against "The supplied material does not state the consent's actual status." The candidate that would break it — a submission positively establishing refusal — is excluded by that same sentence, the submission being part of the described package ("The association's submission makes no representation that it has secured the building owner's consent for rehearsal use."). The clause asserts nothing about the fact of consent, only about what the submission establishes, so the converse candidate leaves it standing too. Upheld.
- **T1/C1 possessives, "Bellwick Hall's wing", "its former storage wing."** Set aside at Case D. The material attaches the wing to the hall ("The trustees' paper… proposes letting the former storage wing"; the brief: "the trustees of Bellwick Hall… the former storage wing"), and the draft concedes an open owner question one paragraph later ("makes no claim to have secured the building owner's consent"), which is incompatible with reading the possessive as a title claim. No ownership is asserted. Upheld.
- **C6, "makes a serious case."** Set aside at Case E. It is the author's evaluation in a signed opinion piece, restated as hers in the next sentence ("That concern deserves weight."), and the brief directs it ("treating the trustees' financial concern seriously"). It attributes nothing to the paper beyond "It says predictable income would help meet the hall's maintenance costs." Upheld.
- **C21, "while."** Set aside at Case F. The two facts are supplied side by side in one sentence of the material ("The wing is currently empty; the hall's main rooms continue to host their usual activities."), and the draft draws no inference from the emptiness. No causal or concessive claim beyond the supplied contrast. Upheld.

Independently checked, and supported at subject, scope and qualification:

- "Of 14 local music groups surveyed, nine said they would consider using a room on weekday evenings. Those responses are expressions of interest, not booking commitments." against "Room to Rehearse surveyed 14 local music groups. Nine said they would consider using a rehearsal room on weekday evenings. This is an expression of interest, not a booking commitment." Denominator and numerator both present; no evaluative upgrade of nine; "a room" is weaker than, not stronger than, "a rehearsal room".
- "Our treasurer's signed note confirms that we have not prepared an estimate." against "Its treasurer's signed note explicitly confirms that the association has not prepared a cost estimate for converting the wing." Scope of "an estimate" is fixed by the preceding sentence, "We must be equally clear about conversion costs."
- "I am asking for an eight-week deferral, without claiming the study can be completed within that period." against "There is no evidence that a study can be completed within eight weeks; this is her requested timetable." The draft declines the claim the material warns is unevidenced.
- "Trustees should defer the three-year letting decision for eight weeks, commission the study and publish the comparison before choosing." against "Her proposal is that the trustees defer the three-year letting decision for eight weeks, obtain the feasibility study and publish a comparison of the two uses." All three limbs, in order, at the same modality.
- "a comparison should help establish that", following "Commercial letting may prove the better option". Read in context the pronoun takes the preceding proposition, and the sentence predicts no winner; the stronger reading, that the comparison will vindicate commercial letting, is conceivable but not what the sentence asserts, and a merely conceivable stronger reading is not a defect. Supported by "She accepts that commercial letting may prove the better option" and "publish a comparison of the two uses".
