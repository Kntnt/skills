## 2. Finding dispositions

### Finding 1 — "digital proficiency" for "digital vana" — **REJECTED**

- **Draft passage (exact):** "The report does not measure age, functional ability, or digital proficiency."
- **Source passage (exact):** "Rapporten räknar bokningar, inte unika personer. Den mäter inte ålder, funktionsförmåga eller digital vana. Telefonbokningarna kan därför inte användas för att säga hur stor andel av invånarna som inte kan boka digitalt."

**What the draft asserts in its full context.** The sentence is the third of a three-sentence paragraph whose subject throughout is the pilot report's evidentiary blindness: it counts bookings ("Those are bookings, not individual people"), and it records nothing about the people behind them. The proposition asserted is that the report holds no measurement of any user attribute — age, bodily/cognitive function, or the user's standing with digital services. It is a negative claim about the contents of a named document, and it is used for exactly one inferential purpose, stated in the next sentence: "The phone bookings therefore cannot tell us what share of Lervik's residents cannot book online."

**Why the supplied material supports that proposition.** The source's own "därför" grounds the impossibility of any *ability* statement ("hur stor andel av invånarna som inte **kan** boka digitalt") in the immediately preceding list of non-measurements. For that inference to be the material's own, the list must be read as exhausting what the report knows about users' relationship to digital booking — capability included. The material reinforces this twice: "Rapporten räknar bokningar, inte unika personer" (no person-level record exists to attach an attribute to) and "Den mäter inte … funktionsförmåga" (no ability measurement). The draft's negative claim thus falls inside what the material states, with subject (the report), scope (what it measures about users) and polarity (negation) intact.

**Concrete mismatch in the report's reading.** The finding rests on a constructed case — "the pilot report includes a short check of whether participants could complete the online booking form unaided … but records nothing about how often or how routinely they use digital services" — and asserts that "nothing supplied excludes it". Two supplied statements do bear on it. A per-participant unaided-completion check is a measured attribute of identified users, which "Rapporten räknar bokningar, inte unika personer" denies the report has; and if such an ability measure existed, the material's own "därför" inference — that nothing in the report licenses a statement about who **cannot** book digitally — would not follow from the sentence it is drawn from. The case is therefore not merely unexcluded-and-neutral; it contradicts the reading the material itself performs on that list.

**What remains, and why it is not a defect.** The report is right that *vana* names habituation and that the draft's list item does not carry the habit dimension. That is a lost shade present only in the source, and the task's standard is explicit that a qualification or preferred wording present only in the source settles neither kind of finding. Nothing false or unsupported enters the draft; the habit question is in any case carried elsewhere in the draft on its own supplied basis — "But a phone booking is a reason to investigate why someone chose that option" against "Det är ett skäl att ta reda på varför telefonen fortfarande används" and "be användarna frivilligt ange varför de väljer telefon eller webb". The proposed repair ("digital habits") is a defensible alternative rendering, not a required correction.

This is a rejected finding, not genuine material ambiguity: the draft's proposition is determinate in context and the supplied material settles it.
