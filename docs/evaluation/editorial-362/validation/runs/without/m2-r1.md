## 3. Disposition of the report's findings

The report records **no source-support findings** (§5) and **translation not applicable** (§5, §6). There is therefore no finding to accept or reject. What follows is the verification that this empty finding set is itself correct, plus the verification of the near cases the report cleared, since each is a claim the report asserts about the draft.

### Verification of the cleared near cases

- **Case A — C9 "But the paper names neither a prospective tenant nor a rental figure."** Upheld. The source sentence "No prospective tenant or rental figure is named." sits in the material's consecutive account of the trustees' paper, immediately after "It says predictable income would help meet the hall's maintenance costs." and before "The wing is currently empty". The draft attributes the non-naming to the paper and asserts nothing about whether a tenant exists. Contextual meaning matches; no defect.
- **Case B — C16 "The survey cannot promise that a rehearsal room would pay its way."** Upheld. Reed's supplied position is "The survey is a reason to investigate a rehearsal room, not a promise that it will pay its way," and the material adds "The survey did not ask what they would pay or how often they would attend." The capability form is a statement about this survey's limits, not a general claim about surveys, and the two unasked questions are exactly the inputs such a promise would require. No defect.
- **Case C — C20 "it does not establish whether that consent exists."** Upheld. The draft's subject is the submission, and its predicate is about what the submission establishes, not about the fact of consent. The material supplies both "The association's submission makes no representation that it has secured the building owner's consent for rehearsal use." and "The supplied material does not state the consent's actual status." The draft leaves the status open in both directions, matching the material. No defect.
- **Case D — "Bellwick Hall's wing" (T1), "its former storage wing" (C1).** Upheld. The possessives attach the wing to the hall as a part of it, matching "the former storage wing" whose letting the hall's trustees propose. A title reading is incompatible with the draft's own concession at C19 that the building owner's consent is unsettled. No defect.
- **Case E — C6 "makes a serious case".** Upheld. The frontmatter declares `genre: opinion`, the byline names the author, and the next sentence restates the judgement as hers ("That concern deserves weight."). The brief asks for exactly this posture: "treating the trustees' financial concern seriously". No defect.
- **Case F — C21 "while".** Upheld. The material states the two facts side by side ("The wing is currently empty; the hall's main rooms continue to host their usual activities."), and the draft's "while" carries at most that same contrast. No causal claim is made in either direction. No defect.
