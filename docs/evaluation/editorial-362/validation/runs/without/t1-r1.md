### Finding 1 — "digital proficiency" for "digital vana" — **REJECTED**

- **Draft passage (exact):** "Those are bookings, not individual people. The report does not measure age, functional ability, or digital proficiency."
- **Source passage (exact):** "Rapporten räknar bokningar, inte unika personer. Den mäter inte ålder, funktionsförmåga eller digital vana."

**What the draft actually asserts in context.** The two sentences work as one proposition: the pilot report records no attribute of the people behind the bookings, which is what licenses the next sentence, "The phone bookings therefore cannot tell us what share of Lervik's residents cannot book online." The third list item is one instance of that enumeration, not a free-standing claim about a skill test.

**Why the material supports it.** The supplied passage states the same proposition twice over — the report "räknar bokningar, inte unika personer" and "mäter inte ålder, funktionsförmåga eller digital vana". A report that counts bookings rather than unique persons, and that records neither age nor functional ability, records no per-person attribute; digital skill is such an attribute. The draft's negative claim thus falls inside the territory the material establishes, with subject (the report), scope (what it measures) and polarity (negation) intact.

**Concrete mismatch in the report's reading.** Finding 1's decisive case — "the pilot report includes a short check of whether participants could complete the online booking form unaided — a proficiency measure" — is conceded in the report itself to rest on nothing supplied ("nothing supplied excludes it"). It is a conceivable alternative content for the report, not a reading the material admits; it also runs against "Rapporten räknar bokningar, inte unika personer", which is the material's own characterisation of what that report contains. A merely conceivable stronger reading does not establish a defect.

**The second half of the allegation does not survive either.** "Drops the one it does" — the *vana* dimension — is a qualification present only in the source. The draft asserts nothing false about habit; it simply does not raise it, and the habit question is in fact carried elsewhere in the draft ("a phone booking is a reason to investigate why someone chose that option", against "Det är ett skäl att ta reda på varför telefonen fortfarande används"). A source-only qualification settles neither kind of finding.

**Not a translation finding.** The phrase sits outside quoted speech; the report classifies it correctly as a source-support question (§2 preamble). No resolved composition or quotation guidance bears on it.

**Repair not required.** The report's proposed edit ("digital habits") is an equally supported wording, not a correction of a defect. Wording preference does not settle the finding.
