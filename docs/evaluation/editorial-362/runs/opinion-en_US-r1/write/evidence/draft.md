# Test both booking channels before closing one

*By Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut*

On June 18 the municipal executive board takes up a proposal to end phone booking of Lervik's association venues from September. It should postpone that switch and first run six months with both phone and web open. Six months would give the board what it does not have today: figures on what each route costs and what it is for.

## What the pilot actually shows

The municipality's pilot report of April 8, 2026 covers an eight-week trial in two venues. During it, 96 bookings were made online and 24 by phone. The report counts bookings, not unique people, and it measures nothing about age, ability or digital habits. Those 24 calls therefore cannot tell anyone what share of Lervik's residents are unable to book online.

That is a limit of the report, not a fault in it. It becomes a problem only when the report is used to settle the question it was never built to answer.

## The staff memo asks for more than it establishes

The memo before the June 18 meeting proposes removing phone booking from all seven venues. Its reason is that staff should not have to enter the same details in two separate flows. Double administration is the administration's real objection, and it deserves a serious answer rather than a dismissal. But the documents contain no time measurement and no calculation of what removing the channel would save. A permanent change is being proposed on a saving nobody has yet put a figure on.

## What six months would settle

Öppna beslut proposes keeping both routes in all seven venues for six months. During that time the administration records how much time each route takes per booking and asks users to say voluntarily why they chose the phone or the web.

A booking that comes in by phone is not proof that someone cannot use the web. It is a reason to find out why the phone is still being used. I am not against digital booking. I am against deciding before the municipality can weigh the staff time a channel costs against what it is worth to the people who use it.

## What the board can decide on June 18

The trial costs something, and we have neither funded nor costed it. That weighing is the board's to do. What I ask is that it hold off on the permanent switch and run the six months first. After that it can remove a channel, change it or keep it — on its own figures, rather than on one eight-week count of bookings.
