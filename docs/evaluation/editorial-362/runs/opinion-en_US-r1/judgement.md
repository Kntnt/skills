## 2. F1 — source support: **FAIL** (one defect, one lesser dropped caveat)

### F1-a (decisive) — a cost figure the proposal does not undertake to produce

- **Draft, lead:** "Six months would give the board what it does not have today: figures on what each route
  **costs** and what it is for."
- **Material:** "Under försöket ska förvaltningen registrera **tidsåtgång per bokningsväg**" — and, on what is
  missing today, "Någon **tidsmätning** eller **ekonomisk besparingsberäkning** finns inte i handlingarna."

This is a changed thing measured. The material itself holds time and money apart, listing *tidsmätning* and
*ekonomisk besparingsberäkning* as two separate absent items; recording the first does not produce the second.
A six-month log of minutes per route, with no rate, overhead or channel-level running cost applied, satisfies
"registrera tidsåtgång per bokningsväg" in full and still leaves the board without any figure for what a route
costs. No supplied statement excludes that case: Sanna Ek's "väga **arbetskostnaden** mot värdet för
användarna" states her aim, not that the recorded time will be converted into cost, and "Kostnaden behöver
kommunstyrelsen ta ställning till" is about the cost of the *trial*, which the draft correctly leaves to the
board. The reverse direction fails too — a board handed costed per-route figures would satisfy the draft while
going beyond what the proposal records.

The defect is also internally inconsistent with the rest of the draft, which twice gets the distinction right:
the body says "records how much time each route takes" and the fifth paragraph says "weigh the **labor cost** a
channel carries". Reader effect: the lead sells the six months as producing a costing the association has not
proposed to produce — in a piece whose whole argument is that the board should not decide on figures it does
not have.

Severity note: "costs" can be read loosely as "what it takes". I do not rest the fail on a strict reading
alone, but on the material's own explicit separation of the two measures, reinforced by the draft's own use of
the source's term elsewhere. On this material the loose reading is not available.

### F1-b (lesser) — voluntariness dropped in the lead

- **Draft, lead:** "would give the board ... **what it is for**."
- **Material:** "be användarna **frivilligt** ange varför de väljer telefon eller webb."

The lead states an outcome; the material supports only that users will be *asked*, and may decline. The body
restores the caveat ("asks users to say voluntarily why they chose the phone or the web"), and "would give" is
conditional, so this is a compression rather than an invention. It is the same failure shape as F1-a — the
lead converts a proposed activity into a promised yield — and pass 2 saw it and set it aside on the strength of
the body. I record it as a real but non-decisive caution, not as a second fail.

### F1-c (minor) — design intent imputed to the report

"the question it was never **built** to answer" characterises the report's design purpose. The material
establishes what the report counts and does not measure and that the phone bookings "kan därför inte användas"
for the share question, but says nothing about what the report was built for. A near-equivalent without the
intent claim ("the question it cannot answer") is available. Minor; not a fail driver.

Everything else holds at the strength the material carries, including in both directions across the languages:

## 3. G2 — genre parts (Opinion): **PASS**

- **Early position.** Second sentence of the lead: "It should postpone that switch and first run six months
  with both phone and web open." Named actor, named decision, named alternative, in the modality the brief
  supplies.
- **Support.** Two distinct lines that do different work: what the pilot can and cannot carry (§"What the
  pilot actually shows") and what the decision basis lacks (§"The staff memo asks for more than it
  establishes"). The numbers are attributed to the report where they bear the argument, as the boundaries
  require.
- **Relevant real objection.** "Double administration is the administration's real objection, and it deserves
  a serious answer rather than a dismissal." This is the opponent's actual and strongest objection, taken at
  full strength, with the motives the material rules out (lazy or expensive phone users) pointedly not
  imputed. It is answered, not merely acknowledged: the proposed six months records time per route, which is
  what would price the double administration. A second concession — "The trial costs something, and we make no
  claim to have funded or costed it" — keeps the piece honest about its own ask.
- **Identifiable action and actor.** "What I ask is that it hold off on the permanent switch and run the six
  months first", addressed to the municipal executive board, at a dated meeting, followed by the three options
  it may then take.
- **Byline** is the supplied one; headings orient; no commercial call to action; 443 words against the
  brief's "ungefär 400". The close names the board's possible decisions with the cost uncertainty intact.

Reader effect: a reader knows within two sentences what is asked of whom, why the existing evidence will not
bear the decision, and what the author does *not* claim.

## 4. L1 — language: **PASS**, with two blemishes

The prose reads as American English written by a native, not as translated Swedish: "takes up a proposal",
"hold off on the permanent switch", "a limit of the report, not a fault in it", "comes in by phone", "on its
own figures". Collective agreement is American ("the board ... it", "its own figures"), dates are American
("April 8, 2026", "On June 18"), *labor* is spelled to the locale, and the serial comma is consistently
omitted — a legitimate pick held throughout. The mandative subjunctive "that it hold off" is formal American,
not an archaism. No Swedish syntax survives: no fronted adverbials in the Swedish V2 pattern, no *that*-clause
calques, no run-on coordination.

Blemishes:

- **"Double administration is the administration's real objection"** — *dubbel administration* carried over as
  a bare English noun phrase. English handles this as duplicate work, double handling or entering everything
  twice; "double administration" is not an established collocation, and putting it beside "the administration"
  in the same clause makes one word do two jobs four words apart. A reader stumbles once here.
- **"asks users to say voluntarily why they chose"** — adverb placement tracking *frivilligt ange*; native
  phrasing would be "asks users to volunteer why they chose".

Two local stumbles in 443 otherwise idiomatic words do not make the piece read as a translation, so this is a
pass rather than a fail.
