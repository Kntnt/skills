Boundary compliance, checked directly:

"Timmen är bokad, alltså har något hänt" is the criticised inference voiced, not an occasion asserted — it renders the notes' "tid i kalendern lätt behandlas som ett resultat i sig", and the very next sentence re-marks the whole passage as the author's reflection. Differences I tested in both directions and cleared:

- **"Det här är en iakttagelse av ett dokument"** (source: "av dokumentet"). Definite → indefinite. The referent is the template named in the preceding sentence; nothing falls under the draft's term that does not fall under the source's here, because the sentence is characterising this one statement.
- **"Mallen vill veta när vi ses och vilka som kommer."** "vilka som kommer" glosses the `deltagare` box. A strained reading makes it an attendance claim rather than a planning field; on the ordinary reading of a planning form it names the box's content. No claim changes.
- **"Vad tiden ska leda till frågar den inte om."** Wider than the notes' "ingen ruta för det beslut". It is carried by the notes' own enumeration of which boxes exist, which is presented as exhaustive by the contrastive "men ingen ruta för…", together with "borde få formulera varför vi behöver just varandras tid" — a formulation the form does not currently provide for. Supported.
- **"få fram" for "upptäcka"** (conversations bringing out a question). Same subject, same object clause, same modal "kan", which the draft repeats rather than strengthens.
- **"Jag vet inte om den överlever mötet med en verklig dagordning."** The object of the doubt is recast: the notes doubt whether another box improves meetings, the draft doubts whether the question survives a real agenda. This is a **soft caution, not a defect** — it neither resolves the notes' doubt nor adds knowledge, and "Ändå vill jag prova" establishes the question as untried. It also does the genre's work, because the sentence is the author turning on her own proposal.
- **"får samsas ett tag till"** for "Jag vill behålla både hoppet … och tvivlet …". A wish becomes a permitting statement, and "ett tag till" adds a temporal frame the notes do not state. The strictest reading is that a coexistence lasting "a while yet" implies an eventual end that the notes do not promise. I record this as the **one genuine soft edge in the draft**, below the threshold for a finding: Swedish "ett tag till" leaves the horizon open rather than scheduling a resolution, and the unresolvedness it marks is exactly what the notes assert.

Nothing here rises to an unsupported addition, so F1 passes.

## 3. G2 — column form: **pass**

The required job of a column is personal reflection, without a compulsory anecdote and without a campaign. The draft does all three.

- **Personal reflection, from an allowed opening.** It opens on the concrete document observation, which the boundaries call "en fullgod öppning", and never manufactures a remembered scene to replace it. The author's eye is visible throughout: the noticing, the irritation, the concession, the objection to herself.
- **Not a campaign, no purchase call.** The nearest thing to advocacy is "Ändå vill jag prova en enkel fråga bredvid tiden", framed as her own experiment and immediately doubted. No reader is instructed, no product or service appears.
- **Motivated return.** The box motif is established in the title ("Rutan som inte finns"), used as the observation's hinge, turned into the joke at the author's expense ("lösa ett problem med formulär genom att utöka formuläret"), and closed on ("tvivlet på ännu en ruta"). The return is earned by the argument, not by repetition.
- **Working rhythm.** The two-sentence second paragraph ("En dagordning räknar upp vad vi ska tala om. Den säger inte varför vi behöver göra det tillsammans.") works as a beat between the long opening and the concession, and the short "Timmen är bokad, alltså har något hänt" lands the irritation. Paragraph lengths vary; no paragraph restates its predecessor.
- **Low-key humour, as briefed.** "Missförstå mig rätt", the form-solving-forms joke, and "komiken blir inte mindre av att förslaget kommer från mig" are quiet rather than performed.
- **Ending that keeps the uncertainty.** "jag tänker inte låtsas att jag gör det" and "får samsas ett tag till" refuse the tidy resolution the boundaries warn against.
- Title and byline are present as the brief requires; subheadings were optional and omitted, which suits 291 words.

Reader effect: a professional who plans meetings finishes with one usable question and an honest admission that it may not work — which is what a column, as opposed to an opinion piece, is for.

## 4. L1 — language: **pass**

- Idiom is genuinely Swedish and used correctly: "Missförstå mig rätt" (the established playful formula, not a calque), "retar mig på", "samsas", "ett tag till", "få fram en fråga".
- The best sentence in the draft turns on a Swedish double meaning that could not survive translation: "om den överlever **mötet** med en verklig dagordning" — the encounter with an agenda, in a text about meetings. That is writing in the language, not through it.
- Word order is natural Swedish, including the fronted-object inversions "Vad tiden ska leda till frågar den inte om" and "Det som retar mig är något annat", which an English-shaped draft would not produce.
- "kolleger" is a correct plural and is the notes' own form.
- The only mechanical quibble is the spaced em dash in paragraph 3 where Swedish convention prefers a spaced en dash; this is locale mechanics, which L1 explicitly separates out, and it does not touch the prose.
