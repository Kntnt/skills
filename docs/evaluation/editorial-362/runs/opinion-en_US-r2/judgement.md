## 2. F1 — last draft in evidence (`draft-2.md`, **not delivered**)

**Fail**, on one defect, in the most load-bearing line in the piece.

**Title: "Measure first, then remove the phone."** The material never has Sanna Ek asking for the phone channel to go. Her position is *skjuta upp … och först pröva ett halvår med både telefon och webb*, and the decision after the trial is explicitly three-way: *Därefter kan den avgöra om en kanal ska tas bort, ändras eller behållas*. *Hon motsätter sig inte digital bokning* denies opposition; it endorses nothing. An imperative headline over a signed opinion piece states the author's programme, and this one states removal as the step that follows measurement. It is an attribution defect, not a nuance: it puts a position in a named person's mouth that the supplied material withholds, and it contradicts the draft's own final sentence, which keeps all three outcomes. A reader who reads only the headline — most readers — takes away the opposite of what Ek is on record wanting.

Everything else in the draft holds. Checked and clear: the June 18 meeting, the seven facilities, the September start, the April 8 2026 report, its eight weeks and two facilities, the 96/24 split attributed to the report, *räknar bokningar, inte unika personer*, the three unmeasured attributes, the *kan därför inte användas* modality on what the phone bookings cannot show, the memo's stated motive kept as the memo's, the absence localised to *handlingarna*, the six-month proposal's scope and its two recorded things, the unclaimed costing, the board's duty to weigh the cost, and the three-way ending. No legal requirement, protest, discrimination case, party-political motive or established saving appears; no count-size judgement is made without the report behind it; the cost is left open throughout, as the boundaries require.

Five term and scope shifts I examined and do **not** count as defects, with reasons, since they are the places a stricter reading could differ:

- *funktionsförmåga* → "ability". "Ability" is the broader term, so in principle the report could measure some ability that is not *funktionsförmåga* and the draft's negative would fail where the source's holds. In this enumeration — "neither age, nor ability, nor digital habit" — "ability" is the ordinary English cover term for functional capacity, and the third conjunct already carries digital skill. Supported, narrowly.
- "enter **the same details** in two flows" for *föra in uppgifter i två flöden*. The specification is settled by the material's own name for the burden, *dubbel administration*.
- "close a channel **for the whole municipality**". Rhetorical scaling of *alla sju lokaler* against the pilot's two, in a signed opinion piece that states the count exactly one paragraph earlier. Not inflated beyond the material.
- "measure what each one **takes in staff time**" for *registrera tidsåtgång per bokningsväg*. Whose time is not stated outright, but *förvaltningen* does the recording and *arbetskostnaden* is her stated term, so the work side is the right side.
- "for the board to weigh **against what it would learn**". Her supplied weighing is *arbetskostnaden mot värdet för användarna*; the draft's is cost against knowledge gained. This is the author's framing of her own trade-off rather than a new fact, and *Kostnaden behöver kommunstyrelsen ta ställning till* is carried intact. Thinnest of the five, but inside advocacy.

Unknown is kept apart from absent throughout: the draft says the documents contain no time measurement, never that no one has one, and says the association makes no claim to have costed the trial, never that it did not cost it. Both distinctions were repaired into the draft, and they hold.

## 3. G2 (opinion) and L1

**G2: pass**, with the headline noted. Position early: the lede's second sentence states the ask — postpone, keep both paths six months, measure the staff time. Support: the report's own 96/24 and, harder-hitting, the absence of any time measurement or savings calculation in the decision material. Real objection, engaged rather than strawmanned: "That objection is real: double administration is work, and the administration is entitled to say so" — and the counter is that the documents put no number on it, which is criticism of the *beslutsunderlag* rather than of motive, exactly what the boundary allows. The cost objection is met head-on instead of dodged: "We make no claim to have costed this trial." Identifiable action and actor: a section headed "What the board can do on June 18", naming the municipal executive board and three verbs. The required parts do distinct jobs. The headline is the exception — it performs its orienting job but stakes a position the body then declines to hold, which is why it lands as an F1 defect rather than a missing part. 452 words against a ~400-word target; headings orient; no commercial CTA.

**L1: pass.** The prose reads as written, not translated: the lede's three-verb ask, the "can and cannot tell us" turn, the flat two-beat "It is a reason to find out why the phone is still being used", the mandative "proposes that phone booking be dropped" (standard formal American), the closing's "I am not against… I am against…" antithesis. Sentence lengths vary, and nothing carries Swedish word order. Three blemishes, all minor and none of them enough to fail: "in two flows" is a near-calque of *två flöden* where American usage would say *two systems*; "double administration" is *dubbel administration* carried over, and appears twice; "costed" leans British — an American opinion page would more likely write *priced out*. Reader effect is unimpeded in every case.

## 5. Stop or delivery

**A valid stop.** The last checked draft carries a genuine, unrepaired misattribution in its most-read line. Delivering it would have put a position in a named spokesperson's mouth that her supplied material withholds, under a headline contradicting the piece's own final sentence — precisely the failure the comparison exists to prevent. The run had a one-line fix on record and no budget left to check it, and it chose to stop and say so rather than ship unverified prose. `response.md` does the stop properly: it names the defect, quotes the supplied Swedish that defeats it, gives the exact repair, and tells the user what to change before re-running. It also reports honestly what the material could not carry (no cost figure, nothing about who uses the phone or why, no basis for generalising the pilot's two facilities to seven), which is the useful part of a stop.

The one cost worth naming: the defect is a four-word title change that the first checker cleared and the second caught. A run that spends both comparisons on the body and finds the headline defect only at the end has no way to close it, and the user pays with a re-run for a fix already written down.
