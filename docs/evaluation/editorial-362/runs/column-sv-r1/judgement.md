Three points I record as **cautions**, none of which I count as a failure:

1. **"Timmen är bokad, alltså har något hänt."** (para 3) has no sentence behind it in the notes. It is a gloss that voices the attitude she is criticising, not a claim in her own voice, and it particularises calendar time as an hour where the source says only "tid i kalendern". Since it asserts nothing about the world and nothing measured, it stays inside the note it restates — but it is the one line in the draft that a strict reading could call an addition.
2. **"gör möten bättre" → "gör våra möten bättre"** (para 6) narrows the source's general "möten" to her own workplace's. A narrowing claims less than the source, and it sits well with the preceding disclaimer, so it is safe; it is still a scope change and worth naming.
3. **The headline, "Mallen har en ruta för allt utom poängen."** "allt" is hyperbole over a template with four named boxes, and "poängen" can be read as *the decision* — which would put the headline at odds with a body that explicitly refuses to make decisions the point of every conversation. The better reading is "poängen" = *why we need each other's time*, which para 4 and para 5 do argue for, and the body lists the four boxes within two sentences, so the hyperbole self-corrects. Column-headline licence; not an unsupported claim.

No unknown was turned into an absent, and no absent into a known: the missing decision box is stated as what the template lacks, not as a claim that no such template exists elsewhere.

## 3. G2 — column form: **pass**

The required job for a column is *personal reflection*, without a compulsory anecdote and without a campaign.

- **Personal reflection, visibly hers.** First person throughout, and the reflection moves rather than restates: observation of the document (para 1) → what she values in meetings (para 2) → what irritates her, fenced off as unmeasured (para 3) → the ask she thinks a template should permit (para 4) → the proposed question (para 5) → the doubt about her own proposal (paras 6–7).
- **No fabricated anecdote.** The brief's boundaries say a concrete document observation is a sufficient opening, and the draft opens with exactly that.
- **Low-key humour, as invited.** "bibliotekarien som svarar på en mall med mer mall" and "Kanske blir frågan bara en rad till att fylla i" — self-ironic, not sneering, and aimed at her own proposal rather than at colleagues.
- **Not a campaign, not a pitch.** She concedes non-deciding conversations their value ("Inget av det kräver ett beslut") and demands nothing of the reader.
- **Motivated return.** The "ruta"/"mall" motif opens the piece, drives the joke, and closes it ("tvivlet på ännu en ruta") — a return earned by the argument, not a decorative echo.
- **An ending that keeps the uncertainty**, as the boundaries require: "behålla båda hållningarna så länge" resolves the *stance* without resolving the *question*.

No subheadings; the writer's reply justifies the choice by length, and at 288 words they would indeed have fragmented a piece whose movement is continuous. Correct call.

## 4. L1 — Swedish prose: **pass**

Native syntax and idiom throughout, with no English under the surface.

- Idiomatic fronting and inversion that a translator would not produce: "Någon ruta för det beslut vi ska kunna fatta när vi går därifrån finns inte."; "Timmen är bokad, alltså har något hänt."; "Där börjar tvivlet."
- Genuinely Swedish turns: "tål att motiveras", "med den räckvidd en reflektion har", "så länge" in its Swedish sense of *for now*, "retar jag mig på".
- Rhythm varies deliberately — the two short parallel sentences in para 4 ("Samtal kan skapa förtroende. Samtal kan hitta en fråga…") against the single-sentence pivot of para 5.
- No anglicisms, no calqued prepositions, no abstract noun-stacking, no throat-clearing openers.

One small wobble: "Samtal kan **hitta** en fråga" gives an abstract subject a verb that slightly prefers an agent; "upptäcka", the source's own verb, would have read more naturally. It is a blemish, not an idiom failure.

Locale mechanics, judged separately and only as context: spaced em dash, lowercase after colon, no stray English punctuation — all correct for Swedish.
