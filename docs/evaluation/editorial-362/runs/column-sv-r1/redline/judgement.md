That source caveat has two halves. The second half — *not something I have measured on others* — survives verbatim in force in the retained sentence `Jag har inte mätt det här hos andra`, and the boundary against asserting a general trend survives in `jag påstår inget om möten i allmänhet`. This is a loss of the caveat's explicit framing, not of its protective function. After the edit the paragraph still disclaims measurement and still disclaims any general claim about meetings, so no reader can take the passage as evidence-backed or as a trend claim, which is what the source's boundary exists to prevent. The source also asks for a stance that is `nyfiken och självironisk, inte mötesfientlig`, and for `ett slut som får behålla osäkerheten`; both survive intact, the latter word for word in the final paragraph's `behålla båda hållningarna`.
