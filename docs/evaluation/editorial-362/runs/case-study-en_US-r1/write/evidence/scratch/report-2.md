## 2. Findings (source support) - three, all minor

**F1 - the lead's exclusion is a different exclusion**
Draft: "31 reports entered, emergencies and earlier work excluded". Source: "It excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial."
*What differs:* the source excludes by the date work was **ordered**; the draft excludes by work being **earlier**, which a reader attaches to when the fault arose or was reported.
*Case that stands:* a fault reported in August 2025 but ordered in week two of the trial and entered in the log - counted in the 31 under the source, read as excluded under the draft. Tested the other way too: work ordered before the trial but carried out inside it is excluded by the source yet need not read as "earlier work". Nothing supplied excludes either case; "work ordered before the trial" is the only exclusion given.
*Smallest repair:* "emergencies and work ordered earlier excluded" - same length, and the same scope the draft's own D23 already states.

**F2 - "the maintenance supervisor" asserts uniqueness the material does not supply**
Draft: "Maya Lind, the maintenance supervisor". Source: "maintenance supervisor Maya Lind".
*What differs:* the bare appositive names her role without committing to her being its only holder; the definite article does.
*Case that stands:* Elm Quay runs morning and evening shifts (established by Q1) with a maintenance supervisor on each, Lind being one. The material names one team but never says how many supervisors it has, and supplies no statement excluding this.
*Smallest repair:* "Maintenance supervisor Maya Lind explains the aim and the division of work:" (mirrors the source), or "a maintenance supervisor".

**F3 - "Lind's advice" recasts a stated intention as a recommendation**
Draft: "Lind's advice for the next building concerns the work on those categories:". Source: "I would set that time aside before the next building starts", plus "Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company."
*What differs:* stance and modality. The quotation is a first-person conditional about what she would do; "advice" attributes a recommendation addressed to someone. The one thing the material says about her recommending is a limit on it.
*Case that stands:* Lind states only what she herself would do before Elm Quay's next building, having recommended nothing to anyone - compatible with the quotation as supplied and with the non-recommendation sentence. No supplied sentence says she advised anything.
*Smallest repair:* "What Lind would do differently concerns the work on those categories:".
