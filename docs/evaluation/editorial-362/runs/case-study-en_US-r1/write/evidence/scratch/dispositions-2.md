## Dispositions

All three findings are rejected, each on draft and source evidence. No prose change is made after the second comparison.

**F1 — "emergencies and earlier work excluded" is a different exclusion. REJECTED.**
Evidence. The source criterion is temporal: "It excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial." The draft's compression is temporal in the same direction: work that is earlier. The report's first case — a fault reported in August but ordered in week two and done inside the trial — requires reading "earlier work" as work whose *report* is earlier; the noun in the draft's phrase is the work, and a job ordered and carried out inside the trial is not earlier work. Its second case — work ordered before the trial and carried out inside it — is work whose origin precedes the trial, which "earlier work" does cover. The report's own proposed repair ("work ordered earlier") differs from the draft by word order, which is itself evidence that the thing excluded has not changed. The exact criterion is stated in the body, in the same reader's path: "excluding emergencies and work ordered before the trial." What the report establishes is that the lead is less precise than the body, not that it asserts an exclusion the material denies; a merely conceivable stronger reading does not establish a defect.

**F2 — "the maintenance supervisor" asserts uniqueness. REJECTED, and the closest call of the three.**
Evidence. The definite description is anchored two sentences after "the maintenance team decided to try a shared repair log": it identifies the supervisor of the team just introduced, which is what the material supports — Lind is interviewed as that team's voice ("Customer interview with maintenance supervisor Maya Lind") and speaks for it inside her quotations ("We wanted the evening shift…", "The categories were ours"). The sentence's assertion is what she explains; the number of people holding the title is not part of what it states, and no claim in the piece rests on it. The report's case (a supervisor per shift) is compatible with the material but bears on nothing the draft asserts. Treating an anchored definite role description as an assertion of sole holding would make every definite article before a role a finding. Recorded as the nearest to genuine of the three: the bare appositive the source uses would have carried the same content, and a reviewer may reasonably prefer it. That is a preference about wording, not a proposition the material contradicts.

**F3 — "Lind's advice" recasts a stated intention. REJECTED.**
Evidence. Two different objects are conflated in the report's reading. The material's "Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company" concerns recommending *the supplier* to *other companies*. The draft's bridge concerns *how much preparation time to allow* before *Elm Quay's own next building*, and the quotation it prepares is itself forward-looking and prescriptive at exactly that scope: "I would set that time aside before the next building starts." The bridge addresses no reader, extends nothing to other companies, and her actual words follow immediately, so the reader judges the strength of the remark from the quotation itself. Modality is preserved: "advice for the next building" reports the topic of a first-person conditional, and the draft nowhere says she recommends the log, the supplier or the trial to anybody.
