## 2. Findings

Ordered by severity. Each states the support that is missing or changed, a concrete case compatible with the material in which one statement holds and the other fails, and the smallest supported repair.

### F6 — The workload qualification is attributed to the note (body, "What the note records")

- Draft: "**The note states that** the two periods carried different workloads, and it does not attribute the difference to the software."
- Source: "The periods had different workloads, and **the note** explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software."
- What differs: whose assertion. The material asserts the differing workloads in its own voice and attributes only the non-attribution to the note.
- Case: the 4 December note contains the counts, the two medians and a line declining any causal claim, while the differing workloads are established elsewhere in Elm Quay's records and reach this account that way. Nothing in the material excludes it; no supplied statement places the workload finding inside the note.
- Smallest repair: "The two periods carried different workloads, and the note does not attribute the difference to the software."

### F2 — The internal note is made the maintenance team's (summary)

- Draft: "**The team's own note** records 31 reports entered …"
- Source: "**Elm Quay's internal trial note**, 4 December 2025, says that 31 repair reports were entered."
- What differs: the subject, that is, whose document and therefore whose assertion it is. Internal to Elm Quay does not name the maintenance team as author, and "own" adds a self-reporting frame the material does not supply.
- Case: the note was written by Elm Quay's administration reviewing the trial rather than by the maintenance team that ran it. Compatible with the material; nothing names the note's author.
- Smallest repair: "Elm Quay's own internal note records …" (the body already gets this right).

### F7 — Lind's personal, qualified assessment is presented as the team's verdict

- Draft: heading "**The team's own** verdict", followed immediately by a block quotation with no speaker named in place.
- Source: "Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company. **Her** final quotation is **her actual, qualified assessment**."
- What differs: the subject and whose knowledge it is. The material marks the quotation as Lind's own assessment; the heading promotes it to a collective verdict, and with no attribution above the quotation an ordinary reader takes the team to be speaking.
- Case: two of the six trained staff would not run the trial again, and "I would choose to do the trial again" remains Lind's own view. Compatible with the material, which records no view but hers.
- Smallest repair: retitle "Lind's own verdict" and attribute the quotation in place, e.g. "Lind's overall assessment:" before it.

### F5 — "Chosen on one test" asserts a sole basis for the choice

- Draft: heading "Chosen on **one** test".
- Source: "It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair." / "No comparison with another supplier is available."
- What differs: scope. The material records one test that was carried out; it does not say the decision rested on that test and nothing else. The absence of a supplier comparison in the material is an absence of material, not evidence that no other criterion applied.
- Case: the team also checked price and that telephone reporting could stay open, and the status test is simply the one this account reports. Compatible with the material; no supplied statement excludes other criteria.
- Smallest repair: "Chosen after a status test" (or "What the team tested").

### F1 — The title fixes the number of shifts at two

- Draft: "Elm Quay Housing gave **two shifts** one repair log."
- Source: "The team wanted staff **on different shifts** to see the same information." / Lind: "the evening shift … the morning shift".
- What differs: number. The material names a morning and an evening shift inside one quotation about what the team wanted; it never states that these are all the shifts there are.
- Case: Elm Quay runs morning, evening and night cover, and Lind names the two shifts her example concerns. Compatible with the material; nothing caps the count. The base contract holds this boundary in the title as much as in the body.
- Smallest repair: "Elm Quay Housing gave its shifts one repair log."

### F8 — "not been extended" changes what is denied and drops "yet"

- Draft: "The trial has **not been extended**."
- Source: "The trial has **not yet expanded**."
- What differs: the thing denied (reach → duration or continuation) and the openness carried by "yet".
- Case: after the eight weeks the two buildings carried on using the log while the team checks the categories, so use was in fact continued while the trial was never taken to further buildings. Compatible with the material, which says the trial lasted eight weeks and that a decision is still to come, but never that use stopped.
- Smallest repair: "The trial has not yet expanded to other buildings."

### F3 — The summary gives the count and the comparison without their scope and qualification

- Draft: "The team's own note records 31 reports entered and a median two working days from report to assignment, against three in the eight weeks before — a difference it does not attribute to the software."
- Source: "31 repair reports were entered. **It excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial.** … **The periods had different workloads**, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software."
- What differs: scope of the count, and one of the two qualifications on the comparison. The contract requires chronology, scope and qualifications to be preserved wherever a claim appears, and a count to be given with its exclusions. The summary carries the non-attribution but not the exclusions, so a reader meets "31 reports" as the trial's volume of repair reports.
- Case: 48 reports arrived in the two buildings during the eight weeks, 17 of them emergencies that never entered the log; the note still records 31 entered. Compatible with the material, and precisely what the exclusion sentence allows.
- Smallest repair: "… records 31 reports entered, emergencies and earlier work excluded, and a median two working days …".

### F4 — "the same reports" narrows the team's stated aim (summary)

- Draft: "so that staff on different shifts could see **the same reports**".
- Source: "The team wanted staff on different shifts to see **the same information**."
- What differs: the thing named. "Information" covers the status of each repair and what an earlier shift had already done; "reports" is only the incoming item.
- Case: the evening shift's gain is seeing that the morning shift has already assigned and attended a fault — status, not the report text — which is what Lind's "see what the morning shift had already done" describes. Compatible with the material, and the draft's own body keeps "the same information".
- Smallest repair: "so that staff on different shifts could see the same information".
