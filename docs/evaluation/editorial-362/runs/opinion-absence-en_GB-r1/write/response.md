# Test the rehearsal case before letting the wing

*Amina Reed, chair of Room to Rehearse*

The trustees' paper of 3 July proposes letting the former storage wing commercially for three years. Before they commit the space for that long, they should establish what it could do on weekday evenings instead.

The paper's reasoning deserves respect. Predictable income would help meet the hall's maintenance costs, and that upkeep has to be paid for somehow. Anyone asking the trustees to pause owes them a serious answer on money rather than a sentimental one.

So here is ours, gaps included. We asked fourteen local music groups whether they would consider a rehearsal room on weekday evenings, and nine said they would. We did not ask what they would pay or how often they would come, so I will not dress those nine answers up as bookings. The survey is a reason to investigate a rehearsal room, not a promise that it will pay its way.

We have no costing either. Our treasurer has confirmed in writing that we have prepared no estimate of what converting the wing would take. That is the weakest point in our own case, and it is exactly what a feasibility study — sound insulation, access, likely running costs, potential income — is for. The trustees' paper, for its part, names neither a tenant nor a rental figure.

The wing stands empty today, and nothing I am proposing would displace the usual activities in the hall's main rooms. Commercial letting may still prove the better use of it.

What I am asking the trustees for is eight weeks: defer the three-year decision, commission the feasibility study, and publish a comparison of the two uses before choosing.
