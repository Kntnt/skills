## V1 and the disputed digital-vana finding

Source: “Den mäter inte ålder, funktionsförmåga eller digital vana.” Draft: “The report measures neither age nor functional ability nor familiarity with digital technology.”

The third variable occurs between a distinction about bookings versus people and a conclusion about residents’ ability to book online. The next sentences distinguish using the telephone from inability and ask why that route is used. In that setting, “familiarity with digital technology” naturally denotes users’ practical familiarity with digital use. It does not assert a measured proficiency level, a digital-skills score or knowledge of technology as an academic topic. This is the familiarity/experience sense expressly allowed by V1.

**Source to draft:** a report that counts bookings but does not measure users’ accustomedness to digital use also does not measure that practical digital familiarity. The second checker imagines a quiz of knowledge learned from printed manuals. Such a quiz is compatible with the source, but its existence alone would not falsify the draft’s contextual claim about practical familiarity. To make it a counterexample, the checker first changes the target term into a broader knowledge construct that the article does not separately introduce. Compatibility of an imagined report is not proof of the asserted English meaning.

**Draft to source:** if the report does not measure users’ practical familiarity with digital use, it does not measure digital vana in the source sense. A report that does measure habitual digital use would contradict both statements in this context. Omitting a separate knowledge quiz would not rescue the English negative. The writer correctly identifies that defect in the checker’s reverse scenario, but still accepts its forward scenario without the necessary contextual meaning step.

The wording can be made more explicit. “How accustomed users are to using digital technology” is a supported optional clarification. That does not establish that the existing wording changes the variable. I classify the second report’s allegation **false as a mandatory source-support finding**, with a legitimate wording caution. This conclusion is independent of the first checker’s approval of that passage.
