## Independent source assessment and bidirectional tests

The article accurately distinguishes a proposal from a decision, retains the permanent-switch versus temporary-trial chronology, and attributes the load-bearing figures to the dated pilot report. “A wider trial” has a supplied comparison: two venues in the pilot versus all seven in the proposal. No count is described as high, low or representative. “I do not oppose digital booking” retains lack of opposition without upgrading it to endorsement. “Must” in the ending is Sanna's policy advocacy, not an invented legal obligation.

The remaining problem is the negative measurement statement. In context, `digital vana` concerns experience or familiarity from using digital tools. “Familiarity with digital technology” can also describe knowing or recognising technologies without such use. A report could ask whether respondents recognise QR codes or know what an authentication system does, yet collect nothing about actual digital use or habit. The supplied “Den mäter inte … digital vana” does not exclude that situation. On that reading, the source's absence claim holds while the draft's broader absence claim fails. The surrounding booking argument makes practical experience salient but does not explicitly restrict the English phrase to that experience.

The reverse direction was also tested. Recording frequency of a digital booking routine without testing general technological knowledge might distinguish the measurement instruments, but it does **not automatically prove** absence of familiarity measurement: routine use can itself provide evidence of familiarity. The second report's reverse example is therefore weaker than its forward example. This limits that part of the checker argument; it does not supply support for the draft's broader negative assertion. The smallest supported repair is “experience of using digital tools”, leaving age, functional ability, report scope and lack of measurement intact.

By contrast, “how many residents cannot book online” does not introduce a new ability measure, numerical estimate or claim about all possible ancillary evidence. Immediately before it, “Those figures describe bookings, not unique individuals” identifies the evidential object. The next sentence preserves Sanna's “Att en bokning går via telefon är inte ett bevis för att någon inte kan använda webben.” Those supplied booking figures alone establish neither an absolute resident inability count nor a proportion. In this context, the draft's reasoning is supported. Replacing “how many” with “what proportion” would be closer wording, but proximity is not proof of a necessary repair.
