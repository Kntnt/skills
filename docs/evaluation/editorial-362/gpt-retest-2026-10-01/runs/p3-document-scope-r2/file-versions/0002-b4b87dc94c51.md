**F1 — Source-support finding: the submission's evidential scope is not established**

Draft passage:

> Our submission also makes no claim to have secured the building owner’s consent for rehearsal use; it does not establish whether that consent exists.

Supplied support:

> The association's submission makes no representation that it has secured the building owner's consent for rehearsal use. The supplied material does not state the consent's actual status.

The first draft clause is supported. The second ordinarily makes a broader negative assertion about the submission. What the association represents itself as having secured is distinct from whether the submission establishes that consent exists. The source limits knowledge of actual status in this supplied package; it does not describe every statement or piece of evidence in the submission. This changes the subject of the limitation, its scope and the document whose evidential content is being characterised. It does not warrant a conclusion that consent exists or that it is absent.

Both directions were tested:

1. **Source true, draft clause false — a compatible case that stands.** The owner could have independently granted consent directly to the trustees, and the association's submission could attach the owner's signed permission while making no representation that the association secured it. The supplied summary could still contain exactly the statements given here and leave actual consent status unstated. In that case, the source's two statements hold, but the submission does establish that consent exists, contrary to the draft's second clause. No supplied statement excludes this case. The assertion “The supplied material does not state the consent's actual status” excludes disclosure in this package; it does not exclude the underlying submission containing such evidence.
2. **Draft clause true, source's package-status statement false — a reverse candidate that is excluded.** The submission could leave consent unresolved while another document in the supplied package explicitly records the owner's permission. That would make the draft's second clause true but the package-status statement false. This candidate cannot stand against the actual supplied material, which expressly says: “The supplied material does not state the consent's actual status.” The reverse direction therefore provides no additional compatible case. The surviving first-direction case is enough to establish the scope change.

These are counterexamples for testing the wording, not assertions about what happened in Bellwick. In particular, the finding does not turn missing information into a claim that consent was not obtained.

Smallest supported repair: delete the second clause and finish the supported first clause with a full stop:

> Our submission also makes no claim to have secured the building owner’s consent for rehearsal use.

This retains Reed's voice, the actual document attribution and the distinction between an unmade representation and consent's actual status. It does not require weakening the supported statement about the conversion estimate or changing any other passage. If an express statement of the remaining uncertainty is wanted, its subject must remain the supplied material; the source supports “The supplied material does not state the consent's actual status”, not the broader assertion about the submission.
