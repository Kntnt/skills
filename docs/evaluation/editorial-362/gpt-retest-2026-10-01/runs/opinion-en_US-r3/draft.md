# Lervik should delay ending phone bookings

A report on an eight-week pilot in two association venues counts bookings rather than unique people. Lervik’s municipal executive board should weigh staff workload against the value of each booking channel before making a permanent change for all seven venues.

By Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut

The staff report prepared for the municipal executive board’s June 18 meeting proposes removing phone booking at all seven association venues from September. I urge the board to postpone a permanent switch to digital booking alone and first run a six-month trial with both phone and web booking.

## The report does not measure digital familiarity

The municipality’s pilot report, *Bokning av föreningslokaler*, dated April 8, 2026, records 96 web bookings and 24 phone bookings during an eight-week trial in two venues. These are bookings, not unique people.

The report does not measure age, functional ability, or familiarity with digital technology. Those 24 phone bookings cannot establish what proportion of residents cannot book online. A phone booking is a reason to ask why that channel is used, not proof that someone cannot use the web.

## The workload argument needs a measurement

The staff report gives a concrete reason for removing phone access: avoiding the work of entering information through two workflows. That administrative concern deserves an answer.

But the documents contain no time measurements or calculation of financial savings. A workload argument without time measurements in the decision documents is an inadequate basis for a permanent switch. I do not oppose digital booking; the board must first be able to weigh the labor cost against the value to users.

## The board should authorize a trial and assess its cost

Öppna beslut proposes keeping both booking routes for six months at all seven venues. Staff should record the time spent on each route and ask users to say voluntarily why they choose phone or web.

Our association makes no claim to have funded the trial or estimated its cost. The municipal executive board should postpone the permanent switch, assess the trial’s cost, and authorize it. Afterward, it can decide whether to remove, change, or retain a booking channel.
