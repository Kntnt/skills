## Source-support findings

### SS-1: singular officer provenance

Draft: “The officer’s report for Lervik’s municipal executive board meeting on 18 June”.

Exact source: “Tjänsteutlåtandet inför kommunstyrelsens möte den 18 juni”.

The source names an administrative document, without a singular officer or its authorship. The draft’s singular possessive adds that provenance.

Two-direction test:
- A municipal department could issue the tjänsteutlåtande jointly through several officials, with no single officer owning or authoring it. The supplied material names the administration and document, but contains no statement excluding that case. The source’s designation holds; the singular officer provenance does not.
- Conversely, a single officer could produce a supporting report for the meeting that is not the formal tjänsteutlåtande. The draft’s generic description could hold while the specific source-document designation does not. No supplied statement equates every officer report with that formal document.

Smallest supported repair: replace “The officer’s report” with “The administrative report”. Preserve its meeting, dates, proposal and other substantive details.

### SS-2: geographical restriction on associations

Draft: “all seven venues for local associations”.

Exact source: “alla sju lokaler”; the premises are defined by “bokning av föreningslokaler”.

The source does not restrict associations to being locally based. Local audience and municipal setting do not supply that restriction.

Two-direction test:
- A nationwide association could use one of these association premises. The supplied “föreningslokaler” description remains true, but the restriction to “local associations” need not hold. No supplied statement excludes national associations or their bookings.
- Every venue for local associations is also a venue for associations; there is no reverse counterexample on this restriction. The asymmetry shows that the draft narrows the source.

Smallest supported repair: delete “local”: “all seven venues for associations”.

Both findings concern source support in translated descriptive prose, rather than translation of quoted speech.
