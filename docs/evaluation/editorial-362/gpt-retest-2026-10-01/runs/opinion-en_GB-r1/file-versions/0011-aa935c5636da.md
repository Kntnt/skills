## Source-support findings and bidirectional cases

### F1 — The absent digital variable changes

**Draft:** “It did not measure age, functional ability or familiarity with digital technology.”

**Source:** “Den mäter inte ålder, funktionsförmåga eller digital vana.”

**Difference:** The draft turns a statement about absent measurement of digital experience/practice into a statement about absent measurement of familiarity with digital technology. The latter can concern recognising technology or knowing what it is, without using it. The relevant factual assertion is the absence of that measurement, not the author's view of its importance.

**Source true, draft false:** The report includes a test asking whether participants recognise a QR code and know what an online authentication system does. That measures familiarity with digital technology. It includes no measure of whether or how regularly participants use digital services, so it does not measure “digital vana”. The supplied source excludes measurements of age, functional ability and digital experience; it contains no statement excluding a technology-recognition measure. This case therefore stands.

**Draft true, source false:** A hypothetical report measures the frequency of completing a fixed digital booking routine, thereby measuring digital practice, but includes no assessment of recognition or knowledge of digital technology more generally. The altered draft could accurately say no such familiarity measure exists, while the source’s exclusion of digital practice would fail. This reverse case illustrates the changed variable; it is necessarily excluded by the supplied statement “Den mäter inte … digital vana” and is not a second defect attributed to the actual source.

**Smallest supported repair:** Replace “familiarity with digital technology” with “experience of using digital tools”. Preserve the other two measured variables and the report-scoped absence.

**Classification:** Source-support finding caused by a changed meaning outside quoted speech. There is no translated-quotation finding.

### F2 — An excluded proportion becomes an excluded count

**Draft:** “We cannot use the telephone bookings to calculate how many residents cannot book online.”

**Source:** “Telefonbokningarna kan därför inte användas för att säga hur stor andel av invånarna som inte kan boka digitalt.” Relevant preceding qualifications: “Rapporten räknar bokningar, inte unika personer. Den mäter inte ålder, funktionsförmåga eller digital vana.” Sanna’s distinct point is: “Att en bokning går via telefon är inte ett bevis för att någon inte kan använda webben.”

**Difference:** “Hur stor andel” is a proportion, whereas “how many” reads as an absolute count. A missing population denominator can prevent a proportion without preventing an absolute count. The source also names inability to book digitally; the draft names inability to book online. The web is the discussed digital route, but using the source’s own term avoids expanding this separate population assertion.

**Source true, draft false:** In a compatible situation, the 24 telephone bookings are known through a separate one-booking-per-participant trial protocol to belong to 24 different residents who cannot complete the web booking process. The report itself still counts bookings, rather than reporting a unique-person count; the protocol and direct knowledge of those participants’ booking ability are not measurements of age, functional ability or digital experience. No resident-population denominator is established. The bookings, with that protocol, can yield an absolute number for these residents while they cannot yield their proportion of the municipality’s residents. A telephone booking by itself remains no proof of web inability, as Sanna says; the protocol and separately established inability supply that information. None of the supplied statements says that there is no such protocol, no ancillary booking-ability information or no way to derive a count; they specify the report’s counting unit, three unmeasured variables and the prohibited proportion inference. This case is sufficient to show that the draft’s broader negative has not been established.

**Draft true, source false:** If an independently known prevalence rate establishes a proportion of residents unable to book digitally but the total resident population is unknown, an absolute count may remain unavailable. That establishes that the count and proportion claims are not interchangeable in the opposite direction. Any version of this case in which the telephone bookings themselves establish the proportion is excluded for this material by “Telefonbokningarna kan därför inte användas för att säga hur stor andel …”. No such information is attributed to the actual source.

**Smallest supported repair:** “We cannot use the telephone bookings to calculate what proportion of residents cannot book digitally.” This preserves the source’s precise object and the warranted certainty of the exclusion. Keep the next sentence’s separate, expressly supplied observation that a telephone booking does not prove inability to use the web.

**Classification:** Source-support finding caused by changed measurement scope outside quoted speech. There is no translated-quotation finding.
