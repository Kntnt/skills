# Judgement — p4-person-attribute, round 1

Judge's own reading first, then one section per report, then the summary table.

## My reading of the focus passage

Draft: "Ortiz explained the distinction in her email: 'We kept the uncertain dates visible. An empty field and an estimated year tell the next volunteer different things.'"

The clause makes two separable assertions beyond the quotation it introduces.

**"her" — unsupported personal attribute.** The material names the person four times and never genders her: "Collection coordinator Lena Ortiz sent the following complete quotations", "no ... account of Ortiz's manner or emotions". Where a pronoun would have been the natural choice the material uses the possessive of the name instead, so the supplied package commits to nothing. English "her" is natural gender, not agreement with a noun class, so the draft asserts a fact about a named person — that she is female — which the material does not carry. The given name "Lena" is an inference a reader may draw, not a statement in the material. A case compatible with every supplied sentence exists in which Ortiz is a man or uses "they": the source holds, the draft fails. This warrants a repair before delivery ("in the email" costs nothing), and the draft's own third attribution — "Ortiz wrote." — shows the pronoun was never needed.

**"explained the distinction" — genuinely arguable.** Against it: the material records only that Ortiz *sent* three complete quotations in an email headed "notes for publication", and states outright that there is "no question-and-answer transcript, observation of an archive visit, or account of Ortiz's manner or emotions"; "explained" is mildly factive, endorsing that a real distinction was at issue and that her purpose was to clarify it. For it: the quoted sentence is itself reason-giving — it sets a contrast between an empty field and an estimated year — so the verb reports what the words on the page do, names no tone, emotion, setting or prompting question, and the claim stays inside quotation marks as hers. I do not think this can be settled either way from the fixture, so I class findings against the verb as **disputed** throughout, and I note that the material's explicit exclusion sentence is about *manner and emotions*, which "explained" does not supply, rather than about communicative purpose, which it arguably does.

**Rest of the draft.** Counts, exclusions, modalities, the three verbatim quotations, the preserved absences and the preserved "whether" all match. The only other passage I would call arguable is the present-tense title.

All five reports found the unsupported personal attribute in "her email", and none of them noticed a problem in the focus passage and then accepted the passage anyway. They separate on what else the clause carries — A and B examine "explained" and clear it, C, D and E charge it — and on how far each report reaches beyond that clause: C and E each add two further items, of which one apiece is false on the material and the others (the headline present) rest on a convention competent editors weigh differently.
