## Findings

### Finding 1 — "her email" asserts a natural-gender attribute the material does not supply (source support)

- Draft passage: "Ortiz explained the distinction in her email: …" (section "Keeping uncertainty visible").
- Source passages, in the material's own words: "Collection coordinator Lena Ortiz sent the following complete quotations in an email headed 'Catalog pilot: notes for publication'"; "There is no question-and-answer transcript, observation of an archive visit, or account of Ortiz's manner or emotions."
- What differs: the thing asserted. The source establishes who sent the email and that there was one email; the draft additionally asserts that Ortiz is female. That attribute appears nowhere in the supplied material — not in the pilot note, not in the email framing, not in the brief. A given name is not a statement of a person's gender, and identifying the referent of "her" as Ortiz does not establish the person and gender features the pronoun carries. This is a claim about a real attribute of a named person, not grammatical gender: English "her" is natural-gender.
- Both directions, with a concrete compatible case: suppose Lena Ortiz is a man, or uses they/them. Nothing in the supplied material excludes this — no gendered term is ever applied to Ortiz. In that case the source statement "Ortiz sent the quotations in an email" still holds, while the draft statement "her email" fails. Conversely, if Ortiz is a woman, both hold. Since a compatible case exists in which the draft fails and the source holds, this is a finding, natural though the wording reads.
- Support that is missing: any statement of Ortiz's gender in the brief, the pilot note, or the email.
- Smallest supported repair: drop the gendered possessive and use the definite article, which the single supplied email licenses — "Ortiz explained the distinction in the email:" (alternatively "in that email"). Nothing else in the sentence changes; the quotation, the attribution and the voice are untouched.

No other finding. The remaining claims, counts, exclusions, modalities, attributions and absences are each matched to a source passage above, and the three quotations are complete and verbatim.
