## 1. My own reading of the focus passage

> "In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log and chose Svale Systems after testing whether it could show the status of each repair."

The material carries two separate sentences: "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings" and, with no date of its own, "It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair."

Read as an ordinary reader reads it, the fronted adverbial "In September 2025" governs both conjuncts of "decided … and chose", and "after testing" then places the capability test inside or before that same month. The draft therefore dates two events the material leaves undated — the supplier choice and the test — and compresses a decision → test → choice sequence into a single month. Nothing supplied forecloses a choice made in October: the trial lasted eight weeks and the note is dated 4 December 2025, which leaves room for a later selection. **This is a genuine defect of time, and a repair is warranted** — the smallest being a sentence split, which every report that proposes a repair converges on.

Two further things in the same sentence, which I judge differently:

- **The pronoun "it".** Its nearest antecedent is "Svale Systems", where the material says "the log". But the complement "could show the status of each repair" is something a log does, not something a company does, so both resolutions name the same capability test of the same product. No case compatible with the material makes one true and the other false. Restoring "the log" is a free improvement; alleging that the draft asserts a broader *supplier* evaluation depends on a reading the sentence does not have in context.
- **"in two buildings" and "its own"** are dropped from this sentence, but both survive elsewhere in the draft (the deck carries "in two buildings"; "its maintenance team" keeps the customer as the acting party). No finding.
