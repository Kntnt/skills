## Findings

### Finding 1 — the draft has the submission settle a question only the supplied material is said not to settle

- **Draft:** "Our submission also makes no claim to have secured the building owner's consent for rehearsal use; it does not establish whether that consent exists."
- **Source, first clause:** "The association's submission makes no representation that it has secured the building owner's consent for rehearsal use."
- **Source, nearest passage for the second clause:** "The supplied material does not state the consent's actual status."

**What differs.** The subject and the document scope. The material makes two separate statements: one about what the *submission* does not represent (that consent has been secured), and one about what the *supplied material* does not state (the consent's actual status). The draft transfers the second to the first: "it" resolves to "Our submission", so the draft asserts that the submission itself leaves the existence of consent open in both directions. The material supports only the narrower negative — that the submission does not represent consent as secured.

**Case in which the draft fails and the source holds.** The submission contains a paragraph reading, in effect, "the building owner has declined consent for rehearsal use, and we ask the trustees to seek it afresh." That submission makes no representation that it has secured consent, so the supplied statement holds. The supplied material still does not state the consent's actual status, since the material describes the submission without reproducing it — so that statement holds too. But the submission does then establish whether consent exists: it establishes that it does not. The draft's clause is false in this case.

**Why the case cannot be set aside.** No supplied statement excludes it. "The supplied material does not state the consent's actual status" is a statement about the material's own coverage, not about the submission's contents, and so is silent on whether the submission addresses the status. Nothing else in the material describes the submission beyond the single sentence quoted above.

**The other direction, showing the two are not interchangeable.** Were the status stated elsewhere in the package — say, the trustees' paper reporting that the owner has consented — while the submission itself stayed silent, the draft's clause would hold and the supplied statement would fail. The two statements come apart in both directions; they are not two wordings of one fact.

**Smallest supported repair.** Delete the clause and its semicolon, ending the sentence at the supported negative:

> Our submission also makes no claim to have secured the building owner's consent for rehearsal use.

This keeps the disclosure, the paragraph's candour and the author's voice, and claims exactly what the material supports. If a closing disclaimer is wanted in its place, one that stays inside the material is a statement about her own claim-making rather than about the document's contents — for example "…, and I make none here." Any wording that has the submission, the association or the author settle the consent's status in either direction reintroduces the same defect.
