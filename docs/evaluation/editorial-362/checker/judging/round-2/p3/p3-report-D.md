## 3. Cases tested and set aside

Each case below is a concrete situation compatible with the supplied material in which one statement could hold and the other fail. Each is set aside by a supplied statement, quoted, or shown to fail the two-direction test.

**Case A — "the paper names neither a prospective tenant nor a rental figure" (row 9).** Candidate case: the trustees' paper does name a tenant, and the material's sentence means only that no tenant is named *in this summary*. If that held, the draft would assert something false while the source sentence stayed true. Set aside: the sentence sits inside the material's description of that one document — "The trustees' paper of 3 July 2026 proposes letting the former storage wing for three years. It says predictable income would help meet the hall's maintenance costs. No prospective tenant or rental figure is named." The implicit locus of the passive is the paper described by the two preceding sentences; the material offers no other document in which a naming could have occurred. The candidate case requires reading the paragraph against its own subject, so it does not stand. Not a finding.

**Case B — "using a room" for "using a rehearsal room" (row 13).** Candidate case: a group would consider using some room on weekday evenings but not a *rehearsal* room, so the draft's weaker statement holds where the source's stronger one fails. Tested in both directions: the source statement entails the draft statement, and the draft claims nothing the source does not. A weaker statement entailed by the material is not unsupported. In context the sentence follows "Our survey gives a reason to examine rehearsal use", so an ordinary reader takes "a room" as the rehearsal room under discussion; no reader is licensed to infer a broader interest than the survey found. Not a finding.

**Case C — "it does not establish whether that consent exists" (row 20).** Candidate case: the association's submission states outright that consent has *not* been obtained. Then the submission would settle the question, and a reading of "it" as the submission would make the draft false, while "makes no representation that it has secured the building owner's consent" stayed true. Set aside by a supplied statement: "The supplied material does not state the consent's actual status." The submission is known only through the supplied material, and that material reports the consent's status as unstated; a submission that denied consent would state the status. The draft asserts precisely this unknown and converts neither the silence nor the absence of a claim into a denial, which is what preserving the state of knowledge requires. Not a finding.

**Case D — "The wing is empty" for "The wing is currently empty" (row 21).** Candidate case: the wing is empty today but was occupied until recently, or will be occupied shortly, so a timeless reading of the draft would fail. Both statements are present-tense assertions about the same moment, and the draft makes no claim about any other time; "currently" adds emphasis, not a restriction the draft escapes. No case survives in which the draft holds and the source fails, or the reverse. Not a finding.

**Case E — "makes a serious case" and "That concern deserves weight" (rows 7–8).** Candidate case: an evaluative characterisation without support. Set aside: this is the signed speaker's assessment in an opinion piece written in her voice, and the brief directs it — "Make a clear case while treating the trustees' financial concern seriously." It measures nothing and imputes no external verdict. Not a finding.

**Case F — causation.** The draft contains no causal verb linking the survey, the emptiness of the wing or the deferral to any outcome. "would help meet maintenance costs" is attributed to the paper (row 7); "should help establish" (row 11) is advocacy about a proposed comparison, not a claim that anything caused anything. The "while" of row 21 places two supplied facts side by side without asserting that one produced the other. No sequence or correlation is presented as cause, in the title, in any sentence, or by implication. Not a finding.
