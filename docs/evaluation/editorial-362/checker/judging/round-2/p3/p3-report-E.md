## 2. Person, number and natural-gender accounting

Pronouns and person markers are accounted for separately from their referents.

- First-person singular (“I”, four occurrences: C2/C4 implied, C22, C24): the speaker is the by-lined author, supported by the brief’s “in the voice of Amina Reed”. First-person singular carries no natural-gender assertion, and the draft makes none — no “she”, “he” or gendered noun is applied to Reed or anyone else anywhere in the document. The material’s “She accepts…” is the material’s reference to Reed, not something the draft must reproduce.
- First-person plural (“Our survey”, “We did not ask”, “Our treasurer’s”, “we have not prepared”, “Our submission”, “We must be equally clear”): the referent is Room to Rehearse, supported by “Room to Rehearse surveyed 14 local music groups”, “Its treasurer’s signed note” and “The association’s submission”. The plural asserts institutional agency only; the author is its chair (brief), so the inclusive “we” is warranted. No individual person is asserted to have done any of these acts.
- Third-person plural “they/their” for the trustees (C1, C2, C4, C25) and for the nine groups (C13 “they would consider… they would attend”): number and referent match the material (“the trustees”, “Nine said they would…”). Neither carries a natural-gender assertion.
- “it” at C1/C2 (the wing), C16 (a rehearsal room) and C20 (the submission): singular, inanimate, referents settled in the same sentence; matches the material’s own “it will pay its way”.
- No grammatical-gender feature in the material is converted into a natural-gender assertion in the draft, and no natural-gender claim is made that the material does not supply.

## 3. Near cases tested in both directions and cleared

For each, I looked for a concrete case compatible with the supplied material in which one statement holds and the other fails.

- **Case A — “the paper names neither…” (C9) against “No prospective tenant or rental figure is named.”** Candidate case: the paper does name a tenant and the material simply omits the name. The material presents itself as “the complete source package”, and the sentence sits inside the material's consecutive account of the paper's contents, between what the paper says and what the hall's rooms are doing. Reading the naming as done elsewhere than in the paper would require a second, undescribed document that the complete package withholds. No case survives; not a finding. The draft also stops where the material stops — it does not infer that no tenant exists, only that none is named.
- **Case B — “The survey cannot promise” (C16) against “not a promise that it will pay its way.”** Candidate case: an instrument that is not a promise yet could promise. For this instrument the material forecloses it: the survey “did not ask what they would pay or how often they would attend”, the two inputs a paying-its-way promise would need. A survey that asked neither cannot yield that promise, so the capability form adds no unsupported strength. Not a finding.
- **Case C — “it does not establish whether that consent exists” (C20) against “The supplied material does not state the consent’s actual status.”** Candidate case in one direction: the submission positively states that consent was refused — that would still be “no representation that it has secured” consent while establishing that consent does not exist, so C20 would fail. But the material closes this case in the very next sentence: it declares the consent’s actual status unstated, and the submission is part of the material's described package. Candidate case in the other direction: consent has in fact been granted and C20 falsely denies it — C20 asserts nothing about the fact, only about what the submission establishes, so it survives that case too. The clause preserves the unknown as unknown in both directions, which is what the contract asks. Not a finding.
- **Case D — “Bellwick Hall’s wing” (T1) and “its former storage wing” (C1) against “the building owner’s consent” (C19’s source).** Candidate case: the hall’s trustees occupy a building somebody else owns, so possessive phrasing would assert an ownership the material does not supply. The draft’s possessives attach the wing to the hall as its part (“the former storage wing” of the hall whose trustees propose letting it), not to the trustees as proprietors, and the draft separately concedes at C19 that a building owner’s consent is an open question — which is incompatible with reading the possessive as a title claim. Not a finding.
- **Case E — “makes a serious case” (C6).** Candidate case: an evaluative characterisation presented as established rather than as the speaker’s. The contract admits a speaker assessment, and this one is marked as the author’s in a signed opinion piece and immediately restated as her judgement (“That concern deserves weight”). Not a finding.
- **Case F — “while” at C21.** Candidate case: “while” read as causal or concessive, asserting a relation the material’s semicolon does not. Even under the concessive reading it asserts only the contrast the two supplied facts already state side by side, and no cause is claimed in either direction. Not a finding.
