## My reading of the focus passage

> "In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log and chose Svale Systems after testing whether it could show the status of each repair."

The material carries this as two separate statements, only the first of which is dated: "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings," and, four sentences later and with no date attached, "It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair."

The draft joins them into one coordinated predicate under a fronted time adverbial. An ordinary reader takes "In September 2025" to govern both "decided" and "chose", so the draft places the supplier choice inside September 2025, and with it — since the choice is said to follow the test — the capability test as well. The material dates neither, and its own timeline leaves room for them to fall later: the trial ran eight weeks and the internal note is dated 4 December 2025, so a decision in September followed by testing through October and a choice in early November is fully compatible with every supplied sentence while the draft's sentence is not. This is a change of time, and it warrants repair. The repair is small: a period in place of "and".

Two further features of the same sentence are lesser matters. "it could show the status" replaces the material's "the log"; the nearest noun phrase is "Svale Systems", but in this material the only log in play is Svale's, so both readings pick out the same test and neither can be true while the other is false. Dropping "in two buildings" and "own" from this sentence loses nothing, since the summary carries the scope and the possessive "its" keeps the team inside Elm Quay.
