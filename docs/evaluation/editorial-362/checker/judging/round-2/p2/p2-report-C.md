### Pronouns: person, number, natural gender

Identifying a referent does not settle these features, so they are accounted for separately.

- "its own maintenance team" / "its maintenance team" / "its supervisor": third person singular, non-personal, referring to the organization or the trial. The material uses the same non-personal "its" for Elm Quay. No natural-gender assertion is made or implied. In row 5 "its" sits closest to "the trial" while the intended possessor is the team or the company; this changes no claim, since the material gives Elm Quay exactly one maintenance supervisor, Maya Lind.
- "It chose Svale Systems" (source) / "it could show the status" (draft, row 10): the draft's "it" is third person singular non-personal. Its referent is not settled by the draft's own syntax; see Finding 1.
- Maya Lind: the material uses "Her final quotation", a natural-gender assertion about a woman. The draft makes no pronominal reference to Lind at all, using "Lind" and "Lind's" throughout, so it asserts nothing about her gender and contradicts nothing. No finding.
- "we", "us", "ours", "I" inside the quotations: first person, and the singular/plural split between Lind speaking for herself ("I would leave") and for the team ("We wanted", "helps us") is preserved exactly, because the quotations are verbatim.
- "them into the log" (quotation), "their own implementation" (closing line): third person plural, referring to the categories and to operations managers respectively; both referents are settled by the sentences they appear in.

## Findings

### Finding 1 — September 2025 is extended to the choice of supplier (source support)

Draft: "In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log and chose Svale Systems after testing whether it could show the status of each repair."

Source: "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings." and, as a separate sentence with no date, "It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair."

What is missing or changed: in the material the date governs the decision alone. In the draft the adverbial "In September 2025" governs both coordinated verbs, so it also dates the supplier choice and, by "after testing", the testing that preceded that choice. The material supplies no date for either. Chronology is asserted where the material leaves it open.

Test, both directions. Case compatible with the material: the team decides in September 2025, runs its status test through October, and selects Svale in early November 2025, the trial then running its eight weeks and being written up on 4 December 2025. Under this case the source statement holds and the draft statement fails. Conversely, if both the decision and the selection fell in September 2025, both statements hold; the draft therefore asserts strictly more than the material licenses. (The eight-week trial closing by 4 December 2025 makes an October or early-November selection an ordinary reading of the material, not a strained one.)

Secondary, inside the same sentence: the source tests "the log"; the draft tests "it", whose nearest antecedent is "Svale Systems". The material tests a property of the log, not of the supplier.

Smallest supported repair — split the coordinated verbs and restore the tested thing:

> In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log. The team chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair.

This leaves the date on the decision, where the material puts it, and leaves the choice undated, as the material leaves it.

## Considered and cleared

Recorded so that the absence of a finding is visible as a judgment rather than an omission.

1. **Title scope** (row 1). "Elm Quay gains a shared view of repair reports" states no scope, while the log ran in two of the buildings of a 640-apartment company and has not expanded. Read as an ordinary reader would in context, the title is immediately governed by the first sentence, which states "in two buildings", and the title asserts no company-wide coverage of its own. The present tense is supported by Lind's present-tense "Having one view of the reports helps us" and by "The trial has not yet expanded", which leaves the arrangement standing. No causal claim is made in the title. Not a finding.
2. **"The eight-week trial gave the team a common view of reports"** (row 4). "Gave" is a causal verb, so it was tested against the material's limits. What it asserts caused is the existence of one shared view, which is the log's design and is asserted in Lind's own words; it asserts nothing about assignment time, cost, resident satisfaction or completion time, which are the effects the material declines to attribute. No case compatible with the material makes this fail while the material holds. Not a finding.
3. **Present → past in the closing figures paragraph** (rows 19 and 21). The material says "the note explicitly does not attribute" and "There are no … measurements"; the draft says "did not" and "There were no". A case can be imagined in which measurements were taken during the trial and later discarded, so that none exist now. Against that, the whole paragraph is a consistent narrative past about the trial period, the note is a fixed dated document whose content cannot change, and an ordinary reader takes "There were no measurements of costs, resident satisfaction, or completion times" as saying the trial produced none, which is what the material conveys. No qualification is lost and nothing is added. Defensible as written.
4. **"That assessment was specific to the trial"** (row 24). This could be read as narrowing Lind's stance more than the material does, since "did not recommend Svale to every housing company" leaves open that she would recommend it to some. But the clause characterizes the quotation it directly follows, and that quotation is literally about the trial ("I would choose to do the trial again"). It reads as the author's own framing of the quoted assessment, resting on supported material, and it strengthens rather than weakens the customer's reservation. Defensible as written.
5. **"Before the trial" for "Previously"** (row 9). Tested as a chronology shift and cleared: the material places no change of storage practice between the September 2025 decision and the start of the trial, so no compatible case separates the two statements.
6. **"Flats" → "apartments"** (row 8). A term carried into the draft's variety of English. In this context nothing could be one and not the other, so the same thing is named.
