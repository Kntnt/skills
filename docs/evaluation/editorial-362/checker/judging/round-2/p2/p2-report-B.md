## Findings

### Finding 1 — the choice of Svale Systems is dated to September 2025, which the material does not state (source support)

- Draft: "In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log and chose Svale Systems after testing whether it could show the status of each repair."
- Source: "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings." — and, as a separate sentence carrying no date, "It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair."
- What differs: **time**. The material dates only the decision to trial. By coordinating the two verbs under one fronted adverbial, the draft places the testing and the choice of supplier inside September 2025 as well.
- Case in which the source statement holds and the draft's fails: the team decides on 5 September 2025, tests through late September and early October, and chooses Svale Systems on 8 October 2025; the trial then runs eight weeks from mid-October, and the internal note is written on 4 December 2025. Every supplied statement holds; the draft's sentence is false.
- Reverse direction: the draft is strictly stronger here — it entails the supplied claim plus a date — so no case makes the draft hold where the source fails.
- No supplied statement excludes the case. The material gives no start date for the trial, no date for the testing and no date for the choice; "The trial lasted eight weeks" and the note's date of 4 December 2025 leave the October choice above entirely compatible.
- Smallest supported repair: break the coordination so the date governs only the decision — "In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log. It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair."

### Finding 2 — "whether it could show the status" names the supplier, not the log (source support)

- Draft: "…chose Svale Systems after testing whether it could show the status of each repair."
- Source: "It chose Svale Systems after testing whether **the log** could show the status of each repair."
- What differs: **the thing tested**. In the draft sentence the nearest and most natural antecedent of "it" is "Svale Systems"; the intended antecedent, "a shared log", is further back and is separated by the supplier's name.
- Case in which the source statement holds and the draft's reading fails: the team tests the log itself — a demonstration instance, or the trial configuration — and forms no judgment about the supplier's own capabilities at all. The material's statement holds; the draft's sentence, read with its nearest antecedent, asserts a test of Svale Systems.
- No supplied statement excludes that case; the material names the log as what was tested, and "No comparison with another supplier is available" rules out supplier evaluation rather than supporting it.
- Smallest supported repair: replace the pronoun with the supplied noun — "…after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair." The repair in Finding 1 already carries this wording.

## Cases considered and set aside

Recorded so the next reader need not re-derive them.

1. **Headline scope** — "Elm Quay gains a shared view" names the organization, while the trial covered two of 640 flats and has not expanded. Set aside: the summary directly beneath supplies "in two buildings", "eight-week" and "Expansion remains undecided", and "Having one view of the reports helps us" is supplied in the present tense. An ordinary reader reaches no unsupported claim.
2. **"That assessment was specific to the trial"** — no supplied sentence says the assessment is trial-limited. Set aside: "That assessment" refers to the quotation immediately above, whose content is "I would choose to do the trial again", so the clause describes the quoted assessment's own subject rather than narrowing Lind's views. The clause that follows it is the material's own sentence, unchanged, and the reservation survives.
3. **Present-to-past tense shifts** — "does not attribute" → "did not attribute", "There are no" → "There were no". Set aside: in the draft's past-tense narration both read as the same denials, no qualification is weakened, and the non-attribution of the difference to the software stays explicit.
4. **"compared with three"** — checked against the forbidden inference. Set aside: no causal verb, no causal hedge, no claim of saving, delight or rescue, and the workload qualification and the note's non-attribution follow immediately. Assignment time is never turned into completion time; the draft says so outright.
5. **Length** — roughly 370 words against an approximate 400-word brief. Set aside: the shortfall reflects the material, and nothing is invented to reach the figure.
