## 2. Findings

### Finding 1 — the date is carried onto the choice of supplier and the capability test (source support; time and scope)

**Draft (body, first paragraph):**

> "In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log and chose Svale Systems after testing whether it could show the status of each repair."

**Supplied material:**

> "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings. Previously telephone reports and emails were stored separately. The team wanted staff on different shifts to see the same information. It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair."

**What differs: time.** The material dates exactly one event — the decision to trial a shared repair log. The choice of Svale Systems, and the capability test that preceded that choice, are given in a separate, undated sentence. The draft fronts "In September 2025" over a coordinated verb phrase, so the adverbial scopes over both "decided" *and* "chose". The draft therefore dates the supplier choice to September 2025, and, because the test must precede the choice, dates the testing to September 2025 or earlier as well. Neither date is in the material.

**Case in which the draft fails and the material still holds:** the team decides in late September 2025; it then runs the status-display test through October; it chooses Svale Systems on 3 November 2025; configuration and training follow, and the eight-week trial runs into the period covered by the 4 December 2025 note. Every supplied statement holds in this case — the decision is in September 2025, the choice follows a capability test, the note is dated 4 December 2025 — while the draft's sentence is false, because the choice and the test fall outside September 2025.

**Case in which the material fails and the draft holds:** the decision falls in October 2025. Then the draft holds as to the choice but the material's own date fails, confirming the two statements are not equivalent and that the draft asserts strictly more.

**Setting the case aside:** no supplied statement excludes it. The material gives no start date for the trial, no date for the test, and no date for the choice; "The trial lasted eight weeks" and the 4 December 2025 note date leave the November case open. Nothing in the material states that the decision and the choice fell in the same month.

**Support that is missing:** a supplied date, or a supplied statement placing the choice in the same month as the decision.

**Smallest supported repair** — break the coordination so the date governs only the decision, and restore the material's subject for the test:

> In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log. It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair.

This changes no wording beyond the conjunction and preserves the sentence's voice, order of events and reading rhythm. It also removes the implicit referent noted at B4 at no cost.

## 3. Tested and not reported

Recorded so that the reasoning is visible and the cases are not re-litigated. Each was tested in both directions and no case stood.

### 3.1 "That assessment was specific to the trial"

Read as an ordinary reader takes it in context — immediately after the quoted verdict and immediately before "Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company" — the sentence says that the assessment just quoted was about her own trial rather than a blanket endorsement of the supplier. The quoted words bear that out on their face: "I would choose to do the trial again." A candidate defeating case (Lind privately holds a broader view and would recommend Svale to companies with similar stock, which the material's "not to every housing company" leaves open) does not defeat the sentence, because the sentence characterises the assessment that was given, not the outer limit of what she believes. The reservation the material requires to survive does survive, in the same breath and unhedged.

### 3.2 Present-tense source statements rendered in the past ("did not attribute", "There were no measurements")

Both narrow rather than broaden. The note's content is fixed, so no case separates "does not attribute" from "did not attribute". For the measurements, the material's present-tense "There are no cost, resident-satisfaction or completion-time measurements" excludes any case in which such measurements exist, so the past-tense rendering asserts less than the material supports and introduces nothing unsupported. Whether a past-tense rendering is the better narrative choice is general editorial review and outside this comparison.

### 3.3 "gains" in the title against a completed eight-week trial

A pause of the log after the trial, pending the expansion decision, would defeat the present tense. Lind's present-tense "Having one view of the reports helps us" asserts the single view as a current fact and rules the pause out; "The trial has not yet expanded" describes reach, not cessation.

### 3.4 "640 apartments" for "640 flats"

Same language, US register. In this material nothing could be one and not the other: a single residential stock of 640 dwelling units is described, with no basements, commercial units or houses distinguished.

### 3.5 "compared with three in the preceding eight-week period"

Tested for a smuggled causal reading. "compared with" asserts juxtaposition only; no causal verb and no causal hedge ("suggests that it shortened") appears in the title, summary, headings or body, and the differing-workload caveat and the note's explicit non-attribution follow in the next sentence.

### 3.6 Length against material

The draft runs to roughly 330 words of body and summary against an approximate 400-word brief qualified by "as material allows". Nothing is padded, and no fact, quotation, source, scene or experience is invented to reach the number. Closing the gap would require further supplied material — for example, dated milestones for the test and the supplier choice, the trial's start date, or a further usable quotation — and none is available.
