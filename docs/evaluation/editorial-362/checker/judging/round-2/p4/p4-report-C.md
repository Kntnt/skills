## Findings

### F1 — "her email" asserts a natural gender the material never supplies (source support)

- **Draft:** "Ortiz explained the distinction in her email:" (section "Keeping uncertainty visible")
- **Source:** The material names her only as "Collection coordinator Lena Ortiz" and refers to "Ortiz's manner or emotions". It contains no pronoun for Ortiz and no statement of her gender.
- **What is missing:** Identifying the referent of "her" (the coordinator, the email's writer) does not establish the referent's natural gender. English "her" here is referential, not grammatical: it asserts that Lena Ortiz is female. That is a third claim the material does not carry, added by the draft alone. The given name "Lena" is conventionally feminine, but a conventional association is not a supplied statement, and the contract requires a source passage.
- **Concrete case, compatible with the material, in which the source holds and the draft fails:** Lena Ortiz is the archive's collection coordinator, wrote the email headed "Catalog pilot: notes for publication", and uses he/him or they/them pronouns. Every supplied statement — the title, the name, the email, the three quotations, the absence of any account of manner or emotions — holds unchanged, and "her email" is false.
- **The case cannot be set aside:** no supplied statement excludes it. The material gives no pronoun, no honorific, and no gendered noun for Ortiz anywhere, and explicitly limits itself to the email and the pilot note as the only correspondence and reporting materials.
- **Smallest supported repair:** delete the pronoun. "Ortiz drew the distinction in the same email:" — or, keeping the draft's own verb choice out of it, "In the same email, Ortiz wrote:". Either keeps the sentence, the quotation, the attribution and the voice, and removes only the unsupported gender.

### F2 — "explained" attributes a communicative purpose the material does not record (source support)

- **Draft:** "Ortiz explained the distinction in her email:"
- **Source:** "Collection coordinator Lena Ortiz sent the following complete quotations in an email headed 'Catalog pilot: notes for publication'"; "There is no question-and-answer transcript, observation of an archive visit, or account of Ortiz's manner or emotions."
- **What is changed:** The material records only that Ortiz sent these lines as notes for publication. "Explained" adds two things to "wrote" or "sent": that the lines were offered in order to clarify something — a motive — and, factively, that there was a distinction there to be explained and that her account of it is correct. Motive is circumstantial detail and needs support; the material supplies none, and the draft's other two attributions correctly use "wrote".
- **Concrete case, compatible with the material, in which the source holds and the draft fails:** Ortiz composed the three lines as unprompted standalone notes for the publisher, in response to no question and addressing no one's confusion, with the first line written as a statement of what the team did rather than as an explanation of anything. The material holds in full — she sent complete quotations in an email headed "notes for publication", and there is no question-and-answer transcript — and "Ortiz explained the distinction" fails.
- **In the other direction:** if Ortiz explained the distinction, she wrote it; so the draft's version is the strictly stronger claim, and the strengthening is the unsupported part.
- **The case cannot be set aside:** no supplied statement records a question put to Ortiz, an exchange, or any purpose for the email beyond the heading "Catalog pilot: notes for publication", and the material states outright that no account of her manner exists.
- **Smallest supported repair:** use the verb the material supports and the draft already uses elsewhere: "Ortiz wrote in the same email:". Combined with F1, one sentence carries both repairs: **"In the same email, Ortiz wrote:"**
