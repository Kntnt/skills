### 1. Title

- **Draft:** "Briar County volunteers share records for new donations"
- **Source:** "The note reports that volunteers could consult the same records"; "In February 2026 the archive's collection team chose to test a shared catalog for incoming donations, using Index Lantern."
- **Differences tested:** thing named (share vs. could consult the same records); modality
  (capability vs. actuality); time (headline present vs. the pilot's eight weeks); whose
  assertion (flat statement vs. what the note reports).
- **Assessment:** defensible. A shared catalog in which the same records are available to
  the volunteers is what "share records" names, and the scope restriction to new donations
  is carried in the title itself. The present tense is read here as the ordinary headline
  present for the reported pilot, and the first body sentence immediately fixes that scope
  ("during an eight-week pilot") and the second restores the attribution ("according to the
  archive's pilot note"). Read as an assertion that the practice continues today it would
  fail — the material does not say the catalog is still in use, and Ortiz's "I would use the
  shared catalog again" suggests it is not continuous — but that reading is not the one an
  ordinary reader gives a headline over a pilot report. No finding.

## Findings

### Finding 1 — "her email" asserts a natural-gender attribute the material never supplies

- **Draft passage (under "Keeping uncertainty visible"):** "Ortiz explained the distinction in **her** email"
- **Source passages:** "Collection coordinator Lena Ortiz sent the following complete
  quotations in an email headed 'Catalog pilot: notes for publication'"; "The archive
  supplied that email and the pilot note for this supplier-published case. These are the
  only correspondence and reporting materials supplied. There is no question-and-answer
  transcript, observation of an archive visit, or account of Ortiz's manner or emotions."
- **What differs:** the person attribute conveyed by the pronoun. The referent is settled —
  "her email" unambiguously means the email Ortiz sent, and the material does supply that
  email. But identifying the referent is not the same as establishing the person's features.
  English "her" carries third person, singular, and **feminine natural gender**, and that
  last feature is a substantive claim about a named individual. The material states Ortiz's
  role ("Collection coordinator"), name ("Lena Ortiz") and one act (she sent an email). It
  states nothing about her gender, and it expressly says the supplied package contains no
  further account of Ortiz beyond the email and the pilot note. This is a natural-gender
  assertion, not grammatical gender: English has no grammatical gender agreement that would
  force the choice, so the pronoun is doing referential work about a person.
- **Case in which the source statement holds and the draft fails:** Lena Ortiz, the
  collection coordinator, is a man — the given name Lena is borne by men in several
  languages — or is nonbinary and uses they/them. Either way the coordinator still sent the
  quoted email with that heading, the archive still supplied it, and every supplied sentence
  about the pilot remains true, while "her email" is false. Nothing in the material excludes
  this case: the package names no pronoun for Ortiz, and "There is no ... account of Ortiz's
  manner or emotions" rules out other kinds of personal detail without supplying this one.
  A given name is not a supplied statement about the bearer's gender, so it cannot set the
  case aside.
- **Case in the other direction:** if the pronoun is removed, nothing supported is lost —
  the email is already identified by the preceding clause and by the earlier attribution
  ("Lena Ortiz wrote in an email supplied for this Index Lantern-published case"), so the
  weaker wording still picks out exactly the same email.
- **Smallest supported repair:** replace the pronoun with the definite article:

  > Ortiz explained the distinction in the email: "We kept the uncertain dates visible. ..."

  If a stronger link back to the earlier mention is wanted, "in the same email" is equally
  supported. Nothing else in the sentence, the quotation or the paragraph changes.

No other finding. No further material is needed to repair this one; the repair is a
deletion of an unsupported attribute, not an addition. Were the draft to keep a pronoun,
the missing support would be a supplied statement of the pronoun Ortiz uses — for example a
line in the archive's email or note referring to Ortiz in the third person.
