## My own reading of the focus passage

The clause makes two claims of its own beyond the quotation it introduces, and they need separating.

**"her email" — the material does not carry it.** The material names the person four times and never once with a pronoun: "Collection coordinator Lena Ortiz sent the following complete quotations in an email headed 'Catalog pilot: notes for publication'", and later the neutral possessive "Ortiz's manner or emotions". It adds that the email and the pilot note "are the only correspondence and reporting materials supplied" and that there is "no question-and-answer transcript, observation of an archive visit, or account of Ortiz's manner or emotions" — so the one channel through which a gender might have arrived is explicitly closed. English "her" is not grammatical agreement here; it asserts a personal attribute of a named individual. A world in which every supplied sentence holds word for word and Lena Ortiz uses he/him or they/them is compatible with the material, and in that world the draft is false in print, in a case the customer supplied for publication. The draft's other two attributions ("collection coordinator Lena Ortiz wrote", "Ortiz wrote") are genderless, so the assertion is made once and is removable in one word. A repair is warranted: "in the same email" or "in the email". The contrary view — that a conventionally feminine given name licenses the pronoun — rests on an association outside the material, not on a supplied statement.

**"explained the distinction" — the material does support it.** The quotation printed immediately after the clause is the explanation: "An empty field and an estimated year tell the next volunteer different things" states a distinction and says what turns on it. The material also grounds the distinction as a real feature of the archive's practice — the team "retained a free-text note for uncertain dates", and "Eleven records retained uncertain dates". "Explain" here describes the content of words the reader can see on the page; it is not a manner verb ("explained patiently"), it places Ortiz in no scene, and it does not require that a question was put to her. The material's disclaimer forecloses manner and emotions, not the naming of a speech act performed in writing. I therefore treat allegations against "explained" as resting on a reading the passage does not have in context. The best case for the other side — that "explained" is mildly factive and so endorses her account in a supplier-published case — is real but thin, and does not make one statement true and the other false.

So the focus passage contains exactly one defect, and it is the possessive.
