## 2. Cases tested and set aside

Each case is compatible with the supplied material; each is set aside by a supplied statement that excludes it, or because the case does not in fact make the draft's statement fail.

**Case A — the title commits the author to a box she doubts.**
Case: she tries the question without adding any box, for instance by writing it into the existing *dagordning* field or asking it aloud. Then "prova en enkel fråga bredvid tiden" holds while "en ruta till" does not; the title would have committed her to the very box her notes doubt.
Set aside: the notes themselves put the box in her own mouth as the thing at stake — "Jag vill behålla både hoppet om den frågan och tvivlet på ännu en ruta" and "Det finns en komik i att försöka lösa ett problem med formulär genom att utöka formuläret" — and the draft's own body keeps that doubt intact ("Jag vill prova frågan, men behålla tvivlet på rutan"; "Om tillägget gör möten bättre vet jag inte"). The title is a noun phrase that asserts neither an effect nor a resolution, so no supported qualification is lost at the title.

**Case B — the agenda box already gives the outcome a clear place.**
Case: the template's *dagordning* field is prominent and required, and is where intended outcomes are written; then what the time should yield has a place as clear as *starttid* has.
Set aside: "Där finns rutor för starttid, sluttid, deltagare och dagordning, men ingen ruta för det beslut vi ska kunna fatta när vi går därifrån." The supplied statement asserts that the decision has no box of its own, while time has two labelled boxes. Read in context — the sentence follows directly on the missing decision box — "vad vi ska kunna göra med den" is the outcome, and the comparative "inte samma tydliga plats" is exactly what the quoted note supports.

**Case C — the calendar practice can be possible without being actual.**
Case: in her library, calendar time never is treated as a result in itself, though it easily could be. Then the draft's "hur lätt … kan behandlas" holds and the source's "lätt behandlas" fails.
Set aside: the difference runs in the direction of less assertion, not more. The source asserts the practice; the draft asserts the ease with which it can arise, which the source's statement entails. Nothing is added to the material, no third party is characterised, and the surrounding sentences keep the claim inside the author's own reflection — "Det här är min reflektion, inte något jag har mätt hos andra." A weakening that removes no supported qualification and creates no unsupported claim is not a source-support defect.

**Case D — the narrowed disclaimer leaves room for another measurement.**
Case: she has measured something else about colleagues — how they fill in templates, say — but not how they use their workdays. Then the draft's disclaimer holds while the source's fails.
Set aside: "Det här är min reflektion, inte något jag har mätt hos andra." The supplied statement excludes any measuring of others, so the case it leaves room for cannot arise, and the draft asserts no measurement. What is unclaimed stays unclaimed; the draft states nothing about others' workdays, since the phrase appears only inside the denial.

**Case E — no meeting notice is established to exist.**
Case: the library's *mötesmall* is an internal planning or minutes document that is never sent out, so nothing at this library is a *kallelse*.
Set aside: the sentence is a normative inference in the author's own argument — we can hardly demand a finished question in a meeting notice — not a report of the library's practice, and an ordinary reader takes "kallelsen" generically. The template's own fields, as supplied — "starttid, sluttid, deltagare och dagordning" — are the content of a meeting notice, so the case does not make the statement fail; it would only make it idle.

**Case F — what must be formulated is a different thing in the two versions.**
Case: a team whose need for a joint inquiry is obvious (a complaint arrived) but who never say why these particular people's time is needed. The source's demand ("varför vi behöver just varandras tid") is unmet; the draft's ("varför vi behöver undersöka något tillsammans") could be met, and the reverse case exists too.
Set aside: the draft states both halves across the two adjacent sentences. The immediately following question — "Vad behöver just de här människorna varandra till?" — is the source's "varför vi behöver just varandras tid", and the preceding sentences establish the exploratory case in which the question is not yet ready, so the source's scope qualification "också när syftet är att undersöka något tillsammans" is honoured rather than lost. The passage is the author's own advocacy, and the view it presents as given is the view the notes state.

**Case G — hope is attached to the box rather than the question.**
Case: a reader takes "hoppas på ännu en ruta" as her considered position, contradicting "tvivlet på ännu en ruta".
Set aside: "Det finns en komik i att försöka lösa ett problem med formulär genom att utöka formuläret." The supplied statement is precisely that self-contradiction, named as comedy by the author herself, and the draft frames the sentence as the comedy ("Det finns förstås en komik i detta"). The considered position is restated two sentences later in the source's own terms: "Jag vill prova frågan, men behålla tvivlet på rutan."
