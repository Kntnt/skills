### Checks that produced no pairing because the draft makes no such claim

Recorded so their absence is deliberate rather than overlooked, and because the material's judging boundary rules several of them out as unsupported.

- No legal requirement, protest, discrimination case, party-political motive or saving is asserted anywhere in the draft — the five things the material states are not established.
- No evaluative characterisation of the counts. The draft gives 96 and 24 with their exclusions (B3, B4) and never calls either figure high, low, modest, significant or telling, and never converts 24 into a share of residents. B6 explicitly denies the inference from channel to inability, and the draft nowhere reintroduces it by hedge — there is no "tyder på att", "riskerar att" or equivalent that would smuggle the attribution back in. Chronology (trial → 18 juni meeting → September removal → proposed six-month trial → later decision) is consistent across every passage.
- No time measurement or saving figure is quoted, which matches D3's absence.
- No claim about whether the pilot's two venues are among the seven; the material does not settle it and the draft does not assert it.
- No commercial call to action, consistent with the brief.

### Length

The brief asks for roughly 400 words; the draft runs somewhat under that. Per the Claims contract, a stated length constrains the use of material and never licenses adding to it, and the correct response to thin material is the length the material supports. The draft has taken that route: it is short rather than padded, and no passage invents material to reach the target. This is therefore not a source-support finding. If the gap were to be closed, the material that would close it is a time measurement per booking route and a costing of the proposed six-month trial — precisely the two things the material states do not exist.

None.

Every factual and attributed claim in the draft pairs with a supplied passage at the same subject, scope, time, modality and certainty, and with the same attribution of whose knowledge or assertion it is. In the three places where wording departs from the source — the widened subject in B5, the ascription of the document's motive to the administration in D2, and *behöver* → *måste* in P4 — I looked for a concrete case, compatible with the supplied material, in which the draft's statement holds and the source's fails or the reverse, and in each case a supplied statement excludes it: B4 and B3 for B5, "Förvaltningens verkliga invändning … är dubbel administration" for D2, and for P4 the source's own requirement on the committee. The draft's remaining unsourced passages are the author's argument and advocacy resting on supported material, and the facts and views they present as given are all accounted for above.

The qualifications that do work in this material are all carried: bookings are not unique persons, the report measures no age, ability or digital habit, the figures cannot be read as a share of residents, the reason-giving is voluntary, the association has neither costed nor funded the trial, and the cost remains for the committee to decide. The draft criticises the decision basis sharply — "Ett permanent beslut behöver ett bättre underlag", "besvarar därför inte frågan" — without attributing a false motive to the administration and without neutralising the criticism into vacuity, which is what the judging boundary asks for.

Since draft and material are both in Swedish, no claim crossed a language boundary, so there are no translation findings and no translated quotation to check for idiomatic target-language speech.
