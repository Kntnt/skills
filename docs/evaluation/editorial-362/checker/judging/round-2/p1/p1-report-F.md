## 2. Findings

### Finding 1 — "digital proficiency" names something the source does not (source support)

- **Draft:** "The report does not measure age, functional ability, or digital proficiency."
- **Source:** "Den mäter inte ålder, funktionsförmåga eller digital vana."
- **What differs:** the thing measured. "Digital vana" is accustomedness — how habituated someone is to using digital services. "Digital proficiency" is skill — how capable someone is. These come apart in both directions.
- **Case where the source holds and the draft fails:** the pilot report includes a one-question self-rating of how confident bookers feel with the online form (a proficiency measure) but records nothing about how often or how routinely they use digital services. Nothing in the material excludes this: the material enumerates three unmeasured items without claiming the list is exhaustive, and the report could still fail to support any statement about what share of residents cannot book digitally, because it counts bookings rather than unique persons and its bookers are not the residents at large.
- **Case where the draft holds and the source fails:** the report logs how frequently each booker had used the municipality's e-services before (a habit measure) but never touches skill. Then "does not measure digital proficiency" is true while "mäter inte … digital vana" is false.
- **Why it matters here:** the sentence is the draft's own inventory of what the evidence lacks, and the paragraph that follows rests its central restraint — that the phone counts cannot establish who is unable to book online — on that inventory. Substituting the ability dimension for the habit dimension quietly narrows what the report is said to be silent about.
- **Smallest supported repair:** replace "digital proficiency" with "digital habits" — "The report does not measure age, functional ability, or digital habits." ("how accustomed people are to digital services" is an equally faithful rendering if a fuller phrase is wanted.)

No other finding.

## 3. Considered and not reported

**3.1 "before the board decides whether to remove either option" (lead).** The source's decision space is three-way ("tas bort, ändras eller behållas") and the lead names only removal. Not a finding: the lead asserts only that the trial comes before the decision, which "Därefter kan den avgöra" supports, and the closing sentence restores all three outcomes verbatim, so no reader is left with a narrowed decision space.

**3.2 "community venues" for "föreningslokaler".** A community venue in general need not be an association venue. Not a finding: in this context the term refers throughout to one fixed set — the municipality's seven venues, two of which were piloted — so nothing here can fall under one term and not the other.

**3.3 "Staff would record" for "ska förvaltningen registrera".** "Förvaltningen" is the administration as an organ. Not a finding: the material gives the administration no agent other than "personalen", and no claim in the draft depends on which office within it does the recording.

**3.4 The opinion and advocacy passages.** "The current evidence leaves essential questions unanswered", "Using them to make that claim would overstate the evidence", "Duplicate administration is a real objection that our proposal must address", "That is an inadequate basis for a permanent change" and the closing recommendation are the author's own argument resting on supported material — the two stated absences, the scope mismatch between two piloted venues and seven, and the material's own account of the administration's real objection. Read as an ordinary reader would, each presents facts that hold up; the reasoning and the sharpness are editorial choices the boundaries invite ("Författaren ska kunna kritisera beslutsunderlaget skarpt"), and are preserved.

**3.5 "Those are bookings, not individual people" stated without attribution.** The source attributes the accounting rule to the report ("Rapporten räknar"), the draft states it flatly. Not a finding: the material asserts the rule as fact, and the sentence's subject "Those" refers to the report's own counts named in the preceding sentence.
