# Judgement — p1-opinion-variable, round 2

Fixture: `docs/evaluation/editorial-362/checker/fixtures/p1-opinion-variable.md`. Six reports, neutral names A–F. Judged from the fixture; each report's reasoning weighed as argument, not as evidence.

## My reading of the focus passage

**Draft:** "The report does not measure age, functional ability, or digital proficiency."
**Material:** "Den mäter inte ålder, funktionsförmåga eller digital vana."

The draft reproduces the material's own three-item denial, item for item and in order, with the same subject (the report) and the same polarity. The only question is the third item: *digital vana* is habituation or familiarity from use, and "digital proficiency" leans toward skill. Read in context, that does not change what the passage asserts, and no repair is warranted, for three reasons.

First, the case the flagging reports need — a report that measures digital proficiency but not digital habit — is not compatible with the supplied material. The material draws its own conclusion from this very list: "Telefonbokningarna kan därför inte användas för att säga hur stor andel av invånarna som inte kan boka digitalt." A report that had measured how capable its users are with digital services would bear directly on that share, so the material's "därför" is only available because the report characterises its users in no such way at all. The same paragraph says the report "räknar bokningar, inte unika personer" — it does not reach persons, let alone their skills.

Second, the draft's sentence is a negative claim inside an argument about what the counts cannot show, and the argument it carries is untouched: the next sentence repeats the material's conclusion exactly ("cannot tell us what share of Lervik's residents cannot book online"), and the habit dimension the flagging reports say is lost is stated openly two sentences later ("a phone booking is a reason to investigate why someone chose that option"). An ordinary reader takes from the passage that the report measured no user attribute — which is what the material says.

Third, *vana* in this administrative register sits within the range that English renders as digital experience, familiarity or proficiency. Preferring "digital experience" or "digital habits" is a defensible wording preference; it is not a claim the material fails to carry.

I also read the rest of the draft for source support and found nothing unsupported: every figure, date, document, motive, disclaimer and decision branch pairs with a supplied passage at the same scope and modality, and the evaluative sentences ("an inadequate basis for a permanent change" and the rest) are the author's own argument resting on stated absences, which the material's boundaries expressly license ("Författaren ska kunna kritisera beslutsunderlaget skarpt"). The lead's compression of the three-way decision to "whether to remove either option" is restored verbatim at the close, and the closing paragraph keeps the cost uncertainty. So a report with no findings is the correct outcome on this fixture, not an incurious one.
