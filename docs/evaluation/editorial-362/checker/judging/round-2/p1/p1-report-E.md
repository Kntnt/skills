## 2. Findings

### F1 — "digital proficiency" states a different measurement gap from "digital vana" (source support; meaning changed in crossing languages, outside quoted speech)

Draft: "The report does not measure age, functional ability, or digital proficiency."
Source: "Den mäter inte ålder, funktionsförmåga eller digital vana."

What differs: the thing measured. "Digital vana" is habituation — how used to digital services a person is, from use. "Digital proficiency" is skill or competence. The two come apart in both directions.

Directional case, compatible with the supplied material: suppose the pilot report had included a self-rated digital-skill question but asked nothing about how often residents use digital services. Then "Den mäter inte … digital vana" still holds, while "The report does not measure … digital proficiency" is false. The material fixes only the three listed non-measurements and that the report counts bookings; it does not state that the report assesses skill, so the case is compatible with it and the draft's sentence is not entailed by the material the way D17 is.

Why it matters here rather than being a synonym preference: habituation is the axis the article's own argument turns on. The piece argues that a phone booking is not evidence of inability but a reason to ask why the phone is used; habit is exactly the explanation that separates "chose the phone" from "cannot use the web". Substituting skill for habit both asserts a gap the material does not establish and drops the gap it does establish.

Smallest supported repair: replace "digital proficiency" with "digital experience" — or, if a fuller rendering is wanted, "how accustomed residents are to digital services". Nothing else in the sentence changes.
