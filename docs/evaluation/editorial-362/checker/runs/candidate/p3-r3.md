## Findings

### Finding 1 — the consent's status is attributed to the submission rather than left unstated (source support)

- Draft: "Our submission also makes no claim to have secured the building owner's consent for rehearsal use; **it does not establish whether that consent exists**."
- Source: "The association's submission makes no representation that it has secured the building owner's consent for rehearsal use. **The supplied material does not state the consent's actual status.**"
- What is missing or changed: the second source sentence is a statement about the material as a whole — the status is simply not supplied. The draft converts it into a claim about one document, the association's own submission, asserting that the submission settles the question in neither direction.
- Both directions tested. A case compatible with the supplied material: the submission states plainly that the association has not yet approached the building owner and has no consent. That is fully consistent with "makes no representation that it has secured the building owner's consent", so the source sentences both hold; but the submission would then establish that the consent does not exist, and the draft's clause fails. The reverse case also separates them: the submission could be silent on consent while the status is recorded elsewhere, so that the draft's clause holds and the source's sentence about the supplied material would not. The two statements are therefore not interchangeable, however natural the draft's wording reads.
- Smallest supported repair: delete the clause, ending the sentence at "for rehearsal use." The preceding clause is exactly supported, and removing the addition leaves the consent's status unclaimed, which is the state of knowledge the material preserves. If a sentence about the unknown status is wanted, it must not be pinned to the submission — for example, "I cannot tell the trustees what the position on that consent is" — but the deletion is the smaller repair.

No other finding. Every remaining claim in the draft, including the headline, the byline and the closing ask, is either matched to a source passage above or is the author's own argument, reflection or advocacy resting on supported material.
