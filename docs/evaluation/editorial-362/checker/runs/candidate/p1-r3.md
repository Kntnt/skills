## 2. Findings

### Finding 1 — "digital proficiency" is not what the report is said not to measure (source support)

- **Draft:** "The report does not measure age, functional ability, or digital proficiency."
- **Source:** "Den mäter inte ålder, funktionsförmåga eller digital vana."
- **What differs:** the thing named. "Digital vana" is habituation — how accustomed someone is to
  using digital services, how often and how routinely. "Digital proficiency" is skill or competence.
  These can come apart in this context: a resident may be highly proficient yet unaccustomed to the
  municipality's web booking, and another may be thoroughly accustomed to it without being proficient
  with digital tools generally.
- **Both directions.** A concrete compatible case for the source holding while the draft fails: the
  pilot report includes a short competence check of the two venues' bookers but records nothing about
  how habitually they use digital services. Then "does not measure digital vana" is true and "does not
  measure digital proficiency" is false. The material states which attributes the report omits; it
  does not state that proficiency is among them, so the draft asserts a non-measurement of its own
  and drops the one the material actually gives. In the other direction, a report measuring habit but
  not proficiency is ruled out by the material, so only the first case arises — which is enough.
- **Why it matters here:** this sentence is the support for the next paragraph's inference that the
  phone bookings cannot establish what share of residents cannot book online. The material grounds
  that inference on the absence of habit data; the draft grounds it on absent skill data, which the
  material does not supply.
- **Smallest repair:** replace "digital proficiency" with a rendering of "digital vana" —
  "familiarity with digital services", or "digital habit". Recommended sentence: "The report does not
  measure age, functional ability, or familiarity with digital services."

### Finding 2 — the trial's recording duty is assigned to staff, not to the administration (source support)

- **Draft:** "Staff would record the time spent on each booking method and invite users to explain,
  voluntarily, why they chose phone or web."
- **Source:** "Under försöket ska förvaltningen registrera tidsåtgång per bokningsväg och be
  användarna frivilligt ange varför de väljer telefon eller webb."
- **What differs:** the subject. "Förvaltningen" is the administration as an organisation; the draft
  narrows it to "staff", and it does so in a text that has already used "staff" for the source's
  "personalen" two paragraphs earlier ("staff would no longer have to enter information through two
  workflows"). Within this draft, then, "staff" names the personnel whose duplicate work is the whole
  dispute, and the sentence reads as an additional task placed on those same people.
- **Both directions.** A concrete compatible case: the administration meets the requirement by having
  the booking system log handling time per channel automatically, with no member of staff recording
  anything. The source statement holds — förvaltningen has registered tidsåtgång per bokningsväg —
  while the draft's statement fails. That case is directly compatible with the material, which
  assigns the duty to the administration and says nothing about how it is discharged. The reverse
  direction yields no case, since staff recording is one way the administration records.
- **Why it matters here:** the paragraph's purpose is to answer the duplicate-administration
  objection. Saying that staff would do the recording concedes added staff work that the association's
  proposal does not specify, which weakens the author's own supported position against an objection
  the draft has just called real.
- **Smallest repair:** restore the source's subject: "The administration would record the time spent
  on each booking method and invite users to explain, voluntarily, why they chose phone or web."
