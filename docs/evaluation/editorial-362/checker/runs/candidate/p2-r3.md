## 2. Findings

### Finding 1 — "In September 2025" is extended over the choice of supplier (scope of a time adverbial)

**Draft (paragraph 1 of the body):** "In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log and chose Svale Systems after testing whether it could show the status of each repair."

**Source:** "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings." and, as a separate sentence carrying no date, "It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair."

**Support missing or changed.** The material dates exactly one event: the decision. The choice of supplier is ordered after a period of testing but never dated. By coordinating "decided" and "chose" under a single fronted "In September 2025", the draft places the choice in that month as well, since a fronted time adverbial scopes over both conjuncts of the coordination it heads. Circumstantial detail is a claim, and a date is circumstantial detail; the draft therefore asserts a dated fact the material leaves open.

**Both directions.**
- A case compatible with the material in which the source holds and the draft fails: the team decides in September 2025, tests the status display through October, and signs with Svale in early November, still leaving room for configuration, training over two sessions, an eight-week trial and a note written on 4 December 2025. Every sentence of the material is true in this case; the draft's sentence is false.
- The converse case, in which the draft holds and the material fails, does not exist — the draft's reading entails the material's — which is why the defect is a widening of scope rather than a contradiction. It nonetheless states as known a date the material does not claim.

**Smallest supported repair.** Split the coordination so the adverbial governs only the event the material dates, restoring the material's own two-sentence order:

> In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log. It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair.

This changes nothing else — same events, same order, same criterion, same acting party — and the second sentence carries no date, exactly as the material does not.

**Secondary note, repaired by the same edit.** In the draft as written, the pronoun in "whether it could show the status of each repair" takes "Svale Systems" as its nearest antecedent, where the material tests "the log". This is not filed as a separate finding: on either resolution the act described is the same test of the same product, so no case compatible with the material separates them, and pronoun clarity as such belongs to general editorial review rather than to this comparison. The repair above substitutes "the log" and removes the question.
