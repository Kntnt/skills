## 5. Findings

### Finding 1 — "digital proficiency" for "digital vana" (source support, meaning changed in crossing languages)

- **Draft (line 42):** "The report does not measure age, functional ability, or digital proficiency."
- **Source:** "Den mäter inte ålder, funktionsförmåga eller digital vana."
- **What differs:** the thing said not to be measured. "Digital vana" is habituation — how accustomed someone is to using digital services. "Digital proficiency" is skill or competence. The sentence is a negative claim about the report's instrument, which is exactly where the two come apart.
- **Test, both directions.** Case where the source statement holds and the draft's fails: the pilot report asks respondents to rate their own digital skill but asks nothing about how often or how long they have used digital services. Then "den mäter inte digital vana" is true while "the report does not measure digital proficiency" is false. Case where the draft's holds and the source's fails: the report logs how frequently each booker has used the municipality's digital services before, with no skill question. Then the draft's sentence is true and the source's is false. Both cases are compatible with the supplied material, which states only that age, functional ability and digital vana are unmeasured. The terms therefore do not name the same thing here.
- **Consequence:** the draft asserts an absence in the report's measurements that the material does not establish, and drops one the material does establish. The paragraph's conclusion at pair 11 is unaffected either way, so the repair is confined to the noun phrase.
- **Smallest supported repair:** replace "digital proficiency" with "digital experience" (equally, "how used to digital services people are"). No other wording changes.
