## Findings

### F1 — Natural-gender assertion about Lena Ortiz (source support)

- Draft (line 49): "Ortiz explained the distinction in **her** email".
- Source: "Collection coordinator Lena Ortiz sent the following complete quotations in an email headed 'Catalog pilot: notes for publication'." And: "There is no question-and-answer transcript, observation of an archive visit, or account of Ortiz's manner or emotions."
- What is missing: the material conveys Ortiz's name and role and nothing about her gender. A given name is not a source passage; identifying the email's owner as Ortiz establishes the referent, the person and the number, but not the natural-gender feature the English possessive "her" asserts.
- Both-directions test. A case compatible with all supplied material: Ortiz is a man, or uses "they", and the email is still his or their email. Then "Ortiz's email" holds and "her email" fails. Conversely, "her email" entails "Ortiz's email", so the draft asserts strictly more than the material supports. The case exists, so this is a finding.
- Note that the draft's other attribution (line 53) already avoids the pronoun, so the draft is internally inconsistent about the feature as well.
- Smallest supported repair: replace "her email" with "the email" — "Ortiz wrote in the email:" — or, if the possessive is wanted, "Ortiz's email". Nothing else in the sentence or paragraph needs to change.

### F2 — Manner and intent attributed to Ortiz (source support)

- Draft (line 49): "Ortiz **explained the distinction** in her email".
- Source: "Collection coordinator Lena Ortiz sent the following complete quotations in an email headed 'Catalog pilot: notes for publication'." And: "There is no question-and-answer transcript, observation of an archive visit, or account of Ortiz's manner or emotions."
- What is changed: the material supplies an email of quotations supplied for publication and rules out any account of Ortiz's manner. "Explained" adds two things the material does not carry: a communicative manner and purpose (clarifying something to someone), and the draft's own endorsement that the contrast she states is a real distinction rather than her stated view. The neighbouring attribution in the same draft uses the supported verb, "wrote".
- Both-directions test. A case compatible with the material: Ortiz wrote the three sentences as notes for publication without explaining anything to anyone, and the contrast between an empty field and an estimated year is her working position, which the archive has not tested. Then "Ortiz wrote in the email" holds and "Ortiz explained the distinction" fails. The case exists, so this is a finding.
- Smallest supported repair: "Ortiz wrote in the email:" — which also repairs F1 in the same edit. If the paragraph needs the contrast named, it can be named as hers: "Ortiz set out the archive's reasoning in the email:" is not supported either; keep "wrote".

Combined repair for lines 49, replacing "Ortiz explained the distinction in her email:":

> Ortiz wrote in the email:

Nothing preceding or following it requires adjustment; the quotation itself states the distinction to the reader.
