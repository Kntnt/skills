## Findings

### Finding 1 — Natural gender asserted for a named person the material never genders

- Draft passage: "Ortiz explained the distinction in her email:" (section "Keeping uncertainty visible").
- Source passages, in the source's own words: "Collection coordinator Lena Ortiz sent the following complete quotations in an email headed 'Catalog pilot: notes for publication'"; "There is no question-and-answer transcript, observation of an archive visit, or account of Ortiz's manner or emotions."; "The archive supplied that email and the pilot note for this supplier-published case."
- What differs: the possessive pronoun "her" conveys, separately from its referent, third person, singular number and feminine natural gender for Lena Ortiz. The material identifies the referent — the email is indeed Ortiz's — and supports person and number, but it never states Ortiz's gender. Every mention in the material is "Lena Ortiz", "Ortiz" or "Ortiz's"; no pronoun for her appears anywhere in the supplied package, and no gender is given in the brief, the pilot note, the email header or the quotations. Identifying the referent does not establish this feature. The only thing pointing to it is the given name "Lena", which is an inference from a name, not a statement in the material, and this is a natural-gender assertion about a real person, not grammatical gender agreement (English has no grammatical gender to require here).
- Both directions: a concrete case compatible with all the supplied material — Lena Ortiz is the archive's collection coordinator, sent exactly those three quotations in that email, and uses "he" or "they". Every source sentence still holds; the draft's "her email" fails. Conversely, no case makes the source sentences fail while "her email" holds, since the source says strictly less. The difference is therefore a defect, not a defensible reading.
- Smallest supported repair: drop the pronoun and keep the same referent — "Ortiz explained the distinction in the same email:" (or "in the email:"). This changes three characters' worth of wording, preserves the sentence, the voice, the attribution to Ortiz and the placement of the quotation, and asserts nothing the material does not.

No other findings. Every other draft passage above either matches its source passage in thing, subject, scope, time, modality, certainty and whose assertion, or differs only in ways for which no case exists that separates them.
