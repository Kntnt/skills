## 5. Findings

Ordered by severity. Each names the support that is missing or changed and the smallest repair that restores it.

### F1 — Moderate — The September 2025 date is extended over the supplier choice and the test

- Draft: “In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log **and chose Svale Systems after testing** whether it could show the status of each repair.”
- Claim: C14.
- What changed: the material dates one event — the decision to trial (“In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings”). The choice of Svale is a separate, undated sentence (“It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair”). Coordinating the two verbs under one date adverbial distributes “In September 2025” across both, so the draft dates the test and the supplier choice to a month the material never assigns them, and compresses a decision → test → choice sequence into a single month. Chronology is a claim; this one has no support.
- Source-compatible counterexample: decision in September, testing through October, choice in late October — consistent with every supplied sentence and with the trial note of 4 December 2025.
- Smallest repair — split the sentence so the date governs only the decision:
  > In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log. It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair.
  (This repair also resolves F3.)

### F2 — Moderate — An outcome of the trial is asserted in the publisher’s voice

- Draft (summary): “The eight-week trial **gave the team a common view of reports**, but its supervisor would allow more preparation time before starting again.”
- Claim: C6.
- What is missing: the material supplies no finding about what the trial produced. It supplies a want (“The team wanted staff on different shifts to see the same information”), two medians the note “explicitly does not attribute… to the software,” a report count, and Lind’s assessment “Having one view of the reports helps us.” Stating the result as an unattributed fact in the supplier-published summary raises an attributed assessment to a measured outcome, in the one paragraph a reader is most likely to read alone.
- Smallest repair — attribute it, leaving the rest of the sentence untouched:
  > Its supervisor says the shared log gives the team one view of the reports, but she would allow more preparation time before starting again.
  Or, keeping the draft’s subject: “During the eight-week trial the team worked from one view of the reports, which its supervisor says helps them, but…”

### F3 — Moderate — “it” reads as Svale Systems, changing what was tested

- Draft: “…chose Svale Systems after testing whether **it** could show the status of each repair.”
- Claim: C15.
- What changed: the material names the log as the object of the test (“after testing whether **the log** could show the status of each repair”). In the draft, the nearest antecedent is “Svale Systems,” so the sentence reads as a test of the supplier or the supplier’s system as a whole. That enlarges a narrow feature check into a supplier evaluation, which the material specifically forecloses: “No comparison with another supplier is available.” The supplied context settles the referent, so this is a concrete obstruction, not a preferred synonym.
- Smallest repair — restore the supplied noun:
  > …chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair.

### F4 — Low — The title asserts a present, continuing gain

- Draft (title): “Elm Quay **gains** a shared view of repair reports.”
- Claim: C1.
- What is missing: the same support gap as F2, in the strongest position in the document, plus a tense problem. The trial is over (“The trial lasted eight weeks”; the note is dated 4 December 2025) and the material does not say whether the log is still in use in the two buildings — only that “The trial has not yet expanded.” The present-tense “gains” asserts both an outcome and its continuation.
- Smallest repair — make the title report what the trial was, not what it achieved:
  > Elm Quay trials a shared repair log in two buildings
  Or, if the gain is to stay, attribute it: “Elm Quay’s supervisor: one view of the reports helps us”.

### F5 — Low — Present-tense statements about the note and the measurements are narrowed to the past

- Draft: “the note explicitly **did not** attribute the difference to the software… There **were** no measurements of costs, resident satisfaction, or completion times.”
- Claims: C32, C34.
- What changed: the material states both in the present (“the note explicitly **does not** attribute…”; “There **are** no cost, resident-satisfaction or completion-time measurements”). These are standing properties: the note still contains no such attribution, and no such measurements exist. The past tense turns them into facts about a past moment and leaves open that the note now attributes the difference, or that cost, satisfaction and completion figures have since been produced. For a reader, that quietly weakens the two qualifications that do the most work in this case.
- Smallest repair — restore the present tense in both places:
  > …and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software. These figures concern assignment, not repair completion. There are no measurements of costs, resident satisfaction, or completion times.

### F6 — Low — “specific to the trial” narrows Lind’s assessment beyond the material

- Draft: “**That assessment was specific to the trial**; Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company.”
- Claim: C37.
- What is missing: the material characterises the quotation as “her actual, qualified assessment” that “is not an inference from the figures,” and records one limit only — she “did not recommend Svale to every housing company.” It does not say her view is confined to this trial, and quotation 2 has her looking ahead to “the next building.” The gloss is an added scope restriction, not a supplied one, and the sentence’s second clause already carries the limit that is supplied.
