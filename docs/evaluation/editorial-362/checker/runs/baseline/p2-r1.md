## 3. Findings

Ordered by severity. Each gives the draft passage, the support that is missing or

### F1 — Chronology: the supplier choice is dated to September 2025 (medium)

- Draft: "In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log and chose
  Svale Systems after testing whether it could show the status of each repair."
- Changed support: the fronted time adverbial "In September 2025" scopes over both
  coordinated verbs, so the draft dates the *choice of Svale* — and, through "after", the
  capability testing that preceded it — to September 2025. The material dates only the
  decision: "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair
  log in two buildings." The choice is reported in a separate, undated sentence: "It
  chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each
  repair."
- Counterexample compatible with every supplied fact: the team decides in September
  2025, tests through September and October, chooses Svale in early October, and runs
  the eight-week trial from early October to late November, the note following on
  4 December 2025.
- Smallest supported repair: split the sentence so the date governs only the decision —
  "In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log. It chose Svale
  Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair."
  (This repair also resolves F2.)

### F2 — Referent: "it" attaches to Svale Systems, not the log (medium)

- Draft: "chose Svale Systems after testing whether **it** could show the status of each
  repair".
- Obstruction (not a synonym preference): the nearest and syntactically most available
  antecedent for "it" is "Svale Systems", the immediately preceding noun phrase, which
  makes the draft read as a test of the supplier rather than of the product. The supplied
  context settles the intended referent: "testing whether **the log** could show the
  status of each repair."
- Smallest supported repair: "whether the log could show the status of each repair."

### F3 — State of knowledge: absent measurements moved into the past (medium-low)

- Draft: "There **were** no measurements of costs, resident satisfaction, or completion
  times."
- Changed support: the material states this in the present — "There **are** no cost,
  resident-satisfaction or completion-time measurements" — i.e. none exist, full stop.
  The past tense confines the absence to the trial period and leaves open that such
  measurements exist now. What is unmeasured must stay unmeasured, and no supplied
  statement supports a later measurement. The neighbouring summary line "Expansion
  remains undecided" keeps the material's present tense, so this is not a uniform
  narrative-tense choice.
- Smallest supported repair: "There are no measurements of cost, resident satisfaction,
  or completion time."

### F4 — Tense: the note's non-attribution moved into the past (low)

- Draft: "the note explicitly **did not** attribute the difference to the software".
- Changed support: the material states a standing property of an existing document —
  "the note explicitly **does not** attribute the difference to the software". The
  substance survives and no causal claim is introduced; only the currency of the
  document's own stance is weakened.
- Smallest supported repair: "the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to
  the software".

### F5 — Unsupported narrowing of Lind's assessment (medium-low)

- Draft: "That assessment was specific to the trial; Lind did not recommend Svale to
  every housing company."
- Missing support: nothing in the material confines Lind's final assessment to the

  sentence already carries the guard the material requires, so the first clause adds an
  unsupported characterisation without adding protection.
- Smallest supported repair: delete the first clause — "Lind did not recommend Svale to
  every housing company."

### F6 — The shared-view outcome: narrator voice and company-wide scope (medium-low)

Two passages, one cause.

- Draft (title): "Elm Quay gains a shared view of repair reports."
- Draft (summary): "The eight-week trial gave the team a common view of reports".
- Missing/changed support: the only evidence that the shared view was achieved is Lind's

- Note: this is not a causal-inference defect. The draft nowhere links the software to
  the assignment-time difference, and "gains" in the present tense is consistent with
  Q3's present tense.
- Smallest supported repairs: title — "Elm Quay's maintenance team gains a shared view
  of repair reports in two buildings"; summary — "According to its supervisor, the
  eight-week trial gave the team a common view of reports".
