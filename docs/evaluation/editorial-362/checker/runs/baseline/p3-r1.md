## 12. Findings on source support

### F1. A silence the material scopes to the supplied material is attributed to the association's submission

- **Draft passage (paragraph 4):** "Our submission also makes no claim to have secured the building owner's consent for rehearsal use; it does not establish whether that consent exists."
- **Proposition at issue (P23):** Room to Rehearse's submission does not settle whether the building owner's consent for rehearsal use exists.
- **Supporting passages considered:** "The association's submission makes no representation that it has secured the building owner's consent for rehearsal use." And: "The supplied material does not state the consent's actual status."
- **What is missing or changed:** the material makes two separate statements with two different scopes, and the draft merges them. The first is narrow: the submission does not claim consent has been *secured*. The second is about the supplied material as a whole, not about the submission. The draft's second clause asserts something stronger than either: that the submission settles the question in *neither* direction. Nothing supplied establishes that. The submission's text is not reproduced anywhere in the material, so a submission that reports the owner's refusal, or a lapsed permission, remains source-compatible; such a submission would still make no representation of having secured consent, and the supplied material would still not state the consent's actual status, yet the draft's clause would be false. The direction of the error is not an overstated fact about consent, and the draft's instinct here is right: it keeps the consent's status open, as the contract's state-of-knowledge rule requires. What it gets wrong is whose silence it is, attributing to a named document a completeness of silence that the material asserts only of the package as a whole.
- **Smallest supported repair:** delete the clause, ending the sentence at "rehearsal use":
  "Our submission also makes no claim to have secured the building owner's consent for rehearsal use."
  This keeps every supported element, keeps the consent's status unasserted in either direction, and removes the only unsupported element. The surrounding sentences are untouched, the paragraph's candour is unchanged, and the loss of roughly eight words leaves the piece inside the brief's 250 to 300 word range.
- **Repairs rejected as unsupported:** "and nothing before the trustees states whether that consent exists" swaps one unsupported scope for another, since the material speaks of what it supplies to the writer, not of what is before the trustees. "We make no claim about that consent either way" again asserts more about the association's representations than the material records.
- **Severity:** low. One subordinate clause, no number, date, quotation or attributed position affected, and no reader is misled about the consent itself.

No other finding on source support. No invented fact, source, quotation, scene or opinion appears in the draft; every number, date, name and list is reproduced exactly; no sequence or correlation is upgraded to a cause in the title, the body or the closing paragraph; and no qualification that changes a claim is dropped.

The material is sufficient for the piece as written; no gap needs naming, and after the F1 repair none opens.
