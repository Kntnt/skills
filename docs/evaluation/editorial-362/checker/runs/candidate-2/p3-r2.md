# Source comparison — p3-document-scope

Draft language: en_GB. Source language: English. Single-language package, so no
term is carried across languages and there are no quoted-speech translations to
assess; the translation axis is empty throughout (see the closing note).

## Paired accounting

Each row pairs a draft passage with the source passage it rests on, quoted in the
source's own words, and states what differs.

### Title and byline

**1. "Investigate rehearsal use before letting Bellwick Hall's wing"**
Source: "She wants the trustees of Bellwick Hall to investigate evening rehearsal
use before committing the former storage wing to long-term commercial letting."
Differs: nothing material. The title is an imperative stating the action the
author advocates, and the advocacy is the author's own, resting on supported
material. "Bellwick Hall's wing" names the wing as a part of the hall, which the
material's "the hall's main rooms" and "the former storage wing" together
support; it asserts nothing about who owns the building, so it does not collide
with "the building owner's consent" being a separate, unstated matter. Not a
finding.

**2. "*By Amina Reed, chair of Room to Rehearse*"**
Source: "in the voice of Amina Reed, chair of the local music association Room to
Rehearse". Differs: "local music association" is dropped, which narrows nothing
and adds nothing. Not a finding.

### Paragraph 1

**3. "Before Bellwick Hall's trustees commit its former storage wing to three
years of commercial letting, they should investigate whether it could accommodate
weekday evening rehearsals."**
Source: "The trustees' paper of 3 July 2026 proposes letting the former storage
wing for three years"; "long-term commercial letting" (brief); "Nine said they
would consider using a rehearsal room on weekday evenings."
Differs: modality is handled correctly. The source has a *proposal*; the draft's
"Before ... commit" presupposes only that the commitment has not yet been made,
which the proposal status supports. "three years of commercial letting" combines
the paper's three-year term with the brief's characterisation of the letting as
commercial. "weekday evening rehearsals" matches the survey's weekday-evening
scope. "should investigate" is advocacy. Not a finding.

**4. "As chair of Room to Rehearse, I want that possibility assessed before they
choose between the two uses."**
Source: "Reed accepts this gap and wants the trustees to commission the study
before choosing between uses."
Differs: "the two uses" is definite where the source is bare, but the two uses
are named in the source ("choosing between uses", with the comparison being "of
the two uses"). Not a finding.

### Paragraph 2

**5. "The trustees' paper of 3 July 2026 makes a serious case: predictable income
would help meet maintenance costs."**
Source: "The trustees' paper of 3 July 2026 proposes letting the former storage
wing for three years. It says predictable income would help meet the hall's
maintenance costs."
Differs: the date and the content are exact. The colon carries the attribution:
an ordinary reader takes the clause after it as the content of the case the paper
makes, not as the author's own assertion that predictable income would help, and
the next sentence ("That concern deserves weight") keeps the concern as the
paper's. "makes a serious case" and "deserves weight" are the author's evaluative
stance in an opinion piece about material she has set out, not a factual
characterisation needing separate support. "maintenance costs" drops "the hall's",
which the context restores without changing scope. Not a finding.

**6. "But the paper names neither a prospective tenant nor a rental figure."**
Source: "No prospective tenant or rental figure is named."
Differs: the source's passive leaves the locus of the non-naming implicit within
its account of the paper; the draft pins it to the paper. Tested both ways: if the
paper named a tenant or a figure, the source sentence would be false on any
reading, so the source entails the draft's narrower claim. The draft is the weaker
statement, so there is no case in which the source holds and the draft fails. Not
a finding.

**7. "Commercial letting may prove the better option; a comparison should help
establish that."**
Source: "She accepts that commercial letting may prove the better option."; "Her
proposal is that the trustees ... publish a comparison of the two uses."
Differs: the first clause matches the source's modality ("may prove") exactly. The
second clause is the author's reasoning for why she wants the comparison, resting
on supported material, and asserts no outcome. Not a finding.

### Paragraph 3

**8. "Our survey gives a reason to examine rehearsal use."**
Source: "The survey is a reason to investigate a rehearsal room, not a promise
that it will pay its way."
Differs: nothing. Strength ("a reason", not evidence or proof) is preserved. Not a
finding.

**9. "Of 14 local music groups surveyed, nine said they would consider using a
room on weekday evenings."**
Source: "Room to Rehearse surveyed 14 local music groups. Nine said they would
consider using a rehearsal room on weekday evenings."
Differs: counts, denominator and the reported modality ("would consider") are
exact. "a room" for "a rehearsal room" loses a word, but the preceding sentence
has just named rehearsal use, so the referent is settled by the immediate context
and no other kind of room is in play. Not a finding.

**10. "Those responses are expressions of interest, not booking commitments."**
Source: "This is an expression of interest, not a booking commitment."
Differs: singular to plural, tracking the nine responses the source's "This"
summarises. No change of force. Not a finding.

**11. "We did not ask what groups would pay or how often they would attend."**
Source: "The survey did not ask what they would pay or how often they would
attend."
Differs: the source states this of the survey; the draft states it of the
association ("We"), which ran the survey, and generalises "they" to "groups".
Tested: the source's sentence is a statement about the instrument, whose questions
are the same for every respondent, so a case in which the survey omitted the
question for the nine but put it to the other five is not a reading the source
supports. Not a finding.

**12. "The survey cannot promise that a rehearsal room would pay its way."**
Source: "The survey is a reason to investigate a rehearsal room, not a promise
that it will pay its way."
Differs: "is not a promise" becomes "cannot promise", which is if anything the
same claim stated as a capacity; the referent of the source's "it" (a rehearsal
room) is made explicit, and the supplied context settles that referent. Not a
finding.

### Paragraph 4

**15. "Our submission also makes no claim to have secured the building owner's

### Paragraph 5

**16. "The wing is empty, while the hall's main rooms continue their usual

Source: "The wing is currently empty; the hall's main rooms continue to host their
usual activities."
Differs: "currently" is dropped, but the present tense carries the same time
scope. "continue their usual activities" makes the rooms the subject of the
activities rather than their host; nothing in the material could be a usual
activity of those rooms without being an activity they host, so no case separates
the two. Not a finding.

**17. "I want the trustees to commission a feasibility study covering sound

Differs: the study's four-item scope is verbatim. The source's "a comparison of
the two uses" becomes "a comparison with commercial letting", which names the same
pair, since the two uses under discussion are rehearsal use and commercial
letting. The association's proposal is voiced as Reed's want, which the source
supports directly ("Her proposal is ..."). Not a finding.

### Paragraph 6

**18. "I am asking for an eight-week deferral, without claiming the study can be
completed within that period."**
Source: "Her proposal is that the trustees defer the three-year letting decision
for eight weeks"; "There is no evidence that a study can be completed within eight

Differs: nothing. The draft states the timetable as a request and explicitly
declines the feasibility claim the material warns against, preserving the state of
knowledge in the direction the source requires. Not a finding.

**19. "Trustees should defer the three-year letting decision for eight weeks,
commission the study and publish the comparison before choosing."**
Source: "Her proposal is that the trustees defer the three-year letting decision
for eight weeks, obtain the feasibility study and publish a comparison of the two

Differs: "obtain" becomes "commission", which the source itself uses elsewhere for
the same act ("wants the trustees to commission the study"). Ends with the
decision sought, as the brief requires. Not a finding.

## Pronouns: person, number and natural gender, accounted separately

- The draft speaks in the first person singular ("I") and plural ("we", "our"); the
  material speaks of Reed in the third person singular ("She", "Her") and of the
  association as "it"/"Its". The shift of person is licensed by the brief's "in the
  voice of Amina Reed", and referent identification is settled by the byline.
- Natural gender: the material asserts female natural gender for Reed through
  "she"/"her". The draft's first-person pronouns convey person and number but no
  natural gender, so the draft neither adds nor contradicts a gender assertion; it
  simply does not restate one. Not a finding.
- The association: the material's "it"/"Its" is the ordinary non-personal pronoun
  for an organisation and asserts no natural gender. The draft's "we"/"our" shifts
  to first person plural while keeping the same referent, and likewise asserts no
  gender.
- The treasurer: neither the material ("Its treasurer's signed note") nor the draft
  ("Our treasurer's signed note") assigns the treasurer a gender or a number beyond
  singular. No gender information is invented.
- The groups: the material's "they" (the surveyed groups) is third person plural
  with no natural gender; the draft's "they"/"groups" matches. No change.

## Findings

### Finding 1 — source support: the silence is attributed to the wrong document

**Draft:** "Our submission also makes no claim to have secured the building owner's
consent for rehearsal use; **it does not establish whether that consent exists**."

**Source:** "The association's submission makes no representation that it has
secured the building owner's consent for rehearsal use." and, separately, "The
supplied material does not state the consent's actual status."

**What is missing or changed.** The material makes two distinct statements about
two distinct documents. Of *the submission*, it says only that the submission makes
no representation of having **secured** consent. Of *the supplied material as a
whole*, it says that the consent's actual status is not stated. The draft's second
clause takes the second statement and attaches it to the first document: read in
context, "it" refers to the submission, so the draft asserts that the submission
settles nothing either way about whether consent exists. Nothing in the material
supports that property of the submission. The material does not reproduce the
submission's text; it reports one thing the submission does not do, not that the
submission is silent on the subject.

**Concrete case, compatible with the supplied material, where the source holds and
the draft fails.** Suppose the association's submission contains the line: "We have
not approached the building owner, and we hold no consent for rehearsal use."
Then the source's statement holds — the submission makes no representation that it
*has secured* consent — while the draft's clause fails, because the submission does
establish whether that consent exists: it establishes that it does not. This case
is compatible with the supplied material, which nowhere quotes the submission and
asserts only that *the supplied material* does not state the consent's status; a
package that summarises rather than reproduces a submission can be silent on the
status while the submission itself is not.

**The other direction.** A case in which the draft's clause holds and the source's
statements fail is not available, and none is needed: the difference is
one-directional. The draft's clause is strictly stronger about the submission than
anything the material licenses, which is precisely why it is a finding. The first
clause of the draft's sentence is fully supported and is not in question.

**Smallest supported repair.** Delete the second clause and end the sentence at the
supported first clause:

> Our submission also makes no claim to have secured the building owner's consent
> for rehearsal use.

This loses nothing the material supports and keeps the state of knowledge intact:
the sentence still does not imply that consent exists, which is the caution the
clause was reaching for. If the author wants the uncertainty stated outright rather
than left standing, the supported form is a refusal to claim in her own voice
rather than an assertion about the document — for example, "... for rehearsal use,
and I make no claim about its status." That is a speech act she performs in the
sentence itself, so it needs no support beyond her performing it; what the material
will not support is a statement about what the submission does or does not settle.

## Translation findings

None. The source package and the draft are both in English ("Source language:
English"; draft language en_GB), so no term is carried into another language, no
meaning crosses a language boundary outside quoted speech, and the one quoted
position in the material ("The survey is a reason to investigate a rehearsal room,
not a promise that it will pay its way") is paraphrased within the same language
rather than translated. Its distinctive contrast — a reason, not a promise — is
preserved across draft items 8 and 12.
