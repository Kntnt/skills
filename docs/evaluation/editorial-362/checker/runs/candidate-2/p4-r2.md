## Findings

### Finding 1 — "her email" asserts Ortiz's gender, which the material does not supply (source support)

- **Draft:** "Ortiz explained the distinction in her email: …"
- **Source:** "Collection coordinator Lena Ortiz sent the following complete quotations in an email headed 'Catalog pilot: notes for publication'". The material's only other reference is the possessive "Ortiz's manner or emotions". No pronoun for Ortiz appears anywhere in the supplied material, and no sentence states her gender.
- **What differs:** the natural-gender information the pronoun conveys. The draft asserts that Ortiz is female; the material supports only the person, the role, the name and the singular number.
- **Test, draft → source:** a case compatible with the material — Lena Ortiz is the archive's collection coordinator and uses he/him or they/them pronouns. Every supplied sentence still holds: the name, the title, the three quotations, the email heading, the archive's supply of that email. The draft's "her email" fails.
- **Test, source → draft:** the reverse direction gives no case where the draft holds and the source fails, because the draft here only adds information; the source statement is the weaker one. The one-directional failure is enough: the draft asserts what the material leaves unclaimed. Preserving the state of knowledge means Ortiz's gender stays unclaimed, not that it is settled by a first name.
- **Smallest supported repair:** drop the pronoun — "in the same email" (or "in the email"). This keeps the sentence, the section, the voice and the link to the one supplied email intact.

### Finding 2 — "explained the distinction" states Ortiz's claim as established fact (source support)

- **Draft:** "Ortiz explained the distinction in her email: 'We kept the uncertain dates visible. An empty field and an estimated year tell the next volunteer different things.'"
- **Source:** "Collection coordinator Lena Ortiz sent the following complete quotations in an email". And: "There is no question-and-answer transcript, observation of an archive visit, or account of Ortiz's manner or emotions."
- **What differs:** whose assertion it is, and its certainty. "Explained the distinction" presupposes that the distinction holds and casts Ortiz as clarifying a fact; the material supports only that she stated it in an email supplied for publication. The verb also attributes a communicative manner that the material expressly says is not on record.
- **Test, draft → source:** a case compatible with the material — at Briar County an empty field and an estimated year in practice tell the next volunteer the same thing, because volunteers skip the free-text note, and Ortiz's sentence is her own view of good practice rather than an established distinction. The material still holds in full: she sent that quotation, and eleven records retained uncertain dates. "Ortiz explained the distinction" fails.
- **Test, source → draft:** a case where the source holds and the draft fails is the same one; the source statement ("sent … in an email") survives it, which is what makes the draft's added factivity the difference rather than a shared commitment.
- **Smallest supported repair:** "Ortiz wrote in the same email:" — the neutral reporting verb the material supports, keeping the quotation, its placement and the section's argument unchanged.

Findings 1 and 2 fall in one sentence, so a single repair settles both: **"Ortiz wrote in the same email: …"**

## Considered, not findings

- **Title, "volunteers share records for new donations."** The material supplies a shared catalog for incoming donations that the team chose to test and into which the pilot's 47 donations were cataloged, so "share records" describes the arrangement rather than asserting that each volunteer consulted another's record; the note's weaker "could consult the same records" is not contradicted. The present tense is the ordinary headline tense for the reported event, and an ordinary reader takes the headline with the lead, which dates and bounds the pilot. Defensible as written.
- **"wrote in an email"** for quotations the material says Ortiz "sent … in an email". The material presents them as her own complete quotations supplied for publication; "wrote" is the ordinary rendering and adds no fact the material withholds.
- **Lead stated as fact rather than attributed to the note.** The count is the archive's own record of its own pilot, and the next sentence names the note as the source for the pilot's findings, so the strength is proportionate.
- **"with further testing"** in the summary, for "test the descriptions on more posters". The compression is vaguer than the quotation, not stronger, and the quotation appears in full later in the draft.
- **Absence claims** ("no before-and-after time measurement, cost calculation, or assessment of public access") are carried over as absences *in the note*, matching the material, and are not turned into claims about the pilot's effects.
- **No causal claim** is made anywhere in the draft: the pilot and the 47 donations are reported in sequence without a causal verb or causal hedge.
