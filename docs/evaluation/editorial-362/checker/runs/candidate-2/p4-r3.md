### Pronoun accounting (person, number, natural gender)

The draft contains exactly one pronoun referring to a person: **"her"** in "Ortiz explained the distinction in her email" (passage 14).

- Referent: Lena Ortiz. Settled by the supplied context — she is the only individual named, and the email is hers.
- Person and number (third person singular): supported; the material describes one individual sending one email.
- Natural gender (female): **not supported.** The material names "Collection coordinator Lena Ortiz" and never uses a pronoun for her, never states a gender, and supplies no interview, observation or account of the person. Identifying the referent does not establish this feature. English has no grammatical gender here, so nothing in the draft's possessive is a purely grammatical artifact: "her" is a natural-gender assertion about a real person.
- Every other reference to the person is genderless: "the collection coordinator", "collection coordinator Lena Ortiz", "Ortiz". The gendered form appears once and is the only place the assertion is made.

## Findings

### F1 — Natural gender asserted about Ortiz (source-support finding)

- Draft: "Ortiz explained the distinction in **her** email: …"
- Material: "Collection coordinator Lena Ortiz sent the following complete quotations in an email headed 'Catalog pilot: notes for publication'"; "There is no question-and-answer transcript, observation of an archive visit, or account of Ortiz's manner or emotions."
- What differs: the thing asserted. The material supports the referent (Ortiz), the possession (the email is hers) and the number (one person), but supplies no information about the person's gender. The draft adds that she is female.
- Concrete case, compatible with the supplied material: Lena Ortiz is the archive's collection coordinator and uses he/him or they/them pronouns. Every supplied sentence — the role, the email, the three quotations, the pilot note — remains true word for word, and the draft's "her" is false. In the other direction, "her email" would hold while the material's statements about an unpronouned coordinator would remain equally true, so the two are not interchangeable: the draft says strictly more than the material supports.
- Support that is missing: any pronoun, honorific, gender statement or observation of the person in the material. The material explicitly forecloses the one channel through which such detail would normally arrive ("no … observation of an archive visit, or account of Ortiz's manner or emotions").
- Smallest supported repair: replace the possessive with a determiner that does not encode gender — "Ortiz wrote in the same email:" or "in the supplied email". This leaves the sentence, the quotation and the paragraph otherwise untouched. (Naming the coordinator's pronouns is not an option; the material does not supply them.)

### F2 — Purposive/manner framing of the quotation (source-support finding)

- Draft: "Ortiz **explained the distinction** in her email: 'We kept the uncertain dates visible. An empty field and an estimated year tell the next volunteer different things.'"
- Material: "Collection coordinator Lena Ortiz sent the following complete quotations in an email headed 'Catalog pilot: notes for publication'"; "These are the only correspondence and reporting materials supplied. There is no question-and-answer transcript, observation of an archive visit, or account of Ortiz's manner or emotions."
- What differs: the act attributed to the speaker, and whose knowledge it rests on. The material supports that Ortiz *sent*, in writing, three complete lines for publication. "Explained the distinction" adds a purposive act — clarifying a distinction that was at issue — and the definite article presents that distinction as already on the table, which the material's account of a one-way set of publication notes does not establish. This is circumstantial detail of manner, the one class of detail the material states was not captured.
- Concrete case, compatible with the supplied material: Ortiz composed the three lines as standalone notes for the supplier's case study, addressed to no question and elaborating nothing; nobody had raised a distinction with her, and she was stating a practice rather than explaining it. The material holds in full — she sent complete quotations in an email headed "notes for publication" — while "explained the distinction" fails. Conversely, an email in which she explains a distinction to an interviewer would hold, while the material's statement that no question-and-answer transcript exists still leaves that unsupported.
- Support that is missing: any account of the exchange, its prompt, or Ortiz's manner.
- Smallest supported repair: use the neutral reporting verb the material supports and keep the quotation exactly where it is — "Ortiz wrote in the same email:" (which also resolves F1 in the same clause). If a link to the preceding sentence is wanted, "On the uncertain dates, Ortiz wrote:" stays within the supplied material, since the email's subject is the catalog pilot.

## Near misses tested and judged defensible (not findings)

Read as an ordinary reader reads a headline over a pilot report, the title states that for new donations the records are held in common — which rests on "chose to test a shared catalog for incoming donations" and the 47 donations cataloged in it, not on any claim that a given volunteer in fact opened another's record, and not on continuation after May 6. No concrete case separates it from the material without straining the headline past its ordinary reading. Reported here for the record only.

Attributing it to the note narrows rather than widens the claim and does not change the count, the date or the fact. Not a defect.
- **N3 — "The archive chose the descriptions"** for "The archive decided which descriptions to use". Same subject, same decision, no added scope.
- **N4 — "wrote"** for "sent the following complete quotations in an email". The quotations are in the first person and were supplied by Ortiz for publication; authorship is carried by the material.
- **N5 — Absence handled correctly.** The draft reproduces the missing measurements as missing ("contains no before-and-after time measurement, cost calculation, or assessment of public access") and keeps the older-records decision open. No absence is converted into a negative finding, no comparison with another supplier appears, and no causal verb links the pilot to any outcome.
