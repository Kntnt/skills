## 2. Findings

Two. Both are source-support findings. Neither is a translation finding.

### Finding 1 — A dated decision is extended to cover the undated selection of the supplier (chronology)

**Draft:** "In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log and chose Svale Systems after testing whether it could show the status of each repair."

**Source:** "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings." and, as a separate sentence carrying no date, "It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair."

**What differs — time and scope.** The material dates one event: the decision to trial a shared repair log. The selection of Svale, and the testing that preceded the selection, are left undated. The draft coordinates "chose Svale Systems" onto the same subject under the fronted adverbial "In September 2025", so the adverbial distributes across both verbs and the reader takes the choice of supplier, and the evaluation behind it, to have happened in that month as well. The thing named and the actor are unchanged; the time is not.

**Test in both directions.** A concrete case compatible with the material: the team decides in September 2025 to trial a shared repair log, spends October testing whether the candidate log can show the status of each repair, and selects Svale Systems in early November, before the eight-week trial that the 4 December 2025 note reports on. Nothing in the material excludes this — the trial's start date is never given, only the note's date and the trial's eight-week length. In that case the source sentence holds and the draft's sentence fails. In the other direction, if everything did happen in September, both hold. So the draft asserts something the material does not, and a reader who dated the procurement from this article could be wrong about it.

**Smallest supported repair.** Break the coordination so the date governs only the decision:

> In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log. It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair.

This keeps the sentence order, the actor, the customer as acting party and the undated test-then-choose sequence, and removes only the inherited date. Writing "the log" in place of "it" additionally restores the material's own object for the test, at no cost.

### Finding 2 — Lind's assessment is narrowed to the trial

**Draft:** "That assessment was specific to the trial; Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company."

**Source:** "Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company. Her final quotation is her actual, qualified assessment. It is not an inference from the figures." The assessment itself: "I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation."

**What differs — scope, and whose assertion it is.** The material qualifies Lind's position in exactly one way: she stopped short of recommending Svale to every housing company. The draft's first clause qualifies it in a different and stronger way, by declaring that her assessment reached no further than the trial. That is a claim about the reach of her own assertion, presented to the reader as given, and the material contains no passage saying it. Her words point the other way: "Having one view of the reports helps us" is present tense and about the team's working life, not about the trial period alone, and "before the next building starts" anticipates use beyond the two trial buildings.

**Test in both directions.** A concrete case compatible with the material: Lind holds that the shared log helps Elm Quay's maintenance team generally and expects it in the next building, while still declining to recommend Svale to every housing company because other companies' categories and repair mixes differ. The material is fully compatible with this — indeed her quotations suggest it — and in that case the source passage holds while "That assessment was specific to the trial" fails. Conversely, if her endorsement really did stop at the trial's edge, both would hold. The draft therefore adds a restriction the material does not support.

Note that this cuts against the drafter's evident intent: the clause reads as a protective hedge, and the contract's rule is symmetric — a qualification that the material does not carry is as much a defect as a missing one, because it misstates whose knowledge and assertion is on the page.

**Smallest supported repair.** Delete the unsupported clause and keep the material's own qualification:

> Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company. The team has not yet expanded the trial.

The reservation survives, the sentence stays in the same place, and nothing else in the paragraph changes.
