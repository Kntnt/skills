## Findings

Both findings are source-support findings — meaning changed in crossing languages, outside quoted speech. Neither is a translation-of-quotation finding, and neither introduces a false motive, a figure, or an unsupported causal or evaluative claim. Both are precision defects with one-phrase repairs.

### F1 — "digital proficiency" asserts a non-measurement the material does not support

- Draft: "The report does not measure age, functional ability, or digital proficiency."
- Source: "Den mäter inte ålder, funktionsförmåga eller digital vana."
- What differs: the thing measured. "vana" is habituation — how used to, how practised in, digital booking a person is. "proficiency" is capability — how skilled they are. The draft's sentence is a negative claim about the report's contents, so substituting the attribute changes which absence is asserted.
- Case in which the source holds and the draft fails: the pilot report includes a single self-rated skill question ("how confident are you using online services?") but records nothing about how often or how long respondents have booked digitally. Nothing supplied excludes this — the material lists only what the report does not measure and does not enumerate what it does. In that case "Den mäter inte ... digital vana" holds and "The report does not measure ... digital proficiency" is false.
- Case in the other direction: a report that logs how many times each account has booked online before, but asks nothing about skill, satisfies the draft's negative claim while failing the material's. The two negatives are therefore not the same claim.
- Why it matters here: the sentence is load-bearing for the next paragraph's limit on what the phone bookings can show, and the distinction between being unpractised and being unable is the article's own subject.
- Smallest repair: replace "digital proficiency" with "digital experience" — or, if the plainer phrasing is preferred, "how used to digital booking people are". Leave the rest of the sentence, including its order and attribution, unchanged.

### F2 — the trial's recording duty is assigned to staff rather than to the administration

- Draft: "Staff would record the time spent on each booking method and invite users to explain, voluntarily, why they chose phone or web."
- Source: "Under försöket ska förvaltningen registrera tidsåtgång per bokningsväg och be användarna frivilligt ange varför de väljer telefon eller webb."
- What differs: the subject. The material places both duties on "förvaltningen", the administration as a body; the draft places them on its staff. The material keeps the two words apart — "personalen" in the staff document's rationale, "förvaltningen" here and in "Förvaltningens verkliga invändning" — and the draft collapses them.
- Case in which the source holds and the draft fails: the administration meets the requirement by switching on handling-time logging in the booking system and adding a standard closing prompt to the phone script and the web confirmation page, both configured centrally, with no staff member recording or asking anything. Nothing supplied specifies the mechanism or excludes this.
- Case in the other direction: individual staff keeping their own informal time notes, with nothing registered at administration level, would satisfy the draft's sentence while leaving "förvaltningen ska registrera tidsåtgång per bokningsväg" unmet.
- Why it matters here: the article's dispute is precisely about staff workload, and the sentence as written reads as conceding that the proposal loads a further task onto staff — an implication the material does not support in a paragraph arguing the proposal's case.
- Smallest repair: "The administration would record the time spent on each booking method and invite users to explain, voluntarily, why they chose phone or web." Nothing else in the paragraph changes.
