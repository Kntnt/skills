## 2. Findings

### Finding 1 — source support — the entitlement, its holder and what it covers are all changed

**Draft (paragraph 5):** "Men vi borde kunna försöka formulera varför vi behöver undersöka något tillsammans."

**Source:** "Men den som fyller i en mall borde få formulera varför vi behöver just varandras tid, också när syftet är att undersöka något tillsammans."

**What differs.** Three things at once, and the third is the substantive one.

1. *Whose.* The source's holder is "den som fyller i en mall" — one identified person, the one doing the filling. The draft's is "vi", everyone at the meeting.
2. *Modality.* "Borde få formulera" is a permission the author asks be granted by the template. "Borde kunna försöka formulera" is an ability to attempt, which the template need not grant at all.
3. *The thing to be formulated.* The source's object is "varför vi behöver just varandras tid". Its "också när syftet är att undersöka något tillsammans" is a scope extension — the entitlement holds *even in* the exploratory case, which is the case the author has just conceded is not decision-shaped. The draft makes the exploration itself the object: what is to be formulated is "varför vi behöver undersöka något tillsammans". The general claim is dropped and the qualification that extended it is what remains, now doing different work.

**Case in which the source holds and the draft fails.** A meeting is called so that one department can hand over a routine status report to another; nothing is to be investigated jointly, and the template is filled in for it. The source's claim applies unchanged: the person filling in the template should be allowed to state why we need each other's time — that is precisely a case where the question bites. The draft's sentence says nothing about this meeting at all: there is no joint investigation, so there is no "varför vi behöver undersöka något tillsammans" to formulate, and the draft leaves the routine meeting outside what it asks for. Nothing in the supplied material excludes a meeting of this kind; the notes describe a template with boxes for participants and agenda and say nothing restricting the meetings it is used for.

**Case in which the draft holds and the source fails.** The template carries a free-text field that any attendee may write in, and the group writes "vi behöver undersöka X tillsammans" there, while the person who actually fills in the template is given no room anywhere to say why *these* people's time is needed. The draft's claim is satisfied — the group could try to formulate why they need to investigate something together. The source's is not: the filler was not allowed to formulate why we need each other's time. Nothing supplied excludes this either.

Both directions therefore produce a standing case, so the two statements are not the same claim. The draft's wording is cautious and natural, which does not settle it.

**Smallest supported repair.** Restore the holder, the permission and the object, keeping the qualification as a qualification:

> Men den som fyller i mallen borde få formulera varför vi behöver just varandras tid, också när syftet är att undersöka något tillsammans.

The preceding sentences' "vi" can stay as it is; only this sentence names who is being asked to write something, and the material names that person. If the draft's collective voice is to be kept in this sentence, the object and the scope marker are still the part that must come back: "Men vi borde kunna formulera varför vi behöver just varandras tid, också när syftet är att undersöka något tillsammans." The first version is the closer match, because "borde få" is an entitlement the template withholds and "borde kunna" is not.

### Finding 2 — source support — a *kallelse* the material does not supply

**Draft (paragraph 5):** "Då kan vi knappast begära att den färdiga frågan ska finnas i kallelsen."

**Source:** no passage. The material describes one document, "bibliotekets mötesmall", and its contents: "rutor för starttid, sluttid, deltagare och dagordning". The word *kallelse* appears nowhere in the brief, the notes or the boundaries, and no passage says the template is sent out, circulated, or used to call a meeting.

**What is claimed.** An ordinary reader takes the sentence to present as given that these meetings are called by a *kallelse*, and that this *kallelse* is where a finished question would have to be stated. That is a background fact about how the library's meetings are convened, and background is a claim like any other. The author's argument here — that an unformed question cannot be demanded in advance — is sound and is not what is being tested; the document it locates that demand in is.

**Case in which the draft's presupposition fails.** The mötesmall is a planning and minutes sheet that the convener fills in and keeps, with starttid and sluttid written on it after the fact and deltagare ticked off, while the meeting itself is set up by a bare calendar entry carrying no text. The template is then never a *kallelse*, and there is nothing that the finished question could be demanded to appear in. This is compatible with everything supplied: a box for "dagordning" is consistent with an internal sheet and does not entail that anything is sent to anyone, and no supplied statement excludes it.

**Case in which the material's document is untouched by the draft's claim.** The workplace does issue kallelser, but they are generated from the calendar system and are not the mötesmall the column is about. The draft's sentence is then true of a document the column never examined, while the author's actual point — that the template should not demand a finished question — goes unstated.

**Smallest supported repair.** Name the document the material supplies:

> Då kan vi knappast begära att den färdiga frågan ska finnas i mallen.

"I rutan" also works and ties the sentence to the box the column is arguing about; either keeps the sentence's rhythm, its position in the argument and its voice, and neither adds anything to the material.
