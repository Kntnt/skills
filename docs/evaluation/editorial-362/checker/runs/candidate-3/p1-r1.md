## 2. Findings

One finding. It is a source-support finding (meaning changed in crossing languages, outside quoted speech), not a translation finding.

### Finding 1 — "digital proficiency" asserts a non-measurement the source does not state, and drops the one it does

- **Draft passage:** "The report does not measure age, functional ability, or digital proficiency."
- **Source passage:** "Den mäter inte ålder, funktionsförmåga eller digital vana."
- **What differs:** the thing measured. *Digital vana* is habituation — how accustomed to and practised in digital services a person is. *Digital proficiency* is skill or capability. The Swedish list therefore withholds a claim about habit; the draft's list withholds a claim about ability and says nothing about habit.
- **Case in which the source holds and the draft fails** (compatible with the supplied material; nothing supplied excludes it): the pilot report includes a short check of whether participants could complete the online booking form unaided — a proficiency measure — but records nothing about how often or how routinely they use digital services. Then "Den mäter inte … digital vana" is true while "The report does not measure … digital proficiency" is false. No supplied statement excludes this case: the only nearby statement, "Telefonbokningarna kan därför inte användas för att säga hur stor andel av invånarna som inte kan boka digitalt", limits what the *phone-booking count* can show about the share of *residents*; it does not say the report contains no ability measure at all.
- **Case in the other direction:** the report logs how frequently each booker had used the municipality's web services before — a *vana* measure with no skill assessment. This one is excluded by the supplied "Den mäter inte … digital vana", as required, and is recorded here only to show both directions were tested.
- **Why it matters to the argument:** the draft's own next sentence already carries the ability point ("cannot tell us what share … cannot book online"). Reading the third list item as *proficiency* makes it a restatement of the ability point and quietly discards the distinct dimension the source actually names — habit, which is precisely what the proposed trial sets out to investigate by asking users why they still choose the phone.
- **Smallest supported repair:** replace "digital proficiency" with "digital habits" — i.e. "The report does not measure age, functional ability, or digital habits." ("familiarity with digital services" is an equally supported alternative if the shorter phrase reads oddly.) Nothing else in the sentence or the paragraph needs to change.

Case attempted: Lervik has a hall bookable by residents privately but not by associations, unaffected by the proposal — then "a permanent switch to online-only booking for community venues" would reach further than "byte till enbart digital bokning av föreningslokaler". **3.4 "labor cost" for "arbetskostnaden", "administrative work" for "dubbel administration"/"arbetskostnaden".** Both sides of Ek's balance are carried; where the draft says "administrative work" it is describing what the trial would measure (tidsåtgång per bokningsväg), not restating the cost standard.
