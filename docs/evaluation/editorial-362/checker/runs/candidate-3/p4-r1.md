## 2. Findings

### Finding 1 — Unsupported natural-gender assertion about a named person

**Draft passage.** "Ortiz explained the distinction in her email: ..." (section "Keeping uncertainty visible").

**Source passages.**
- "Collection coordinator Lena Ortiz sent the following complete quotations in an email headed 'Catalog pilot: notes for publication'"
- "The archive supplied that email and the pilot note for this supplier-published case. These are the only correspondence and reporting materials supplied. There is no question-and-answer transcript, observation of an archive visit, or account of Ortiz's manner or emotions."

**What differs.** Whose attribute is asserted, and on whose knowledge. The material supplies Ortiz's name, role, employer and words. It supplies no statement of her gender. The draft's possessive "her" asserts that Ortiz is a woman, with the same flat certainty as the supported facts around it. Identifying the referent — the draft does correctly identify whose email it is — does not establish the person, number and natural-gender features the pronoun additionally conveys. The only sentence in the material that touches Ortiz's personal attributes rules attributes out rather than in. A given name is not a supplied statement of gender, and the material nowhere glosses "Lena".

**Test, both directions.** The draft's claim entails the source's ("an email sent by Ortiz") and adds one further claim, so the asymmetry runs one way; the concrete case is on that side. Case: Lena Ortiz is a man, or is nonbinary and uses they/them. Every supplied statement remains exactly true — the role line, the email and its heading, the three complete quotations, the pilot note, the absence of any transcript or observation — while the draft's "her email" is false, and false in print, in a case the archive itself supplied for publication. The case need only be compatible with the material, and it is: no supplied statement excludes it. Nothing in the material can be quoted to set it aside.

**Smallest supported repair.** Replace the possessive with the definite article, the email having already been introduced in the preceding paragraph:

> Ortiz explained the distinction in the email: "We kept the uncertain dates visible. An empty field and an estimated year tell the next volunteer different things."

This preserves the sentence, the voice, the reporting frame and the editorial choice, and removes only the unsupported attribute. (Repeating "Ortiz" in place of a pronoun would serve equally; "the email" is the smaller change.) The draft's other two attributions, "Lena Ortiz wrote" and "Ortiz wrote", already avoid the problem and need no change.
