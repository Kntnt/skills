## 2. Findings

### Finding 1 — the supplier choice is dated to September 2025 (source support)

- **Draft:** "In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log and chose Svale Systems after testing whether it could show the status of each repair."
- **Material:** "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings." and, as a separate undated sentence, "It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair."
- **What differs:** time. A fronted date adverbial governs both verbs of a coordinated predicate, so the draft places the supplier choice — and the testing that the material says preceded it — inside September 2025. The material dates only the decision to trial. It supplies no date for the testing or the choice.
- **Case, draft fails and material holds:** the team decides in late September 2025 to trial a shared log, tests candidate logs through October, and chooses Svale Systems in early November 2025; configuration, training and the eight-week trial follow, and the internal note is written on 4 December 2025. Every supplied statement is true in this case, including the order "chose … after testing"; the draft's September dating of the choice is false.
- **Case, draft holds and material fails:** none is needed — the direction above is sufficient, and the reverse direction is vacuous because the draft's dating entails the material's.
- **Exclusion check:** no supplied statement sets this case aside. The material gives no trial start date, no testing date and no choice date, and the trial length ("eight weeks") plus the note date (4 December 2025) leave room for the testing and choice to fall after September.
- **Smallest supported repair:** split the coordination so the date governs only the decision — "In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log. It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair."

### Finding 2 — the title claims a company-wide, present shared view (source support)

- **Draft:** "Elm Quay gains a shared view of repair reports" (title).
- **Material:** "its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings"; "The trial lasted eight weeks."; "The trial has not yet expanded; the team will decide after checking how the categories work for larger repairs."; "Having one view of the reports helps us".
- **What differs:** scope and time. The title's subject is the company and its object is "repair reports" without restriction; the supported gain covers the reports logged in two of the buildings that house 640 flats. The present tense asserts a state holding now; the material's last word on the arrangement is an eight-week trial that has not expanded.
- **Case, draft fails and material holds:** the eight-week trial covers two buildings and ends in November 2025; the shared log stays idle while the team checks how the categories work for larger repairs, and reports from the remaining buildings continue to arrive by telephone and email and to be stored separately. All supplied statements are true in this case; "Elm Quay gains a shared view of repair reports" is false both of the company and of the present.
- **Case, draft holds and material fails:** the draft's title entails the material's two-building claim only when read with the lead, not on its own, so the asymmetry runs one way.
- **Exclusion check:** nothing supplied says the log remained in use after the trial or reached other buildings; "The trial has not yet expanded" points the other way.
- **Smallest supported repair:** carry the scope into the title — "Elm Quay gains a shared view of repair reports in two buildings". This leaves the headline's verb, voice and framing untouched.

## 3. Examined and not reported as findings

Recorded so the accounting is complete and these are not re-opened as defects.

- **B4, the referent of "it".** The nearer noun phrase is "Svale Systems", but in this material the log is what Svale supplies and configures, so no claim differs under either reading. A reader is not obstructed on any fact. Naming the log would be a preference, not a repair.
- **B14 and B16, present to past tense.** Both report the same content as the material; neither asserts that the state has since changed, and B16's past-tense claim is entailed by the material's present-tense one. No case separates them.
- **B13, "compared with".** Juxtaposition without a causal verb or causal hedge; the workload caveat and the note's explicit non-attribution follow in the next sentence, and no sequence is converted into cause anywhere in the draft.
- **B19, "specific to the trial".** The narrowing is not stated in the material, but it can only shrink the assessment's reach; no case makes the draft claim more about Svale than the material supports, and the material's own limit ("did not recommend Svale to every housing company") is reproduced beside it.
- **S3, "gave the team a common view".** Rests on Lind's attested experience of one view, not on the medians, and asserts no effect on assignment time, cost, resident satisfaction or completion time.
- **Forbidden inferences.** None present: no software-caused improvement, no saving, no delighted residents, no rescued customer, no assignment-to-completion conversion (B15 guards it explicitly), and the customer's reservation survives verbatim at B18 and in the summary at S4.
- **Translation findings.** None. Source and draft are both English; all three quotations are verbatim, so no meaning or voice crosses a language boundary. The en_US renderings "apartments" (B1) and "December 4, 2025" (B11) are locale mechanics, outside this comparison and in any case name the same thing and the same date.
