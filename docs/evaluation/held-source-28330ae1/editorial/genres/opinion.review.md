# Opinion — review guidance

Identify the early position, the grounds supporting it and the action the ending leaves with somebody. If *it is time to act* names no act, look for the existing proposal to make explicit; supply no new policy or decision.

Check factual premises and predictions against the support visible in the text, loaded figures for attribution, and objections for fair treatment. Treat a clearly proposed target with its stated uncertainty as an authorial choice. A support finding identifies a premise or inferential dependency the argument actually asserts; preserve choices that make no such factual promise. An internal contradiction or broken inference is a finding; external reference checking is outside this review.

An opinion often argues over a decision still to be taken, asks for a step before it — try both, measure first, decide afterwards — and names the change that decision would make as the text has proposed it: *before the branch closes*, *until the switch*, *ahead of the merger*. Where the headline, the standfirst or the lead sets that change out as proposed or pending, such a reference takes for granted only that the change is on the table, which the text says, and asks for the step to come first. It is not the text stating the outcome as settled, and it is no forecast or concession that the change will follow the step, in the standfirst as in the body. Read it as written: it needs no rewording to say whether the change will come, since the passages around it already leave the decision open, and it is no finding. A finding needs a passage that asserts the outcome itself: one that says the change is already decided, or will come whatever the step shows, while another passage leaves it open, which is a contradiction; or one that predicts what the decision or the step will show with nothing in the text to carry it.

Preserve a successful polemic, its authorial responsibility and its qualifications. A pointed closing or rhetorical return is not a defect because a neutral report would omit it. Clarify a broken argument without replacing its position.
