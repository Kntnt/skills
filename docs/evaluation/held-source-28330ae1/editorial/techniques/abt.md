# ABT

*And, But, Therefore*: Randy Olson's structure connecting a situation and what it puts at stake, a genuine complication or question, and the consequence the material supports. It shapes the reasoning or story without prescribing three paragraphs or visible labels.

## What this technique asks for

Each part moves from the general to the specific. The And gives the situation, then what is at stake in it: what it makes possible or puts at risk, as the material states it. The But names the complication briefly, then its causes. The Therefore says what follows or is done, then how, where something is done. The general orients every reader; the specific gives the part its force.

The reader knows why the situation matters before the complication arrives. A text that opens on its problem leaves the reader asking *so what?* Stakes are facts from the material, never a claim that the subject is important. A calm text often shows them through its situation alone: who has to decide what, and what depends on it. Where the material holds no stakes, state the situation plainly and invent none.

The And holds what the complication needs in order to bite and nothing else, and the complication comes early. How much situation that takes depends on what the reader already knows: somebody inside the subject needs little, somebody outside it more.

An arc has one complication. Other problems are ordered under it as its causes, not beside it, since two problems of equal rank competing for one consequence leave the reader unsure which to follow. Independent arcs are not that: sections of web copy that each answer their own reader question, a teaser's arc, or a nested or revisited arc.

The consequence answers what the complication raised. Each part of it takes up a cause the text has given, so a list of actions is sound when every action answers one; an action against a problem the text never raised is an addition, not a consequence. Where the complication is an open question, or its causes are unknown, the But states it without causes and the consequence takes up the question, for example by naming the next investigation. An open or conditional result is a valid consequence.

The relations must hold even without the words *and*, *but* and *therefore*. A step that only adds, this and then that, where a turn or a consequence belongs, is a sequence rather than the relation. A question need not be a crisis, and a response need not be a triumph. For example: a school records room temperatures before deciding whether to change its heating; the readings locate cold periods but do not explain them; the next investigation links readings to room use.

Apply the structure at the level the genre's task needs. Where a genre's own rules compress, withhold or reorder part of the arc, as when a teaser withholds its resolution, web copy puts the useful words first or an opinion states its position early, the genre governs. A standfirst or lead may give the result early, open question included; the order above governs where the body develops the arc, not that early answer.

Nest or revisit an arc when that helps a complex explanation. In medias res is one possible opening into an event the material actually supplies: the scene itself carries the situation and what is at stake, in the same sentences that move it. It fails when the situation is laid out before the scene, and when it is left out altogether.
