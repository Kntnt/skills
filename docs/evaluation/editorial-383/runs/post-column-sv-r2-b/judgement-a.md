## 4. R1

**Fail.**

The input is a clean, coherent, signed column. It contains exactly one concrete visible defect — the English spaced em dash — and the Skill fixed it correctly. Everything else it did was rewriting a clean text to satisfy a structural guideline, by the reply's own admission ("en ingress är tillagd där anatomin kräver en"): a working headline replaced, three section headings imposed on a 7-paragraph personal column, and three sentences of new prose written into the author's voice above her byline.

The passage that decides it is the added ingress, and within it:

> "Du får en fråga att ta med till nästa dagordning."

set against the closing sentence the returned text retains:

> "Jag vet inte om den överlever mötet med en verklig dagordning, och jag tänker inte låtsas att jag gör det."

The column's whole argument is a hope held next to a doubt ("Hoppet om frågan och tvivlet på ännu en ruta får samsas ett tag till"). The new ingress sells the reader the hope as a deliverable, generalises a single-document observation into a claim about meeting forms, and refers to the first-person author as "skribenten". That is working voice, argument and claim-scope altered outside any finding, on a text whose only findable defect was a dash — which is what R1 forbids.
