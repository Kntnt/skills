## 4. R1

**Fail.**

The passage that decides it is the headline, together with the reply's own account of why it changed:

- Input: `# Keep the telephone until Lervik knows why it is used`
- Returned: `# Keep the telephone until Lervik has the figures`
- Reply, finding 3: "This followed from the repair of the headline's word-count norm, and settling it means choosing between the eight-word form and the original angle."

R1 forbids rewriting clean text to satisfy taste or numerical guidelines, and requires claims outside findings to be preserved. The input headline is not a visible defect: it is grammatical, idiomatic en_GB, and it states the exact condition the body argues for. It was shortened to meet a word-count norm, and that shortening moved the piece's central claim — from *knowing why the telephone is used* to *having the figures* — which inverts the argument, since the whole piece exists to say that counts are not the answer. The Skill names the damage itself and leaves it in place with its budget spent, so the returned text goes back to the author with its thesis altered and the alteration unresolved.

A second passage compounds it. The invented standfirst asserts "A municipal pilot counted 96 web bookings and 24 by telephone" over "Lervik's seven association venues", stripping the "eight-week trial in two venues" bound; by the Skill's own words this invites "the seven-venue picture, which is the reading the whole piece exists to refuse." A standfirst can be a legitimate repair, but this one was published in a state the Skill knew made the text assert more than it arrived asserting.

What passes, and why it does not rescue the verdict: the body is untouched paragraph for paragraph; all eleven limiting sentences survive verbatim, including the refusal form "We make no claim to have funded or costed that trial"; there is no hardening of any kind; no argument, voice or quotation is damaged; and the account is honest and specific about all three unresolved findings. Preservation is strong and disclosure is strong. R1 still fails, because two of the four changes the Skill chose to make to a clean text altered what it claims, one of them at the headline.
