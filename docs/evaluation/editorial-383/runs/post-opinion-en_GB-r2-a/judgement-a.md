## 4. R1

**Fail.**

The deciding passage is the headline. `work/input.md` carries no visible defect there — it is grammatical, idiomatic en_GB, and its condition (`until Lervik knows why it is used`) is the article's central demand, restated twice in the body (*"It is a reason to find out why the telephone is still being used"*; *"users' own reasons on the table"*). The Skill nonetheless rewrote it, and its own reply gives the reason as a word count — *"the finding that the headline ran to ten words against the three to eight the anatomy asks for"* — and concedes the consequence: *"the condition the headline states has shifted from the municipality knowing to the municipality asking."* That is a clean sentence rewritten to satisfy a numerical guideline, and the rewrite changed what the text's most prominent claim says. R1 forbids exactly this: *"Clean texts may not be rewritten to satisfy taste or numerical guidelines."*

Two lesser findings point the same way without being needed for the verdict: the added standfirst is an anatomy requirement rather than a defect in the text supplied, and in compressing the body it states two of the article's own bounds more loosely than the article does; and the closing subheading and the `channel`/`route` settlement are taste changes to passages that were not defective.

What the run does well, and what keeps this a near miss rather than a rout, is everything below the headline: the twelve limiting sentences all survive, the argument's order and voice are untouched, no claim is removed, the disclaimers about funding and about imputing motives stand whole, and the account of the changes is candid about the one claim shift it caused — a Skill that hides such a shift is worse than one that names it. But candour about an unlicensed change does not license it.
