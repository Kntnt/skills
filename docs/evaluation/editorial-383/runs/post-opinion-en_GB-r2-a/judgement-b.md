## 4. R1

**Fail.**

The Skill did find and fix real, concrete defects — the *administration* sense-collision (difference 4) and the pronoun left without an antecedent (difference 7) — and its preservation of the argument is otherwise exemplary: every limiting sentence intact to the character, every body paragraph but one untouched, no mechanical churn, and an account honest enough to volunteer the one claim it moved. That is most of what R1 asks for.

It fails on the clause that R1 states outright: *clean texts may not be rewritten to satisfy taste or numerical guidelines.* The deciding passage is the headline.

> `# Keep the telephone until Lervik knows why it is used` → `# Keep the telephone until Lervik asks users why`

The input headline had no visible defect: grammatical, clear, en_GB, and the exact demand the body argues for — *"It is a reason to find out why the telephone is still being used."* It was rewritten for one reason, which the reply states plainly: it *"ran to ten words against the three to eight the anatomy asks for."* That is a numerical guideline, and meeting it cost the article the meaning of its own title claim, trading the condition *knows* for the weaker *asks*. A text whose whole argument is that a decision should wait until something is **known** is not served by a headline that now asks only that somebody **ask**.

Two further rewrites of clean material compound it rather than decide it: a standfirst of roughly 60 words composed where the input had none, and a subheading changed from a label to a verdict — both to satisfy the same structural guideline, neither repairing a visible defect, and the second dragging a further edit (difference 7) behind it. Four of the nine differences are guideline-driven rewrites of clean passages, against two genuine repairs.

**Verdict: fail on R1**, on difference 1.
