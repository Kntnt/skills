## 4. R1

**Fail.**

The passage that decides it is the inserted lead:

> "The papers before the council executive board on 18 June include a pilot report from two of the seven community venues and the officers' report on the proposal to end phone booking in September. Neither says what either booking route costs staff, and that is what the board should know first."

R1 requires the Skill to address concrete visible defects while preserving arguments and claims outside findings, and forbids rewriting a clean text to satisfy taste. Nothing in `work/input.md` is a visible defect that this lead repairs; the Skill manufactured two assertions the input does not make — that the papers before the board are these two documents, and that the pilot's two venues are two of the seven community venues — and asserted them as fact about material it never saw. That is the addition of unverifiable source description, which the criterion's "No unavailable-source verification" exists to bar, and it is the opposite of preserving the claims outside findings.

The rest of the run compounds the verdict rather than deciding it: the headline, two of four subheadings, the byline's position and two paragraph breaks were changed on a text whose only defect the Skill itself could name — the third subheading repeating its first sentence — it left unfixed. The reply's own framing shows the driver was template conformance, not defect repair: "Two parts the anatomy requires were put in place". And the claims section, "Nothing. No passage carrying a claim was removed, and no claim was left standing with its scope … moved", reports a clean bill by accounting only for removals and movements, while the headline lost the scope marker "Lervik's" and two new claims were added.

Everything that carries the argument survives word for word, and every limiting sentence survives word for word. That is what keeps this a near miss rather than a rout — but a clean text came back with claims it did not have, and R1 fails on that.
