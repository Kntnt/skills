# Judgement b — run `pre-column-sv-r2`

Files read: `work/input.md` and `response.md` in this run directory. The returned text is the
fenced `markdown` block in `response.md`; I extracted it to `returned-b.md` here and compared it
byte for byte against the input.

## 1. Differences

Eight differences. Body prose is otherwise identical paragraph for paragraph; the frontmatter is
identical byte for byte.

1. **Headline.** Before: `# Rutan som inte finns`. After: `# Mötesmallen saknar en ruta för
   syftet`. Class: change of taste. The input headline is an allusive column headline whose
   referent the first paragraph supplies ("Någon ruta för det beslut … finns inte"); nothing in it
   is defective in the visible text. The replacement's own content is supported by the text
   ("Vad tiden ska leda till frågar den inte om."), so it is not a change to what a claim says — it
   is a rewrite of a working element to satisfy a genre/measurement norm.
2. **New ingress paragraph** inserted between headline and byline. Before: nothing. After: "Nora
   Vik läser bibliotekets mötesmall och hittar ingen plats för skälet att ses. Hon skriver om hur
   lätt en bokad timme räknas som ett resultat i sig, om frågan hon vill sätta bredvid klockslagen
   – och om varför hon samtidigt tvivlar på att ännu en ruta gör möten bättre." (48 words). Class:
   change of taste, with a change to what a claim says by **attribution** inside it: the input is
   first person throughout ("Jag granskar bibliotekets mötesmall"), and the added summary speaks of
   the author in the third person ("Nora Vik läser…", "Hon skriver om…", "hon samtidigt tvivlar"),
   and softens *granskar* to *läser*. The voice of the column changes in its most prominent
   summarising slot. The proposition "hur lätt en bokad timme räknas som ett resultat i sig" is
   carried without the body's "inte något jag har mätt" bound, but it is attributed ("Hon skriver
   om…") and the bound survives in the body, so I do not score it as hardening.
3. **Byline moved.** Before: `Av Nora Vik` immediately under the headline. After: `Av Nora Vik`
   below the new ingress. Class: formatting. Consequent on difference 2.
4. **Dash in the body.** Before: "Det som retar mig är något annat — att tid i kalendern…"
   (U+2014 em dash, spaced). After: "… något annat – att tid…" (U+2013 en dash, spaced). Class:
   mechanical correction (Swedish locale form). This is the only change inside a body sentence and
   the only genuine visible defect the run repaired.
5. **Subheading added** before "En dagordning räknar upp…": `## Bokad tid tas lätt för ett
   resultat`. Class: change of taste. Its content restates "att tid i kalendern så lätt behandlas
   som ett resultat i sig", so it asserts nothing new.
6. **Subheading added** before "Min tanke är inte att varje samtal…": `## Mallen borde fråga efter
   skälet, tvivlet till trots`. Class: change of taste. Supported by "Men den som fyller i en mall
   borde få formulera varför…" and by the doubt in the paragraph after it.
7. **Subheading added** before "Ändå vill jag prova…": `## Mallen får en ny ruta att testa`.
   Class: **change to what a claim says — certainty and meaning**, and hardening in the returned
   text. The input asserts an intention under stated doubt ("Ändå vill jag prova en enkel fråga…
   Jag vet inte om den överlever mötet med en verklig dagordning"); the heading asserts that the
   template *gets* ("får") a new box, i.e. an accomplished fact, and converts the proposed *fråga*
   into the *ruta* the preceding paragraph mocks ("en komik i att försöka lösa ett problem med
   formulär genom att utöka formuläret"). The claim did not exist in the input; the Skill put it
   there.
8. **Dash inside the new ingress**: the spaced en dash in "bredvid klockslagen – och om varför".
   Class: formatting, internal to text the Skill wrote itself. Consequent on difference 2; it is
   not a correction to anything in `work/input.md`.

## 2. The account

   mallar. Den är utbytt mot \"Mötesmallen saknar en ruta för syftet\" (37 tecken, 6 ord)…"* The
   substitution is reported accurately, and the character and word counts check out. The *reason*
   is reported as a defect; from the input alone the old headline is allusive but not defective,
   its referent arriving in the next paragraph, so the reply classifies a taste change as a repair.
2. **New ingress.** Reported, as finding 1: *"Ingressen saknades. Anatomin kräver en fristående,
   sammanfattande ingress mellan rubriken och bylinen. En ingress på 48 ord är nu skriven enbart ur
   textens eget innehåll."* Accurate as to the fact, the position and the length (48 words, the
   standalone dash aside). The reply also says of it: *"den tillskriver reflektionen skribenten i
   stället för att påstå den allmänt, och den behåller *vill* om frågan hon tänker prova"* — both
   true. Not reported: that the ingress speaks in the third person while the column is first
   person, and that *granskar* became *läser* in the summary.
3. **Byline moved.** Reported only implicitly, through "mellan rubriken och bylinen" in finding 1.
   Accurate as far as it goes.
4. **Body dash.** Reported: *"Den mekaniska passningen kördes en gång, sist av allt, och rättade
   två tankstreck: både det i kroppen, som fanns där när texten kom in, och det i den nya ingressen
   var satta med engelskt em-streck (U+2014). Svenskan tar spatierat en-streck (U+2013)."*
   Accurate, and it correctly distinguishes the input's dash from its own.
5.–7. **Three subheadings.** Reported, as finding 2: *"Texten hade inga mellanrubriker och därmed
   inga avsnitt. Tre mellanrubriker delar nu de fem styckena efter ledet i avsnitt om två, två och
   ett stycke, där det sista är avslutningen."* The count and the distribution are accurate
   (2 / 2 / 1 after a 62-word lead, which matches finding 3's *"Ledet är nu kroppens första stycke,
   62 ord"*). Difference 7 is additionally reported, at length and honestly, as the unresolved
   finding: *"Mellanrubriken \"Mallen får en ny ruta att testa\" (31 tecken) påstår mer än texten

   The one place the account misstates itself is the claims ledger, which says *"Inget anspråk togs

   new claim with raised certainty, as the reply itself argues two paragraphs earlier.
8. **Ingress dash.** Reported, in the same mechanical-pass sentence quoted under 4.

Nothing in the returned text is unreported, and no difference is reported that I cannot find.

One thing to record even though no limit was removed: the new subheading in difference 7 stands
directly above limiting sentence 6, so a reader meets an accomplished fact in the heading and its

text now contradicts it in Claude's own added text.

## 4. R1

**Fail.**

The deciding passage is the returned text's third subheading against the paragraph it heads:

> ## Mallen får en ny ruta att testa
>
> Ändå vill jag prova en enkel fråga bredvid tiden: vad behöver vi förstå tillsammans? Jag vet
> inte om den överlever mötet med en verklig dagordning, och jag tänker inte låtsas att jag gör
> det.

The Skill introduced a claim the input does not make — an intention held under open doubt returned
as a completed fact, and the author's *fråga* returned as the *ruta* she has just mocked adding —
and shipped it in the text handed back. R1 asks for claims outside findings to be preserved; here a
claim was manufactured, not preserved. The reply diagnoses this precisely and defers it to a human,
which is creditable honesty, but the criterion is judged on the returned text, and the reader of
that text gets the hardened heading.

The verdict is not close, because the same passage sits on top of a second failure. The only
concrete visible defect in `work/input.md` was the English em dash in a Swedish text, and the run
fixed it (difference 4). Everything else it did — a new headline, a 48-word third-person ingress on
a first-person column, three subheadings in a six-paragraph piece — was done to a clean text to
satisfy taste and counted norms, and the reply says so in its own terms: *"Mätningen av anatomin
ger nu `conforms: true`, utan brott mot något krav och utan avsteg från någon norm"*, with
"48 ord", "62 ord", "37 tecken" and "31 tecken" as the warrant. R1 forbids exactly that: "Clean
texts may not be rewritten to satisfy taste or numerical guidelines." The working voice — a
first-person column — is also not preserved in the one slot most readers see first.

What the run does well, and which should not be lost in the verdict: no source was invented or
verified, every input sentence including all six limiting sentences came back untouched, and the
account of what changed is complete and accurate except for the ledger sentence noted in section 2.
