# Cost Lervik's phone booking before closing it

On 18 June the council executive board is asked to remove phone booking from all seven community venues from September. The administration's report gives one reason: staff should not have to enter the details in two flows. That is a fair thing to want. It is not yet a reason to make the change permanent.

By Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut

All seven of Lervik's community venues take bookings by phone, and the council executive board is being asked to stop them doing so. Two questions are worth answering first: what the pilot report actually measured, and what keeping the phone route, or closing it, would cost.

## Counting bookings says nothing about who can use the web

The pilot report *Bokning av föreningslokaler*, dated 8 April 2026, covers an eight-week trial in two venues. It records 96 bookings made on the web and 24 made by phone.

Those figures are the report's, and so are their limits: it counts bookings rather than unique people, and it measures nothing about age, functional ability or how used to digital services people are. Twenty-four phone bookings therefore cannot tell us what share of Lervik's residents are unable to book digitally. Nobody should claim they can.

## A motive on the table, but no figures under it

The administration's objection to keeping both routes is double administration, and I take it at face value: it is not a claim that people who ring are lazy or expensive.

But the documents contain no measurement of how long either route takes staff, and no calculation of what removing one would save. The board is being asked to close a channel for good on the strength of an inconvenience the documents put no number on. That is thin ground for making the change permanent.

## Find out why the phone is still used, and what it costs staff

Öppna beslut, the group I speak for, proposes a six-month trial in all seven venues with both booking routes kept open. During it, the administration records the time each booking route takes and asks users to say, voluntarily, why they chose the phone or the web. We make no claim to have funded or costed that trial. What it would cost is for the board to weigh.

A booking made by phone is not proof that somebody cannot use the web. It is a reason to find out why the phone is still being used.

I am not against digital booking. I am against settling its shape before the council can set the work each route costs staff against what it is worth to the people who use it.

## Let the trial produce figures before a channel closes

Postpone the permanent switch and run the six months with both routes open. When the trial has produced figures, decide whether a channel should be removed, changed or kept. That decision is the board's either way, and it will be a better one for being taken with the cost of both routes on the table.
