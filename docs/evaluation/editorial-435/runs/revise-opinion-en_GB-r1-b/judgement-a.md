## 1. Differences

The frontmatter is identical. Paragraphs 2 to 7 of the body and the last section are word-for-word identical. Four differences:

1. **Byline added under the headline.** Before: nothing between the headline and "On 18 June…". After: a new paragraph, "By Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut". Class: a change of taste (layout; the signature moves from the end to the top and "— " becomes "By"). It does not change what a claim says: the author and role are word-for-word the same. One could argue it repairs a visible defect, since the input uses "I", "We" and "Öppna beslut proposes" before the reader learns who is speaking. But a sign-off at the end is a normal convention for an opinion piece, so I treat this as taste.
2. **Sign-off removed from the end.** Before: "— Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut" as the last line. After: absent. Class: consequent on difference 1 (the other half of the same move). Nothing is lost.
3. **Opening sentence.** Before: "On 18 June the council executive board is asked…". After: "On 18 June Lervik's council executive board is asked…". Class: a change to what a claim says, by **scope** (the board is now explicitly Lervik's). The headline ("Lervik's phone booking") and section 1 ("Lervik's residents") already support this, so no new fact comes in. The input was not defective here: the headline names the council.
4. **Third subheading.** Before: "## Six months, both routes, and something to measure". After: "## Weigh what phone and web cost staff against their worth to users". Class: a change of taste that is also a change to what a claim says, by **meaning**. The old heading announced the section's proposal: a six-month trial, both routes open, and measurement. The new heading announces an imperative demand to weigh cost against worth. It drops the trial and the measuring step that the section says must come first. There is a borderline **chronology** shift as well. In the input, the weighing can only happen after the trial ("before the council can set…"; "When the trial has produced figures, decide…"). The new imperative heading reads as though the weighing is the ask now. The old heading was not defective: a verbless heading made of nouns is a matter of style.

Total: 4 differences (3 independent, 1 consequent). 2 are changes to what a claim says: difference 3 (scope) and difference 4 (meaning, arguably also chronology).
