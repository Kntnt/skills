## 1. Differences

I compared `work/input.md` with the fenced text block in `response.md` line by line. The frontmatter, the headline, the other two headings and every body paragraph are identical. There are three differences.

1. **Byline moved and reworded.** Before: last line "— Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut" (after the final paragraph). After: "By Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut" as its own line under the headline, with the closing sign-off removed. The blank line before the old sign-off goes with it, as a consequence of the move. Class: **change of taste** (placement and formatting). The input's sign-off was a plain, visible attribution in a normal form for an opinion piece, so there was no defect to repair. The author and the role are unchanged, so attribution does not change.

2. **Section 2 heading.** Before: "## A motive on the table, but no figures under it". After: "## The documents put no figure on the officers' reason for the switch". Class: **change of taste**, and also a **change to what a claim says: meaning**. The heading now states that the section's "double administration" objection *is* the officers' reason from the opening paragraph. The input left that for the reader to infer. It also drops the concession framing ("on the table") and the image that the closing line echoes ("with the cost of both routes on the table"). The limit it carries ("no figures") survives, in "put no figure on". Attribution shifts from "the administration's" (body) to "the officers'" (heading), but the opening already attributes the reason to "the officers' report", so I count that as part of the meaning change rather than a new actor.

3. **Section 3 heading.** Before: "## Six months, both routes, and something to measure". After: "## Measure staff time on each channel and ask users why they choose it". Class: **change of taste**, and also a **change to what a claim says: scope and meaning**. On scope, the heading no longer names the proposal's core terms: six months and both routes kept open. It names only the measurement, narrowed to *staff* time, which the body says only indirectly ("the time each booking route takes"). On meaning, it becomes an unaddressed imperative, and it drops "voluntarily", although the body keeps that word. It is not consequent on either of the other differences.

## 4. R1

**Fail.** The deciding passage is the section 3 heading: "Six months, both routes, and something to measure" became "Measure staff time on each channel and ask users why they choose it". The input's heading had no concrete visible defect: repeating the section's key terms is not one. The rewrite is a change of taste to a clean passage, and it changes what the heading says. It drops the six-month, both-routes proposal, which is the piece's actual ask, and narrows the measurement to staff time. The section 2 heading repeats the pattern: a contestable "motive" defect was used to replace the author's image, which the closing line echoes. The byline move is also taste. In its favour, the Skill preserved every body sentence, quotation and limiting sentence. It reported all three changes, left the long paragraphs and headline alone rather than trim them to a numerical guideline, and reported the missing standfirst instead of inventing one. But the only three edits it made were not repairs of visible defects in a clean text.
