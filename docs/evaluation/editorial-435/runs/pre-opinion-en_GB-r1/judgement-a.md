## 1. Differences

1. **Byline moved and reworded.**
   - Before: the last line was `— Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut`, after the closing paragraph.
   - After: that line is gone. A new line, `By Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut`, stands between the headline and the opening paragraph. The dash sign-off became a "By" byline.
   - Class: **change of taste.** A sign-off at the end is an ordinary form for an opinion piece, and nothing in the input is defective about it. The name and the role are the same, so attribution does not change. The removal at the end and the addition at the top are one move. The change from "—" to "By" follows from that move.

2. **Section 2 subheading.**
   - Before: `## A motive on the table, but no figures under it`
   - After: `## The documents put no figure on the officers' reason for the switch`
   - Class: mainly a **change of taste**, with a small **change to what a claim says by attribution**. The input's heading gives the motive no owner. The new heading calls it the officers' reason. The section body calls it "the administration's objection", and the opening calls it the reason in "the officers' report". The attribution is supported inside the text, but the heading now states it, where before it was left implicit. The limit "no figures" survives as "no figure". The allusive image (something "on the table" with nothing "under it") is replaced by a flat declarative heading. Whether "motive" is a visible defect is doubtful: in English it means a reason for acting and does not necessarily suggest a hidden one.

3. **Section 3 subheading.**
   - Before: `## Six months, both routes, and something to measure`
   - After: `## Measure staff time on each channel and ask users why they choose it`
   - Class: **change to what a claim says, by scope and meaning.**
     - Scope: the heading no longer names the six-month duration or keeping both routes open, which are the core of the proposal.
     - Meaning: a noun-phrase summary of the proposal becomes an imperative with no stated addressee, and it now says "staff time". The sentence under it says only "the time each booking route takes". Section 2 and the final paragraph of section 3 ("the work each route costs staff") support "staff", so no new fact comes in, but the heading narrows what is measured. The heading also drops "voluntarily".
   - The finding behind it (the heading repeats the first sentence) points to repetition within normal limits for a heading that previews its section. That makes the change taste-driven.

No mechanical corrections. No changes to the frontmatter, the quotations (the italic report title), the figures, the dates or the body prose.
