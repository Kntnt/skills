# Judgement b

## 1. Differences

The returned text (the fenced block in `response.md`) differs from `work/input.md` in two places. The frontmatter, the byline, the paragraphing and the Markdown formatting are identical.

1. **Headline.** Before: `# Mallen har en ruta för allt utom poängen`. After: `# Mötesmallen har en ruta för allt utom poängen`. Class: a **change to what a claim says, by scope**. The headline's claim moves from "the template" to "the meeting template": the referent is narrowed and made specific. It is not the repair of a visible defect. The input headline is grammatical, and the first sentence of the body resolves "Mallen" at once ("bibliotekets mötesmall"). Nothing in the text is broken. The motive the reply gives is a genre-anatomy guideline: a keyword first, and a measured length. So the edit is also a change of taste. The narrowing is supported by the body and does not raise what the text asserts, so it is not hardening.
2. **Dash in paragraph 7.** Before: `utöka formuläret — bibliotekarien`. After: `utöka formuläret – bibliotekarien`. Class: **mechanical correction (a locale form)**. Swedish sets a parenthetical dash as a spaced en dash, not an em dash. The spaces were already there, so only the character changed.

Neither difference follows from the other.

## 4. R1

**Fail.** The case is narrow. The body is untouched apart from a correct locale fix to the dash. Voice, arguments and every limiting sentence are preserved, and the reply refuses to write the missing parts it lists (a standfirst, subheadings, a call to action) rather than put words in the author's mouth. That part is exemplary.

The deciding passage is the headline rewrite and its justification: "sa inte vilken mall det gällde. Man behövde läsa texten för att förstå att kolumnen handlar om möten … Rubriken inleds nu med nyckelordet ”Mötesmallen” och mäter 45 tecken och 8 ord." The input headline has no concrete visible defect. A definite-article teaser headline is ordinary column practice, and the first sentence resolves it. The change serves a keyword-and-length anatomy guideline, and it alters the scope of a claim in a text that was otherwise clean. R1 says "Clean texts may not be rewritten to satisfy taste or numerical guidelines," so this one edit fails the criterion. The edit is small, supported by the body, and transparently reported.
