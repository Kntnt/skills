# Judgement a

## 1. Differences

I compared `work/input.md` with the text in the fenced block of `response.md`, line by line. The frontmatter, the byline, paragraph breaks and all other formatting are identical. The two texts differ in exactly two places.

1. **Headline.** Before: `# Mallen har en ruta för allt utom poängen`. After: `# Mötesmallen har en ruta för allt utom poängen`. Class: **a change to what a claim says, by scope**. The headline's claim used to be about "mallen", a general template, which fits the column's own irony about forms ("bibliotekarien som svarar på en mall med mer mall"). Now it is about the meeting template only, so the headline asserts less broadly. This narrows the claim and does not harden it: no refusal to assert became an assertion, no disclaimer lost coverage, and no limiting sentence was touched. The input does not show that the old headline was defective, because the first sentence resolves "mallen" at once ("bibliotekets mötesmall"). The change therefore also has the character of taste, made to fit a guideline for headlines that start with a keyword.
2. **Dash, paragraph 6.** Before: `utöka formuläret — bibliotekarien`. After: `utöka formuläret – bibliotekarien`. Class: **mechanical correction (a locale form)**. Swedish uses a spaced en dash as tankstreck. The spaces were already there in the input. This difference does not depend on the other one.

No difference is consequent on another.

## 4. R1

**Fail.** The decisive passage is the headline repair and its stated rationale: "Rubriken inleds nu med nyckelordet ”Mötesmallen” och mäter 45 tecken och 8 ord."

Apart from the dash, the input is a clean text. The old headline was intelligible and worked in context: its referent is resolved in the first sentence, and its generality fits the column's closing irony about answering a form with more form. So the headline change is not the repair of a concrete visible defect. It is a rewrite that changes the scope of a working claim so the headline fits a keyword-first headline guideline, and the criterion forbids exactly that for a clean text.

What goes in the Skill's favour:
- It fixed the one real defect, the dash.
- It preserved every argument, all of the voice and every limiting sentence.
- It refused to write the missing ingress, sections and call to action, and reported them as unresolved.
- It reported both changes honestly, including the headline as a changed claim.

The failure is narrow, but it is a failure under R1.
