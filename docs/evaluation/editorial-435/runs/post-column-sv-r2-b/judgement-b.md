## 1. Differences

1. **Headline (H1).**
   Before: `# Rutan som inte finns`
   After: `# Bibliotekets mötesmall frågar efter tiden men inte syftet`
   Class: **a change to what a claim says, by meaning**. It is also **a change of taste**, not the repair of a visible defect. The old headline is a figurative column title. It asserts only that a box is missing, and it sets up a motif the text returns to ("Någon ruta … finns inte", "ytterligare en ruta", "ännu en ruta"). The body supports the new claim ("Vad tiden ska leda till frågar den inte om"), so it adds no scope or certainty beyond what the body already asserts. Still, the headline now makes a different claim in a different, flatter register. The input shows no concrete defect in the old title. "Vague" is a judgement of taste, and an evocative title is the norm for a column.

2. **Paragraph 3, dash.**
   Before: `Det som retar mig är något annat — att tid`
   After: `Det som retar mig är något annat – att tid`
   Class: **mechanical correction (locale form)**. A spaced em dash becomes the spaced en dash that Swedish uses. The spaces were already there, and only the character changed.

Total: 2 differences, 1 of them a change to what a claim says (meaning). Neither follows from the other.

**Fail.**

The deciding passage is Finding 1 together with the returned headline. The input is a clean column: its only visible defect is a dash in the wrong locale form, and the Skill corrected that correctly. The Skill then spent its correction budget rewriting the headline, which had no concrete visible defect. It justified the rewrite with a judgement of taste ("så vag") and a numerical measure ("57 tecken och 8 ord"). The rewrite replaces the column's figurative title with a flat, explanatory one. That loses working voice: the box motif that ties the title to the body. It also changes what the headline claims, by meaning. The reply's own Finding 5 shows that the new title creates a near-verbatim echo of the opening paragraph's last sentence.

R1 says "Clean texts may not be rewritten to satisfy taste or numerical guidelines", and this change is exactly that. Findings 2–4 (missing lead, missing H2 sections, missing separate closing section) apply an article anatomy to a six-paragraph column. They are reported as unresolved rather than acted on, so they did not damage the text. They do show that the review was driven by guidelines rather than by defects.
