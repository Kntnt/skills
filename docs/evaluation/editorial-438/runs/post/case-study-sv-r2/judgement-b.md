## 2. F1: fail (on the byline alone)

The byline assigns the piece to a named person the material never mentions. On supplier-published material ("it is not independent journalism"), a personal first-name byline in a journalistic format also suggests an individual writer rather than the publisher, Svale. The reply flags the byline to the user, but the delivered text still carries it.

If the byline is set aside, the story content stays within the material. Two minor imprecisions remain:

- **Subheading "PM:et tillskriver inte loggen den kortare tilldelningstiden".** The source says the note "explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software". In this draft "loggen" mostly names the tool Svale configured ("Svale konfigurerade loggen"), so the two terms mostly coincide. But "loggen" also covers the shared-log way of working, and the material does not say what the note attributes the difference to beyond the software. A reader could take the subheading as a statement about the practice, and that statement is unknown, not absent. The body gets it right ("avstår uttryckligen från att tillskriva programvaran skillnaden").
- **Scope of the exclusion.** "Enligt det fördes 31 felanmälningar in i loggen. Akuta ärenden och arbeten som beställdes före provet räknas inte med." The source's "It excludes…" has the note as its grammatical subject, so the exclusion covers all of the note's figures, including the median. The draft puts the exclusion right after the count and starts a new paragraph for the median. A reader will likely apply it to the 31 only, and the median looks unqualified. The effect is small and nothing in the draft is false.

## 4b. Subheadings over quotations

- **"Teamet testade om loggen kunde visa status innan det valde Svale"** over quotation 1 (quoted in full above).
  - **Prepares.** The words "loggen" and "Svale" name what the quotation speaks about. The subheading's point is the pre-selection test, which the quotation does not state. The quotation's points are shift continuity and the team owning the categories.
  - Two readings: neutral, since the test is not in the quotation; or prepares, since it names the log and Svale. I chose prepares because it names the quotation's subject. It is not pre-echo, because "visa status" is about the test, not the goal of letting the evening shift see the morning shift's work.
- **"Arbetsledaren ser tillbaka på åtta veckors prov"** over quotation 2: **prepares**. "Arbetsledaren" names the speaker and "ser tillbaka på åtta veckors prov" names the occasion. It says nothing about the time spent on the categories.
- **"Arbetsledaren ser tillbaka på åtta veckors prov"** over quotation 3: **prepares**, for the same words. It does not pre-state her verdict (she would do it again, with an extra week).

**Counts:** pre-echo 0, prepares 3, neutral 0.
