## 2. F1 — fail (narrow: one changed subject; everything else stays within the material)

**The defect.** Before the second quotation the draft has the narrative question "Would Vale make the same choice again, and what would change?" The source question is "…and what would *you* change?"

- The draft drops Vale as the one who acts. "What would change" is impersonal and wider: it could cover changes to outcomes, to the product, or to things outside her control.
- The quotation that follows ("I would allow another week…") answers the narrower, personal question.
- This is a changed subject, and it also changes how the reader takes the quotation. It misstates no fact, so the defect is small.

**Checked and found faithful:**

- **Headline**, "Fenwick reports staff could see instruments awaiting parts". It is attributed to the workshop's report and not stated as a measured fact. This matches "It says staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts."
- **"Put … on a six-week trial"** and **"began a six-week trial"**. Neither claims that the trial ran its full six weeks.
- **"a brass and woodwind repair workshop"**. The source says "services brass and woodwind instruments", and the business is named "Instrument Repairs". "Repair" and "services" cover the same work here, read either way.
- **Chronology**: January 2026 start, a summary dated 23 February 2026, and "email answers". All correct. The draft omits the 2 March interview date, which is an omission and not a distortion.
- **Division of labour**: the workshop defined the statuses and who would update them, and Benchline configured them and trained four staff in one online session. Kept exactly.
- **Telephone**: "Collection arrangements were still discussed … by telephone." This matches "continued to use telephone calls".
- **Limits of the summary**: "It does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction" is kept word for word. The heading "leaves repair speed unmeasured" is a fair rendering of "does not measure repair duration". The draft suggests no improvement anywhere, so unknown stays unknown and is not presented as absent.
- **The decision**: "Vale approved continued use for new jobs but postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions." This is supported, and the source identifies the manager as Vale.
- **Standfirst**, "which step would get an extra week". It keeps the conditional ("would") and refers to the status-name check before the backlog is added.
- **No invented emotions, visit, spoken exchange or expression.** "Replied" and "explains" are neutral verbs and fit an email exchange.

**Cautions, not counted as failures:**

- **The ending generalises.** "A manager weighing a shared schedule might start with the question Fenwick had to answer: what does 'ready for work' mean on your benches?"
  - The source says the material is "not … a recommendation for every workshop".
  - The line is hedged ("might"), recommends no product, and draws only on Vale's own point. It is a publisher's suggestion and does not claim a result. I let it stand, but it is as far as the material allows.
- **The heading "The manager weighs the decision"** uses the present-tense verb "weighs". Vale has already approved continued use, and her quotation is a hindsight judgement, not a decision still being weighed. This is a slight change of tense and stance.
- **The byline "By Thomas"** names an author the material does not supply. The brief says no author name is supplied, and the interview was conducted by "Benchline's case writer". The reply discloses the default and says to change it. It is metadata, not a claim about the subject, so I do not count it under F1.

## 4. Bridges into quotations

1. **Quotation 1.**
   - Bridge: "Asked what the team had to settle before the schedule was useful, workshop manager Priya Vale replied:"
   - Quotation: "‘We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench.’"
   - **Class (b).** "Asked what the team had to settle … replied" gives the occasion (the interview question) and "workshop manager Priya Vale" gives the attribution. It does not say what had to be settled.
   - Other reading: (c), because the bridge carries the question, which the quotation does not. I chose (b) because a question the speaker was asked is the occasion, which (b) covers, and it adds no fact about the subject.
2. **Quotation 2.**
   - Bridge: "Would Vale make the same choice again, and what would change?"
   - Quotation: "‘I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog.’"
   - **Class (b).** "Would Vale make the same choice again" names the subject and the speaker. It gives neither the answer ("again for new jobs") nor the change ("another week").
   - Other reading: (a), because the bridge sets out both halves the quotation answers. I rejected it because the bridge does not deliver the answers, so the quotation still adds everything.

No quotation stands without a bridge.

- **Counts:** (a) 0, (b) 2, (c) 0.
- **Interviewer attribution:** the draft attributes no question to an interviewer. The second bridge is a narrative question, not presented as the interviewer's words, but it paraphrases the interviewer's question with the subject dropped ("what would change?" for "what would you change?").
- **Unsupported assertion in a bridge:** none. The second bridge's only departure from the material is that dropped subject, covered under F1.

## 4b. Subheadings over quotations

1. **Quotation 1.**
   - Subheading: "The workshop set the rules and Benchline configured them"
   - Quotation: "‘We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench.’"
   - **Prepares.** "The workshop set the rules" names the subject: the workshop defining the statuses.
   - Other reading: pre-echo, because "set the rules" comes close to "we had to agree what ready for work meant". I chose prepares because the quotation's point is that "ready for work" was ambiguous (a part in the building is not a part at the right bench), and the subheading does not state that.
2. **Quotation 2.**
   - Subheading: "The manager weighs the decision"
   - Quotation: "‘I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog.’"
   - **Prepares.** "The manager" names the speaker and "the decision" names the subject. It says neither that she would choose it again nor what she would change.

- **Counts:** pre-echo 0, prepares 2, neutral 0.
