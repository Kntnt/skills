# Fenwick reports seeing which instruments awaited parts

A six-week trial put jobs awaiting parts and jobs ready for work on one shared schedule at Fenwick Instrument Repairs. Manager Priya Vale explains what the team had to agree before it was useful, and what Vale would change.

By Thomas

Fenwick Instrument Repairs services brass and woodwind instruments. In January 2026 its workshop team began a six-week trial of a shared bench schedule from Benchline, the scheduling software supplier publishing this account. Before then, jobs awaiting parts and jobs ready for work appeared on separate paper sheets.

## The workshop set the rules and Benchline configured them

The team chose Benchline after checking that it could tell those two statuses apart. The workshop defined the statuses and decided who would update them; Benchline configured those choices and trained four staff in one online session. Collection arrangements were still discussed with customers by telephone.

Asked by email what the team had to settle before the schedule was useful, workshop manager Priya Vale wrote: ‘We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench.’

## The summary counts jobs, not repair times

The workshop's own trial summary, dated 23 February 2026, says staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts. It records 38 jobs entered in the schedule. It does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction.

## Fenwick's manager settles the next step

Vale approved continued use for new jobs but postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions. Would Vale make the same choice again, and change anything? ‘I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog.’

This is one team's reported experience, not a measured result. A manager weighing a shared schedule might begin with what Fenwick had to settle: agreeing what each status means at their own benches.
