The body keeps the attribution ("says staff could see"), but the headline is what readers see first and it drops the attribution. The second checker found this and the writer did not fix it.

The byline puts a personal name on the piece that the material does not carry. The reply admits the name came from the system account.   The sentence also leans towards advice for other workshops. The source limits itself to "not … a recommendation for every workshop". "Can begin with" is mild, but the scope is still wider than the source. The second checker found the "had to settle" stretch and the writer did not fix it.

The source says the manager "postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions". "Left the backlog off" can be read as a final exclusion, and it drops both the delay and its condition. The body paragraph under the heading restores the condition, so the harm is limited to a reader who skims the headings. No checker is reported to have seen this.
