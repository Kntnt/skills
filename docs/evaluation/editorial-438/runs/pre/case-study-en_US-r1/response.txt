# Elm Quay trial gives shifts one repair log

Elm Quay Housing's maintenance team spent eight weeks testing a shared repair log from Svale Systems in two buildings. This account covers why the team started, how the work was divided, what supervisor Maya Lind would change before the next building, and what the trial figures can and cannot show.

By Thomas

Before the trial, telephone reports and emails at Elm Quay Housing were stored separately. The company manages 640 flats, and in September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to try a shared repair log in two buildings. The aim was for staff on different shifts to see the same information. After eight weeks, the team has its first figures and its supervisor has lessons about preparation.

## Elm Quay chose Svale after a test of repair status

Maintenance supervisor Maya Lind, who answered questions by email, describes what the team was after:

> “We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log.”

The team picked Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair. It kept telephone reporting open for residents, and Svale trained six staff during two sessions.

## The trial note does not credit the log with the shorter median

Elm Quay's internal trial note, dated December 4, 2025, says that 31 repair reports were entered during the trial. It excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial began.

The median time from report to assignment was two working days during the trial, against three in the preceding eight weeks. The two periods had different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software.

The figure concerns assignment, not completion. There are no measurements of completion time, cost, or resident satisfaction.

## The supervisor looks back on eight weeks

Lind's reflections start with the preparation:

> “We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts.”

Lind's assessment of the trial as a whole comes with a reservation, and it stops short of recommending Svale to every housing company:

> “I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation.”
