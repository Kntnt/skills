### Finding 1 — the date stretched over a second event — WOULD BE ACCEPTED

- Draft: "In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log and chose Svale Systems after testing whether it could show the status of each repair."
- Source: "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings." The choice stands in its own undated sentence: "It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair."
- Evidence of the mismatch: in the draft the adverbial "In September 2025" governs both conjoined predicates, so it dates the choice of supplier and the test that preceded it. The supplied material dates one event only. The sequence it gives — decision, then a test, then a choice — leaves the test and the choice free to fall after September, and a decision late in September followed by an October test and a November choice satisfies every supplied statement while falsifying the draft. The draft is strictly the stronger statement, and nothing supplied asserts what it adds. This is a concrete mismatch in the draft, not a merely conceivable stronger reading.
- Smallest repair the checker proposed: "In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log. It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair."

### Finding 2 — the pronoun moves what was tested — WOULD BE REJECTED AS NOT ESTABLISHED

- Draft: "…after testing whether **it** could show the status of each repair."
- Source: "It chose Svale Systems after testing whether **the log** could show the status of each repair."
- Evidence against the finding: the report rests the allegation on proximity alone — "the nearest and most available antecedent for 'it' is 'Svale Systems'" — and then concedes that the other reading "says what the material says". Proximity does not settle an antecedent against the sentence's own content. What is tested is the ability to "show the status of each repair", which is a property of a log, and the draft's subject throughout the clause is the team acting on the shared log it had just decided to try. The report establishes no reading on which the draft asserts something the material denies; it establishes only that a stronger reading is conceivable, which the task says does not establish a defect. The checker itself calls the defect "that the sentence supports two different factual claims", which is a preference for a clearer pronoun rather than a claim the material contradicts.
- Note on what survives: the repair the checker proposes for Finding 1 writes "the log" out in full anyway, so accepting Finding 1 removes the ambiguity without Finding 2 having to be accepted.
