## Disposition of each finding

### Finding 1 — the September date stretched over the choice of supplier — GENUINELY UNRESOLVED

Draft: "In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log and chose Svale Systems after
testing whether it could show the status of each repair."
Source: "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two
buildings." and, in a separate undated sentence, "It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log
could show the status of each repair."

The fronted adverbial governs both conjoined predicates, so in its actual contextual meaning the draft
dates the choice of supplier, and the test preceding it, to September 2025. The material dates only the
decision. This is not a merely conceivable stronger reading: it is what the coordinated sentence says.
Nor does the rest of the material close the gap — the trial ran eight weeks and the note is dated
4 December 2025, which places the trial's start in October and leaves the choice free to fall after
September. The report's repair (end the sentence after the decision; let the choice stand undated) is the
smallest one that restores the material's own scoping.

Not rejected. It is a real unresolved question of support, and it is what the editor settles, not an
established fact about the text.

### Finding 2 — "it" said to move the tested thing from the log to the supplier — REJECTED

Draft: "…decided to try a shared log and chose Svale Systems after testing whether **it** could show the
status of each repair."
Source: "It chose Svale Systems after testing whether **the log** could show the status of each repair."

Rejected on the draft's own evidence. The predicate "could show the status of each repair" is a property
of a repair log, and the same sentence supplies "a shared log" as its subject's object; that is what the
pronoun picks out in context. "Svale Systems" is named as the party chosen, not as a thing tested, and
nothing in the clause predicates a test of it. Proximity alone does not establish the supplier reading
against the sentence's own sense, and the report offers no evidence beyond proximity. The report itself
concedes that on the reading it does not prefer "the draft says what the material says". A conceivable
alternative reading is not a defect, and the report has not established its allegation.

This rejection is a decision evidenced from the draft and the source. It is not the checker's approval of
the sentence, and it leaves Finding 1's repair to the same sentence untouched: that repair would write
"the log" out in full and close this reading as a by-product.
