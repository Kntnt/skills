### F1 — An undated event is given a date (chronology). Line 44.

**Draft:** "In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log **and chose Svale Systems** after testing whether it could show the status of each repair."

**Material, in its own words:** "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings." … "It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair."

**What changed:** the material dates one event — the decision to trial. It gives no date for the supplier choice and none for the testing that preceded it, and it places both in a separate sentence. In the draft, "In September 2025" is a single fronted adverbial governing a coordinated pair of verbs, "decided … and chose", so it dates both. The testing, which the material puts before the choice, is pulled inside September too.

**Case in which the material holds and the draft fails:** the team decides in September 2025 to run a trial; it then tests candidate logs through October, choosing Svale Systems on 3 November 2025, and the eight-week trial runs from early November to the trial note of 4 December. Nothing in the material excludes this — no supplied statement dates the choice, the testing, or the trial's start — and an eight-week trial ending near a 4 December note is at least as compatible with this as with a September start. On this case "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial" is true and "In September 2025 … chose Svale Systems" is false.

**The other direction:** not needed for a finding, but for completeness — if the choice were in fact made in September, the material's wording still holds, since it simply does not say when. Silence is not a date; preserving the state of knowledge means leaving the choice undated.

**Smallest supported repair** — split the sentence so the date governs only the decision:

&gt; In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log. It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair.

This repair also settles F4.

### F2 — An added restriction on the reach of a named person's stated view. Line 62.

**Draft:** "**That assessment was specific to the trial;** Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company."

**Material, in its own words:** "Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company. Her final quotation is her actual, qualified assessment. It is not an inference from the figures."

**What changed:** scope, and whose assertion. The material says what Lind did not do — recommend Svale universally — and insists her final quotation is her own real, qualified assessment. The draft converts that into a positive claim about the boundary of her view: that it reached the trial and no further. That boundary is the draft's, not hers, and it is stated flatly, in the publication's voice, about a named person's opinion.

**Case in which the material holds and the draft fails:** Lind's view is that a shared log of this kind is worth trying at any housing company with the same two-shift handover problem, and she would say so to such a company — but not to every housing company, since she would not press it on a landlord with a handful of units or on one whose repairs run through a single shift. Then "Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company" is true and "That assessment was specific to the trial" is false. No supplied statement excludes this case: the material limits her recommendation only by "not … every housing company" and nowhere confines her assessment to the trial.

**The other direction:** if her view really were confined to the trial, the material's sentence still holds, since not recommending universally follows from that. The material's wording is true under both readings, the draft's under only one — so the draft asserts more than the material carries.

That the wording is cautious, and cuts against the publisher's interest, does not preserve it; a case stands against it either way.

**Smallest supported repair** — delete the added clause and keep the material's own sentence:

&gt; Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company. The team has not yet expanded the trial.

If a statement of the assessment's standing is wanted, the supported one is that it is her own and not read off the figures: "Her assessment was her own, not an inference from the figures."
