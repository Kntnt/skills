### 2.1 Finding 1 — the September 2025 date is extended over the choice of supplier

**Draft passage.** "In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log and chose Svale Systems after testing whether it could show the status of each repair."

**Source passages.** "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings." / "It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair."

**What differs — time.** The material dates one event, the decision to trial. The choice of Svale is given only in sequence, after an intervening step: the team tested whether the log could show the status of each repair, and chose afterwards. The draft coordinates "decided" and "chose" under one fronted time adverbial, so "In September 2025" governs both verbs, and the draft asserts that the selection of the supplier also fell within September 2025.

**Both directions.**

- A case in which the material's statements hold and the draft's fails: the team decides on 26 September 2025 to trial a shared log; it tests whether the candidate log can show the status of each repair through October; it chooses Svale Systems on 13 October 2025. Every supplied sentence holds — the decision is in September 2025, and the choice comes after the testing — while the draft's sentence is false, because the choice was not made in September 2025.
- A case in which the draft's statement holds and the material's is not thereby established: decision and choice both fall in September 2025. The material is compatible with this but does not state it, so the draft turns an unclaimed date into an asserted one.

**No supplied statement excludes the first case.** The material gives no end date for the testing, no start date for the trial, and no date for the choice. The only other dates are "In September 2025" for the decision and "4 December 2025" for the trial note; an eight-week trial ending before 4 December is compatible with a choice made in October 2025.

**Secondary point inside the same sentence.** The material's "whether the log could show the status of each repair" becomes "whether it could show", whose nearest antecedent is "Svale Systems". The repair below restores the material's noun and removes the ambiguity as a by-product.

**Smallest supported repair.** Split the conjunction so the date attaches only to the event the material dates:

&gt; In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to try a shared log. It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair.

This keeps the date, the sequence, the actor, the customer as acting party and the absence of any supplier comparison, and adds nothing.
