# On writing things down twice

Every team that keeps a decision log also keeps the decisions somewhere else — in a thread, in a ticket, in somebody's head — and the log is the copy that goes stale. This is usually treated as a discipline problem. It is more often a design problem: the log is written after the decision, by someone who has stopped thinking about it, for a reader who does not exist yet.

The logs that survive are the ones written during the argument rather than after it, by whoever is losing. A record of what the objection was, made by the person who made the objection, is worth more six months later than a tidy summary of what was agreed, because the thing a later reader needs is almost never the decision. It is the reason the alternative was rejected.
