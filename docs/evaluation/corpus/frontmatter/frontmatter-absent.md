# What a duty roster is actually for

A duty roster looks like a scheduling artefact and is really a promise about attention. The names on it say who is paid to be interrupted this week, and — much more usefully — who is not. The second half is the half that gets lost, because it is written nowhere and enforced by nobody.

Teams that treat the roster as scheduling produce fair rotas that nobody trusts. Everyone is on the rota, everyone answers anyway, and the person whose week it is gets no more uninterrupted time than the person whose week it is not. The rota is correct and the promise is broken.

The fix is not a better rota. It is the boring habit of routing the interruption to whoever is on duty even when you know exactly which colleague could answer it faster, and accepting the slower answer as the price of the promise. Most teams find this harder than any scheduling problem they have ever solved.
