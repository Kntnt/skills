Interview with Petra Halldin, workshop manager at Nordvik Verkstad, recorded 11 March. Transcribed as spoken, including fillers and repetition. Interviewer marked Q, Halldin marked PH.

Q: Can you tell me what changed when you started taking in electric vans?

PH: So, I mean, the — okay, the first thing is, it's not the engine, everyone thinks it's the engine, it's, you know, it's the whole, the whole booking side of it. Because a van comes in and it, it might be here for two days now instead of, instead of four hours, right, and we only have the one charger, we had one charger then, so, yeah. You end up planning a week ahead which we never, we never used to do.

Q: How many did you take in that first year?

PH: Eleven. No — sorry, eleven that we did the full service on. There were more that came in for, for like a tyre change or something, that doesn't count, I wouldn't count that. Eleven.

Q: And the training?

PH: Yeah, so two of the guys did the high-voltage course, that was, that's a three-day thing in Gothenburg, and, and honestly I thought it would be, I thought it would be harder to get them to go. They wanted to go. That surprised me. The third one, Jonas, he — I mean he's been here twenty-two years, he said, he said something like, look, I'll do it when the work is here, and, and that's fair enough, that's fair. It wasn't here yet.

Q: Would you do it again?

PH: Yes. Yeah. I'd do the charger first though. We bought the charger after, and that was, that was just wrong, that was the wrong order to do it in, we lost, I don't know, we lost weeks on that. Do it first.

Q: Anything you would warn other workshops about?

PH: Um. Don't, don't promise a date. Not in the first year. Because you, you don't know yet, you genuinely don't know how long it takes, and the customer, they remember the date, they don't remember that you said probably.
