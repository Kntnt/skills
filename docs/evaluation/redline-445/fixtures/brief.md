## 1. What is the assignment?
A short notice about the Fen Repair lamp assessment desk, for the community noticeboard; genre general.
## 2. Who is the reader?
Local residents with broken lamps who do not know whether repair is possible and need practical visit details.
## 3. What makes the text RIV for the reader?
Relevant because they have a lamp to assess; interesting because they may be able to keep it; valuable because they know where, when and how to attend.
## 4. Which brand is the sender?
Fen Repair speaks about its own desk, in plain, helpful language.
## 5. What is the text's angle?
How to get a broken lamp assessed at the local desk.
## 6. What should the reader have understood afterwards?
The Fen Repair desk can assess a lamp but cannot guarantee a repair.
## 7. What should the reader feel, think or do afterwards?
Book a slot by emailing desk@example.org.
## 8. How should the text be structured?
Free structure: practical details first suit a short notice.
## 9. What is the hook?
Can my broken lamp be repaired?
## 10. What sources and evidence do you have?
sources.md contains all event details. Do not add unsupported facts.
## 11. What should be left out?
No claims about cost or successful repairs, and no reminiscence about old possessions.
## 12. What is the working title?
Visit the repair desk.
## 13. What frame applies to the text?
British English (en_GB), a helpful plain voice, roughly 50–80 words, no search phrase because this is a printed noticeboard.
