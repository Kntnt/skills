# Headline validation brief

You read one short Swedish article and judge its headline as a reader would. The article is `text.md`, in the directory named in the message that sent you here. Read that file and nothing else — no other file in that directory or anywhere else, no repository file, no ticket, no style guide. You are not told where the text came from or why it is being read, and you must not guess or try to find out.

The headline is the line that starts with `#`. Everything under it is the article.

## What to write

Write `validation-<your letter>.md` in that directory, with the letter the message gave you. Answer every heading.

**1. The headline alone.** Cover everything but the headline, as a reader does who meets it alone in a list of articles or a search result. Say in one or two sentences what that reader would take the article to be about. Then say whether that reader can tell what the article is about: `yes` or `no`, with the words of the headline that decide it.

**2. The headline against the article.** Read the whole article. Does the headline say anything — a fact, a figure, a scope, a judgement, a degree of certainty — that the article does not support, or support at that strength? Quote the headline's words and the article's sentences that decide it. Say `yes` or `no`.

**3. Class.** Give the headline exactly one of these three classes, and one sentence saying why:

- **working** — a reader who sees only the headline can tell what the article is about, and the headline says nothing the article does not support;
- **unclear** — a reader who sees only the headline cannot tell what the article is about;
- **overclaiming** — a reader who sees only the headline can tell what the article is about, but the headline says something the article does not support, or says it more strongly than the article does.

Judge whether the headline does its job, not whether you would have written it differently: a headline you would word otherwise can still be working.

Then reply in at most 60 words: the class, and the path you wrote.
