# Validation a

Headline: "Björkskolans temperaturgivare ger halva svaret"

## 1. The headline alone

A reader who sees only the headline would take the article to be about temperature sensors installed at a school called Björkskolan, and about a measurement that answered only part of what someone wanted to know about temperatures there. The reader would not know what the question was, but would know the subject and the outcome: the sensor results are incomplete.

Can the reader tell what the article is about: **yes**. The words "Björkskolans temperaturgivare" give the place and the subject, and "ger halva svaret" gives the outcome: a partial result.

## 2. The headline against the article

Does the headline claim more than the article supports: **no**.

- "halva svaret" is a figure of speech, not a measured share, and the article splits the result into two parts that match it: the measurements show when the air was cold, but not why. The article says: "Fyra veckors mätningar pekar ut tillfällen att undersöka närmare, men förklarar inte varför luften var kall." and Rask says: "Vi vet när vi behöver titta närmare. - "Björkskolans temperaturgivare" matches the scope of the article: "fyra veckors mätningar i Björkskolans sex klassrum".
- The headline does not claim that the classrooms were too cold, gives no figure, and states no cause. So it does not go further than the article, which says that the limit is the office's own ("Gränsen är kontorets egen, vald för det här försöket") and that the sensors did not measure draughts or how the pupils felt.

**working** — the headline alone tells the reader that the article is about Björkskolan's temperature sensors and that their results are incomplete, and the article supports this claim: the measurements show when the air was cold but not why.
