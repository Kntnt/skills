# Validation a

## 1. The headline alone

A reader who sees only the headline would take the article to report a measurement trial at Björkskolan. They would expect the trial to have found that the school's classrooms are generally too cold.

Can that reader tell what the article is about? **yes.** The words that decide it are "Mätförsöket", "Björkskolans klassrum" and "för kalla". They name the subject (classroom temperature at one named school), the source (a measurement trial) and the finding.

## 2. The headline against the article

**yes.** The headline says more than the article supports, and says it with more certainty.

- "visar att" presents a firm, proven result. The article says the measurements only "pekar ut tillfällen att undersöka närmare, men förklarar inte varför luften var kall". The technician quoted says: "Vi vet när vi behöver titta närmare. Vi vet ännu inte varför det blev kallt just då."
- "klassrummen är för kalla" is a general, present-tense judgement about all the classrooms. The article reports something narrower. "14 lektionspass av 120 med minst en mätning under den gränsen" means about 12 percent of lesson periods had at least one low reading. The article also says "Hur länge temperaturen låg under den anger rapporten inte", so the duration is unknown.
- "för kalla" sounds like a fixed standard or a judgement of comfort. The article says the 20-degree limit is "kontorets egen, vald för det här försöket". It also says "Ett lågt värde säger alltså när luften var kall, men inte hur rummet kändes", because draught and the pupils' experience were not measured.
- The article does not say how the 14 periods were spread across the six rooms. "Björkskolans klassrum" therefore covers more rooms than the evidence does.

## 3. Class

**overclaiming.** The headline makes the subject clear, but "visar att … är för kalla" turns a few low readings against a local working limit into a proven, general verdict on the school's classrooms. The article itself refuses to draw that conclusion.
