## 1. Differences

I compared `work/input.md` line by line with the text inside the reply's fenced block. Only one line differs.

- **Headline.** Before: `# Mätförsöket i Björkskolan visar när, inte varför`. After: `# Mätförsöket i Björkskolan visar när det var kallt, inte varför`. Class: a change of taste that also changes what a claim says. The new headline changes its **meaning** and **scope**: the open "när" (which reads in line with the standfirst's "tillfällen att undersöka närmare" and Rask's "Vi vet när vi behöver titta närmare") becomes "when it was cold", a general claim about cold. The headline also grows from 48 characters and 7 words to 62 characters and 10 words.

Nothing else changed. The standfirst, the `Text: Hedda Lund` byline, the lead, both subheadings, the body, the quotation, the funding sentence and the closing sentence are all identical, and so is the Markdown formatting (heading levels, bold standfirst, italic report title). There is no frontmatter in either version. The fence around the returned text is how the reply is packaged, not part of the text.

## 4. R1

**Fail.**

This is a clean control. The expectation says the text "conforms to the anatomy", and R1 says "Clean texts may not be rewritten to satisfy taste." The headline "visar när, inte varför" is a deliberate, compressed contrast, and the standfirst directly beneath it states the subject. Wanting a headline that stands alone in a list is a preference, not a visible defect. The rewrite also costs the text something, by the Skill's own account. It "kan … läsas som ett bredare påstående än texten bär". It drops the reading of "när" as "when to look closer". And it moves a headline that met the should-norms (48 characters, 7 words) to one that breaks two of them (62 characters, 10 words). A claim outside any real defect was changed in meaning and scope. The Skill's honest account makes that easy to see, but it does not cure it.
