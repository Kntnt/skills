# Lervik should not hang up the phone in September

*Sanna Ek, spokesperson for Öppna beslut*

On June 18 the municipal executive board will consider a staff memo that proposes ending phone booking for all seven association facilities in September. The board should postpone that switch and first run six months with both phone and web booking in place. I am not against digital booking. I am against deciding this on the documents now in front of the board.

## The pilot counted bookings, not people

The municipality's pilot report *Bokning av föreningslokaler*, dated April 8, 2026, covers eight weeks in two facilities: 96 bookings through the web and 24 by phone. The report counts bookings rather than unique individuals, and it measures nothing about age, functional ability, or digital habits.

So those 24 bookings cannot tell us what share of Lervik's residents are unable to book digitally. They may come from 24 different people or from a handful who call often; the report was not built to tell the difference. Eight weeks in two facilities is thin ground for a permanent change in seven.

## The documents don't measure what the change saves

The memo's stated motive is that staff should not have to enter the same information in two flows. Double administration is a real objection, and I will be precise about it: the administration is not claiming that people who call are lazy or costly.

But the documents contain no time measurement and no savings calculation. The board is being asked to remove a booking route without being told what keeping it costs, or what removing it would free up.

## Six months, seven facilities, both ways in

Öppna beslut proposes keeping both routes for six months in all seven facilities. During that period the administration records how much time a booking takes by each route, and asks users to say, voluntarily, why they chose the phone or the web.

A booking that comes in by phone is no proof that someone can't use the web. It's a reason to find out why the phone is still being used. Six months would give the board what the current documents lack: a labor cost that can be set against a value for users.

## What the board can decide on June 18

Postpone the permanent switch and start the trial. When it ends, the board can remove the phone route, change it, or keep it — knowing this time what each route costs and why people use it. What the six months themselves cost, we have not calculated, and we do not pretend otherwise. That figure is for the board to weigh, and weighing it is still a better decision than making the choice blind.
