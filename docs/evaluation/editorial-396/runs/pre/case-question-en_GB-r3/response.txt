# Fenwick reports staff could see jobs awaiting parts

Fenwick Instrument Repairs has trialled a shared bench schedule. Its manager, Priya Vale, explains what the team had to settle before the schedule was useful and what Vale would change, while the workshop’s trial summary shows what was and was not measured.

By Thomas Barregren

Jobs waiting for parts and jobs ready for work used to appear on separate paper sheets at Fenwick Instrument Repairs, which services brass and woodwind instruments. In January 2026 the workshop team began a six-week trial of a shared bench schedule, choosing Benchline after checking that it could tell those two statuses apart. Workshop manager Priya Vale has since approved continued use for new jobs, but postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions.

## ‘Ready for work’ needed an agreed meaning

The workshop defined the statuses and decided who would update them. Benchline configured those choices and trained four staff in one online session, while the team kept discussing collection arrangements with customers by telephone.

Asked in an email interview what the team had to settle before the schedule was useful, Vale wrote: ‘We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench.’

## The trial recorded visibility rather than faster repairs

The workshop’s trial summary, dated 23 February 2026, records 38 jobs entered in the schedule and says staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts. It does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction.

The summary and the interview establish the team’s reported experience, not a measured improvement in repair speed or a recommendation for every workshop.

## Status definitions come before the backlog

Asked whether the same choice would be made again, and what would change, Vale replied: ‘I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog.’

A manager weighing a similar trial can begin by deciding, as Fenwick’s workshop did, what each status means and who updates it, and, as Vale would, by allowing time to check those names before adding a backlog.
