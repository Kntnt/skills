## 2. F1: fail

Each item below was checked against `work/source.md`.

- **Ending, unsupported generalisation and changed modality.** The ending reads: "A workshop weighing a shared schedule of its own could start where Fenwick had to: by agreeing what each status means, and checking the names, before any backlog goes in."
  - *Modality.* The material supports "had to" only for agreeing what "ready for work" meant ("We had to agree…"). Checking the names before the backlog goes in was not a necessity. It combines the manager's decision to postpone the backlog "until the team had reviewed its status definitions" with Vale's hindsight ("I would allow another week…"). The colon extends "had to" over both, so a choice becomes a necessity.
  - *Scope.* The material says it establishes "the team's reported experience, not … a recommendation for every workshop". The ending turns one workshop's experience into guidance for any reader's workshop. The hedge "could" softens this but does not remove it. It is advice, and the material does not license it. The brief supplies no call to action, so this ending takes the place of one without a basis.
- **Byline, unsupported attribution.** "By Thomas Barregren" names an author, but the brief says "No author name … is supplied". The material names only "Benchline's case writer" as the interviewer. Readers will take the named byline to mean that this person wrote the piece and ran the interview, and the material does not support either. The reply discloses the choice and asks for it to be changed. That makes the issue visible, but the defect is still in the text.
- **Standfirst, "held back the backlog".** The standfirst drops the condition "until the team had reviewed its status definitions". "Held back" is a fair word for "postponed". The body restates the full condition two sections later, so a reader gets the caveat. I class this as a compression, not a defect.
- **Standfirst, "After trialling".** The material does not date the approval. But the manager approved "continued use", which can only follow the trial, and the approval is reported in the same paragraph as the end-of-trial summary. The order is a sound inference, not an addition.
- **Lead, "having chosen Benchline".** The trial was run on Benchline: Benchline configured the statuses and trained the staff. The team chose Benchline "after checking that it could distinguish these statuses", which is a check made before selection. The choice therefore came before the trial. Not a defect.
- **Standfirst, "not the software's".** This is supported: "The workshop defined the statuses … Benchline configured those choices." "The account makes clear" is loosely worded ("the account" could mean either the article or the summary), but it adds nothing false.
- **Terms checked both ways.** Each pair below means the same in this context, in both directions:
  - "repairs awaiting parts" and "instruments waiting for parts";
  - "tell those statuses apart" and "distinguish these statuses";
  - "Vale wrote" and "email interview".
- **Unknown kept apart from absent.** The draft states that repair duration, revenue and customer satisfaction were not measured. It does not report them as unchanged. This is correct.
