# Fenwick says staff could see repairs awaiting parts

After trialling a shared bench schedule, Fenwick Instrument Repairs' manager approved it for new jobs but held back the backlog. The account makes clear that agreeing what each status meant was the workshop's work, not the software's.

By Thomas Barregren

Fenwick Instrument Repairs services brass and woodwind instruments. Before the trial, jobs awaiting parts and jobs ready for work appeared on separate paper sheets. In January 2026 the workshop team began a six-week trial of a shared bench schedule, having chosen Benchline after checking that it could tell those statuses apart. The workshop's manager, Priya Vale, describes what had to be settled and what would be done differently.

## The workshop set the statuses, Benchline configured them

Asked in an email interview what the team had to settle before the schedule was useful, Vale replied: 'We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench.'

The workshop defined the statuses and decided who would update them. Benchline configured those choices and trained four staff in one online session. The team continued to use telephone calls to discuss collection arrangements with customers.

## Trial summary leaves repair speed unmeasured

The workshop's trial summary, dated 23 February 2026, records 38 jobs entered in the schedule. It says staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts. It does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction.

What the summary and interview establish, then, is the team's reported experience rather than a measured improvement in repair speed. Vale approved continued use for new jobs but postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions.

## Vale would spend longer checking the status names

Would Vale make the same choice again? 'I would use it again for new jobs,' Vale wrote. 'I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog.'

A workshop weighing a shared schedule of its own could start where Fenwick had to: by agreeing what each status means, and checking the names, before any backlog goes in.
