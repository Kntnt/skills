# Fenwick says staff could see which instruments awaited parts

A six-week trial of a shared bench schedule from Benchline began at Fenwick Instrument Repairs in January 2026. Here is what the team had to agree before it was useful, what the trial did and did not establish, and what the workshop's manager would change.

By Thomas Barregren

Fenwick Instrument Repairs, which services brass and woodwind instruments, used to list jobs awaiting parts and jobs ready for work on separate paper sheets. For a six-week trial of a shared bench schedule, begun in January 2026, the team chose Benchline after checking that it could tell those two statuses apart. Afterwards, the workshop's manager named one thing to do differently.

## 'Ready for work' needed an agreed meaning

Benchline configured the statuses the workshop had defined, together with its choices of who would update them. It also trained four staff in one online session. Collection arrangements were still discussed with customers by telephone.

Asked by email what had to be settled before the schedule was useful, workshop manager Priya Vale named one status. 'We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench.'

## The summary counts jobs but does not time repairs

The workshop documented the trial in its own summary, dated 23 February 2026. It records 38 jobs entered in the schedule and says that staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts.

It does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction. Like the manager's answers, it reports the team's experience. Neither shows faster repairs or makes a case for every workshop.

## Fenwick kept the schedule for new jobs and held back the backlog

Asked whether Vale would make the same choice again, and what the manager would change, Vale wrote: 'I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog.' Vale approved continued use for new jobs but postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions.

A workshop weighing a shared schedule can start as Fenwick did, by deciding what each status means and who updates it, and then allow time to check the status names before any backlog goes in.
