# Fenwick keeps shared bench schedule for new jobs

Jobs waiting for parts and jobs ready for work used to sit on separate paper sheets at one instrument workshop. Asked about a six-week trial of a single schedule, its manager explains what the team had to agree before the schedule was useful, and what would get an extra week.

By Thomas Barregren

Fenwick Instrument Repairs services brass and woodwind instruments. Its jobs awaiting parts and its jobs ready for work used to appear on separate paper sheets. In January 2026 the team began a six-week trial of a shared bench schedule from Benchline, chosen after checking that it could tell those statuses apart. Workshop manager Priya Vale explains what the team had to settle first, and names one thing to change.

## The workshop set its own status rules

Benchline configured the workshop's choices and trained four staff in one online session. The workshop itself had defined the statuses and decided who would update them. The team continued to discuss collection arrangements with customers by telephone.

Asked in an email interview on 2 March 2026 what the team had to settle before the schedule was useful, Vale wrote:

'We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench.'

## The trial summary leaves repair speed unmeasured

The workshop's trial summary, dated 23 February 2026, records 38 jobs entered in the schedule. It says staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts.

It does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction. What it documents is the team's reported experience, not a measured change in how fast repairs are done.

## Vale on making the same choice again

The manager approved continued use for new jobs, but postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions. Asked whether the choice would be the same again, and what would change, Vale wrote:

'I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog.'

Managers weighing a similar trial can begin with the choices Fenwick made: what each status means, and who updates it.
