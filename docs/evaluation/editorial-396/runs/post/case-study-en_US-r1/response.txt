# Elm Quay pilots one view of repair reports

Elm Quay Housing's maintenance team spent eight weeks testing a shared repair log from Svale Systems in two of its buildings. The company's internal trial note sets out what its figures do and do not show, and maintenance supervisor Maya Lind describes how the team prepared and what she would plan differently next time.

By Thomas Barregren

Before the trial, telephone reports and emails about repairs were stored separately at Elm Quay Housing, which manages 640 flats. In September 2025, the company's own maintenance team decided to test a shared repair log in two buildings. This account follows how the team set it up, what the trial measured and what remains to be decided.

## Why the maintenance team wanted a shared log

Maya Lind, Elm Quay's maintenance supervisor, describes both the aim and who did what: “We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log.”

The team chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair. It also kept telephone reporting open for residents. Svale configured the log and trained six staff members in two sessions.

## Median assignment time was shorter; the note does not credit software

The trial ran for eight weeks. According to Elm Quay's internal trial note, dated December 4, 2025, 31 repair reports were entered, not counting emergencies or work ordered before the trial began.

Median time from report to assignment was two working days during the trial, compared with three in the preceding eight-week period. The two periods had different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software. There are no measurements of costs, resident satisfaction or completion times.

## Maya Lind weighs the trial

Looking back, Lind names where the time went: “We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts.”

Her overall assessment is qualified: “I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation.” Interviewed by email, Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company.

## Expansion awaits a test of the categories on larger repairs

The trial has not yet expanded beyond the two buildings. The team will decide after checking how its categories work for larger repairs.

This account is published by Svale Systems. Operations managers considering a similar trial can read the [implementation checklist](https://example.invalid/svale/checklist).
