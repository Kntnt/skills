## 2. F1: fail

The judged draft is the delivered draft.

1. **Headline: dropped attribution and caveat.** "Fenwick staff could see which instruments awaited parts" states as fact what the source gives only as the content of the workshop's summary ("It says staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts"). The source's closing caveat says the summary establishes "the team's reported experience". The body keeps the attribution ("says staff could see"), but the headline, the most-read line, drops it. The step from reported to asserted is a change of certainty.
2. **Standfirst: changed duration and completion.** "spent six weeks trialling a shared bench schedule" asserts that six weeks of trial took place. The source says only that the team "began a six-week trial" in January 2026, and the summary is dated 23 February 2026. The start date within January is unknown. So is whether the trial ran its planned length before the summary, or ran on after it. "six-week" is a planned length in the source, and the draft turns it into an elapsed one. The lead gets this right ("In January 2026 its team began a six-week trial"), so standfirst and lead now disagree slightly.
3. **Byline: invented attribution.** "By Thomas Barregren." The brief says "No author name … is supplied", and the material names only "Benchline's case writer" as the interviewer. The byline gives authorship, and so implicitly the email interview, to a named person the material does not carry. The reply discloses this and asks for it to be changed. It is still an addition in the delivered text.
4. **Minor shift of scope: "held back the backlog".** The source says the manager "postponed adding the backlog" to the schedule. "Held back the backlog" can also be read as holding back the backlog jobs themselves, which would delay the repair work. The source does not say that. The reading the source supports is available but not forced. This is minor.
5. **Ending: author advice at the edge of the source caveat.** "A manager planning a similar trial can start with the question Fenwick had to settle" is the publisher's own suggestion. It is not attributed to Vale, and it is framed as a starting point, not a claim that the approach works elsewhere. So it stays inside "not … a recommendation for every workshop". Not counted as a defect.

**L1: pass, with blemishes.**

The prose is British English with native syntax in most places ("trialling", "tell those two statuses apart", "held back", "was still discussed … by telephone"). Two passages read as stilted:

The avoidance of pronouns is a deliberate choice, disclosed in the reply. It costs fluency but does not import foreign phrasing. The passages are clumsy, not non-native, so L1 passes.

## 4. Bridges into quotations

**Q1.**

- Bridge: "Asked by email what the team had to settle before the schedule was useful, workshop manager Priya Vale replied:"
- Quotation: 'We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench.'
- **Class (b).** "Asked by email what the team had to settle" gives the occasion (the interviewer's question and its medium), and "workshop manager Priya Vale replied" gives the attribution. The bridge gives no answer. The quotation alone says what had to be settled and why it was hard.
- Second reading: (c), on the grounds that "by email" is a fact the quotation does not carry. I chose (b) because the medium of the question is its occasion, which class (b) names.

**Q2.**

- Bridge: "Asked about choosing again, and what would change, Vale wrote:"
- Quotation: 'I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog.'
- **Class (b).** "Asked about choosing again, and what would change" names the question, and "Vale wrote" names the speaker and the medium. It does not give her answer.
- Second reading: the sentence before it, "Vale approved continued use for new jobs but held back the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions", could be taken as part of the lead-in. Read that way, it partly anticipates the quotation ("new jobs", "backlog") while carrying a fact the quotation lacks: the actual approval decision taken after the trial. That makes it (c) with an echo. I chose the speech-tag clause as the bridge because it stands immediately before the quotation. The earlier sentence is a separate reported fact from the summary.

**Quoted term, not counted.** The ending's "'ready for work'" puts Vale's term in quotation marks inside the author's own question. It is not quoted speech, so it is not counted as a quotation.

**No quotation stands without a bridge.**

- **Counts:** (a) 0, (b) 2, (c) 0.
- **Interviewer utterances:** both interviewer questions the draft paraphrases ("what the team had to settle before the schedule was useful"; "choosing again, and what would change") are in the supplied material, and no question or utterance is invented for the interviewer.

## 4b. Subheadings over quotations

**Q1.**

- Subheading: "## Fenwick defined its own job statuses"
- Quotation: 'We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench.'
- **Prepares.** "defined its own job statuses" names the subject, status definitions, and the workshop as the one acting. The quotation's point is that one status was not obvious, because a part in the building is not a part at the right bench. The subheading does not give that.
- Second reading: pre-echo, because "defined its own job statuses" comes close to "We had to agree what ready for work meant". I chose prepares because the subheading reports that defining took place, not that it was hard or why. The quotation's second sentence, which carries its point, stays unannounced.

**Q2.**

- Subheading: "## Vale looks ahead to the backlog"
- Quotation: 'I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog.'
- **Prepares.** "Vale" names the speaker and "the backlog" the subject. "Looks ahead" signals looking forward without stating what she would do: use it again, and allow an extra week.

**Counts:** pre-echo 0, prepares 2, neutral 0.
