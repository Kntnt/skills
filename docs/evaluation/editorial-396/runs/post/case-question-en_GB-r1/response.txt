# Fenwick reports staff could see instruments awaiting parts

Fenwick Instrument Repairs has approved continued use of a shared bench schedule for new jobs, though not yet for its backlog. Workshop manager Priya Vale explains what the team had to agree first, and what Vale would do differently.

By [author name]

At Fenwick Instrument Repairs, which services brass and woodwind instruments, jobs awaiting parts and jobs ready for work used to sit on separate paper sheets. In January 2026 the team began a six-week trial of a shared bench schedule from the supplier Benchline. The trial left a count, a decision and, from the workshop's manager, a lesson.

## Fenwick defined its statuses before Benchline configured them

Fenwick chose Benchline after checking that the software could tell those two statuses apart. The workshop defined the statuses and decided who would update them; Benchline configured those choices and trained four staff in one online session. Collection arrangements were still discussed with customers by telephone.

In an email interview, workshop manager Priya Vale named what had to be settled before the schedule was useful: ‘We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench.’

## The trial summary counts jobs, not repair times

The workshop's summary, dated 23 February 2026, records 38 jobs entered in the schedule and says staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts. It does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction.

Vale approved continued use for new jobs but postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions.

## The manager looks back on the trial

Asked whether the same choice would be made again, and what would change, Vale replied: ‘I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog.’

Managers weighing a shared schedule can start with Fenwick's question: what does ready for work mean at their own benches?
