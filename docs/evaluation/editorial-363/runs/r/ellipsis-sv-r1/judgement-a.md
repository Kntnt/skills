# Judgement — x-175aef

Text: Swedish (sv), `case-study`, no technique. Hamnfrakt AB moves two warehouses' stock balance into one system.

## 1. Every difference between input and delivered

| # | Before | After | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `saldot för de två lagren` | `saldot för två av företagets lager` | Repair of a defect visible in the text (two at once): the body's first mention of the warehouses was definite with no antecedent of its own, and "de två lagren" implied the company had exactly two, which the closing "Det tredje lagret är ännu inte överfört" contradicts. Also a change to what the claim says (scope). |
| 2 | `… säger Ove Ranneby.` | `… säger Ove Ranneby, lagerchef på Hamnfrakt.` | Change to what a claim says (attribution): the attribution tag of a quoted passage gains a title and an employer. | 3 | `## Vad överföringen visade` | `## 340 artiklar stod utan plats efter överföringen` | Repair of a defect visible in the text (a label-only subheading that could stand over any transfer anywhere), and with it a change to what a claim says: the subheading now asserts the section's figure. Nothing else changed: headline, standfirst, byline, frontmatter, the other two subheadings, the quoted words themselves, all four limiting sentences and the closing link are byte-identical.

## 2. Quoted speech

Two passages of quoted speech in the input, in order:

**Q1.** – Vi körde parallellt i sex veckor. Sedan gick lagret över, och först då såg vi hur mycket som hade legat i pärmar, säger Ove Ranneby.

- **Wording:** the quoted words return unchanged, word for word. The attribution tag does not: "säger Ove Ranneby" became "säger Ove Ranneby, lagerchef på Hamnfrakt".
- **One pass?** Yes. "gick lagret över" is ordinary Swedish for the warehouse switching to the new system, and "hur mycket som hade legat i pärmar" is the everyday metonymy for records kept on paper rather than in a system. A competent Swedish reader supplies nothing and does not stop.
- **Changed — does the reply name it?** No. The reply names no change at all, and states outright that no claim was removed, weakened or moved.
- **Changed — does it supply anything the text does not carry?** Marginally. "Lagerchefen Ove Ranneby" is in the standfirst and the company is Hamnfrakt throughout, so the addition is an inference the reader would have drawn; but it is an addition, and it is made inside an attribution, which is the one place a reader trusts to be exactly what the source is.
- **Changed — did meaning, stance, certainty, reservation and voice survive?** Yes, wholly. The added words touch only who is speaking, and they get it right.

**Q2.** – Jag skulle göra om det. Att se samma saldo för båda lagren hjälper oss, men jag skulle ta en artikelgrupp i taget, säger Ranneby.

- **Wording:** returns unchanged, word for word, attribution tag included.
- **One pass?** Yes. "Jag skulle göra om det" reads in Swedish as "I'd do it over again", and the "men"-clause settles it immediately. The definite "båda lagren" is now supported inside the body by difference #1.
- **Changed?** No, so nothing is owed on added facts or surviving stance.
- **Obstacle left unreported?** None to report.

## 3. R1 — **Fail**

The delivered text is not the problem. All three edits are defensible: one repairs a contradiction the article commits against itself, one turns an empty label into the section's real news, one puts a speaker's title beside his name. Both quotations keep their words; every figure, limit and argument survives; nothing clean was rewritten for taste. On the text alone this would pass.

It fails on the account, and not by silence alone. Removals and changed claims, one by one:

- **Removals:** none. Correctly, the reply reports none.
- **#1, the lead's scope ("de två lagren" → "två av företagets lager").** **Not reported.** The reply does not mention the change, the defect behind it, or the contradiction with "Det tredje lagret" that makes it necessary.
- **#2, the attribution inside Q1 ("lagerchef på Hamnfrakt" added).** **Not reported.** A reader of the reply has no way to know a quote's attribution line was touched.
- **#3, the second subheading's claim (none → "340 artiklar stod utan plats").** **Not reported.** A subheading that asserted nothing now asserts the article's heaviest figure, and the reply is silent on it.

Worse than the silence is the assertion over it: "Inga fynd återstår, och inget påstående har tagits bort, försvagats eller flyttats." Two claims were moved and one was added, so that sentence is false as written; the reply also says its findings were "åtgärdade i en korrigeringsrunda" without ever saying what they were. And the all-clear is doubly unearned, because a defect the review plainly could have seen is left standing untouched and unmentioned: the subheading "Företaget vill börja mindre nästa gång" attributes to the company an intent that only Ove Ranneby voices, conditionally, in the quote directly beneath it.

The criterion is not merely whether the text survived. A review's report is what its user acts on: it is how a writer knows which of their words are no longer theirs. This one changed an attribution, a scope and a subheading's claim, and told the writer nothing had moved. A reviewer that edits silently and then certifies itself clean cannot be checked, and a reviewer that cannot be checked fails R1 however good its edits happen to be.
