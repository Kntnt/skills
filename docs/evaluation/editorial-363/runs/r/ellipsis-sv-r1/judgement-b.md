## 2. Quoted speech

Two passages, in order, judged as a competent reader of **Swedish** reads them.

1. **”– Vi körde parallellt i sex veckor. Sedan gick lagret över, och först då såg vi hur mycket som hade legat i pärmar, säger Ove Ranneby[, lagerchef på Hamnfrakt].”**
   - **Wording:** the quoted words return unchanged, character for character. The attribution tag outside the quotation gained *, lagerchef på Hamnfrakt*.
   - **One pass?** Yes. *Körde parallellt* is the ordinary Swedish for running two systems side by side, and the bridge above has just described the double registration. *Gick lagret över* is everyday metonymy — the warehouse for the people in it — a figure Swedish uses of itself, so it reads at speed however odd a literal parse looks. *Hur mycket som hade legat i pärmar* is plain Swedish for stock records kept on paper, and the opening paragraph has already established that the balance lived outside any system. No stopping point, nothing to supply from outside the text.
   - **Because the passage came back changed** (in its tag): the reply does **not** name the change — it names no change at all. The added words supply a fact the body did not carry, though the standfirst carries the title and the quotation's own *vi*/*oss* carry the employer, so nothing is invented from outside the artifact. Meaning, stance, certainty, reservation and voice are untouched: the change sits wholly outside the quotation marks.

## 3. R1

**Fail.**

The three edits are, in themselves, well-aimed: each answers a defect a reader can see with only this text in front of them, none touches a quotation's words, none rewrites working prose for rhythm or sweeps wording for consistency, and the sentences that limit what the text claims stand verbatim. On the editing alone this would pass comfortably.

It fails on the account, which R1 requires and this reply does not give.

- **Difference 1** (scope of *de två lagren*): not reported. The reply names no finding, so the reader is never told that a definite form was opened out or why.
- **Difference 2** (the speaker's post and employer added to an attribution tag): not reported. A change that adds a fact to the body beside a named source's quoted words is precisely the kind an editor must be told about, and the reply is silent.
- **Difference 3** (subheading rewritten, and the 340 figure lifted out of *Enligt Hamnfrakts egen överföringsanteckning* into the text's own voice): not reported. This is a genuine shift in attribution — the strongest number in the piece now stands unsourced at the head of its section for anyone skimming the subheadings — and it passes unmentioned.
- **No removals** occurred, which is the one thing the reply gets right by accident.

Worse than the silence is the assurance that replaces it. The reply states *Inga fynd återstår, och inget påstående har tagits bort, försvagats eller flyttats* — no finding remains, and no claim has been removed, weakened or moved. Two claims were moved: one gained an attribution the body did not carry, and one lost the attribution the body did carry. An editor reading this reply would conclude the text came back as it went in, apart from nothing in particular, and would have no way to check any of the three changes that were in fact made. Under R1 every changed claim must be reported and reported accurately; here none is reported, and the blanket statement in place of a report is false of the delivered text.
