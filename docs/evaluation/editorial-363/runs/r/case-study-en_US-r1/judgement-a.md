## 2. F1 — pass, with one caution

Every figure, date, exclusion and qualification is carried at the source's scope, time, subject and certainty.

- "manages 640 apartments" = "manages 640 flats": in this context — a housing company's managed residential units — nothing could fall under one term and not the other, in either direction.
- "In September 2025 its maintenance team decided to test a shared repair log in two buildings" carries date, actor, act and scope; *test* renders *to trial* in American English and names the same act, and the draft asserts no start date for the trial itself.
- "records 31 repair reports entered during the eight weeks. The count excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial." Both exclusions kept; the count is given with no evaluative characterisation of its size, which the material could not support.
- "Median time from report to assignment was two working days during the trial and three in the preceding eight-week period. The two periods had different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software." Both figures, the statistic, both periods, the workload caveat, the attribution of the disclaimer to the note and the word *explicitly* are all present.
- Assignment time never becomes completion time: the metric stays report-to-assignment, and "how long repairs took to complete" is named as unmeasured in the next sentence.
- "Nothing was measured about cost, resident satisfaction or how long repairs took to complete" matches "There are no cost, resident-satisfaction or completion-time measurements": the material asserts the non-existence of the measurements, not merely their absence from the note, so *nothing was measured* is not an overreach.
- No causal verb and no causal hedge connects the software to the shorter time; no cost, satisfaction or rescue claim; no helpless customer.
- The reservation survives twice — in "Lind does not recommend Svale to every housing company", with the scope of the negation word for word, and inside Q3's *but*. It is not presented as drawn from the figures.
- "No comparison with another supplier is available" is the material's own sentence; unknown is kept apart from absent.
- Supplier narration is third person throughout ("The supplier it picked, Svale Systems, publishes this account"), first person only inside quotations, and the publishing relationship is disclosed in the lead rather than buried.
- "The log has not yet moved beyond the two buildings" against "The trial has not yet expanded": the following sentence places the decision to go further still ahead, so the open state is preserved and no rollout is denied or promised.
- The link text describes its destination as the brief describes it, and the URL matches exactly.
- Headline: "Elm Quay's shifts shared one repair log" asserts that the sharing happened, which the wanting alone would not support; Lind's "Having one view of the reports helps us" states the shared view as achieved, and 31 reports were entered during the trial. Supported.
- Standfirst: "spent eight weeks putting repair reports from two buildings into one log" asserts no universal quantifier, and "does not credit the software for the difference" states less than the material's "explicitly does not attribute", which cannot manufacture support.

**Caution — the manner of the statements.** The draft attributes with "sets out" and "she says", and nowhere tells the reader that "All interviews occurred by email". *Says* is ordinary English attribution for a supplied written statement and asserts no spoken exchange on a strict reading; but with the channel never named, the piece leaves an English reader with the default picture of an interview conducted in speech, which is a circumstance the material settles the other way. One word — *writes* — or a naming clause would close it at no cost to any other claim.

**Byline.** "By Thomas Barregren" is an attribution the material does not carry; the brief says "No author name supplied." The reply states the name comes from the invocation and tells the user to change the line before publication, so it is disclosed, but on the material alone it is an addition.

**Tense.** "Lind does not recommend" moves the material's past into a standing present. Defensible: the material calls the final quotation "her actual, qualified assessment" in the present.

## 4. L1 — pass

The prose is native American English: plain declaratives, concrete subjects, no strain. "Its own trial note counts 31 reports, measures the time to assignment against the previous eight weeks, and does not credit the software for the difference" is a three-verb series that carries a great deal without a subordinate clause. "What the trial note counts, and what it does not" is idiomatic and does real work as a heading. Nothing imports another language's phrasing — no calqued idiom, no continental sentence shape, no participial pile-up. Register suits an operations manager: informative, unsold, unhurried.

Locale mechanics, judged apart from L1 and noted only for completeness, are consistent with en_US — *apartments*, "December 4, 2025", the comma inside the closing quotation mark.

Reader effect: nothing needs re-reading, and the reader finishes knowing precisely which claims rest on measurement and which on one supervisor's judgement.

Source: identical wording, with the terminal period becoming a comma before the attribution — standard American practice, no effect on meaning. One pass, no stop. The attribution "she says" is the one place the draft's own framing sits at a slight angle to the material's "All interviews occurred by email" — it does not change what she said, but it suggests a channel the material rules out.

**Missing quotations:** none. All three supplied quotations appear, none welded to another, none trimmed of a hedge or smoothed, and no fourth is assembled. Each carries experience the narration does not.

Every passage of quoted speech in this draft is speech an English reader takes on one pass with the source's meaning, stance, reservation and voice intact, the only reservation being that "she says" frames as spoken what the material says was written.
