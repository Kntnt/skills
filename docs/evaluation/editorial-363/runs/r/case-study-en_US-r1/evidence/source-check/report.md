### Checks that produced nothing

- **Causation.** No passage says or hedges that the software shortened assignment time, saved money, pleased residents or rescued the customer. The draft states the two medians, the workload difference and the note's non-attribution, and stops there.
- **Assignment vs completion.** Kept distinct throughout, including in the deck.
- **The customer's reservation.** Survives in the body sentence and in the unedited third quotation.
- **Supplier voice.** Svale appears only in the third person; no "we".
- **Invention.** No fact, figure, name, date, scene, emotion or remembered dialogue appears that the material does not supply; consistent with "All interviews occurred by email, and no physical scene, emotion or remembered dialogue is provided."
- **Evaluative characterisation.** The count of 31 is given with its exclusions and no assessment of its size; the two-versus-three-day gap is given without being called an improvement.
- **Pronouns, person, number and natural gender.** "its" (Elm Quay, third singular) and "it" (Elm Quay, in "The supplier it picked") match the material's own "its"/"It"; "she"/"her" for Lind is supported by the material's "Her final quotation is her actual, qualified assessment", not merely by the referent being identified; first-person forms occur only inside verbatim quotations. No grammatical-gender artefact is read as a natural-gender assertion.
- **Quotations.** All three are reproduced verbatim from the "complete usable quotations" list. Nothing is welded together from separate remarks, no hedge is removed or added, no distinctive wording is smoothed, and no fourth quotation is assembled. Each carries experience the narration does not: the shift-level motive and ownership of the categories; the preparation-versus-entry comparison; the qualified would-do-it-again with its "but".
- **Translation findings: none.** Source and target are both English; the quotations are not rendered across languages and appear unchanged. Outside quotation, the only cross-variety renderings are "flats" → "apartments" and "4 December 2025" → "December 4, 2025", neither of which changes what is named or measured. Reading the draft in American English with the material out of sight, there is no place where a reader must supply what the text does not: no verb whose subject cannot perform it, no time or place adverbial whose event is missing, no reference the text never introduces.

### Considered, reported here rather than as a defect

- **"explicitly" dropped.** The material says the note "explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software"; the draft says "it does not attribute the difference to the software" (body) and "does not credit the software for the difference" (deck). The draft's claim is entailed by the material's, so it is supported, but a reader of the draft cannot tell whether the note carries an explicit disclaimer or is simply silent. This is an editorial question about strength, not a defect: restoring "explicitly" would be defensible, and leaving it out is defensible too. If Finding 1's repair is applied, the sentence can carry it at no cost: "…and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software."

## Findings

### Finding 1 — source support — the workload difference is attributed to the trial note

- **Draft:** "The note records that the two periods had different workloads, and it does not attribute the difference to the software."
- **Material:** "The periods had different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software."
- **What differs:** whose knowledge or assertion it is. The material asserts the workload difference in its own voice and attributes to the note only the refusal to credit the software. The draft makes the note the source of both, so it tells the reader something about the note's contents that the material does not supply.
- **Concrete case, compatible with the material, in which the material's statement holds and the draft's fails:** the two periods' workloads differ by Elm Quay's staffing and job records, which the maintenance team knows and reported in the interview, while the December 4 note confines itself to the 31 reports, the two medians and the sentence declining to credit the software. Nothing in the material excludes this: it never lists what the note contains beyond the count, the exclusions, the medians and the non-attribution, and a note can decline to attribute a difference without stating why. In that case "The periods had different workloads" holds and "The note records that the two periods had different workloads" fails.
- **Opposite direction:** if the note does record the workload difference, both statements hold, so the difference is one-directional — the draft claims strictly more about the note than the material does.
- **Smallest supported repair:** drop the attribution and keep the sentence's structure and both facts —
  "The two periods had different workloads, and the note does not attribute the difference to the software."
  (or, taking the note in with "explicitly": "The two periods had different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software.")

No other findings. Every remaining factual and attributed claim in the draft, including the title, deck, headings and the closing link, rests on a material passage at the same scope, time, subject, modality and certainty, or is the writer's own transition resting on supported material.
