# Elm Quay's shifts shared one repair log

A housing company with 640 apartments spent eight weeks putting repair reports from two buildings into one log. Its own trial note counts 31 reports, measures the time to assignment against the previous eight weeks, and does not credit the software for the difference. Here is how the team set it up, and what its supervisor would change.

By Thomas Barregren

Telephone reports and emails were stored separately at Elm Quay Housing, which manages 640 apartments. In September 2025 its maintenance team decided to test a shared repair log in two buildings. The supplier it picked, Svale Systems, publishes this account.

## How Elm Quay chose the log and set it up

The team chose Svale after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair. No comparison with another supplier is available.

Maya Lind, Elm Quay's maintenance supervisor, sets out what the team wanted and where the categories came from: “We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log.”

Residents could still report repairs by telephone during the trial. Svale configured the log and trained six staff in two sessions.

## What the trial note counts, and what it does not

Elm Quay's internal trial note of December 4, 2025 records 31 repair reports entered during the eight weeks. The count excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial.

Median time from report to assignment was two working days during the trial and three in the preceding eight-week period. The two periods had different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software. Nothing was measured about cost, resident satisfaction or how long repairs took to complete.

Lind's account of the eight weeks puts the weight on preparation: “We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts.”

## Where the trial stands now

The log has not yet moved beyond the two buildings. The team will decide after checking how the categories work for larger repairs, and Lind does not recommend Svale to every housing company.

“I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation,” she says.

An operations manager weighing the same step can read Svale's [implementation checklist](https://example.invalid/svale/checklist). It is a document to read, not a booking or a trial.
