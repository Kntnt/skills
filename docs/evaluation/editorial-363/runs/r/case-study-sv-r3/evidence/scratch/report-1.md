# Source-support and translation comparison — case-study-sv-r3

Draft: `scratch/draft.md` (Swedish). Material: `work/source.md` (English, the entire package).
Context: `scratch/checker-context.md`. Byline `Av Thomas Barregren` comes from the invocation, not the
material; its absence from the material is no finding, per the supplied byline note.

Method: every factual and attributed claim in the draft — title, summary, headings, body, quotations,
the closing disclosure and the link — is set beside the source passage it rests on, quoted in the
source's own words. Where the two differ, a concrete case compatible with the material is named in
which one statement holds and the other fails. Pronoun person, number and natural gender are
accounted separately from referent identification. General editorial and mechanical review is out of
scope.

## 2. Person, number and natural gender

Identifying a referent is accounted separately from the person, number and natural-gender features
the pronoun itself conveys.

- `hon`, `sin`, `henne`-class forms at lines 21 and 33 — third person, singular, **female natural
  gender**. Supported: the material writes “**Her** final quotation is **her** actual, qualified
  assessment.” Female natural gender is therefore asserted by the material, not inferred from the
  name *Maya*.
- `Det` at line 11, referring to `Underhållsteamet` — third person singular, **grammatical neuter**
  agreeing with *-teamet*; no natural-gender assertion. The referent matches the source's “It chose
  Svale Systems”, whose antecedent is “The team”.
- `det` at line 33 (`när det har sett`) — same grammatical neuter, referent `teamet`. No natural
  gender claimed.
- `Vi`, `oss`, `våra`, `jag`, `Den tiden … jag` inside the three quotations — first person plural and
  singular, matching “We”, “us”, “ours”, “I” exactly in person and number. The plural *vi* is the
  maintenance team, as in the source.
- `De boende` (line 19), `de boendes` (line 29) — third person plural, no gender. Source: “residents”.
- `du`, `ditt`-class address at line 37 — second person singular addressed to the reader. This is
  the Swedish default address per the supplied composition guidance and carries no factual claim
  about any person; the brief's audience is “operations managers at small housing companies”.
- `Arbetsledaren` (line 3) — definite common-gender noun, not a pronoun; no natural-gender assertion
  is made before Lind is named at line 13.

No pronoun in the draft asserts a natural gender the material does not state, and no purely
grammatical gender has been read as a natural one.

## 3. Source-support findings

### Finding 1 — the heading asserts a capability the material only says was tested for

- **Draft (line 9, heading):** `Valet föll på en logg som kunde visa status`
- **Source:** “It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each
  repair.” Also: “No comparison with another supplier is available.”
- **What differs:** modality and state of knowledge. The source reports a test and withholds its
  result — *whether* it could. The heading reports the result as established. The body sentence
  immediately below gets this right (`efter att ha prövat om loggen kunde visa status`), which makes
  the heading the sole place the upgrade happens; and the base contract holds this boundary “in the
  title, summary, headings and body alike”.
- **Case in which the source holds and the draft fails:** the team tests the log against status
  display, finds it shows status only for the categories it designed itself and not for repairs
  logged against a building rather than a flat, and chooses Svale anyway — there being no compared
  alternative on the table. Every sentence of the material remains true; the heading's *kunde visa
  status* is false. Nothing in the material excludes this: it states only that the test happened and
  that the choice followed it.
- **Case in the other direction:** the log does show status for everything entered, but the team's
  test never got that far and it chose Svale on the training offer. The heading holds; “after testing
  whether the log could show the status” — as the ground of the choice — is not what happened.
- **Smallest supported repair:** `Valet föll på Svale Systems efter en prövning av statusvisningen`
  — keeps the section's subject and drops only the asserted outcome.

### Finding 2 — the link's framing describes a destination the material does not

- **Draft (line 37):** `Vill du veta vad ett eget test skulle kräva, kan du läsa [Svale Systems
  checklista för införande](https://example.invalid/svale/checklist).`
- **Source:** “The optional next step is the supplied implementation checklist at
  https://example.invalid/svale/checklist; it is a document to read, not a consultation booking or a
  product trial.”
- **What differs:** the thing named. The material names an *implementation* checklist; the framing
  sentence promises the reader what *ett eget test* — a trial of their own — would require. The link
  text itself (`checklista för införande`) is accurate; the sentence around it is not. The brief
  makes this an explicit judging point: “whether the suggested link accurately describes its
  destination”.
- **Case in which the source holds and the draft fails:** the checklist is a rollout document —
  contract, data migration, category workshop, training, go-live — written for a company that has
  already decided, and says nothing about scoping or running an eight-week trial in two buildings. A
  reader who followed the draft's promise stops, because what the page requires is an implementation,
  not a test. This is fully compatible with the material, which calls it an implementation checklist
  and describes it no further.
- **Case in the other direction:** were the page in fact a trial-design guide, the draft's sentence
  would hold and the material's “implementation checklist” would be the wrong name for it.
- **Smallest supported repair:** `Vill du veta vad ett införande skulle kräva, kan du läsa …` — one
  word, and the sentence then describes the destination the material supplies.

### Finding 3 — a sequence turned into a determinant, and the deciding party dropped

- **Draft (line 31, heading):** `Nästa steg avgörs av de större reparationerna`
- **Source:** “The trial has not yet expanded; the team will decide after checking how the categories
  work for larger repairs.”
- **What differs:** causation and subject. The material puts the check *before* the decision in time
  and names the team as the party who decides. The heading makes the larger repairs the thing that
  decides, i.e. that the outcome turns on how they go. The body sentence under it is faithful
  (`teamet avgör frågan när det har sett …`); the heading is where the shift occurs.
- **Case in which the source holds and the draft fails:** the team checks the categories, finds they
  work poorly for larger repairs, and expands to the remaining buildings anyway because the shift
  handover matters more to it than category fit. The material's sentence holds exactly — the team
  decided after checking — while the heading's *avgörs av de större reparationerna* is false of that
  decision. Nothing in the material excludes it; the material says when the team will decide, not
  what will decide it.
- **Case in the other direction:** a supplier deadline forces the expansion decision before any check
  is made, but on the larger-repair evidence gathered so far; the heading's dependence holds while
  “will decide after checking” fails.
- **Smallest supported repair:** `Nästa steg avgörs efter en titt på de större reparationerna` — or,
  keeping the acting party the customer as the brief requires, `Teamet avgör nästa steg efter de
  större reparationerna`.

### Finding 4 — the title states as the publisher's fact what the material carries as the customer's assessment, and widens it

- **Draft (title):** `Gemensam logg gav överblick i två hus`
- **Source:** “Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for
  preparation.” (Lind); “The team wanted staff on different shifts to see the same information.”;
  “It excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial.”
- **What differs:** whose assertion it is, and the thing measured. The material supports *one view of
  the reports*, present tense, as Lind's own qualified assessment; the title asserts, unattributed
  and in the past tense, that the log produced *överblick i två hus* — an overview of the two
  buildings. The causal verb *gav* needs causal support for the thing it names, and the material's
  causal support reaches only as far as the reports that were entered.
- **Case in which the source holds and the draft fails:** the log carries the 31 non-emergency
  reports, while emergencies and work ordered before the trial are handled outside it — exactly the
  exclusions the note records. An evening-shift supervisor opening the log sees the reports but not
  the two buildings' maintenance picture; *en samlad bild av anmälningarna* is there, *överblick i två
  hus* is not. Lind's quotation stays true throughout. The material never says the excluded work was
  visible in the log.
- **Case in the other direction:** a log that gives a building-level overview but that Lind, on the
  eight-week evidence, does not say helps — the title holds, her quotation is not what she said.
- **Smallest supported repair:** `Gemensam logg samlade felanmälningarna i två hus` — states what the
  material establishes, keeps the shared log as the subject and the two buildings as the scope, and
  drops only the benefit the material has solely as Lind's.

### Finding 5 — non-availability rendered as non-existence

- **Draft (line 11):** `Någon jämförelse med en annan leverantör finns inte.`
- **Source:** “No comparison with another supplier is available.”
- **What differs:** whose knowledge. “Is available” speaks about what this material has to offer;
  *finns inte* states that no such comparison exists anywhere.
- **Case in which the source holds and the draft fails:** the maintenance team did put a second
  supplier's demo log side by side with Svale's in an internal note that was not handed over for this
  case. The comparison exists; it is not available. The material's sentence is true, the draft's is
  false. Nothing supplied excludes this — the material says the comparison is unavailable, not that
  none was made, and the base contract holds that “what is unknown or unclaimed is not thereby known
  not to have happened.”
- **Case in the other direction:** none is needed for the finding, but: a comparison that exists and
  is available yet was never consulted would falsify the source sentence while leaving *finns inte*
  equally false — the two are not interchangeable in either direction.
- **Smallest supported repair:** `Någon jämförelse med en annan leverantör redovisas inte.`

### Finding 6 — *ärende* is wider than “repair”

- **Draft (line 11):** `efter att ha prövat om loggen kunde visa status för varje ärende`
- **Source:** “after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair”
- **What differs:** the thing named. In this context an *ärende* in a *logg för felanmälningar*
  includes a reported fault that turns out to need no repair; a *repair* does not. A term carried
  into another language names the same thing only where nothing here could fall under one and not the
  other, and something can.
- **Case in which the source holds and the draft fails:** the log shows a status line for each repair
  it carries, but a report closed as “no fault found” drops out of the status view without one. The
  source's sentence is satisfied; *status för varje ärende* is not.
- **Smallest supported repair:** `… om loggen kunde visa status för varje reparation`.

## 4. Translation findings (quotations)

Each of the three quotations was read in Swedish alone, with the English out of sight, before being
set beside the source.

- `— Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var våra;
  Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen.` Read cold: no verb whose subject cannot perform it, no
  adverbial whose event is missing, no reference the passage has not introduced — *Svale* and *loggen*
  are both introduced at line 11. Idiomatic spoken Swedish, not English syntax. Against the source:
  meaning, stance and certainty match; the semicolon's two-part shape, which is the speaker's, is kept.
- `— Vi lade mer tid på att enas om kategorierna än på att registrera de första anmälningarna. Den
  tiden skulle jag avsätta innan starten i nästa hus.` Read cold: *Den tiden* points back to the time
  spent agreeing, which the previous sentence supplies; *starten i nästa hus* has its event introduced
  by the lead-in *inför nästa hus*. No stop. Against the source: “I would set that time aside” →
  *skulle jag avsätta* keeps both the conditional and the forward-looking advice.
- `— Jag skulle välja att göra testet igen. Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss, men
  jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser.` Read cold: idiomatic, one pass, nothing to
  supply. Against the source: the conditional *skulle välja*, the present-tense *hjälper oss* and the
  *men*-qualification all survive, so the assessment stays the qualified one the material insists on.
  *en samlad bild* for “one view” is an established Swedish way of saying what she said and is not a
  smoothing of a distinctive phrase; “one view” is ordinary English, not an image or a piece of slang.

**No translation findings.** The quotation-level renderings preserve meaning, stance, certainty and
voice in idiomatic Swedish, and none of the three is a welded or assembled quotation. The three
source-language shifts reported above (Findings 4, 5 and 6, and the heading upgrades in 1 and 3) all
fall outside quoted speech and are therefore reported as source-support findings, as required.

## 5. Editorial questions (reported, not scored as defects)

**A. The customer's valuation in the publisher's headline.** Finding 4 is reported as a defect on the
widening of *one view of the reports* to *överblick i två hus*. The separate question of whether a
supplier-published case may state its customer's assessment unattributed in the headline at all is an
editorial one; if the answer is no, the repair is attribution rather than rewording
(`Arbetsledaren: gemensam logg gav oss en samlad bild`), and the material supports that form too.

**B. `ställde in loggen` (line 17, heading).** Read alone, as a heading is, *ställa in* admits a
cancellation reading (*ställa in ett möte*). Because a log is not an event, the configure reading
wins, and the body immediately says *konfigurerade loggen*. Defensible as written; if the heading is
to be read in isolation anywhere, `Svale Systems konfigurerade loggen och höll två utbildningspass`
removes the second reading at no cost.

**C. `Ett bostadsbolag med 640 lägenheter` (line 3).** “Manages 640 flats” is not “has 640 flats”, and
a managing company need not own its stock. Line 7 supplies *förvaltar*, and the Swedish phrase is
ordinarily read as the portfolio handled. Not reported as a defect.
