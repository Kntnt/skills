# Judgement — case study, English material → Swedish draft

## 1. Outcome

**Delivered**, with one disclosed unrepaired finding. **Two comparisons ran.** The first returned six
source-support findings and no translation findings; all six were accepted and repaired. The second
read the repaired text and returned one source-support finding and no translation findings; it was
validated as standing and left in place.

The delivered prose **is identical** to the last prose a checker saw: `delivered.md` minus its
frontmatter is byte-for-byte `evidence/scratch/draft.md` (one leading blank line aside), and the
Markdown block quoted in `response.md` is byte-for-byte `delivered.md`. Nothing was touched after the
second comparison. The pre-repair draft is not in evidence, so report 1's six dispositions can be
judged only from the passages that report quotes and from `validation.md`.

Judged draft: the delivered one.

## 2. F1 — source fidelity

**Pass**, with two items cited below and two sub-threshold notes. The body is tight to an unusual
degree, and every hazard the brief names by name is held:

- No causal claim anywhere. The note's refusal to attribute is carried **twice** — once in the
  standfirst (`skriver inte det på programvarans konto`) and once in the body with the intensifier
  intact (`avstår uttryckligen från att tillskriva`), in the same sentence as the differing workloads
  and in the same paragraph as the figures.
- Assignment time is never turned into completion time. `från anmälan till tilldelning` is the
  measured interval throughout, and `tid till avslutat arbete` appears only in the list of what was
  not measured.
- The count 31 is given with both exclusions (`Akuta ärenden och arbeten som beställts före testet är
  inte medräknade`) and with **no evaluative characterisation** — no *bara*, *hela*, *blygsamma*. The
  criterion's count-size rule is respected, since no comparison is supplied.
- The reservation survives twice: in narration (`Lind rekommenderade inte Svale Systems till alla
  bostadsbolag`) and inside her own final quotation (`men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka`).
- Supplier narration stays third person; the customer stays the acting party (the team decides,
  chooses, tests, keeps the phone line open; Svale only configures and trains).
- Nothing invented: no scene, no emotion, no remembered dialogue, no cost, satisfaction or completion
  figure, no comparison with another supplier, no expansion that has not happened, no date for the
  eight weeks the material does not place in the calendar.
- Unknown is kept apart from absent in the one place it matters most:
  `Någon jämförelse med en annan leverantör redovisas inte` under-claims the material's
  "No comparison with another supplier is available", rather than asserting none exists.

### Cited items

**(a) The byline `Av Thomas Barregren`.** The material states outright: "No author name supplied."
The draft nonetheless attributes the text to a named individual. The name does not come from the
material; `evidence/scratch/checker-context.md` and `response.md` both state that it comes from the
invoking user, and the reply flags it under its own heading and tells the reader to correct it before
publication if the text is to run unsigned or under another name. That provenance is outside my remit
to verify, but it is the one line in the delivered text asserting something the supplied package does
not support, and it is an attribution, which F1 names explicitly. I record it as a **disclosed
deviation with a stated non-material source**, not as a fabrication, and do not fail F1 on it — both
because the run declares it and because it makes no claim about the case.

**(b) The headline `Gemensam logg gav överblick över felanmälningarna`.** This is the run's own
disclosed finding, and it has partial force. What is real: the sentence states the benefit flatly, in
the preterite, unattributed, in the publisher's voice, where the material carries it only as Lind's
first-person present-tense and explicitly *qualified* assessment ("Having one view of the reports
helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation" — which the material additionally marks as
"her actual, qualified assessment", "not an inference from the figures"). That is a shift of author
perspective and of certainty.

What is **not** real, and where report 2 over-reaches: the scope objection. Report 2 argues that the
definite plural `felanmälningarna` covers the repair reports as a class while the material's only
count excludes emergencies. But the source's own words are "one view of **the reports**" — equally
definite. Whatever over-coverage the Swedish definite carries, the English carries too, in the
speaker's own mouth. The emergency-exclusion case report 2 builds falsifies Lind's English sentence
just as readily as it falsifies the headline, which means it is not a difference between draft and
source. The `gav` tense is likewise defensible: the article frames a completed eight-week trial, and
the preterite reports what the log did during it.

So the residue is one thing only — an unattributed publisher-voice statement of a benefit the
customer attests. In a genre whose headline names the customer's result, that is within tolerance,
and the substance behind it is supported by the material's own quotation. **Not a fail**, but the one
line in the text I would not defend as tightly as the rest, and the run is right not to claim
fidelity for it.

### Sub-threshold, recorded

- `Kostnader, de boendes nöjdhet och tid till avslutat arbete mättes inte` renders "There are no cost,
  resident-satisfaction or completion-time measurements". Strictly, *no measurements exist* becomes
  *they were not measured* — absence in the dossier read as absence in the world. The material's
  phrasing here is flat and unhedged, and it marks the availability reading explicitly elsewhere ("is
  available"), so the reading is defensible. Report 2 §5.4 saw this and set it aside on the same
  ground.
- `i två av **sina** hus` (standfirst) asserts the two buildings are the company's own; the material
  says only "in two buildings", for a company that manages 640 flats. A near-certain implicature, not
  a claim the material contradicts.
- `Telefonanmälningar lagrades för sig, e-post för sig` carries no lexical *tidigare* for the source's
  "Previously". The preterite plus the following sentence's September 2025 supply the before-state; a
  Swedish reader does not take it as describing the trial period.

### Translated terms, checked both ways

- `felanmälningar` ↔ "repair reports": in a housing-maintenance log, both cover a reported fault;
  neither admits something the other excludes in this context. Clean.
- `tilldelning` ↔ "assignment": clean, and kept distinct from `avslutat arbete` / "completion".
- `förvaltar` ↔ "manages": exact.
- `notering` ↔ "note": clean; ownership (`bolagets egen`, `intern`) carried.
- `status för varje **reparation**` ↔ "the status of each repair": exact after repair. The pre-repair
  `varje ärende` was genuinely wider — an *ärende* in a `logg för felanmälningar` includes a report
  that turns out to need no repair, and a *repair* does not. Repairing it was right.
- `införande` ↔ "implementation": exact; the link text names the destination the material names.
- `arbetsledare` ↔ "maintenance supervisor": with `för underhållet` appended, clean.

## 3. G2 — required parts, distinct jobs

**Pass.**

- **Headline** — `Gemensam logg gav överblick över felanmälningarna`. Names the subject and the
  result. (Its fidelity is discussed above; as a *part*, it does its job.)
- **Standfirst** — carries scale (640 flats), the intervention, the duration, the one measured
  comparison *with its caveat in the same breath*, and the supervisor's qualified verdict. It does a
  standfirst's job: it tells a reader what the case shows and what it does not.
- **Byline** — present as `Av Thomas Barregren`. Formally satisfied; see F1(a).
- **Lead** — `Telefonanmälningar lagrades för sig, e-post för sig.` opens on the problem in six words,
  then names the company, the actor, the month and the documenting note. A genuinely good first line
  for operations managers, who recognise the situation immediately.
- **Sections** — four, each with a distinct job: why Svale was chosen and on what test; what the
  supplier did and what the residents kept; the figures and their limits; the undecided expansion.
  No section repeats another.
- **Ending** — the publisher disclosure (`Texten publiceras av Svale Systems och är inte oberoende
  journalistik`) followed by a call to action built from the one supplied route, described as what it
  is: a checklist to read. No booking, no demo, no trial.

Case-account specifics: **situation** (separate storage, shifts unable to see each other's work),
**action** (the team decided, designed its own categories, tested the status display, kept telephone
reporting open), **results** (31 reports with exclusions; 2 vs 3 working days with the workload caveat
and the non-attribution; the list of what was not measured), **appraisal** (Lind's final quotation
plus her refusal to recommend Svale to every housing company). Publisher stance is truthful and
unhedged. The customer is the acting party throughout.

**One weakness, not a fail.** The lead overlaps the standfirst heavily for a 411-word text: *640
lägenheter*, *gemensam logg för felanmälningar*, *två … hus* and *åtta veckor* all appear twice within
four sentences. The lead earns its place on what it adds — the before-state, the company name, the
team as actor, September 2025, the note — but a reader feels the repetition. No checker raised it; it
falls outside a source-support remit.

## 4. L1 — Swedish prose

**Pass**, and comfortably. This reads as Swedish written by a Swede, not as English rendered.

- **V2 is correct everywhere**, including where an English draft normally fails: `Hos Elm Quay
  Housing, …, bestämde underhållsteamet i september 2025 …`; `Två arbetsdagar var mediantiden …`;
  `Vill du gå vidare, är nästa steg att läsa …`; and inside a quotation, `Den tiden skulle jag
  avsätta …`.
- **Compounds are solid**: *felanmälningar*, *underhållsteamet*, *telefonanmälningar*,
  *arbetsbelastning*, *bostadsbolag*, *arbetsledare*. None split.
- **Double definiteness is intact**: *de åtta veckorna före*, *de första anmälningarna*, *de boendes
  nöjdhet*, *den 4 december 2025*.
- **s-passive where Swedish uses it**: *Loggen prövades*, *registrerades 31 felanmälningar*, *Testet
  har ännu inte utvidgats*, *Nästa steg avgörs* (in the repaired form, *Utvidgningen är inte avgjord*).
- **Genitive without apostrophe**: *bolagets*, *programvarans*, *Svale Systems checklista* — the last
  correctly taking nothing after the *s*.
- **Speech dashes**, not quotation marks, for all three interview answers: the Swedish default.
- **Native idiom rather than translation**: `skriver inte det på programvarans konto` for "does not
  attribute … to the software" is an idiom a Swedish writer reaches for and an English one cannot
  produce by transfer; so are `vad teamet var ute efter`, `ringa in felen`, `dessförinnan`, and
  `avstår uttryckligen från att tillskriva`. `en samlad bild av anmälningarna` for "one view of the
  reports" is the Swedish way to say it.
- **No calque, no stranded *man***. The generic pronoun never appears; every sentence has a real
  subject (the team, the company, the note, Lind, the residents). `du` is used for the reader, as
  Swedish prose does.
- Sentences are short and lightly subordinated, which is what Swedish wants.

The one thing I would query is `göra om testet` in the standfirst, which admits a faint *redo it
properly* reading beside the intended *do it again*; the following `med en vecka till för
förberedelser` settles it, and Lind's own quotation says `göra testet igen` outright. Not a defect.

## 5. Quoted speech

Three passages, in order. The material offers three complete usable quotations and **all three are
used, whole**; none is missing, none is welded, no fourth is invented.

**Q1.** `— Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var
våra; Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen.`
Renders: "We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories
were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log."
*One pass?* Yes. `kvällsskiftet`/`morgonskiftet` for the people on those shifts is a metonymy Swedish
uses itself, so it reads without a stop however it parses literally. `dem` takes `kategorierna`,
supplied by the preceding clause; `Svale` and `loggen` are both introduced by the article above.
Nothing to supply.
*Substance?* Intact. The want stays a want, not an outcome. The emphatic ownership of `Kategorierna
var våra` keeps her stress, and Svale's role stays the narrow one she gave it — `hjälpte oss att lägga
in dem`, not *skapade* or *valde*. Her two-part semicolon shape survives.

**Q2.** `— Vi lade mer tid på att enas om kategorierna än på att registrera de första anmälningarna.
Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan starten i nästa hus.`
Renders: "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set
that time aside before the next building starts."
*One pass?* Yes. The fronted object `Den tiden` with V2 inversion is ordinary Swedish emphasis, not a
stumble; `Den tiden` points back to the time the previous sentence has just measured; `starten i nästa
hus` nominalises the source's clause into a phrase whose event the lead-in (`inför nästa hus`) has
already set up. Nothing to supply.
*Substance?* Intact. The comparison of the two expenditures, the conditional `skulle`, and the
forward-looking remedy all survive. `avsätta` is the exact Swedish for *set aside* in this sense, and
nothing turns her own resolution into advice to anyone else.

**Q3.** `— Jag skulle välja att göra testet igen. Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss,
men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser.`
Renders: "I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would
leave an extra week for preparation."
*One pass?* Yes. Idiomatic throughout; the infinitive subject `Att ha …` is ordinary Swedish, and the
`men`-clause lands where a Swedish reader expects the reservation.
*Substance?* Intact, and this is the one that mattered most. The conditional `skulle välja`, the plain
present `hjälper oss` (not upgraded to a result), and the `men`-qualification are all preserved, so
the *qualified* assessment the material insists on — and insists is not an inference from the figures
— survives exactly. `en samlad bild av anmälningarna` neither widens "one view of the reports" into a
claim about outcomes nor smooths a distinctive phrase, since "one view" is ordinary English.

**In one sentence:** every passage of quoted speech in this draft is speech a Swedish reader takes on
one pass, with the source's meaning, stance, certainty, reservation and voice intact.

## 7. Delivery

**A valid delivery.**

The text is faithful to a demanding package that was built to bait four specific failures — causal
attribution of the shorter assignment time, assignment-time-into-completion-time, loss of the
customer's reservation, and a link described as something it is not — and it avoids all four. Every
usable quotation is used whole and reads as native Swedish speech. The one finding left standing is a
headline that states an attested benefit in the publisher's voice; it is half-right at most, it is a
one-line edit for the user, and the run neither hid it nor claimed fidelity it had not established.

The reason given for not repairing it is the right one: with the second and final comparison spent,
repairing the headline would have shipped a line no comparison had read, trading a disclosed and
minor over-claim for an undisclosed and unchecked one. Delivering the exact text the last comparison
read, with the disagreement stated on top, is the honest trade.

Two things the user must act on before publication, both of which the reply itself names: the byline
carries a name the material does not supply, and the headline is the reply's own open question.
