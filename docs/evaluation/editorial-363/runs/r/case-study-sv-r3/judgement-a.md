## 2. F1 — source support: **fail**, on one passage

The draft is source-tight almost everywhere. One passage is not.

**The headline: `# Gemensam logg gav överblick över felanmälningarna`.**

Three shifts happen at once in that line:

- *Whose assertion.* The material carries the benefit only as Maya Lind's own assessment — "Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation" — and the material states expressly that this is "her actual, qualified assessment. It is not an inference from the figures." The headline states it flatly, unattributed, in the publisher's voice. Since the publisher is the supplier, the supplier is made to assert its customer's hedged view as fact.
- *Modality and tense.* "helps us", present tense and hedged, becomes `gav`, a preterite resultative. `gav` is a causal verb and needs causal support for the thing it names; the material supports an intention ("The team wanted staff on different shifts to see the same information"), a capability that was only *tested for* ("after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair"), and Lind's present-tense assessment. It supports no finished effect in the publisher's voice.
- *Scope, in both directions.* `överblick över felanmälningarna` — definite plural — is the fault reports as a class. The material's only count is 31 entered reports that "excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial". The material never says every report reached the log, and "what is unknown or unclaimed is not thereby known not to have happened": emergencies taken by telephone and dispatched outside the log are entirely compatible with every sentence supplied. Term check in the other direction as well: `överblick` (a grasp of the whole picture) is wider than "one view of the reports" (a single place where the reports are seen); a claim can fall under `överblick över felanmälningarna` without anything in the material supporting it, and Lind's "one view" reaches only the reports that were entered. The draft's own rendering of the same idea inside the quotation — `en samlad bild av anmälningarna` — is the accurate one.

Everything else checked and found supported (the full list, since a fail on one line should not be read as a general finding):

- `640 lägenheter`, `två av sina hus`, `åtta veckor`, `september 2025`, `4 december 2025`, `sex medarbetare`, `två tillfällen`, `31 felanmälningar`, `två arbetsdagar … mot tre` — all as the material has them, with the two exclusions attached to the count and not to the log's coverage.
- `Mediantiden … var då kortare` states a comparison and no cause; `bolagets egen notering skriver inte det på programvarans konto` and `noteringen avstår uttryckligen från att tillskriva programvaran skillnaden` carry the note's explicit non-attribution twice, with `uttryckligen` intact. Assignment time is never turned into completion time — `tid till avslutat arbete` appears only among the things not measured.
- `Någon jämförelse med en annan leverantör redovisas inte` under-claims "is available" and cannot overstate.
- `om loggen kunde visa status för varje reparation` keeps the modality (*whether* it could) and uses `reparation`, not the wider `ärende`.
- Lind's reservation survives twice: `Lind rekommenderade inte Svale Systems till alla bostadsbolag` carries the same not-all negation scope as "did not recommend Svale to every housing company", and the reservation is in her own words in the closing quotation.
- `Testet har ännu inte utvidgats` and `Utvidgningen är inte avgjord än` keep unknown apart from absent — not expanded yet, not decided against.
- The disclosure `Texten publiceras av Svale Systems och är inte oberoende journalistik` matches "supplier-published material; it is not independent journalism".
- The link: `Vill du gå vidare, är nästa steg att läsa [Svale Systems checklista för införande](https://example.invalid/svale/checklist)` describes a document to read, carries the optionality of "optional next step" in the conditional clause, and promises no booking, demo or trial. The URL is the supplied one.

Two borderline items I weighed and did not count as failures, both worth naming:

- `Kostnader, de boendes nöjdhet och tid till avslutat arbete mättes inte.` The material says "There are no cost, resident-satisfaction or completion-time measurements." *Mättes inte* asserts that the measuring did not happen; the source sentence could in principle mean only that no such measurements are at hand. The material marks the availability reading explicitly where it means it ("No comparison with another supplier **is available**"), so the flat "There are no …" reads as the world. Defensible, but it is the one place where the draft resolves an ambiguity in the stronger direction.
- The byline `Av Thomas Barregren` names a person the material does not; the brief says "No author name supplied." The evidence's checker context settles it as coming from the invocation, and the reply tells the user in plain terms that the line carries their own name and must be corrected before publication if the piece is to run unsigned or under another name. Not scored as an F1 defect, but it is an attribution that stands outside the material and only the disclosure makes it honest.

## 3. G2 — required parts: **pass**

All required parts are present, in order, each doing its own job: headline (line 8), standfirst (line 10), byline (line 12), lead (line 14), four sections (lines 16–42) and an ending (line 44).

- **Situation:** `Telefonanmälningar lagrades för sig, e-post för sig` plus `Underhållsteamet ville att personal på olika skift skulle se samma uppgifter`.
- **Action, with the customer acting throughout:** the team decides (`bestämde underhållsteamet`), tests and chooses (`Det valde Svale Systems efter att ha prövat …`), and keeps the telephone channel open (`Underhållsteamet lät de boende fortsätta anmäla fel per telefon`). The supplier appears only in third person and only doing what the material gives it — `Svale Systems konfigurerade loggen och utbildade sex medarbetare vid två tillfällen`. No sentence has the supplier rescuing a helpless customer.
- **Results:** the section under `Noteringen förklarar inte skillnaden med programvaran` gives the medians, the differing workloads, the note's refusal to attribute, the count with its exclusions, and what was not measured. The heading itself is a piece of honest work — it takes the one line a supplier-published case is most tempted to spin and states the refusal as the heading.
- **Appraisal:** Lind's closing quotation plus `Lind rekommenderade inte Svale Systems till alla bostadsbolag`.
- **Publisher stance:** stated, unhedged, in the close.
- **Call to action:** built from the one supplied offer, and described as what it is.

Weakness, not a failure: the standfirst and the lead overlap more than they should in a 400-word piece — 640 flats, a shared fault-report log, two buildings and eight weeks appear in both. Each part still carries something the other does not (the standfirst has the median comparison, the non-attribution and the supervisor's verdict; the lead has the before-state, the company's name and the two dates), so the parts remain distinct jobs; the reader pays about one sentence for the repetition.

The three quotations each carry experience the narration does not: the shift-to-shift purpose in her own terms, the time actually spent agreeing categories against entering reports, and her own qualified verdict. None of them restates a neighbouring sentence.

## 4. L1 — language: **pass**

The draft reads as Swedish written by a Swede, not as English rendered into Swedish.

- V2 is respected everywhere, including the places an English-drafted text loses it: `Hos Elm Quay Housing, … bestämde underhållsteamet …`, `Två arbetsdagar var mediantiden …`, `Den tiden skulle jag avsätta …`, `Vill du gå vidare, är nästa steg att läsa …` (a V1 conditional, ordinary Swedish, not a dropped *om*).
- Compounds are whole: `felanmälningar`, `underhållsteamet`, `arbetsbelastning`, `kvällsskiftet`, `morgonskiftet`, `telefonanmälningar`. Double definiteness holds: `de åtta veckorna före`, `de första anmälningarna`, `de större reparationerna`.
- The s-passive is used where Swedish uses it (`lagrades`, `registrerades`, `prövades`, `publiceras`), `du` is the address, and `man` appears nowhere — every sentence has a real subject.
- Idiom is native rather than translated: `skriver inte det på programvarans konto` is a Swedish idiom, not a calque; `avsätta tid`, `enas om kategorierna`, `ringa in felen`, `en samlad bild` are all what a Swedish writer reaches for. Sentences are short and stay out of long relative constructions, which is what Swedish tolerates.
- Reader effect: the results section in particular reads plainly and unhurriedly, and the honesty of `Noteringen förklarar inte skillnaden med programvaran` lands as confidence rather than hedging.

One blemish: `Det valde Svale Systems efter att ha prövat …` opens a sentence with `Det`, where a fronted-object reading (*that*, Svale Systems chose) is formally available and where sentence-initial `Det` is first taken as an expletive. The following non-finite clause and the absence of any propositional antecedent resolve it, and a reader recovers within the sentence, but `Teamet valde Svale Systems …` would cost nothing and remove the hesitation. Report 2 saw this (§5.2) and set it aside as a source-support question, which it is; as a matter of prose it was left standing. It is not enough to fail L1.

## 5. Quoted speech

**Q1 — line 22.** `— Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var våra; Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen.` Renders: "We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log."
*One pass:* yes. `kvällsskiftet … se` is a metonymy Swedish uses itself (a shift sees, hears, hands over), so it needs no unpacking; `dem` has `Kategorierna` one clause back; `loggen` and `Svale` are both introduced in the section above. Nothing to supply.
*Substance:* meaning, tense sequence (`redan hade gjort`), the emphatic ownership of `Kategorierna var våra`, and Svale's strictly limited role (`hjälpte oss att lägga in dem`, not *skapade* or *valde*) all match. Stance and certainty unchanged; nothing added.

**Q2 — line 30.** `— Vi lade mer tid på att enas om kategorierna än på att registrera de första anmälningarna. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan starten i nästa hus.` Renders: "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts."
*One pass:* yes. `mer tid på X än på Y` is the ordinary Swedish comparative frame; `Den tiden` points back to the time the previous sentence has just quantified; `starten i nästa hus` names an event the lead-in (`inför nästa hus`) has set up. No stop.
*Substance:* the comparison of the two expenditures, the conditional `skulle`, and the forward-looking remedy are intact. `avsätta` is "set aside" in the sense of reserving time, which is what she said; no recommendation is added to anyone else, and no hedge is removed.

**Q3 — line 42.** `— Jag skulle välja att göra testet igen. Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser.` Renders: "I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation."
*One pass:* yes. `Att ha … hjälper oss` is a natural Swedish infinitival subject, and the `men`-clause closes cleanly.
*Substance:* the three things that had to survive do. The conditional (`skulle välja`) keeps it a stated preference rather than an announced plan; the plain present `hjälper oss` keeps the certainty she had about the present, without letting it become a claim about results; and the `men`-qualification keeps the reservation attached to the endorsement, in the same breath she said it. `en samlad bild av anmälningarna` for "one view of the reports" stays on the reports and does not widen into a claim about outcomes — which is exactly the widening the headline commits outside the quotation. `lägga in en extra vecka` for "leave an extra week" is a small idiomatic shift from allotting to scheduling and changes neither the amount nor the stance.

*Missing quotations:* none. The material offers three complete usable quotations and the draft uses all three, whole, none welded to another and no fourth invented.

**In one sentence:** every passage of quoted speech in this draft is speech a Swedish reader takes on one pass, with the source's meaning, stance, reservation and voice intact.

## 7. Stop or delivery

**A valid delivery, with a reservation.**

Valid, because the run does not claim what it cannot support. The reply opens by saying the draft is delivered with a known defect, names the passage, states what the material carries instead, gives both minimal repairs the comparison proposed, and ends the item with `Jag hävdar ingen källtrohet för rubriken` — it explicitly withholds the fidelity claim for the one line that does not earn it, and says the finding is the comparison's allegation for the user to settle. Everything the material did not supply is named in `Var materialet tog slut`, and the delivered prose is exactly the prose the last comparison read, so nothing unexamined reached the user.

The reservation: the delivered text still contains an F1 defect, in its most prominent line, with a one-line repair already in hand. The rule that kept it — no prose after the final comparison — preserved the integrity of the evidence chain at the cost of shipping a headline whose claim the run itself does not stand behind. A reader who acts on the disclosure loses nothing; a reader who skims the reply and publishes the block as it stands publishes a supplier asserting its customer's hedged assessment as fact. The disclosure is what makes this a delivery rather than a delivery that should not have happened, and the honesty of it is doing real work.
