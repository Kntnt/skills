## 2. Quoted speech (English; three passages)

**Q1 — "We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log,"**
- Wording: unchanged, word for word. Only the attribution outside the quotation marks changed (#4).
- One pass: yes. "the evening shift" and "the morning shift" are ordinary English metonymies for the staff on duty; "The categories were ours" is plain predicative possession; "them" has one antecedent. Nothing has to be supplied.
- Unchanged, no stop, so nothing owed in the reply about it.

**Q2 — "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts."**
- Wording: unchanged.
- One pass: yes. The comparative ellipsis ("than [we spent] entering") is a figure English uses itself; "that time" points back within the sentence; "before the next building starts" is everyday shorthand for the next building's rollout.
- Unchanged, no stop. The bridge above it changed (#5), but the change removes an assertion rather than adding one, and her voice and stance are untouched.

**Q3 — "I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation."**

## 3. R1

**Pass, narrowly.**

- **Defects addressed.** Two genuine, text-visible defects were repaired well: both bridges pre-stated the quotations beneath them (#5, #7), and each became a neutral lead-in without a word of either quotation being touched. The body also stopped relying on the standfirst to introduce its speaker (#4) and to say what kind of company Elm Quay is (#2).
- **Removals, both reported accurately.** #5 and #7 are each named in the reply, with the old wording quoted, the reason given (pre-echo), and a correct statement of where the removed assertion still stands — the standfirst and the quotations do both carry the reservation, and the quotation under #5 does say what she would do differently.
- **Changed claims, all reported.** #1, #2 and #4 are listed explicitly. The reply's accounts are accurate, and two of them are volunteered against its own interest: it states that the new headline "puts the organisation behind a willingness the text gives to one person", quotes the closing sentence that bounds it, and notes that the body has the company undecided; and it states that "the maintenance team's supervisor" is "slightly more than the text states". That is the honesty R1 rewards — no change is excused by material the Skill never had, and nothing was invented to fill the missing byline, which is reported as missing.
- **Preservation.** Voice, argument, all three quotations, every figure and every bounding sentence survive untouched: the note's refusal to attribute the median difference to the software, the assignment-not-completion limit with the absent cost and satisfaction measures, and the closing sentence keeping Lind's willingness her own.

**What nearly sinks it.** The delivered text ships a headline that contradicts its own closing paragraph. The old headline was itself loose ("Elm Quay finds value"), so the run did not invent the company-level attribution — but it moved that attribution onto the one claim this text explicitly personalises, at the most-read line on the page, and then left it there. Two of the four new subheadings are also weak in the way the reply says: #8 states nothing about its section and leans on a figure from two sections back, and #9 repeats the sentence under it. What keeps the verdict a pass is that all four residues are found, named precisely, attributed to the round's own repairs, and left for a person rather than smoothed over, while every quotation, figure and limiting sentence came through intact.
