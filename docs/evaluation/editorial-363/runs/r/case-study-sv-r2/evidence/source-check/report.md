# Source and quotation comparison — case-study-sv-r2

Draft language: Swedish (`sv`). Source language: English. Quotations are translated; translation of the quotations is permitted by the material ("Translation of the quotations is permitted, preserving stance and qualification").

Scope: source support for every factual and attributed claim, plus a separate translation check of the three quotations. General editorial and mechanical review is out of scope. The byline "Av Thomas Barregren" comes from the invocation, not the material, and is therefore not accounted for here.

## 3. Quotation check (translation)

Each quotation was first read in Swedish alone, then set beside the English.

**Q1 — "Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var våra; Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen."**
Read in Swedish alone: one pass, no stop. Every verb has a subject that can perform it, *dem* is settled by *kategorierna* in the same sentence, and *loggen* has been introduced by the preceding narration. Beside the source ("We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log."): same meaning, same stance — ownership of the categories asserted, Svale's part kept to help — same certainty, nothing added or removed. No finding.

**Q2 — "Vi lade mer tid på att enas om kategorierna än på att mata in de första anmälningarna. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan vi börjar i nästa hus."**
Read in Swedish alone: idiomatic, V2 correct with *Den tiden* fronted, and *Den tiden* points back to the time just named. No obstruction. Beside the source ("We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts."): the comparison and the conditional *skulle* are preserved. The draft makes the implicit agent explicit — *innan vi börjar i nästa hus* for *before the next building starts* — which the supplied context settles, since it is the team that would start in a next building; both forms presuppose a next building equally, and the conditional *skulle* keeps the remark from asserting that the expansion will happen. No finding.

**Q3 — "Jag skulle välja att göra försöket igen. Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser."**
Read in Swedish alone: one pass, no stop. Beside the source ("I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation."): the conditional, the present-tense benefit and the reservation after *men* all survive in the same order and the same strength. *En samlad bild av anmälningarna* renders *one view of the reports* as the consolidation it names, without widening it into an evaluation. No finding.

No quotation has been welded together from separate remarks, none is a summary presented as words, no hedge is removed or added, and nothing additive appears. All three supplied quotations are used; none is altered beyond translation.

**Translation findings: none.**

## 4. Findings

### F1 — source support: the title (and deck) claim the fault reports as a closed set

- Draft: "Elm Quay samlade felanmälningarna från två hus" (title); "prövade … att samla felanmälningarna från två av sina hus" (deck).
- Source: "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings."; "Elm Quay's internal trial note, 4 December 2025, says that 31 repair reports were entered. It excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial."; "The maintenance team designed its categories and kept telephone reporting open for residents."
- What is missing: support for the totality the Swedish definite plural carries. *Felanmälningarna från två hus* names the fault reports of those two buildings as a set and says they were gathered. The material supports only that 31 reports were entered in an eight-week trial, and it names a class — emergencies — that stands outside that count.
- Case in which the draft fails and the material holds: during the eight weeks, out-of-hours emergency reports from the two buildings continue to be taken on the phone line that was kept open for residents and are written on the duty sheet, never entered in the shared log. Every supplied statement still holds — the note's 31 entries, its exclusion of emergencies, the open telephone channel — while "Elm Quay samlade felanmälningarna från två hus" does not: what was gathered is the non-emergency reports. No supplied statement excludes this case; the material's own exclusion of emergencies makes it a natural reading.
- Converse: if every report including emergencies was entered, the draft holds and the material still holds, so the two are not equivalent in either direction.
- Smallest supported repair: drop the definite in both places — title "Elm Quay samlade felanmälningar från två hus", deck "att samla felanmälningar från två av sina hus". That removes the closed-set claim and leaves the event, the actor, the two buildings and the trial framing untouched. The body's qualifications need no change.

### F2 — source support: emailed answers are attributed as speech

- Draft: "säger Maya Lind, arbetsledare för underhållet" (line 13); "säger hon" (line 15); "säger Maya Lind" (line 25).
- Source: "All interviews occurred by email, and no physical scene, emotion or remembered dialogue is provided."
- What is changed: the manner of the statement, which is a claim in its own right. The material states that the answers were written in email; the draft presents them with an unqualified *säger*, which a Swedish reader takes as spoken delivery unless a written channel is named (the common *säger hon i ett pressmeddelande* names it). Nothing else in the draft tells the reader the answers were written.
- Case in which the draft fails and the material holds: Lind answers the questions by typing them into an email and never speaks the words to anyone. That is exactly what "All interviews occurred by email" describes, and no supplied statement says she also said them aloud; the draft's "säger Maya Lind" then asserts a manner that did not occur.
- Converse: were the answers spoken and later transcribed, *säger* would hold while the material's "All interviews occurred by email" would not.
- Smallest supported repair: change the three attributions from *säger* to *skriver* — "skriver Maya Lind, arbetsledare för underhållet", "skriver hon", "skriver Maya Lind". The speech-dash form stays, as Swedish composition prescribes for a rendered interview answer, and no wording inside the quotations changes.
- Note on strength: Swedish journalism does use *säger* for statements that were written when the channel is named in the same clause. The defect here is the unqualified form, and naming the channel instead of changing the verb ("säger Maya Lind i ett mejlsvar") is an equally small repair.

## 5. Considered and not reported

- "Någon jämförelse med en annan leverantör redovisas inte." — reports less than the source's "No comparison … is available"; defensible as written.
- "Akuta ärenden och arbeten som beställts före försöket är undantagna." — the unanchored *undantagna* is read against the count of 31 immediately before it, which is the source's own anchor.
- "Kostnader, de boendes nöjdhet och tiden till färdigt arbete har inte mätts." — an act not performed rather than measurements not existing; no case separates the two here.
- "Hon rekommenderar inte Svale till alla bostadsföretag." — present tense for a standing assessment the material calls "her actual, qualified assessment"; negation scope preserved.
- "två av sina hus" — *sina* covers a managed portfolio in ordinary Swedish and asserts no ownership the material denies; "before the next building starts" excludes a two-building portfolio.
- "förvaltningens underhållsgrupp" — refers back to Elm Quay as the manager named in the same sentence group; introduces no third party.
- "innan vi börjar i nästa hus" — the agent made explicit is settled by the supplied context, and *skulle* keeps the remark conditional.
- Draft length (about 330 words against the brief's approximate 400) — a length the material supports; not a source-support matter.
