# Judgement — x-e054e5

Text: Swedish (sv) case study, published by the supplier, whose discipline is that it reports
two measurements and explicitly declines to credit the software. Judged on the input, the
reply and the delivered text only. Quoted speech judged as Swedish.

## 1. Every difference between input and delivered text

| # | Before | After | Kind |
|---|--------|-------|------|
| 1 | `# Elm Quay testade en delad felanmälningslogg` | `# Elm Quays arbetsledare ser nytta med delad felanmälningslogg` | **Change to what a claim says** — subject, predicate and modality all move. The subject goes from the company to its supervisor; the assertion goes from an event that occurred ("testade") to a standing evaluation ("ser nytta"). The evaluation is attributed in the headline itself and is carried word for word by her quotation ("Att ha en samlad bild av felanmälningarna hjälper oss"), so it is not stronger than the text. But the input headline was clean and non-repetitive, and the replacement leads a supplier-published trial report with the customer's endorsement rather than with the trial. |
| 2 | `Här är hur teamet lade upp arbetet` | `Här beskrivs hur teamet lade upp arbetet` | Mechanical/locale correction. "Här är hur …" is a calque of English "Here is how …" and is not idiomatic Swedish; "Här beskrivs …" is the Swedish construction. No claim moves. **Not reported by the reply.** |
| 3 | `Teamet ska bestämma efter att ha kontrollerat` | `Teamet ska bestämma sig efter att ha kontrollerat` | Mechanical correction (grammar). Swedish "bestämma" is transitive and wants an object; the intransitive "decide" is "bestämma sig". A real error, correctly fixed. **Not reported by the reply.** |
| 4 | `## Den samlade bilden hjälper Elm Quay, enligt arbetsledaren` | `## Arbetsledaren skulle lägga in en extra förberedelsevecka` | Repair of a defect visible in the text, carrying a change of claim at heading level. The old subheading pre-said the quotation directly beneath it in nearly the same words ("Att ha en samlad bild … hjälper oss"), which is a genuine, visible defect. The replacement asserts instead what she would change; that too is carried word for word by the same quotation ("jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser"). Net effect: the benefit claim leaves the subheading and reappears, promoted, in the title — the reply does not notice that interaction. |
| 5 | `Hon rekommenderade … journalistik. Vill du pröva …` (one paragraph) | the same three sentences as three paragraphs | Change of taste (paragraphing). Not one word changes; the disclosure sentence and the closing link sentence are verbatim. **Not reported by the reply.** |

Nothing else differs. Frontmatter, byline, lede, both body sections, every figure, every
bounding sentence and all three quotations are byte-identical.

## 2. Quoted speech

Three passages, in order. All are Swedish *pratminus* quotations with the attribution clause
(*anföringssats*) set inside or after the quoted matter — the standard Swedish convention,
and read as such.

**Q1 — "– Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort, skriver
Maya Lind, arbetsledare för underhållet på Elm Quay, i en intervju per mejl. Kategorierna var
våra. Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen."**
- Wording: returns unchanged.
- One pass: yes. The interpolated *anföringssats* mid-passage, and the two further sentences
  that continue the same speaker's words after it without a repeated dash, are exactly how
  Swedish sets running quoted speech; a Swedish reader carries through without supplying
  anything. "kvällsskiftet"/"morgonskiftet" as metonymy for the people on those shifts is
  ordinary Swedish. "dem" resolves to "kategorierna" in the sentence before.
- Changed? No.
- Unchanged with an obstacle? No obstacle, so nothing was owed as an unresolved finding.

**Q2 — "– Vi lade mer tid på att enas om kategorierna än på att registrera de första
felanmälningarna. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan nästa hus kommer i gång, skriver hon."**
- Wording: returns unchanged.
- One pass: yes. The comparative keeps its second "på att", so the parallel is explicit;
  "Den tiden" points back to the preparation time just named; "kommer i gång" is the everyday
  Swedish idiom for a building's roll-out starting. Nothing to supply.
- Changed? No.
- Unchanged with an obstacle? No obstacle.

**Q3 — "– Jag skulle välja att göra testet igen. Att ha en samlad bild av felanmälningarna
hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser, skriver Maya Lind."**
- Wording: returns unchanged.
- One pass: yes. Conditional, then present-tense assessment, then the reservation after "men",
  all in one speaker's voice and all with named referents.
- Changed? No.
- Unchanged with an obstacle? No obstacle.

No quotation was touched, condensed, re-attributed or moved, and the reservation in Q3 —
the "men" clause that qualifies the only positive thing she says — stands intact. Its wording
is now, however, partly repeated in the subheading above it (difference 4), where the old
subheading repeated the other half.

## 3. R1 — **PASS**

**Defects addressed.** Two of the five changes repair something visibly wrong: the subheading
that pre-said its own quotation (4) and the two language errors (2, 3), one an English calque,
one a missing reflexive. The paragraph split (5) is cosmetic and harmless. The delivered text
is grammatically and idiomatically better Swedish than the input, and no repair overreaches
into the body.

**Preservation.** All three quotations verbatim. Every figure, date and exclusion verbatim.
Every sentence that bounds what the text asserts stands unaltered: "Någon jämförelse med en
annan leverantör finns inte redovisad", "anteckningen tillskriver uttryckligen inte
programvaran skillnaden", "Kostnad, nöjdhet bland de boende och färdigställandetid är inte
mätta", "Hon rekommenderade inte Svale till alla bostadsbolag", and the disclosure that the
piece is published by Svale Systems and is not independent journalism. The body sections were
not touched at all. Voice and argument survive whole.

**Removals.** None. No sentence, clause, figure, name or qualification was dropped; the reply's
"Inget påstående togs bort" is correct.

**Changed claims, and whether the reply reports them accurately.**
- Difference 1 (the title): **reported, and reported accurately.** The reply states what the
  headline now asserts, that the fact of the trial is no longer claimed there, where that fact
  still stands, that the new assertion is attributed to her in the headline itself and is no
  stronger than her quotation, and — correctly and to its credit — that the median times do
  not support it because the note declines to attribute the difference to the software. That
  last point is the right check and it made it.
- Difference 4 (the last subheading): **reported, and reported accurately.** It names the
  defect, names what the subheading no longer claims, and says where that claim still lives.
- Differences 2, 3 and 5 change no claim, and the reply's claims section is therefore not
  wrong. But they are **not reported at all**: the reply opens "Inga anmärkningar kvarstår"
  and then accounts for exactly two changes, so a reader comparing the two texts meets three
  more it was not told about. That is an incomplete account of the round's own work, not a
  misstatement.

**The reservation.** Difference 1 is the weakest moment. The input headline was clean, and the
stated reason for replacing it is a genre requirement rather than a defect anyone can see in
the text; the replacement puts an endorsement at the top of a piece whose distinguishing
virtue is that it refuses to claim a result. The change stays inside what the text carries
and is attributed, so no false claim enters, and it is declared. It does not fail R1, but it
is a taste-driven move on clean material of exactly the kind R1 warns about, and it is the
one change here a human should be asked to confirm. Nothing outside the findings moved, no
quotation was disturbed, no claim was strengthened. **Pass.**
