## 2. Quoted speech

**Q1 — "Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var våra; Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen."**
- Wording unchanged; only the dash marker before it changed (#4), which is typography, not wording.
- A competent Swedish reader takes it on one pass. "Kategorierna var våra" is ordinary Swedish possessive shorthand for "we made them / they were our own"; "dem" plainly refers back to kategorierna. No point where the reader must supply anything.
- Not applicable (unchanged), and no obstacle to report.

**Q2 — "Vi lade mer tid på att enas om kategorierna än på att registrera de första anmälningarna. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan starten i nästa hus."**
- Wording unchanged.
- One pass. "Den tiden" is a normal Swedish anaphor to the time just named, the time spent agreeing on categories; the contrast clause makes the referent the marked one. The reader supplies nothing. Note that the surrounding bridge changed (#6), which removes a duplication of "nästa hus" outside the quote but leaves the quote's own reference intact and still qualified.
- No obstacle to report.

**Q3 — "Jag skulle välja att göra testet igen. Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser."**
- Wording unchanged.
- One pass. "Jag skulle välja att göra" is slightly padded, but it is idiomatic and reads straight through; the conditional stance, the concession and the reservation are all carried by ordinary means.
- No obstacle to report.

Summary: all three passages return with wording, meaning, stance, certainty, reservation and voice intact. No quoted passage was rewritten, tightened, merged, trimmed or supplied with any event, fact, name or figure. The delivered text introduces no information the input did not carry.

## 3. R1 verdict: **pass** (narrow)

**Removals and changed claims, and whether the reply reports them accurately:**

- #1 title claim removed — **reported, accurately.** The reply names the removed claim, says why it went (a result asserted in the text's voice that only the quote bears), and points out that Lind's own valuation survives verbatim in the closing quote. It also declares the replacement a defect it did not resolve.
- #2 sentence removed — **reported, accurately.** The reply names the sentence, the reason, and correctly states that the attribution moved from the text to the speaker.
- #5 subheading claim removed — **reported, accurately,** including the fact that the underlying fact survives in the body, and including the honest admission that the replacement duplicates the quote beneath it.
- #6 "inför nästa hus" removed — **reported, accurately,** with a correct account of where the presupposition still stands and how it is qualified there.
- #3 bridge rewording — **only implicitly reported.** It falls under the "triple pre-announcement" repair the reply describes, but it is not listed among the changes taken or moved, and it does quietly drop a small framing claim.
- #4 dash substitution (three places) — **not reported.** Harmless and correct for the locale, but it is an undeclared change to delivered text.
- #7 "arbetsbelastning" → "arbetsbelastningar" — **not reported.** A taste edit to a clean sentence, undeclared.

**Why it passes.** Every claim-bearing removal and every change of what the text asserts is reported and reported correctly. No quotation was touched. No claim was strengthened, broadened, re-attributed or supplied from outside the text. Every sentence that limits what the text may be taken to mean — no vendor comparison, the note's refusal to attribute, what is excluded from the 31 reports, what went unmeasured, Lind's refusal to recommend Svale to all housing companies, and the sponsorship disclosure — stands word for word, as the reply claims. The defects it went after were visible in the text on their own terms: the same thought stated three times around the first quote, and the text's own voice presupposing a next house that the closing section says is undecided.

**What holds the pass to narrow.** Change #5 is taste applied to a working part: the old subheading was accurate and named something the section actually says, and its replacement paraphrases the quote below it, which the reply itself files as an unresolved defect. Together with #1, the run leaves the delivered text carrying three defects it names, at least two of which are absent from the input — the text is worse at the title and at that subheading than it arrived. The reply's candour about this is real and counts in its favour, but a reported regression is still a regression. Changes #3, #4 and #7 are undeclared, and #7 in particular edits a sentence that had nothing wrong with it.
