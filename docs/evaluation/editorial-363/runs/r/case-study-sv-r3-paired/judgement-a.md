## 1. Every difference between input and delivered text

After: "Bostadsbolaget tillskriver inte loggen kortare tid". **Change to what a claim says** (subject, polarity and content all change), made as the repair of a defect visible in the text: the old title asserted a result — that the shared log gave an overview — in the text's own voice, while the body carries that judgement only inside Maya Lind's closing quote ("Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss") and elsewhere explicitly declines to credit the software. 2. **Sentence removed.** Before: "Underhållsteamet ville att personal på olika skift skulle se samma uppgifter. Det valde Svale Systems efter att ha prövat…". After: "Underhållsteamet valde Svale Systems efter att ha prövat…". **Change to what a claim says** (a claim in the text's own voice about the team's aim is removed) made as the repair of a defect visible in the text: the same thought stood three times in a row — this sentence, the bridge "beskriver vad teamet var ute efter", and then the quote itself, "Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort". 4. **Quote dash, first quote.** Before: "—" (em dash). After: "–" (en dash). **Mechanical correction (locale form)**: Swedish pratminus is set with tankstreck, the en dash. Benign, and correct for `language: sv`.

5. **Subheading 2.** Before: "Boende kunde ringa in felen som tidigare". 6. **Bridge qualifier removed.** Before: "Lind säger vad hon skulle göra annorlunda inför nästa hus:". After: "Lind säger vad hon skulle göra annorlunda:". **Change to what a claim says** (a presupposition in the text's own voice that a next house exists is removed) made as the repair of a defect visible in the text: the closing section states "Testet har ännu inte utvidgats, och teamet avgör frågan…", so the bridge presupposed in the publication's voice what the text elsewhere says is undecided. 7. **Number.** Before: "Perioderna hade olika arbetsbelastning". After: "Perioderna hade olika arbetsbelastningar". **Change of taste** — a distributive plural. Both forms are idiomatic Swedish; no claim moves.

8. **Quote dashes, second and third quotes.** Before: "—". After: "–". **Mechanical correction (locale form)**, as in 4.

## 2. Quoted speech

Three passages of quoted speech, in order. All three are Swedish pratminus paragraphs; only the dash glyph before them changed, never a word inside them.

**Q1 — "Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var våra; Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen."**
- Wording: **unchanged**, word for word.
- One pass: **yes.** A competent Swedish reader takes it in one reading. "Kvällsskiftet" and "morgonskiftet" as metonymies for the staff on those shifts are ordinary Swedish and read straight through; "Kategorierna var våra" is an idiomatic possessive claim of authorship, and "dem" attaches to "kategorierna" without ambiguity. Nothing has to be supplied.
- Came back unchanged and no obstacle was judged, so nothing was owed as an unresolved finding here.

**Q2 — "Vi lade mer tid på att enas om kategorierna än på att registrera de första anmälningarna. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan starten i nästa hus."**
- Wording: **unchanged**, word for word.
- One pass: **yes.** "Den tiden" picks up the time spent agreeing the categories from the sentence before it, which is the nearest and only candidate; "innan starten i nästa hus" is plain. Nothing has to be supplied. Note that the bridge above it lost "inför nästa hus" while the quote keeps "i nästa hus" — that does not make the reader stop, because the quote supplies the frame itself.
- Unchanged, no obstacle, so nothing was owed.

**Q3 — "Jag skulle välja att göra testet igen. Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser."**
- Wording: **unchanged**, word for word.

- Unchanged, no obstacle, so nothing was owed.

No quoted passage was reworded, cut, merged, re-punctuated inside the quote, or re-attributed. No quote gained any event, fact, name or figure. Meaning, stance, certainty, every reservation and the speaker's voice survive in all three.

## 3. R1 — **Pass**

The run went after defects that are visible in the text alone, and it did not invent a source to justify anything. The four things it moved against are all real and all findable without the absent material: a headline asserting in the publication's voice a result the body declines to credit and only the source's quote endorses; the same thought stated three times in the opening section; a bridge presupposing a next house that the closing section says is undecided; and a subheading covering one of the section's two facts. Everything outside those findings is preserved exactly — all three quotations verbatim, every figure, date and name, and, importantly for a sponsored case study, every limiting sentence: the missing vendor comparison, the note's refusal to attribute, what is excluded from the 31, what was not measured, Lind's refusal to recommend the vendor generally, and the disclosure that the text is published by Svale Systems and is not independent journalism. The reply itself lists those survivals and the list checks out against the delivered text.

**Removals and changed claims, one by one, against what the reply reports:**

- *Headline claim removed* — **reported, accurately.** The reply names it as a removal, says why (the claim stood in the text's own voice while only Lind's quote bears it) and correctly notes the surviving carrier, "Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss".
- *"Underhållsteamet ville att personal på olika skift skulle se samma uppgifter." removed* — **reported, accurately**, including the consequence the reply states plainly: the content survives only as Lind's quote, so "attributionen flyttade från texten till talaren". That is exactly what happened.
- *Subheading "Boende kunde ringa in felen som tidigare" removed* — **reported, accurately**, with the correct surviving body sentence quoted.
- *Bridge qualifier "inför nästa hus" removed* — **reported, accurately**, with the right reason (the contradiction with the closing section) and the right note that the presupposition remains where it is attributed.
- *New subheading's Lind-attributed claim added* — reported indirectly: it is not in the removals list, but unresolved finding 2 quotes the new heading in full, names it as the quote's first sentence rewritten, and says so as a defect. Since the claim is the quote's own and is marked "säger Lind", nothing is asserted that the text does not carry.

**Where the reporting falls short, without reaching a failure:**

- Two differences are not reported at all: the three em dashes changed to en dashes (4, 8) and "arbetsbelastning" → "arbetsbelastningar" (7). The dash change is a correct Swedish locale form and the plural is a wash; neither touches a claim, a quotation's wording or a hedge, so nothing is hidden by the silence — but an account that says what it changed should have said it.
- The reply asserts "De fyra bristerna som granskningen fann är åtgärdade" without ever stating those four findings as findings. They are recoverable only obliquely, from the removals list and from the causal clauses inside the three unresolved items. A reader of the reply alone cannot cleanly enumerate what was found.
- One change is taste, not defect repair: replacing an accurate subheading (5). The reply's stated reason — that the old heading covered "bara en bisak" — is thin, since the section holds two facts and the heading named one of them. This is the one place where a working element was rewritten to satisfy taste, and it is also the change that did the most damage.

**The self-inflicted damage, and why it does not fail R1.** The delivered text is left with three repetition defects the run created and could not clear: the headline now says the standfirst's second sentence and the third subheading over again, so the non-attribution is stated three times and the headline no longer names what the piece is about; the second subheading pre-states the quote beneath it; and the first subheading now sits directly above its own paraphrase. That is a real cost. What keeps this a pass is that R1 asks whether visible defects were addressed while voice, arguments, quotations and claims outside the findings were preserved, and on that test the run is clean: no quotation was touched, no hedge weakened, no figure or attribution silently moved, and every removal and every changed claim is reported and reported accurately. The run also declares the damage itself, in the text's own terms, traces each new defect to the specific repair that caused it, and hands it to a human rather than papering over it — which is the honest disposition for a budget that ran out mid-repair, not a concealment. A reviewer that had stopped one step earlier would have delivered a better text; this one delivered a worse headline and truthful books.
