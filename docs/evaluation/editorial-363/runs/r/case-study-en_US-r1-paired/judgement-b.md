## 2. Quoted speech

Three passages of quoted speech, in order of appearance. All three are in en_US and are
judged as an en_US reader takes them.

**Q1 — "We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The
categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log."**
- Wording: returns unchanged.
- One pass: yes. "the evening shift" / "the morning shift" are the ordinary English
  metonymy for the people on those shifts and need no unpacking; "The categories were ours"
  resolves against the log just named. A reader carries straight through.
- Changed? No. (Its framing changed; the quotation did not.)
- Unchanged with an obstacle? No obstacle, so nothing was owed as an unresolved finding.

**Q2 — "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I
would set that time aside before the next building starts."**
- Wording: returns unchanged.
- One pass: yes. The elided second "on" ("than entering") is normal English comparative
  ellipsis and is read, not repaired; "that time" points back to the preparation time just
  described; "the next building starts" is the everyday shorthand for the next building's
  roll-out. Nothing has to be supplied.
- Changed? No.
- Unchanged with an obstacle? No obstacle.

**Q3 — "I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I
would leave an extra week for preparation,"**
- Wording: returns unchanged.
- One pass: yes. Conditional then present then conditional, all with the same speaker and
  the same referent; no gap to fill.
- Changed? No.
- Unchanged with an obstacle? No obstacle.

No quotation was touched, shortened, tidied, re-attributed or moved. The speaker's voice,
her reservations ("but I would leave an extra week") and her refusal to generalise all
stand.

## 3. R1 — **PASS**

**Defects addressed.** Every repair traces to something visible in the text and nothing
else: a bridge that pre-said its quotation (2), a dangling "the eight weeks" (5), a false
coordination that manufactured a consequence between two independent facts (7), and an
internal inconsistency in serial-comma use that the text itself set up (6). Item 4 is a
plain locale correction. These are concrete, they are correctly diagnosed, and the repairs
are the minimum that fixes them.

**Preservation.** Everything outside the findings survives. All three quotations are verbatim.
Every sentence that bounds what the text asserts stands unaltered: "No comparison with another
supplier is available", "The count excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial",
the note's explicit non-attribution, "Nothing was measured about cost, resident satisfaction,
or how long repairs took to complete", and "It is a document to read, not a booking or a
trial". No argument was reordered, no voice flattened, no clean sentence rewritten for taste.
The one intervention that is purely taste-driven — the three subheadings — is at least
defensible as a house convention rather than a rewrite of clean prose.

**Removals.** One removal: the bridge clause "sets out what the team wanted and where the
categories came from" (difference 2). It is legitimate — it carried no fact of its own, only
an announcement of what the following quotation says — and the reply reports it exactly,
naming the clause and the reason.

**Changed claims, and whether the reply reports them accurately.**
- Difference 3 is the serious one, and the reply reports it **accurately and in full**. It is
  raised as finding 6, "the one to settle"; it names all three components of the overreach —
  "fell" asserting a movement the text withholds, "a day" dropping the unit, "with the cause
  left open" carrying only one of the two limits — it says plainly that the round's own repair
  created it, and it prescribes the fix. That is an honest report of an irreparable finding,
  not a cover.
- Difference 1 narrows a section heading's scope; the reply reports the rewrite but does not
  note the narrowing. The asserted content is quote-backed, so nothing false enters.
- Difference 8 shifts aspect from future to present; **not reported**. Minor and harmless.
- Differences 4, 5, 6, 7 change no claim. The reply reports 5, 6 and 7 accurately, and
  reports 4 as the single mechanical correction. It does not report 8 as a claim matter,
  which is right, since it is not one.

**The reservation.** The delivered text asserts, in a subheading, the one thing the body
spends two sentences declining to assert. That is a genuine loss of stance and it shipped.
It does not fail R1, because it was not smuggled: the reply puts it at the top of what
remains, explains the damage in the text's own terms, traces it to its own repair, says the
budget stopped the loop there, and hands the reader the remedy. A changed claim that is named,
explained and handed back is the outcome R1 asks for when a round runs out of room; a changed
claim that is silent is not. Nothing outside the findings moved. **Pass.**
