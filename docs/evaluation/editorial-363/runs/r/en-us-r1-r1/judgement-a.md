**Passage 2 — "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts," Lind said.**
- Wording: comes back unchanged, word for word.
- One pass: the first sentence, yes. The second, no — a reader stops at "before the next building starts" and has to supply the elided noun: buildings do not start; what starts is the next building's rollout or trial. English does not carry this ellipsis the way it carries, say, "before the next term starts", so the reader must fill it from the article's account of a two-building trial that may expand.
- Unchanged and a reader stops: the reply does report it, as finding 7, and reports it exactly — it names the same words, names what the reader must supply, and gives the right reason for leaving it: repairing it would put words in Lind's mouth, and the text does not settle the point in her words.
