## 3. R1 — fail

**Fail.**

Removal and changed claim, one by one:

- Bridge claim "gav arbetet en erfarenhet …" removed — **reported, accurately**, with the reason (the writer's voice presupposing a next building) and the surviving conditional pointed to.
- Bridge claim "rymmer ett förbehåll" removed — **reported, accurately**, with the surviving reservation pointed to.
- H2 "Teamet utformade kategorierna" replaced — **reported twice**: in the claims account, and again as an unresolved observation that names the real problem precisely, that a vendor-published case study should not hand the actor's role to the vendor in a subheading.
- New H2 "Skiftena skulle se varandras arbete" — **reported**, as a defect the round created, with the accurate complaint that it covers half its section and pre-empts the quotation.
- "i två byggnader" added to the lead — **not reported**.
- Attribution "underhållsansvariga Maya Lind" — **not reported**.
- New H2 "Teamet kontrollerar kategorierna innan det beslutar om utökning" — **not reported**, and it firms an intention into something present and names the decision's subject.
- "Bolaget" prefixed to the lead — **not reported**.
- The vendor-paragraph reorder — **not reported**.

What is genuinely good here deserves saying first, because it is not small. All three quotations come back verbatim. The summary, the headline and the entire results section — every figure, every exclusion, every caveat — are byte-identical. No fact was invented anywhere. The review twice declined to fabricate: it left the byline empty and said so, and, catching that "Underlaget" enters the text with no referent introduced, it left that sentence untouched on the explicit ground that naming a referent would either invent one or merge two sources the text keeps apart. That is exactly the right instinct, and it is the sharpest single observation in any part of this reply.

What fails it is the account. Five differences are absent from it, and two of them are changes to claims: a scope added to the lead, and an attribution filled out. Both draw only on words already in the text, so the delivered text is not made less true by either — the harm is that a reader is told something else. The claims section presents itself as an inventory ("Ett påstående togs bort … Två påståenden flyttades …") and then lists which limiting sentences survived verbatim, which is precisely the kind of precision that persuades a reader to stop checking. Behind it sit a scope change, an attribution change, a new heading that converts "ska först kontrollera … och därefter fatta beslut" into a present-tense activity with a named object, a paragraph moved, and a word added to a sentence's opening. The criterion asks that every before-and-after claim be compared and every removal and change reported; an inventory that omits two claim changes and states no exceptions does not meet it. That the same reply catches and explains four other findings with real care makes the omission harder to read as a lapse of effort and easier to read as a lapse of method: the changes it reported were the ones it had framed as findings, and the ones it made along the way went unrecorded.
