# Elm Quay values one view of repair reports

Elm Quay Housing’s maintenance team tested a shared repair log in two buildings to help staff on different shifts see the same information. Supervisor Maya Lind valued the shared view, but her assessment came with a reservation: she would allow more preparation time before repeating the trial.

In September 2025, the maintenance team at Elm Quay, which manages 640 apartments, decided to trial a shared log after storing telephone reports and emails separately. The team chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair.

## The team set the log up around its own categories

“We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log,” Supervisor Maya Lind said in an email interview.

The maintenance team kept telephone reporting open for residents. Svale configured the log and trained six staff members over two sessions.

## Preparation took more time than the first reports

Lind also spoke about the categories:

“We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts.”

## The figures cover assignment, not completion

The trial lasted eight weeks. Elm Quay’s internal trial note, dated December 4, 2025, recorded 31 repair reports, excluding emergencies and work ordered before the trial.

The median time from report to assignment was two business days during the trial, compared with three in the preceding eight-week period. The periods had different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software. These figures concern assignment, not completion; there are no measurements of repair completion time, costs, or resident satisfaction.

## Repeating the trial would mean an extra week

Lind gave her appraisal:

“I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation.”

## The decision waits on larger repairs

The trial has not yet expanded. The team will decide after checking how the categories work for larger repairs. Lind’s willingness to repeat the trial is her assessment of Elm Quay’s experience, not a recommendation of Svale for every housing company.

## Findings that remain

All six are unresolved. Five of them the correction round put there; the first it was told not to repair.

1. **The byline is missing.** The anatomy requires one line naming the author, `By <author>` in English. `*Published by Svale Systems.*` names the publisher, not an author, and nothing in the text, its frontmatter or the invocation names one. The measurement confirms `byline — absent`. A byline nobody can name is reported and left unfilled rather than invented, so this needs the author's name from you.

2. **The publisher credit now closes the text.** `*Published by Svale Systems.*` stood directly under the standfirst, where a reader learned before reading the account that the supplier published it. It now sits after the call to action, so that disclosure reaches the reader only once the account and the link are behind them. No finding named this line. The round moved it because, with the byline correctly left unfilled, a second paragraph in the front matter is counted into the lead, and the lead's one-paragraph requirement could not otherwise be met. Restoring the byline is what frees the credit to go back where it was.

3. **Four of the five new subheadings repeat the wording of the sentence beneath them.** `Preparation took more time than the first reports` sits above "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports"; `The figures cover assignment, not completion` above "These figures concern assignment, not completion"; `Repeating the trial would mean an extra week` above "I would leave an extra week for preparation"; `The team set the log up around its own categories` above "The categories were ours". A subheading and the first sentence under it are meant to say different things in different words, and here the reader is told twice and learns nothing the second time. Over the two quotations this also re-creates the defect the round was asked to remove: Lind's verdict is stated before she states it.

4. **`The team set the log up around its own categories` claims more than its section supports.** The section's second paragraph says Svale configured the log; what the team supplied was the categories. The subheading gives the setup to the team, at a strength the text does not.

5. **`Lind also spoke about the categories:` prepares nothing.** It replaced a bridge that pre-said its quotation, and it now carries no preparation at all — the subheading directly above has already oriented the reader, and `also` points back to the previous section's subject rather than into this one. The attribution could sit with the quotation.

6. **The headline and the standfirst turn on the same word.** `Elm Quay values one view of repair reports` above "Supervisor Maya Lind valued the shared view". Headline and standfirst complement each other rather than repeat. Low.

The claim still stands in her own words — "The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log" — and the distinct fact beside it, that telephone reporting stayed open for residents, was kept.

And `*Published by Svale Systems.*` stands verbatim, in the same wording, scope and strength, but at the end of the text rather than under the standfirst — the relocation described in finding 2.

**Nothing else moved.** Both sentences that bound what the text asserts are untouched: the one holding the assignment figures to what they measure ("The periods had different workloads… there are no measurements of repair completion time, costs, or resident satisfaction") and the one holding Lind's appraisal to Elm Quay ("…not a recommendation of Svale for every housing company").
