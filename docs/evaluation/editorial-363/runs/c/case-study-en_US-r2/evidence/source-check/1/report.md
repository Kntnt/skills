## 1. Claim accounting

Each entry gives the draft passage, the supplied passage it rests on quoted in the source's own words, and what differs.

### Title and deck

1. **Draft:** “Elm Quay's shifts shared one repair log” (title)
   **Source:** “In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings.” / “The team wanted staff on different shifts to see the same information.” / “Having one view of the reports helps us” / “The trial has not yet expanded”
   **Differs:** scope. The source restricts the shared log to two buildings of a 640-flat portfolio and states the trial has not expanded; the title states the sharing of Elm Quay's shifts without restriction. → **Finding 1.**

2. **Draft:** “In late 2025 the maintenance team at Elm Quay Housing ran an eight-week trial of shared repair reporting in two buildings.”
   **Source:** “In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings.” / “The trial lasted eight weeks.” / “Elm Quay's internal trial note, 4 December 2025”
   **Differs:** nothing material. The trial is bounded between September 2025 and 4 December 2025, so it falls wholly within late 2025; “shared repair reporting” names the same shared log the body then names outright. Subject, number and scope (two buildings) are carried. No finding.

3. **Draft:** “Thirty-one reports were entered, and the internal note is careful about what they prove.”
   **Source:** “Elm Quay's internal trial note, 4 December 2025, says that 31 repair reports were entered.” / “the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software”
   **Differs:** whose assertion. The figure appears here without the “the note says” frame the source gives it, but the same sentence names the internal note and the body attributes the figure to it explicitly. The characterisation “careful about what they prove” is the author's reading of the note's stated non-attribution, not a new claim about the note's contents. No finding.

4. **Draft:** “The team's supervisor would do the trial again, and says what she would give more time to first.”

   **Differs:** nothing. Modality (conditional “would”), the fact that the extra time is preparation, and the ordering (“before the next building starts” → “first”) are all in the quotations. No finding.

## 2. Person, number and natural gender carried by pronouns

Accounted separately from referent identification.

- “says what she would give more time to first”, “She sums up the eight weeks” — third person, singular, natural gender female, of Maya Lind. The material asserts the same features: “Her final quotation is her actual, qualified assessment.” English marks no grammatical gender on nouns, so “her” there is a natural-gender assertion and licenses the draft's. Supported.
- “its maintenance team”, “its categories”, “itself”, “it set out to get”, “Elm Quay will decide” — third person, singular, no natural-gender assertion; the material uses the same neuter singular (“its own maintenance team”, “It chose Svale Systems”). Collective singular agreement is the resolved en_US guidance. Supported.
- “It excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial” — third person singular, referring to the note and its count exactly as the source's own “It” does. Supported.
- “what they prove” — third person plural, the 31 reports. Supported.
- “beyond them” — third person plural, the two buildings; referent settled by the material (see item 24). Supported.
- “it is not independent reporting” — third person singular, this account; the source's own “it”. Supported.
- “We”, “us”, “ours”, “I” inside the three quotations — first person, Lind and her team, exactly as the material gives them. No first-person supplier voice appears anywhere in the narration. Supported.
- “your”, “you” in the closing — second person, the reader; a mode of address, not a claim about any person.

## 4. Cases considered and set aside

**A. “The trial note does not credit the software.”** A note could decline to attribute the median difference to the software and still credit it elsewhere — for instance by recording that both shifts now saw the same picture — and the material nowhere says its account of the note is exhaustive. Set aside on reading in context: the heading sits over a section that is wholly the median comparison, and over that section it says only what the source says, “the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software.” It understates nothing the material claims and adds no causal reading.

**B. “No … measurements were made.”** An existential absence (“There are no …”) and a denial that anyone measured come apart only where a measurement was taken and then lost. Set aside by quoting the supplied statement, “There are no cost, resident-satisfaction or completion-time measurements.”, which denies that any such measurement exists for this trial.

## 5. Findings

### Finding 1 — source support: the title drops the two-building scope

**Draft:** “# Elm Quay's shifts shared one repair log”

**Source:** “In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings.” / “Elm Quay Housing manages 640 flats.” / “Previously telephone reports and emails were stored separately.” / “The trial has not yet expanded; the team will decide after checking how the categories work for larger repairs.”

**What is missing:** the restriction to two buildings. The material supports shifts sharing one log inside the two trial buildings and nowhere else; it states positively that reports and emails were stored separately before, and that the trial has not expanded.

**Case in which the draft fails and the source holds:** at Elm Quay's other buildings, the morning and evening shifts went on logging phone reports and emails separately for the whole trial, as they had before — which is what “The trial has not yet expanded” describes for a 640-flat portfolio of which two buildings were in the trial. Read alone, the title says Elm Quay's shifts shared one repair log, and that is false of that company; the source's claim, which is about two buildings, holds. In the other direction, no case: wherever the title is true, the source's scoped claim is true.

**Case in which the source fails and the draft holds:** none available, which is the asymmetry — the title is the wider statement.

No supplied statement excludes the case. The deck that follows does say “in two buildings”, which is why the body is sound, but the title is a claim in its own right and travels on its own in listings and shared links, and the base contract requires scope to be preserved wherever a claim appears.

**Smallest supported repair:** add the scope to the title, leaving its wording and voice otherwise untouched —

> `# In two buildings, Elm Quay's shifts shared one repair log`

or, keeping the existing word order, `# Elm Quay's shifts shared one repair log in two buildings`.
