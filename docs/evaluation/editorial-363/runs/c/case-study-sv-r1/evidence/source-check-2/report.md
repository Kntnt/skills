# Source-support and translation check

Draft: `draft.md` (sv) · Material: `material.md` (en) · Contracts: `claims.md`, `quotations.md`, `composition-sv.md`

Method: every factual and attributed passage of the draft — frontmatter, headline, standfirst, byline, subheadings, body, quotations, link text — is set beside the supplied passage it rests on, quoted verbatim in English. For each pair I name what differs (thing named or measured, subject, scope, time, modality, certainty, whose assertion) and then test both directions with a concrete case compatible with the material. A case is set aside only by quoting a supplied statement that excludes it. The byline *Av Thomas Barregren* comes from the invocation and is excluded from the comparison by instruction.

## 3. Translated quotations

Per `quotations.md`, each rendering was first read in Swedish alone, with the English out of sight, and only then set beside the source.

**Q1** — "Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var våra; Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen."

Read in Swedish alone: nothing obstructs. *Kvällsskiftet* and *morgonskiftet* are the ordinary compounds, written closed; the subordinate clause with *skulle* is how Swedish expresses a wanted-for outcome; *lägga in dem i loggen* is the idiom a Swedish reader expects. Beside the source: meaning, stance (the team's ownership of the categories, Svale in a helping role) and certainty are unchanged. Nothing additive, nothing removed.

**Q2** — "Vi lade mer tid på att komma överens om kategorierna än på att lägga in de första anmälningarna. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan arbetet i nästa hus börjar."

Read in Swedish alone: *lade mer tid på … än på …* is the natural comparative frame; *Den tiden skulle jag avsätta* fronts the object with the finite verb second, which is idiomatic and keeps the speaker's emphasis. Beside the source: "before the next building starts" is elliptical in English — Swedish supplies no such ellipsis, and "innan nästa hus börjar" would read as the building itself beginning. Supplying *arbetet* is the target language saying what the speaker said, and the supplied context settles the referent ("before the next building starts" in a trial that ran in two buildings). Modality (*would*/*skulle*) and the reservation are intact. Not additive.

**Q3** — "Jag skulle välja att göra om försöket. Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser."

Read in Swedish alone: nothing obstructs. *Göra om* carries both "do again" and "remake"; after a completed trial, followed by *men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka*, the repeat reading is the one a Swedish reader takes, and *göra försöket igen* is a preferred synonym rather than a repair of an obstruction. *En samlad bild av anmälningarna* is the Swedish idiom for reports brought into one place, which is what "one view of the reports" says. Beside the source: the concessive *men* preserves the qualification, and the distinction between *avsätta* (set aside, Q2) and *lägga in* (leave, add, Q3) is kept rather than levelled. Stance — a qualified endorsement, not an unqualified one — survives, and no certainty is added or removed.

**Translation findings: none.** No place was found where a reader of Swedish stops to supply a word the language does not supply there.

## 4. Findings

### Finding 1 — the party that kept telephone reporting open is lost (source support)

- **Draft** (`draft.md`, section *Sex medarbetare fick utbildning vid två tillfällen*): "Telefonanmälan hölls öppen för de boende."
- **Source**: "The maintenance team designed its categories and kept telephone reporting open for residents."
- **What differs**: the subject. The material makes the maintenance team the party that kept telephone reporting open — a decision by the customer. The draft's agentless s-passive names nobody, and the next sentence of the same paragraph names Svale as the actor ("Svale konfigurerade loggen och utbildade sex medarbetare vid två tillfällen"), so the nearest actor a reader has to hand is the supplier.
- **Case that stands**: Svale's standard configuration retained a telephone intake channel by default, and the maintenance team made no decision about it. That case is compatible with the supplied material — no supplied statement excludes it — and under it "Telefonanmälan hölls öppen för de boende" holds while "The maintenance team … kept telephone reporting open for residents" fails. In the other direction, where the team kept the channel open against a configuration that would have closed it, the source holds and the draft's sentence, read in its paragraph, credits the wrong party.
- **Missing support**: the attributed agent. `claims.md` requires "exact attribution"; `composition-sv.md` asks for the real subject where the sentence has one to hand.
- **Smallest repair**: "Teamet höll telefonanmälan öppen för de boende."

### Finding 2 — "bolagets hus" asserts ownership the material does not give (source support)

- **Draft** (`draft.md`, opening): "…för att pröva en gemensam felanmälningslogg i två av bolagets hus."
- **Source**: "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings." and "Elm Quay Housing manages 640 flats."
- **What differs**: the relation between company and buildings. The material gives management only ("manages 640 flats") and locates the trial in "two buildings" without saying whose. The genitive *bolagets hus* adds that the buildings are the company's own — a background circumstance, which `claims.md` counts as a claim.
- **Case that stands**: Elm Quay Housing is a managing company whose 640 flats are owned by the housing associations that engage it, two of whose buildings were in the trial. No supplied statement excludes this — "its own maintenance team" makes the team the company's, not the buildings. Under that case "manages 640 flats" and "in two buildings" both hold while "två av bolagets hus" fails.
- **Missing support**: any supplied statement of ownership.
- **Smallest repair**: "…i två hus." — the wording the headline and standfirst already use.

### Finding 3 — the checklist is offered as trial preparation (source support)

- **Draft** (`draft.md`, closing): "Vill du förbereda ett eget försök kan du läsa igenom [Svales checklista för införande](https://example.invalid/svale/checklist)."
- **Source**: "The optional next step is the supplied implementation checklist at https://example.invalid/svale/checklist; it is a document to read, not a consultation booking or a product trial."
- **What differs**: what the destination is for. The link text itself is accurate — a checklist for *införande*, offered to read, not a booking and not a product trial. The lead-in adds that reading it prepares a trial of the reader's own, which the material does not say; it describes the document as an implementation checklist and says nothing about its contents.
- **Case that stands**: the checklist covers a full post-purchase rollout — contract, data migration, staff accounts, go-live — and contains nothing about scoping or running a bounded trial in two buildings. Nothing supplied describes its contents, so nothing excludes this case. Under it, "the supplied implementation checklist" holds while the draft's recommendation to read it in order to prepare a trial fails.
- **Missing support**: any supplied statement about what the checklist helps a reader do.
- **Smallest repair**: "Vill du förbereda ett eget införande kan du läsa igenom …" — the link text, the act offered and the sentence otherwise unchanged.

## 5. Considered and not reported

**5.1 Headline, "samlade felanmälningarna".** The definite plural could be read as every report in the two buildings, and the count of 31 "excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial". But that exclusion is stated of the note's count, not of the log, and no supplied statement says any report stayed out of the log. The headline says what "a shared repair log" against reports "stored separately" says. Defensible as written.

**5.2 Heading, "prövade statusvisningen".** The heading presupposes a status display where the material gives a test of "whether the log could show the status of each repair". The sentence immediately beneath it states the material's version exactly ("kontrollerade teamet om loggen kunde visa status för varje enskild felanmälan"), and a heading read in that context is resolved before a reader can take the presupposition away. Compression, not a defect.

**5.3 Heading, "Mätningen säger inget om varför tiden skilde sig".** Its subject is the median comparison, not the trial note, and a comparison of two periods that "had different workloads" does establish nothing about cause — `claims.md`: "A sequence or correlation establishes no cause." Had the subject been *noteringen*, the case of a note that names the lighter workload as the likely reason would have stood; it does not stand against *Mätningen*.

**5.4 Heading, "Nästa beslut hänger på de större reparationerna".** *Hänger på* states a dependency where the material gives a sequence ("will decide after checking how the categories work for larger repairs"). The body sentence directly beneath restores the sequence verbatim, and the check the material names is a check of the very question the decision turns on. Defensible as a heading.

**5.5 "finns inte i underlaget".** Narrower than "No comparison with another supplier is available", and entailed by it, so no qualification is lost and no certainty is added. Whether an article should refer to its own source material is an editorial question outside this comparison.

**5.6 "mättes inte" for "There are no … measurements".** A case where a measurement was taken and then lost would separate the two, but the supplied sentence is the ordinary way of saying none was made, and the draft carries all three absences.

**5.7 Genre and mechanics.** Supplier narration stays in the third person throughout; the customer is the acting party in every sentence but the one at Finding 1; the quotations carry experience the surrounding narrative does not (why the shared view was wanted, where the time actually went, what she would change). Sentence-level mechanics, punctuation and register are outside this comparison and were not reviewed.
