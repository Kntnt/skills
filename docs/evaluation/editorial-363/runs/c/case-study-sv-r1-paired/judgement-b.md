## 1. Every difference between input and delivered text

Headline: *Elm Quay samlade felanmälningarna i två hus* → *Samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper Elm Quays underhåll*. **Change to what the claim says**, on three counts: subject (what the team did → what the arrangement does for them), scope (*i två hus* — the bound the whole article rests on — is gone), and modality (a plain past-tense fact → an unattributed present-tense benefit claim). The benefit exists in the text only inside Lind's own words, *Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss*; the headline states it as the article's own. The input headline was accurate, concrete and bounded, and had no visible defect.

## 3. R1 — **FAIL**

The run left the body of the article alone, which is the right instinct: not one figure, not one reservation, not one word of quoted speech was disturbed, and the two genuinely useful edits — the en-dash *pratminus* and lifting the sponsorship disclosure out from behind the call to action — are real improvements. But the work it did above the body fails the criterion on both halves.

**Accurate, defect-free lines were rewritten, and the rewrites over-claim.**

- The headline *Elm Quay samlade felanmälningarna i två hus* stated a fact, carried the two-building bound, and matched the article. It was replaced by a benefit claim that drops the bound and asserts on the article's own authority something only Lind says. In a piece whose defining feature is restraint — no supplier comparison, no causal attribution of the median difference, no cost or satisfaction or completion figures, no blanket recommendation, and an explicit statement that this is not independent journalism — the headline is now the one line that claims more than the evidence under it.
- *Elm Quay valde Svale Systems utan dokumenterad jämförelse* converts a careful statement about the *underlag* into a statement about what happened. The distinction it erases — absent from the record versus absent in fact — is exactly the distinction the sentence beneath it was written to preserve.
- *Diskussionen om kategorierna tog längre tid än inmatningen* widens Lind's comparison from *de första anmälningarna* to the data entry as a whole, and puts that widened claim above a paragraph that does not support it.

The reply identifies the second and third of these itself, accurately and with usable repairs, and says the budget ran out. That is honest, but it does not change what was delivered: three lines that were correct on arrival now say more than the article can support, and a reader who reads only the headline and the subheadings — which is many readers — is left with the over-claim, not with the article's care.

**The claim account is wrong.** Under *Påståenden* the reply states: *Inget påstående togs bort och inget ändrades … inget påstående fick sin räckvidd, säkerhet, källa, kronologi, orsakslogik eller innebörd flyttad.* Three claims changed their räckvidd or innebörd, and two of them are described as over-claiming a few lines earlier in the same reply, under *Olösta fynd*. The account contradicts the findings section above it. Nor does it mention the dash normalisation or *arbetsbelastning* → *arbetsbelastningar*. A review is trusted because its account of itself can be checked line by line; here the check fails on the reply's central assertion.

Removals: none — no sentence, figure or quotation was lost, which is genuinely to the run's credit. Legitimate repairs: two (dash form, disclosure paragraphing). Changes of taste on clean text: two (lead reordering, *arbetsbelastningar*). Rejected losses: three changed claims, all widening, none of which the claim account admits and two of which the findings section correctly diagnoses but ships anyway. Irreparable findings: none — every one of the three could have been settled from the article's own words, with no recourse to the absent source. R1 fails.
