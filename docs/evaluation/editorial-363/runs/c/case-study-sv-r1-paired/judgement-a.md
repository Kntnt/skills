Text: Swedish (sv) case study about Elm Quay Housing's eight-week shared repair-report log in two buildings.

## 1. Every difference between input and delivered text

Nine differences, in document order. Frontmatter, standfirst, byline, the third subheading, every figure (640 lägenheter, 31 anmälningar, 4 december 2025, två mot tre arbetsdagar, åtta veckor, sex medarbetare, två tillfällen) and the link are otherwise untouched.

1. **Headline.** Before: `# Elm Quay samlade felanmälningarna i två hus`. After: `# Samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper Elm Quays underhåll`. — **Change to what a claim says**, on three axes at once. Modality: a statement of what was done becomes a statement of effect (*hjälper*). Attribution: the effect claim exists in the text only inside Maya Lind's own words, *Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss*; the headline states it in the article's voice, unattributed. Scope: the input's bound *i två hus* disappears, so the headline speaks of Elm Quay's maintenance as a whole, while the body says the trial has not been extended beyond the two buildings (*Försöket har ännu inte utvidgats*). In a case study the supplier has approved for publication, and which the text itself says is not independent journalism, turning the customer's qualified remark into the headline's own benefit claim is the heaviest change in the run.

2. **Lead paragraph, rebuilt.** Before: `I september 2025 bestämde sig underhållsteamet hos Elm Quay Housing för att pröva en gemensam felanmälningslogg i två av bolagets hus. Då bestämde sig underhållsteamet för att pröva en gemensam felanmälningslogg i två av bolagets hus.` — **Change of taste** (background first, decision second). No fact, figure or date is lost, added or moved in time: *hade fram till dess sparats var för sig* and *Fram till september 2025 sparades … var för sig* say the same thing, and *Då* carries the September 2025 anchor forward. The rebuilt version is competent Swedish, but so was the original; nothing in the text marks the old order as a defect.

3. **First subheading.** Before: `## Teamet prövade statusvisningen innan det valde`. After: `## Elm Quay valde Svale Systems utan dokumenterad jämförelse`. — **Change to what a claim says** (scope and source). The section says *Någon jämförelse med en annan leverantör finns inte i underlaget* — a statement about the material. The new subheading says the choice was made *utan dokumenterad jämförelse* — a statement about the world. The input's subheading claimed only what the section's first sentence states.

4. **Speech dash, three places** (before each of Lind's three passages). Before: `—` (em dash). After: `–` (en dash). — **Mechanical correction, locale form.** Swedish sets pratminus with tankstreck (en dash), so this is right for `sv` and applied consistently to all three.

6. **Evidence paragraph, number.** Before: `Perioderna hade olika arbetsbelastning`. After: `Perioderna hade olika arbetsbelastningar`. — **Change of taste, and a doubtful one.** The singular is idiomatic Swedish in this distributive construction and was not an error; the plural is not wrong either, but nothing was repaired.

7–9. **Final paragraph split.** The disclosure sentence `Svale Systems har godkänt publiceringen av det här kundcaset, som alltså inte är oberoende journalistik.` is moved out of the closing paragraph into a paragraph of its own; wording identical. — **Repair of a defect visible in the text**: the disclosure sat behind a call to action, and standing alone it is read rather than skimmed past. Nothing is claimed differently.

**Q1** — `Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort, skriver Maya Lind, arbetsledare för underhållet. Kategorierna var våra; Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen.`
- Wording: **unchanged.** Only the dash that opens the passage changed glyph (em to en), which is the locale's own mark for pratminus and not part of the speech.
- One pass: **yes.** A Swedish reader takes the whole passage without supplying anything. The inquit *skriver Maya Lind, arbetsledare för underhållet* interrupts and the second sentence continues as her speech — ordinary Swedish setting, however it looks parsed literally. *dem* resolves to *Kategorierna* in the same sentence; *loggen* is the log named in the lead; the shifts need no introduction beyond the sentence itself.

**Q2** — `Vi lade mer tid på att komma överens om kategorierna än på att lägga in de första anmälningarna, skriver Maya Lind. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan arbetet i nästa hus börjar.`

No passage was altered, trimmed, joined, re-attributed or moved; none gained an event, fact, name or figure; the speaker's verb (*skriver*, not *säger*) is preserved in all three, and with it the fact that these are written answers.

## 3. R1

**Fail.**

The run does some real work. The speech dashes are corrected to the Swedish form in all three places. Splitting the disclosure into its own paragraph is a defensible repair that strengthens rather than weakens the text's honesty. Every figure, date and quotation survives, and the whole evidence section — the exclusions, *noteringen förklarar uttryckligen inte skillnaden med programvaran*, the list of what was not measured, Lind's refusal to recommend Svale to every housing company, the not-yet-extended trial — is untouched. Two of the three claim changes it made are diagnosed by the reply itself, precisely and in the right terms.

It fails on the claim account and on what it shipped.

- **The reply's claim account is false as written.** It states: *Inget påstående togs bort och inget ändrades … inget påstående fick sin räckvidd, säkerhet, källa, kronologi, orsakslogik eller innebörd flyttad.* Three claims changed. The headline changed modality, attribution and scope at once. The first subheading moved a claim about the source material to a claim about the world. The second widened a comparison the speaker bounded. The reply contradicts itself inside its own reply: the two unresolved findings describe exactly how two of those subheadings now claim what the text does not, immediately above a section asserting that no claim changed. A reader who trusts the claim account is told the opposite of what the delivered text contains.
- **The headline overclaim is not reported anywhere.** The two subheading overclaims are at least declared unresolved, with the fix named and the body correctly left alone. The headline — the single most exposed line in the piece, and the one that lifts the customer's *hjälper oss* into the article's own voice while dropping the two-building bound — is listed only as one of the findings the round repaired. There is no account of what it now claims, and no acknowledgement that the bound is gone.
- **Working text was rewritten and came back weaker.** Both input subheadings were accurate and covered their sections. Both were replaced by subheadings that overclaim, one of which no longer describes its own section at all. The lead was reordered without any defect being named, and *arbetsbelastning* was pluralised for nothing. This is the pattern R1 exists to catch: a clean passage rewritten to taste, at a cost to accuracy.

The removals are the easy part of the verdict: nothing was removed, and the reply is right about that. The changed claims are the problem. Of the three, one is reported accurately (subheading two), one is reported accurately (subheading one), and one is not reported at all — and all three are contradicted by the summary sentence that says no claim changed. A supplier-approved case study whose merit is its scope discipline was handed back with a benefit claim in its headline and two subheadings the run itself knows to be too wide.
