# Source and quotation comparison — case-study, en_GB

Draft: `draft.md` · Material: `material.md` · Reference: `context.md`

## Scope and method

Every factual and attributed claim in the draft — title, standfirst, byline, headings and body — is set beside the material passage it rests on, quoted in the material's own words. Source language and target language are both English, and the material permits but does not require translation; the draft reproduces the quotations verbatim, so the translation check below is a verbatim check plus a target-language reading. Person, number and natural gender carried by pronouns are accounted for separately in section 3. General editorial and mechanical review (register, punctuation style, length) is outside this comparison; where a mechanical choice bears on a claim it is noted and not counted.

Test applied throughout: where draft and source differ, a concrete case compatible with the material is named in which one statement holds and the other fails. A case is set aside only by quoting a supplied statement that excludes it.

## 1. Claim accounting

### Title and standfirst

**C1 — Title (L1): “Two Elm Quay buildings shared one repair log”**
Source: “In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings”; “It chose Svale Systems after testing whether **the log** could show the status of each repair”; “Svale configured **the log**”; Lind: “Having **one view of the reports** helps us”.
Differs: the draft asserts a single log across both buildings; the material's phrase “a shared repair log in two buildings” could in isolation describe one shared log per building, shared between shifts. Case tested: each building keeps its own log, shared only across shifts. Set aside by the material's consistent singular “the log” for the trial as a whole and by Lind's “one view of the reports”, which no per-building arrangement satisfies.
Verdict: **supported**. Simple past “shared” reports a completed eight-week trial; it makes no claim that use has ceased, and L29 states the log is still confined to the two buildings.

**C2 — L3: “Elm Quay Housing manages 640 flats.”**
Source: “Elm Quay Housing manages 640 flats.” Verbatim. Verb, number and scope identical.
Verdict: **supported**.

**C3 — L3: “In 2025 its maintenance team spent eight weeks testing …”**
Source: “In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings”; “The trial lasted eight weeks”; “Elm Quay's internal trial note, 4 December 2025, says that 31 repair reports were entered.”
Differs on time: the material dates the decision, not the eight weeks. Case tested: the eight weeks straddled the year end. Excluded by the note of 4 December 2025 reporting the trial's counts and its “during the trial” medians — the trial's period is complete within 2025, and a September decision leaves no reading on which it is not.
Verdict: **supported**.

**C4 — L3: “… testing whether two of its buildings could work from a single record of repairs”**
Source: “decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings”; “The team wanted staff on different shifts to see the same information.”
Differs on the object of the test: the material states the team's want (shift-level visibility) and what was tested before the supplier was chosen (“whether the log could show the status of each repair”); the draft restates the eight weeks as a building-level feasibility question. Case tested: the trial tested only shift handover inside each building. Set aside by the same statements as C1 (one log, one view). Given one log across the two buildings, trialling it is testing whether they can work from a single record.
Verdict: **supported**.

**C5 — L3: “and an internal note recorded what happened”**
Source: “Elm Quay's internal trial note, 4 December 2025, says that 31 repair reports were entered.”
Differs: the draft leaves the note's owner implicit and describes its contents broadly. The note's owner is settled for a reader by the surrounding clause, whose subject is Elm Quay's own maintenance team. See EQ1.
Verdict: **supported**.

**C6 — L3: “what it declined to conclude”**
Source: “the note **explicitly** does not attribute the difference to the software.”
Modality and knowledge-holder match: “declined” asserts a deliberate withholding, which “explicitly does not attribute” supports. Referent of “it” is the note.
Verdict: **supported**; this is where the material's explicitness is carried.

**C7 — L3: “what the supervisor would change”**
Source: Lind: “I would set that time aside before the next building starts”; “I would leave an extra week for preparation.”
Conditional modality preserved; no intention or plan is asserted on her behalf.
Verdict: **supported**.

**C8 — L5: “By Thomas Barregren”**
Source: brief — “No author name supplied.” `context.md` records that the byline comes from the invocation, so its absence from the material is no finding.
Verdict: **outside the comparison**.

### “Elm Quay does not credit the software for the shorter time”

The draft carries “working days” across to the second clause, where the material leaves “three” bare under the same modifier, and carries “median” likewise — both carry-overs are correct. The draft attributes the figures to the note, which the material states only for the 31 and implies for the difference (“the note … does not attribute the difference”). See EQ2.

**C26 — L19: “There are no measurements of cost, resident satisfaction or completion time.”**

## 4. Source-support findings

### F1 — L15 heading attributes the note's non-attribution to Elm Quay as a whole

**Draft (L15):** “Elm Quay does not credit the software for the shorter time”

**Source:** “The periods had different workloads, and **the note** explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software.”

**What differs:** whose knowledge and whose assertion. The material supports non-attribution as a property of one document — Elm Quay's internal trial note of 4 December 2025. The heading makes it the position of the organisation. The measured difference itself (“two working days … and three”) is supported, so “the shorter time” is not at issue; the subject of the refusal is.

**Concrete cases, both directions:**
- Source holds, draft fails: the note declines the attribution in terms, while Elm Quay's maintenance supervisor tells the interviewer she does put the shorter assignment time down to the shared log. Nothing supplied excludes this. The material bounds only what may be quoted — “complete usable quotations” — not what she or her colleagues hold, and “Her final quotation … is not an inference from the figures” says her assessment does not rest on the figures, not that no one at Elm Quay reads a cause into them. On that case the heading's claim about the company is false while the material's claim about the note stands.
- Draft holds, source fails: Elm Quay's settled position is non-attribution, while the working note does contain a line crediting the software that the company later disowns. Then the heading stands and the supplied statement does not.

**Missing support:** no supplied statement gives Elm Quay, as an organisation, a position on the cause of the shorter time.

**Smallest supported repair:** “Elm Quay's note does not credit the software for the shorter time”. Three words changed; Elm Quay stays the acting party as the brief requires, the reservation the material insists must survive is kept, and the note's owner becomes explicit at the point where it matters most (which also settles EQ1 below).

No other source-support finding was reached. In particular, no claim in the draft lacks a source passage, and the two passages that are the author's own — the standfirst's “Here is what that note says …” and the transition at L23 — present no facts, events, experiences or views beyond the supported material they introduce.

## 5. Translation findings

**None, and none possible in the ordinary sense.** The material's source language is English, the target is en_GB, and the material permits translation of the quotations (“Translation of the quotations is permitted, preserving stance and qualification”) without requiring it. The draft does not translate: all three quotations are reproduced word for word.

Two checks were nevertheless run.

**Verbatim check.** Each of the three quotations was compared character by character against the material (C19, C29, C31). All three match word for word. Two changes are mechanical and outside this comparison: the material's double quotation marks become single outer marks, consistently across all three, which is the majority British house style named in the composition guidance; and each quotation's closing full stop becomes a comma before the attribution (`…,' she says`), the ordinary British form. No word, hedge, connective or item of punctuation inside the quoted wording differs.

**Target-language reading.** Each quotation was read in en_GB alone, with the material out of sight, before being set beside it. None presents a concrete obstruction — no place where a British reader stops to supply a word the language does not supply there. “The categories were ours”, “I would set that time aside”, “one view of the reports” and “leave an extra week for preparation” are all ordinary British idiom. “Having one view of the reports helps us” is a shorthand the language itself uses; it is not a figure imported from elsewhere. The distinctive voice — the shift-to-shift concreteness, the plain admission that agreement took longer than data entry, the “but” that holds the reservation open — is present in the target language because the words are the speaker's own. Nothing was smoothed into the register of the surrounding article, no hedge was added or removed, and no implicit referent was made explicit: “that time” at L23 and “the next building” are left exactly as she left them, which is right, since the supplied context does not settle either beyond what her own sentences say.

No quotation was assembled from separate remarks; each of the three is one supplied quotation used whole, and none is welded to another.

## 6. Editorial questions — recorded, not findings

These are places where the draft is defensible as written and a different choice is available. None is reported as a defect and none carries a repair.

- **EQ1 — “An internal note” (L17), “an internal note” (L3):** the material says “**Elm Quay's** internal trial note”. The owner is left implicit. A reader takes it from the surrounding clause, whose subject is Elm Quay's own maintenance team, and the supplier is named explicitly wherever the supplier acts. The repair proposed under F1 makes the ownership explicit in any case.
- **EQ2 — “The same note gives the median time …” (L19):** the material attributes the 31 to the note and then gives the medians without attaching them to it, mentioning the note again only for the non-attribution. Attributing the figures to the note is the reading the paragraph invites, and is the more careful of the two available forms (the alternative is a bare unattributed figure). A note that discusses the difference without printing the two medians is conceivable but is not what the material reads as.
- **EQ3 — “it decided” (L7):** the antecedent is the maintenance team on the natural reading, and the parallel “it chose” is the material's own pronoun. Naming the team again would remove the residual ambiguity with “the company” in the preceding clause.
- **EQ4 — title tense (L1):** the simple past reports a completed trial; L29's present perfect reports the log's continuing confinement to the two buildings. The two are consistent, but a reader could take the past as implying the sharing has ended.
- **EQ5 — “she says” (L13, L23, L25):** the material records that “All interviews occurred by email”. “Says” is the conventional attribution for an interview answer and no scene, emotion or remembered dialogue is supplied anywhere in the draft; a publication that distinguishes written answers would write “she writes”.
- **EQ6 — “two of its buildings” (L3):** the material says Elm Quay “manages 640 flats”. The possessive reads as the stock in its charge rather than as an ownership claim, which is ordinary usage for a housing company.
