# Source and quotation comparison — case-study, en_GB

Draft: `draft.md` · Material: `material.md` · Reference: `context.md`

## Scope and method

Every factual and attributed statement in the draft, including the title, the
standfirst, the byline line and all four section headings, is set beside the
passage of the supplied material it rests on, quoted in the material's own
words. Where draft and source differ, the difference is named (thing named or
measured, subject, scope, time, modality, certainty, whose assertion) and tested
in both directions by naming a concrete case compatible with the material in
which one statement holds and the other fails. A case is set aside only by
quoting a supplied statement that excludes it.

Source language of the material is English; the resolved target language is
en_GB. Nothing is translated. The quotation check is therefore a fidelity check
against the English originals, and the translation section below records that
no translation findings are possible, with the verification that was run.

The byline `By Thomas Barregren` comes from the invocation, not the material
(`context.md` §Byline); its absence from the material is not treated as a
finding. General editorial and mechanical review (register, rhythm, spelling
convention, quotation-mark style, the ~400-word target — the body runs 391
words) is outside this comparison.

## 2. Pronoun accounting — person, number, natural gender

Referent identification is recorded separately from the person, number and
natural-gender features each pronoun asserts. The target language is English,
which has no grammatical gender on nouns, so every gendered pronoun below is a
natural-gender assertion and there is no grammatical-gender/natural-gender
divergence to separate.

- **"its" (L3 "its maintenance team", "its blocks"; L29 "its categories")** —
  third person, singular, neuter. Supported: the material uses the same features
  of the same referents — "its own maintenance team", "The maintenance team
  designed its categories". The possessive in "its blocks" claims no ownership
  beyond management, which is ordinary usage for a body that "manages 640 flats".
- **"it" (L7 "it decided", "it chose")** — third person, singular, neuter.
  Supported: the material's "It chose Svale Systems". The referent is the
  maintenance team, the subject of the preceding clause, and the material makes
  the team the decider and the chooser; the reading in which "it" resumes "the
  company" leaves the acting party as Elm Quay either way, so no claim changes.
- **"it" (L3 "what it declined to conclude"; L19 "before it"; L29 "where it
  started")** — third person, singular, neuter, referring to the note, the trial
  and the log respectively. Supported; all three are inanimate in the material.
- **"she" (L13, L23, L25) and "She" (L25)** — third person, singular, feminine.
  Natural gender is asserted, and it is supported by the material's own pronoun
  use of the same person: "Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing
  company. **Her** final quotation is **her** actual, qualified assessment." The
  name alone would not establish this; the material's "her" does.
- **"we", "us", "I", "ours", "them" (inside the three quotations)** — first
  person singular and plural, and third person plural for the categories.
  Reproduced verbatim from the material's quotations; no feature altered.
- **"The team will decide" (L29), "the maintenance team designed" (L11)** —
  collective nouns take singular agreement throughout the draft, consistently, as
  en_GB permits when the body acts as one; the material's own agreement ("The
  team wanted", "It chose") is likewise singular. No claim depends on it.

## 3. Source-support findings

### Finding 1 — the title asserts a present, continuing state (L1)

**Draft:** "Two Elm Quay buildings share one repair log"
**Support that is missing:** the material establishes the shared log for the
eight weeks of the trial and says nothing about its use afterwards: "The trial
lasted eight weeks." / "The trial has not yet expanded; the team will decide
after checking how the categories work for larger repairs."
**Case in which the source holds and the draft fails:** at the end of the eight
weeks the team paused the log in both buildings while it checks how the
categories handle larger repairs, and the two buildings are back on telephone
reports and emails until the decision is taken. That is compatible with an
unexpanded trial and an undecided team; no supplied statement excludes it. In
that case the material is satisfied and the two buildings do not now share one
repair log. The verb is stative, so the headline present reads as an ongoing
state rather than as the journalistic present for a past event.
**Smallest supported repair:** "Two Elm Quay buildings shared one repair log".

### Finding 2 — "blocks" names a building type the material does not give (L3)

**Draft:** "two of its blocks"
**Support that is changed:** "decided to trial a shared repair log in two
buildings". The material says *buildings* throughout, as does the draft itself at
L11 ("The trial covered two buildings").
**Case in which the source holds and the draft fails:** the two trial buildings
are a pair of converted terraced houses, each containing six flats. They are
buildings; a reader of en_GB would not call either a block. Nothing in the
material excludes this — 640 flats under management is compatible with
low-rise stock — so the wider source term holds where the narrower draft term
does not.
**Smallest supported repair:** "two of its buildings".

### Finding 3 — the note's authorship is attributed to the maintenance team (L3)

**Draft:** "its maintenance team spent eight weeks testing ... and wrote down
what happened"
**Support that is changed:** "Elm Quay's internal trial note, 4 December 2025,
says that ...". The material attributes the note to Elm Quay and names no author.
**Case in which the source holds and the draft fails:** the note was written by
Elm Quay's operations manager, who ran the trial's reporting and compiled the
medians, while the maintenance team supplied the entries. The note is then
Elm Quay's internal trial note as the material says, and the maintenance team
did not write down what happened.
**Smallest supported repair:** "... spent eight weeks testing whether two of its
buildings could work from a single record of repairs, and Elm Quay wrote down
what happened."

### Finding 4 — "stored separately" becomes a definite two-location arrangement (L7)

**Draft:** "Telephone reports were stored in one place at Elm Quay Housing and
emails in another."
**Support that is changed:** "Previously telephone reports and emails were
stored separately." The material claims only that the two were not kept
together; it makes no claim that either medium was itself kept in one place.
**Case in which the source holds and the draft fails:** telephone reports were
written up by each shift supervisor in their own notebook — three notebooks —
and emails sat in individual staff inboxes. The two kinds of report were stored
separately, as the material says, and there was no one place for telephone
reports and no other one place for emails. This is also the arrangement that
makes the team's stated wish — "staff on different shifts to see the same
information" — most intelligible, and no supplied statement excludes it.
**Smallest supported repair:** "Telephone reports and emails were stored
separately at Elm Quay Housing."

### Finding 5 — the non-attribution is widened in subject and scope (L15)

**Draft (heading):** "Elm Quay does not credit the software"
**Support that is changed:** "the note explicitly does not attribute the
difference to the software". The non-attribution is the note's, and its object
is "the difference" — the two-versus-three working days in median assignment
time — not the software's contribution generally.
**Case in which the source holds and the draft fails:** the material itself
supplies it. Lind, for Elm Quay, credits the log with exactly what the team
wanted from it — "Having one view of the reports helps us" — while the note
declines to put the shorter assignment time down to the software. Elm Quay
therefore does credit the software, for one thing and not for another, and the
heading as written denies both. The section under the heading concerns the
figures, which mitigates but does not remove the claim the heading makes on its
own.
**Smallest supported repair:** "Elm Quay's note does not credit the software for
the difference".

## 4. Translation findings

**None are possible, and none are reported.** The material's source language is
English and the resolved target is en_GB; `context.md` records that "The
quotations are reproduced verbatim from the English material; nothing is
translated, so source and target language are the same." No meaning crosses a
language boundary anywhere in the draft, inside quoted speech or outside it.

The fidelity check that was run in place of a translation check: each of the
three supplied quotations was read in the draft first, on its own, and then
matched character-for-character against the material. All three are reproduced
exactly, with no repair applied and none needed — no filler, false start or
abandoned clause appears in the supplied material. The only change is the
terminal full stop becoming a comma before each attribution. All three supplied
quotations are used, and no fourth quotation appears; nothing is welded together
from separate remarks, and no hedge is added or removed. Meaning, stance,
certainty and distinctive wording are therefore untouched by construction:

1. "We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done.
   The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log." — exact.
2. "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first
   reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts." —
   exact.
3. "I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps
   us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation." — exact, with the
   qualification "but I would leave an extra week for preparation" intact and
   the surrounding text ("Lind's own assessment is a qualified one"; "She did
   not recommend Svale Systems to every housing company") marking it as hers
   rather than as an inference from the figures.

No implicit referent was clarified in any quotation, so nothing was settled that
the supplied context does not settle.

## 5. Editorial questions (no repair proposed)

These are recorded rather than reported as defects, because each is a matter on
which the draft is defensible as written.

- **"she says" for an email interview (L13, L23, L25).** The material states:
  "All interviews occurred by email, and no physical scene, emotion or remembered
  dialogue is provided." The quoted words are the speaker's own and verbatim, and
  the draft stages no scene, no manner and no setting; *says* for a written
  interview answer is ordinary journalistic attribution. Whether this publication
  prefers "writes" or "said by email" is a house decision, not a source-support
  defect.
- **No disclosure that the case is supplier-published.** The material states:
  "The supplier approved publication of this case as supplier-published material;
  it is not independent journalism." The draft carries a personal byline and no
  publisher statement. It makes no claim of independence, so there is no
  unsupported assertion to repair; whether the publisher line belongs in the
  text is an editorial decision for the commissioning party.
- **Attribution of the medians to the note (entry 24).** Recorded here as the
  closest call among the non-findings, with the reasoning given in §1.5.
