## 2. F1 — **Fail**

### Cited defects

**1. The subheading changes whose position it is, and widens it.** "## Elm Quay does not credit the software for the shorter time" (L15). The material supports this of one document only: "the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software." Nothing supplied gives Elm Quay, as a company, a position on what caused the shorter assignment time — and the material supplies evidence the other way, since Lind speaks for the team and credits the log with exactly what the team wanted from it ("Having one view of the reports helps us"). The subheading asserts an organisational stance the material does not carry. Delivered knowingly unrepaired; the reply names it and withholds any claim of fidelity for it.

**2. The supplier-published disclosure is dropped.** The material states: "The supplier approved publication of this case as supplier-published material; it is not independent journalism." The delivered text carries none of this. It carries instead a personal byline, four sections of third-person reporting and a closing link, which is the exact surface of independent journalism. The reader is not told the publisher is the supplier whose product is under discussion. This is the single most consequential caveat in the package and it is gone. Both checkers logged it as an editorial question rather than a defect on the reasoning that the draft "makes no claim of independence"; that reasoning treats the reader as a logician. A byline over an unattributed case study is a claim of independence in practice.

**3. "No comparison with another supplier is available" is dropped.** The lead says "it chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair" and stops there. To an operations manager — the stated reader — "chose after testing" reads as the end of a selection process among candidates. The material supplies the caveat that closes that reading off, and the draft omits it, in a piece published by the chosen supplier. The omission is not neutral: it is the omission that most flatters the publisher.

**4. "explicitly" is dropped where the claim is made.** L19: "the note does not attribute the difference to the software," against the material's "the note **explicitly** does not attribute the difference to the software." Mere silence satisfies the draft's sentence; an affirmative disclaimer is needed to satisfy the material's. Partly mitigated — the standfirst's "what it declined to conclude" does carry the explicitness — so I weigh this as a weakening rather than a clean error, but the weakening falls on the sentence doing the causal guarding.

**5. "fault" for the material's "repair report" (minor).** "Residents could still report a fault by telephone" renders "kept telephone reporting open for residents", where every other mention in the material is of repair reports. A fault report and a repair report largely coincide in British housing usage, so little turns on it; it is a term the source does not use, introduced where a supplied one was available.

## 3. G2 — **Fail**

The sequence is right — headline, standfirst, byline, lead, four sections, ending — and the middle three sections take the action, the figures and the appraisal in turn, each doing its own job. The ending is a real ending: it leaves the decision open, which is where the material leaves it, and hands over the one supplied next step described accurately as a document to read.

Two failures.

**The publisher stance is absent.** For a customer account this part is required, and the material states it in terms the draft could have lifted. Its absence is not a shortfall in polish; it changes what the reader thinks they are reading. Every other required element of the account is present — the customer's situation (separate telephone and email records, shifts blind to each other), its action (two buildings, own categories, telephone kept open, staff trained), its results (31 reports, the two medians with the workload caveat, the four absences) and its appraisal (all three quotations, with the reservation) — and the customer is unambiguously the acting party throughout, with Svale confined to configuring, training and publishing a checklist. That makes the missing line the one conspicuous hole.

**The standfirst and the lead collide.** The standfirst opens "Elm Quay Housing manages 640 flats." The lead's second sentence is "The company manages 640 flats, and its maintenance team wanted staff on different shifts to see the same information." The same fact, in nearly the same words, twice in four lines, is the standfirst and the lead failing to do distinct jobs. The reader effect is worse than the redundancy suggests: because the repetition is wedged into the middle of the lead, the 640 flats arrives as a non sequitur between the storage problem and the motive, and the lead loses the shape it otherwise has.

## 4. L1 — **Pass**

Idiomatic British English with no imported phrasing. "Trial" as a verb, "report a fault by telephone", "six members of staff", "hold up for larger repairs", "weighing a similar trial", the single quotation marks and the trailing attribution ("…,' she says") are all native usage, and the collective nouns take consistent singular agreement, as the register allows. Sentence lengths vary; the figures paragraph in particular is plain reporting, without hedging or padding: "The periods had different workloads, and the note does not attribute the difference to the software."

Two sentences that land well and show the register is under control: "The supervisor is specific about where the work actually went", which earns the quotation that follows; and "The shared log has not gone beyond the two buildings where it started", which states the non-expansion without a hint of apology. The subheading "Maya Lind would repeat the trial, with one more week" is compressed to the edge of telegraphic but reads on one pass.

The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log,' she says.** — renders the material's first complete usable quotation, word for word, in one unbroken unit. 2. **'We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. Modality "would" intact, and the framing keeps her horizon at Elm Quay's own next building rather than extending it to other companies.
3. **'I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation,' she says.** — the third supplied quotation, word for word. One pass: yes. The reservation stays attached where the source attaches it; the preceding sentence ("Lind's own assessment is a qualified one") and the following one ("She did not recommend Svale Systems to every housing company") mark it as her own judgement and not a reading of the figures, which is what the material insists on.

**Missing.** None. All three quotations the material offers and permits are used in full, nothing is welded, no hedge is removed, no fourth quotation appears.

**One note, short of a fault.** "she says" is used three times for answers the material records as having come by email. British desks vary; "says" for an interview answer is ordinary, and the draft stages no scene, manner or setting anywhere, so nothing the material forbids is implied.

**One sentence:** Every passage of quoted speech in this draft is speech a British-English reader takes on one pass, with the source's meaning, stance, reservation and voice intact.

**Did a real defect escape every checker?** Yes, two.

Under a criterion that requires a truthful publisher stance, and in a case published by the supplier of the product under discussion, that is the wrong call; the omission is the reader's biggest unmet need in the piece.

## 7. Stop or delivery

**A delivery that should not have happened in this form.** Not because the prose is unfaithful in its detail — line by line the body is careful, the quotations are exact, the reservation survives and every forbidden inference is avoided — but because the piece reaches a reader as apparently independent journalism about a supplier's product, published by that supplier, with the disclosure the material supplies left out and the "no comparison available" caveat left out with it. Those two omissions pull in the same direction, and it is the publisher's direction.

The reply's handling of the one finding it did carry forward is sound: it names the subheading, quotes the sentence that contradicts it, gives the proposed repair, explains why applying an unchecked repair after the last comparison would be worse, and withholds any assertion of fidelity. That is the right way to hand over a known defect. The trouble is that the reply files the missing publisher line under "editorial questions … publication decisions rather than fidelity ones", inheriting the checkers' classification instead of testing it. One line of supplied text, placed in the ending beside the checklist link, would have made this a valid delivery.
