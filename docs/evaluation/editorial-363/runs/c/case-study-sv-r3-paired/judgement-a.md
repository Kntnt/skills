Text: Swedish (sv) case study about Elm Quay Housing's eight-week shared repair-report log in two buildings.

## 1. Every difference between input and delivered text

Four differences. Frontmatter, standfirst, byline, all four subheadings, all three quoted passages, every figure (640 lägenheter, 31 felanmälningar, 4 december 2025, två mot tre arbetsdagar, åtta veckor, sex medarbetare, två tillfällen), the link and the disclosure wording are otherwise identical.

1. **Headline.** Before: `# Elm Quay testade gemensam felanmälningslogg i två hus`. After: `# Elm Quays arbetsledare skulle upprepa loggförsöket`. — **Change to what a claim says** (subject and modality). The old headline stated an event, bounded to two buildings and naming its subject matter. The new one states the supervisor's verdict. Two things are worth separating. What it does *not* do: it does not widen anything. The verdict is attributed (*Elm Quays arbetsledare*), it is conditional (*skulle*), and it rests on Q3's *Jag skulle välja att göra om försöket* — so the text's own reservation is not converted into an unqualified benefit claim, and no result is claimed for a company whose trial covered two buildings. What it does cost: the subject word. *Loggförsöket* does not say what the log was for; *felanmälningslogg* did. The *i två hus* bound also goes, but since the new headline claims a person's judgement rather than a result, nothing is over-extended by its absence.

2. **Body, first paragraph, second sentence.** Before: `I september 2025 bestämde underhållsteamet att prova en gemensam logg i två av husen, och försöket pågick i åtta veckor.` After: `I september 2025 bestämde underhållsteamet att under åtta veckor prova en gemensam logg i två av husen.` — **Change to what a claim says** (modality, and with it chronology). The eight weeks were a fact about what the trial did; they are now part of what the team decided in September, that is, a plan rather than an outcome. The fact itself is not lost from the text: the standfirst says *Under åtta veckor hösten 2025 provade underhållsteamet*, and the evidence section twice measures over *de åtta veckorna*. Read on its own, though, the body paragraph now places the plan where the outcome stood.

3. **Second paragraph under *Så förbereddes försöket i de två husen*.** Before: `Svale konfigurerade loggen och utbildade sex medarbetare vid två tillfällen. Vad loggen skulle lösa hade teamet redan bestämt.` After: `Svale konfigurerade loggen enligt vad teamet redan hade bestämt och utbildade sex medarbetare vid två tillfällen.` — **Change to what a claim says** (scope, plus a new connection). Two moves in one sentence. The object of the team's decision widens: *vad loggen skulle lösa*, a specific thing, becomes *vad teamet redan hade bestämt*, an open one. And a link is asserted that the input left unstated — that Svale configured the log *according to* the team's decision. The link is supported inside the text, by the quotation three lines down (*Kategorierna var våra, och Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen*), so nothing is imported from outside; but the narrative now states as fact a relation the input only implied. The repair answers a real defect: the removed sentence told the reader in advance what the quotation then says in the speaker's own words.

4. **Final paragraph split.** The disclosure `Texten publiceras av Svale Systems, som var leverantör i försöket, och intervjun med Maya Lind gjordes via mejl.` is lifted out of the closing paragraph into one of its own; wording untouched. — **Repair of a defect visible in the text.** The disclosure previously trailed a call to action, where it is read as a sign-off; standing alone it is read as a disclosure. No claim moves.

## 2. Quoted speech

Three passages, in order. The text is Swedish, and each is judged as a Swedish reader takes it, including the pratminus convention where the speech runs across sentences and the inquit arrives at the end.

**Q1** — `– Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var våra, och Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen, säger Maya Lind, arbetsledare för underhållet.`
- Wording: **unchanged**, dash, comma placement and inquit included.
- One pass: **yes.** Nothing must be supplied. The two-sentence passage under one dash with the attribution at the close is ordinary Swedish setting. *dem* resolves to *Kategorierna* in the same sentence; *loggen* is the log the section has just described Svale configuring; the shifts are self-explaining in context.
- Unchanged and readable, so no obstacle is owed, and none is claimed. Worth noting that the sentence immediately above it changed (difference 3) and now states that Svale configured the log according to the team's decision — a claim this quotation supports rather than contradicts, so the passage is not put to work saying anything it does not say.

**Q2** — `– Vi lade mer tid på att enas om kategorierna än på att lägga in de första anmälningarna. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan nästa hus kommer igång, säger Lind.`
- Wording: **unchanged.**
- One pass: **yes.** *Den tiden* points back to the time spent agreeing on categories in the sentence before it, inside the same passage; *nästa hus* is carried by a text that has placed the trial in two of the company's buildings and, in the paragraph directly above, says it has not yet been extended to more. The short-form attribution *säger Lind* works because she was named in full at her first appearance.
- Nothing owed.

**Q3** — `– Jag skulle välja att göra om försöket. Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser, säger hon.`
- Wording: **unchanged.**
- One pass: **yes.** The reservation sits inside the passage, carried by *men*. *säger hon* reaches back across a subheading to Lind, which a Swedish reader resolves without effort — she is the only person in the text, she is *arbetsledaren* in the standfirst, and the subheading above (*Omdömet gäller inte varje bostadsbolag*) speaks of her verdict.
- Nothing owed. The headline draws on this passage, but takes the conditional (*skulle*) and the attribution with it, so the speaker's certainty is not raised.

No passage was altered, trimmed, joined, re-punctuated, re-attributed or moved; none gained an event, fact, name or figure; every speaker's stance and reservation stands as it arrived.

Observation, not a finding against the run: all three passages are tagged *säger* while the closing disclosure says the interview was conducted by email. That tension is in the input, the text discloses the method itself, and the run left both the tags and the disclosure alone — which is the right side to err on, since rewriting *säger* to *skriver* would be changing a speaker's words' framing on an editorial preference.

## 3. R1

**Pass.**

The run left the load-bearing parts of the text exactly as it found them: the sentence that no comparison with another supplier exists, the exclusions from the count, *noteringen kopplar uttryckligen inte skillnaden till programvaran*, the paragraph saying cost, resident satisfaction and completion time were not measured, the not-yet-extended trial, the refusal to recommend Svale to every housing company, and the disclosure that Svale publishes the piece. All three quotations came back verbatim. Every figure and date is intact. It made three edits to claims and one structural edit — a small footprint in a text of this length, and no passage of working prose was rewritten for the sake of a better ear.

Reporting, item by item:

- Headline change — **reported, and reported accurately, including against itself.** The reply raises it as the one unresolved finding, states what the new headline keeps (the verdict is the supervisor's and it is conditional), states plainly what it lost (*ordet loggförsöket säger inte vad loggen var till för*), explains the consequence for a reader who meets the headline alone in a feed, and says the original carried that word. It does not mention that *i två hus* also went; that omission costs nothing, because the new headline claims a person's judgement rather than a result and so is not over-extended without the bound. The reply is right that the fix is a person's call and that both the angle and the subject can fit inside the length limit.
- The eight weeks moving from outcome to plan — **reported, and reported accurately.** The reply quotes both versions, names the shift in as many words (*De åtta veckorna var förut något försöket gjorde; nu är de en del av vad teamet beslutade i september*), notes that the standfirst still carries the fact, and concedes that read alone the body now puts the plan where the outcome stood. That is a candid account of a subtle modality change that a less careful report would have called a rewording.
- The team's decision widening, and the new link to the configuration — **reported, and reported accurately.** The reply states that *vad loggen skulle lösa* became the opener *vad teamet redan hade bestämt*, and it volunteers that the repair asserts a connection the earlier wording left unsaid, naming the quotation that carries it. It does not hide the strengthening behind the defect it was fixing.
- The paragraph split — **not reported.** It is the one edit outside the account. Nothing was reworded, nothing claimed differently, and the change makes a disclosure more visible rather than less, so it does not undermine the report; a complete account would still have named it.

The reply's claim account is the kind R1 is asking for: it does not assert that nothing changed, it lists what moved, and it describes each move in terms of what a reader loses. Two of the three claim changes are trade-offs that a person could reasonably reverse, and the reply gives a person enough to reverse them. Nothing was supplied that the text does not carry, no quotation was disturbed, no scope was widened past what the text supports, and no defect was excused by material the run never had.
