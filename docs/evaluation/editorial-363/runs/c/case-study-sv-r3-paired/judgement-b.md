# Judgement — x-23bc8c

Text: Swedish (sv), case study, 394 words in, 387 delivered. Judged as Swedish throughout.

## 1. Every difference between input and delivered text

1. Headline: *Elm Quay testade gemensam felanmälningslogg i två hus* → *Elm Quays arbetsledare skulle upprepa loggförsöket*. **Change to what the claim says** — subject (the company's action → the supervisor's verdict), scope (*i två hus* gone), and topic (*felanmälningslogg* → *loggförsöket*, which drops the word that says what the log holds). Modality is handled well: *skulle upprepa* keeps Lind's conditional exactly as she gives it, and the verdict stays attributed to *arbetsledaren* rather than being asserted by the article. The secondary effect is a dangling definite: *loggförsöket* is a definite noun for a trial the reader meeting the headline alone has never been introduced to. The input headline was accurate, bounded and self-contained.
2. Body, first paragraph: *I september 2025 bestämde underhållsteamet att prova en gemensam logg i två av husen, och försöket pågick i åtta veckor.* → *I september 2025 bestämde underhållsteamet att under åtta veckor prova en gemensam logg i två av husen.* **Change to what the claim says** — modality and chronology. *försöket pågick i åtta veckor* is a statement of what happened; *bestämde … att under åtta veckor prova* is a statement of what was planned. The body of the article no longer asserts that the trial in fact ran eight weeks; only the standfirst does, and the later *31 felanmälningar under de åtta veckorna* implies it. Repairs a real defect — the clause restated the standfirst's *Under åtta veckor hösten 2025 provade underhållsteamet* — but pays for it with the fact.
3. Section 1, second paragraph: *Svale konfigurerade loggen och utbildade sex medarbetare vid två tillfällen. Vad loggen skulle lösa hade teamet redan bestämt.* → *Svale konfigurerade loggen enligt vad teamet redan hade bestämt och utbildade sex medarbetare vid två tillfällen.* **Change to what the claim says**, in two directions at once: something is lost (the object of the team's decision was *vad loggen skulle lösa*, now only *vad teamet redan hade bestämt*) and something is added (a relation the input did not state — that Svale configured the log *in accordance with* what the team had decided). The addition is supported by the quotation two lines down, *Kategorierna var våra, och Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen*, so it introduces no fact the text does not carry; but it is an addition, and it means the narrative still says a part of what the quotation is about to say, which is the defect it was meant to remove.
4. Final paragraph split: *Texten publiceras av Svale Systems, som var leverantör i försöket, och intervjun med Maya Lind gjordes via mejl.* moved out from behind the call to action into its own paragraph, wording identical. **Repair of a defect visible in the text**: the sponsorship disclosure and the email-interview disclosure were trailing a reading recommendation; standing alone they are harder to skip. Not mentioned anywhere in the reply.

Untouched: frontmatter, standfirst, byline, all four subheadings, all three quotations, every figure (640 lägenheter, september 2025, två hus, sex medarbetare, två tillfällen, 4 december 2025, 31 felanmälningar, två/tre arbetsdagar, åtta veckor), *Någon jämförelse med en annan leverantör finns inte*, the exclusions sentence, *noteringen kopplar uttryckligen inte skillnaden till programvaran*, *Kostnader, nöjdhet hos de boende och tid till avslutat arbete mättes inte*, *Försöket har ännu inte utvidgats till fler hus*, *Hon rekommenderar däremot inte Svale till alla bostadsbolag*, and the link.

## 2. Quoted speech

Q1 — *– Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var våra, och Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen, säger Maya Lind, arbetsledare för underhållet.*
- Wording: **unchanged**, word for word, including the dash, the trailing attribution and the apposition naming her role.
- One pass, for a Swedish reader: **yes**. Standard *pratminus* setting with the attribution at the end; *dem* takes *kategorierna* from the clause before it.
- Nothing to report: no change, no obstacle. Worth noting that the narrative edit above it (difference 3) reduced but did not remove the pre-echo of this quotation's content; the quotation itself is untouched and is not weakened by arriving second.

Q2 — *– Vi lade mer tid på att enas om kategorierna än på att lägga in de första anmälningarna. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan nästa hus kommer igång, säger Lind.*
- Wording: **unchanged**, word for word.
- One pass: **yes**. *mer tid på … än på …* is fully spelled out, no ellipsis to resolve; *Den tiden* points one clause back; *innan nästa hus kommer igång* is ordinary Swedish metonymy (the house for the work in it) and reads straight through.
- Nothing to report.

Q3 — *– Jag skulle välja att göra om försöket. Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser, säger hon.*
- Wording: **unchanged**, word for word. The hedge (*skulle välja*), the concession (*men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka*) and the following *Hon rekommenderar däremot inte Svale till alla bostadsbolag* survive, so the endorsement stays bounded.
- One pass: **yes**. *säger hon* refers back to Lind, named in the subheading's neighbourhood and in the previous section; a Swedish reader carries the referent without effort.
- This is the quotation the new headline rests on, and the headline treats it honourably: *arbetsledaren skulle upprepa* keeps both the attribution and the conditional, and adds no certainty she did not give.

All three passages came back with their wording intact, and none of them stops a Swedish reader.

## 3. R1 — **FAIL**, narrowly

This run has the best self-accounting of any delivery I would expect to see, and a body that came through almost untouched. It still fails, and it fails on the line the reader meets first.

To its credit:

- **Nothing was removed and nothing invented.** Every figure, every reservation, every disclosure and all three quotations survive word for word. The article's four refusals to over-claim — no supplier comparison, no causal attribution of the median difference, no cost/satisfaction/completion measurement, no general recommendation — are all intact, and the email-interview disclosure is now more visible than it was.
- **The headline rewrite respects the evidence.** Where a weaker review would have written the benefit as the article's own claim, this one kept it as the supervisor's and kept her conditional. That is the hardest thing to get right in a supplier-published case study, and it is right here.
- **The two claim shifts are reported precisely.** The reply states, without prompting, that the eight weeks moved from outcome to plan and that *vad loggen skulle lösa* became the looser *vad teamet redan hade bestämt*; it names the relation its own edit added and says which finding each repair answered. It also raises the headline's lost topic word as unresolved and says plainly that this came from its own repair. Checked against the diff, every one of these statements is accurate.

Why it fails:

- **The headline lost the article's subject.** *loggförsöket* does not say what the log was for. The reply concedes exactly this: a reader meeting the headline in a list *kan därför inte avgöra om texten angår henne*. It also lost *i två hus*, the bound on a trial inside a 640-apartment portfolio, which the reply does not mention. And in the definite form it introduces a referent the headline has not supplied. The input headline had none of these problems: it named the company, the thing, and the scope in 52 characters. A headline carrying both the angle and the subject was available inside the same limits, as the reply itself says. Trading an accurate, self-contained headline for one that is more pointed but less informative is a loss delivered, not a defect repaired.
- **A stated fact became a stated intention.** *försöket pågick i åtta veckor* is the kind of small, checkable claim this article is built from, and the body no longer makes it. The repetition it removed was real, but the clause could have gone without the fact going with it.
- **The reply's own summary line overstates the preservation.** *Ingen passage och inget påstående togs bort* is not quite true of *försöket pågick i åtta veckor* as an assertion, and the paragraph split of the disclosure is absent from the account altogether. The detailed paragraphs that follow are accurate enough to correct the first impression, which is why I weigh this lightly.

Removals: none outright, one fact demoted to a plan. Rejected losses: the headline's subject word and its two-building bound. Irreparable findings: none — the headline could have been settled from the article's own vocabulary, with nothing from the absent source. Changed claims: three, all reported, two of them reported with real precision. The accounting passes; the delivered text does not.
