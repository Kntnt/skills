# Source-support and translation comparison — case-study-sv-r2 / sc2

Draft: `draft.md` (Swedish, sv). Material: `material.md` (English, complete package including brief).
Contracts applied: `claims.md` (Claims), `quotations.md` (Quoting speech, incl. translation), `composition-sv.md` (Swedish composition).

Scope: every factual and attributed claim in the draft, including front matter, title, deck, byline, headings, body, quotations, implications and the link description. General editorial and mechanical review is excluded. The byline *Av Thomas Barregren* comes from the invocation, not the material; its absence from the material is not treated as a finding.

Method note: each translated quotation was first read in Swedish alone, then set beside the English. Each candidate difference was tested in both directions by naming a concrete case compatible with the material in which one statement holds and the other fails; a case was set aside only by quoting a supplied statement that excludes it.

### Deck (line 10)

**1.3** D: "Telefonsamtal på ett ställe, mejl på ett annat."
S: "Previously telephone reports and emails were stored separately."
Δ: Scope — the draft names two storage locations; the source asserts only that the two channels were apart. Thing named — *telefonsamtal* (calls) for "telephone reports"; the reports were made by telephone, so the same events are named.
Case tested: telephone reports were logged in three shift binders and emails in two mailboxes — still "stored separately", yet not "one place… another". Not excluded by any supplied sentence. Set aside nonetheless as defensible: read as an ordinary Swedish reader reads it in a deck, *på ett ställe … på ett annat* is the idiom for "kept apart", not a count of locations, and the sentence that follows ("Så såg det ut… innan") fixes that reading.
V: Defensible as written. No finding. Recorded because the case is not excluded by quotation.

**1.4** D: "Så såg det ut hos bostadsföretaget Elm Quay Housing innan underhållsteamet prövade en delad logg hösten 2025."

Case tested on *delad*: Swedish *delad* can read as "divided", the opposite of the intended sense, and the preceding sentence has just described division. Set aside: *delad* is the established Swedish rendering of "shared" for a shared resource, and *Så såg det ut … innan* frames the log as the remedy, which settles the reading; the body then uses the unambiguous *gemensam* (1.7). A preferred synonym is not an obstruction.
V: Supported. No finding.

Not excluded by quotation. Set aside: the deck frames the story's span, and the body dates each element in the material's own order without placing the run-up inside the trial.
Case tested on *visar*: the note "explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software", so a reading of *visar* as "demonstrates" would overclaim. Set aside: in "vad anteckningen visar", coordinated with "vad teamet gjorde", *visar* is a listing of contents, and the body attributes the non-attribution to the note explicitly (1.13).

## 3. Translated quotations

Each was read in Swedish alone first, with the English out of sight, then set beside it.

**Q1 (line 22)** — "Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var våra; Svale hjälpte oss att få in dem i loggen."
Read in Swedish alone: nothing is left for the reader to supply. *Kvällsskiftet / morgonskiftet* are the ordinary Swedish shift terms, written as compounds. "Kategorierna var våra" lands the ownership emphasis where the speaker put it. "Få in dem i loggen" is ordinary spoken Swedish for getting something entered into a system.
Beside the source: meaning, stance (ownership asserted, the supplier's part limited to help) and certainty are the same. No hedge removed or added, no fact, name or connection added. The rendering is Swedish syntax, not English syntax carried over.
No translation finding.

**Q2 (line 36)** — "Vi lade mer tid på att komma överens om kategorierna än på att mata in de första anmälningarna. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan nästa hus drar i gång."
Read in Swedish alone: the comparison is clear and the fronted object with V2 ("Den tiden skulle jag avsätta") is ordinary spoken Swedish. *Den tiden* has one available antecedent in the sentence before it, so the reader does not stop.
Tested: does "nästa hus drar i gång" leave a Swedish reader supplying a word Swedish does not supply there? The source has the same metonymy (a building does not start; the work in it does), and Swedish reaches for the same figure in rollout and building-project speech — *när nästa hus drar i gång*. The target language has a figure of its own here, so the figure carries over rather than needing to be unpacked. *Drar i gång* is a register match for the plain source, not an added colouring.
Beside the source: the comparison stays a comparison, "would" stays conditional, and the implicit "that time" is left implicit rather than resolved into a fluent interpretation.
No translation finding.

**Q3 (line 42)** — "Jag skulle välja att göra om testet. Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser."
Read in Swedish alone: this is a qualified endorsement — she would do it again, it helps, and she would add preparation time. No word is missing.
Tested: *göra om testet* can in principle be read as "redo it differently" rather than "do it again", which would shift stance from endorsement towards dissatisfaction, and the following clause about an extra week could pull that way. Set aside: with a *test* or *försök* as object, *göra om* is the ordinary Swedish for running it again (*jag skulle göra om det*), and "Jag skulle välja att…" preserves the source's framing as a choice she would make, which is the endorsement reading.
Tested: *en samlad bild av anmälningarna* for "one view of the reports" — an overview of the reports as against a single view of them. In this material the two cannot come apart: there is one log holding the reports. The rendering is idiomatic Swedish and neither widens nor narrows.
Beside the source: meaning, stance, certainty and the adversative structure are the same; the reservation the brief requires to survive is intact in her own voice.
No translation finding.

## 6. Completion status and unresolved findings

**Status: complete.** The whole draft was compared against the whole supplied material, including the brief. Confirmed findings: none — no source-support finding and no translation finding.

**Unresolved (an editorial question, not a defect with a repair):** how far the exclusion sentence at line 26 reaches. The source places "It excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial" between the count and the median figures, attached to the note as a whole; the draft places it at the end of the paragraph carrying the count, with the medians opening a new paragraph. A reader could take the medians as computed over all reports in the two periods rather than over the note's excluded set — for instance if emergencies were assigned the same day, a median including them would differ from the note's two working days. The source's own scoping is genuinely unsettled here: "work ordered before the trial" cannot sensibly exclude anything from the *preceding* period's median, which suggests the exclusion governs the count. Because the ambiguity is inherited from the material rather than introduced by the draft, it is reported as a question for the editor rather than as a finding, and no repair is proposed.
