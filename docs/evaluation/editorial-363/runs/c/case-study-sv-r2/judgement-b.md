## 1. Outcome

## 2. F1 — **fail**

**(a) The dropped disclosure.** The material states: "The supplier approved publication of this case as supplier-published material; it is not independent journalism." The delivered text says neither thing. It carries a byline, a standfirst, speech dashes and a reporter's third-person narration, ends on a link to the supplier's checklist, and never tells the reader who is publishing it. Every other element of the piece works to make it read as trade journalism, which is exactly the impression the material's last caveat exists to prevent. This is a dropped caveat, and it is the most consequential thing in the draft: the reader of a case study weighs it differently once they know the supplier published it, and this reader is not given the chance. The first checker saw the omission, recorded that "the draft neither discloses Svale's publisher role nor claims independence", and ruled it outside a source-support comparison; the reply then passed it to the editor as an open question. Nobody put it in the text.

**(b) "Någon rekommendation som gäller alla bostadsföretag ger hon inte."** The material's absence is specific: "Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company." The draft's sentence negates *any* recommendation holding for all housing companies, and moves it into the present. Lind could perfectly well tell every housing company to settle its categories before starting — her second quotation is close to that advice — while still not recommending Svale to all of them; then the material holds and the draft does not. The second checker tested this and set it aside because *någon rekommendation* takes its referent from the assessment it follows. That is a fair reading of how it lands, and it is why I put this second rather than first, but the wording asserts more than the material gives, and "Hon rekommenderar inte Svale till alla bostadsföretag" costs the same number of words.

**Checked and not counted:**

- "visa var varje felanmälan låg" for "show the status of each repair": both checkers tested the term in both directions, and I agree with them. *Var ett ärende ligger* is the ordinary Swedish for the stage a case has reached, and narrowing "repair" to "felanmälan" makes the claim no wider than the source's in a log that holds repair reports.
- "Akuta ärenden och arbeten som beställts dessförinnan är undantagna" attaches the exclusions to the count, which is where the material attaches them; *dessförinnan* takes its anchor from "under testet" in the sentence before.
- "Perioderna hade olika arbetsbelastning, och anteckningen tillskriver uttryckligen inte skillnaden programvaran" states the workload flatly and attributes only the refusal to the note — exactly the material's division, and the repair from the first round.
- The heading "Mediantiden gäller tilldelning, inte färdigt arbete" forecloses the assignment-to-completion slide in the one place a skimming reader could make it; "tid till färdigt arbete" then appears only among the unmeasured things.
- No causal claim and no causal hedge anywhere; no cost, satisfaction or rescue claim; "Någon jämförelse med en annan leverantör redovisas inte" keeps an absence as an absence; nothing is invented to complete the form; Elm Quay acts and Svale only configures, trains and helps.
- Byline "Av Thomas Barregren" is not in the material; the reply states it comes from the invocation and must be changed before publication. Not counted.

## 3. G2 — **fail**

The failure is a missing required part, not a weak one: **there is no publisher stance.** For a customer account the criterion asks for a truthful one, the brief names the publisher ("The publisher is the supplier, Svale Systems"), and the material supplies the words. The draft's closing sentence hands the reader the supplier's checklist and stops. A reader finishing this piece has no way to know it is supplier-published material rather than independent reporting, and the last thing they are given is a link to the supplier. That is the one omission in this draft that changes how everything before it should be read.

Everything else in G2 is well done, which is what makes the omission conspicuous:

- Headline, standfirst, byline, lead, four sections and an ending, in order. The standfirst does not repeat the headline — it sets the before-state, the season and the three things the piece will deliver — and the lead does not repeat the standfirst, opening instead on the portfolio, the date and the team's motive.
- The sections divide the work cleanly: choice and setup, what the note measured and did not, what happens next, and how the reader might weigh a trial of their own. Each heading describes its own section, and two of them (`Mediantiden gäller tilldelning, inte färdigt arbete`, `Beslutet om fler hus dröjer`) do real guarding work.
- Situation, action, results and appraisal are all present and in the customer's hands. Elm Quay is the acting party throughout — including in "Teamet höll telefonanmälan öppen för de boende", which the first comparison had to put right.
- The call to action is built from the single supplied offer, describes it as a document to read ("börja med att läsa igenom"), and keeps it optional.
- The author bridges before the last two quotations ("Samma kategorier är det Lind pekar på…", "Sitt eget omdöme om testet lämnar Lind med en reservation inbyggd") set each quotation up without stealing its content, and the second of them tells the reader in advance that the assessment is qualified — the material's own emphasis, carried into the structure.

## 4. L1 — **pass**

The Swedish is native and often good. The lead's fragment-plus-inversion ("Telefonsamtal på ett ställe, mejl på ett annat. Så såg det ut hos…") is a real Swedish opening, not a translated one. V2 holds throughout, including after fronted adverbials and objects ("Dittills hade telefonanmälningar och mejl lagrats var för sig", "Ännu har försöket inte utvidgats", "Den tiden skulle jag avsätta"). Compounds are closed (felanmälningslogg, leverantörsvalet, arbetsbelastning, boendenöjdhet, implementeringschecklista, kvällsskiftet). *Tillskriver … skillnaden programvaran* uses the Swedish construction rather than the English *attribute to*. Idiom is native: *var för sig*, *dra i gång*, *lägga in*, *väga ett eget försök*, *läsa igenom*.

Four strains, none of them an English import except the first:

- "en **delad** logg" in the standfirst is "shared" carried across; the body's own word, *gemensam*, is the Swedish one, and the draft uses it three lines later.
- "Sitt eget omdöme om testet lämnar Lind med en reservation inbyggd." Fronting the reflexive *sitt* ahead of the subject that binds it is a construction careful Swedish avoids, and *lämnar … med en reservation inbyggd* is strained on top of it. The sentence is doing a useful job and doing it awkwardly; a Swedish reader takes it, but not smoothly.
- "Teamet ska först kontrollera … **och bestämmer därefter**." The modal and the present indicative sit oddly together, and *bestämmer* is left without an object where Swedish wants *bestämmer sig* or *beslutar*.
- "och valde Svale Systems **efter det**" is flat where *därefter* was available.

Reader effect: an operations manager reads competent Swedish trade prose. The strains are editor-level polish, not translation tells.

## 5. Quoted speech

**Q1.** Draft: "– Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var våra; Svale hjälpte oss att få in dem i loggen, skriver Maya Lind, arbetsledare för underhållet."
Source: "We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log."
One pass: yes. Nothing to supply; the shift compounds are the ordinary ones and *få in dem i loggen* is how a Swede says getting something entered into a system.
Meaning, stance, certainty, voice: intact. The semicolon is kept, so the ownership claim and the limit on Svale's part stay in the single breath the speaker gave them. *Skriver* matches "All interviews occurred by email".

**Q2.** Draft: "– Vi lade mer tid på att komma överens om kategorierna än på att mata in de första anmälningarna. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan nästa hus drar i gång, skriver hon."
Source: "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts."
One pass: yes, and this is the best solution to the hardest line in the material that I have seen. The source's metonymy — the building standing for the work in it — is one Swedish reaches for itself with *dra i gång*, so the figure carries over instead of being unpacked. Nothing is supplied, and nothing is left hanging: "Den tiden" has exactly one antecedent.
Meaning, stance, certainty, voice: intact. The comparison keeps its Swedish *på att … än på att* frame, the conditional *skulle* is kept, the implicit referent stays implicit — which is right, because the material does not settle what starts.

**Q3.** Draft: "– Jag skulle välja att göra om testet. Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser, skriver hon."
Source: "I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation."
One pass: yes. *Göra om* carries a redo-it-differently reading in the abstract, but with a completed *test* as its object the repeat reading dominates, and "Jag skulle välja att" holds the source's framing as a choice she would make.
Meaning, stance, certainty, reservation, voice: intact. The marked "I would choose to" is not smoothed away; "hjälper oss" stays present-tense and first-person plural rather than being generalised; the reservation after *men* keeps its place and its weight. The bridge above it names the reservation in advance instead of burying it.

Missing quotations: none. All three appear, each carrying experience the narration does not — the motive, where the time actually went, and the qualified verdict.

**In one sentence:** every passage of quoted speech in this draft is speech a Swedish reader takes on one pass, with the source's meaning, stance, reservation and voice intact.

## 7. Delivery

**A delivery that should not have happened as it stands.** The machinery worked: three real defects were found and fixed on the first pass, the repairs were the minimal ones, the second comparison read the repaired prose in full and found nothing, and what went out is what it read — no editing slipped past the check. The prose is the most assured of its kind and the quotations are handled better than the material's hardest line usually allows. But the piece goes out as journalism about a supplier, paid for by that supplier, with no word to the reader that this is what it is, after a checker had noticed precisely that and been told it was somebody else's department. The fix is one sentence the material itself supplies. The run should either have added it or stopped and asked; declaring an unrelated pair of "open questions" in the reply while the piece carries no disclosure is not a substitute.
