# Judgement — w-8b739a

Judged: `delivered.md` (Swedish, ~390 words) against `work/source.md`.

## 1. Outcome

**Delivered.** Two full comparisons ran (`evidence/source-check-1/report.md`, `source-check-2/report.md`). The delivered prose is **identical** to the prose the second checker read — `source-check-2/draft.md` and `delivered.md` are byte-identical. All three of the first checker's findings were repaired before the second comparison; the second returned no finding of either kind.

## 2. F1 — **Fail**, on three counts, one of them an omission the whole piece turns on.

1. **The publisher stance is absent.** The material states: "The supplier approved publication of this case as supplier-published material; **it is not independent journalism**", and the brief states "The publisher is the supplier, Svale Systems." The draft says neither. It ends on a call to action for Svale's checklist with no indication anywhere that Svale is publishing it. A reader who opens this text takes it for an independently reported trade case; every figure, every caution and Lind's qualified assessment land with a weight they have not earned. This is the material's own caveat dropped, and it is the single most consequential difference between this draft and the others written from the same package. The first checker saw the omission and filed it as an editorial question on the reasoning that "an omission is not an unsupported claim"; the second did not raise it at all. It is also a G2 failure, treated below.

2. **"Någon rekommendation som gäller alla bostadsföretag ger hon inte."** The material says "Lind **did not recommend Svale** to every housing company." The draft negates *any* recommendation holding for all housing companies. A compatible case: asked what she would tell other housing companies, Lind says every one of them should agree its categories before starting — which is close to what her second quotation already says — while still declining to recommend Svale to all of them. The draft's sentence is then false and the material's true. The second checker tested this passage and set it aside on the ground that the preceding quotation settles the referent; the preceding quotation is about the trial, not about Svale, so it does not.

3. **"Telefonsamtal på ett ställe, mejl på ett annat."** The material says "telephone **reports** and emails were stored **separately**". Two changes in one line: phone calls are not reports and are not the thing stored, and *separately* becomes two specific locations. A compatible case: the emails sat in individual inboxes. The draft's own later sentences use *felanmälningar* and *telefonanmälan* correctly, so the looser wording is a stylistic choice in the lead rather than a term the piece works with.

Recorded, not counted:

- **"Akuta ärenden och arbeten som beställts dessförinnan är undantagna."** *Dessförinnan* has to reach past "under testet" and past "den 4 december 2025" to find "före försöket"; a reader may anchor it on the note's date. The exclusion itself is correctly attached to the count. Ambiguity rather than a changed claim.
- **"Teamet undersökte om loggen kunde visa var varje felanmälan låg."** *Var ett ärende ligger* is the ordinary Swedish for the stage a case has reached, and both checkers tested the substitution in both directions. I agree it does not come apart here.
- **"i två av husen"** implies the houses are the company's; softer than a genitive, and the material's "in two buildings" in a company's portfolio carries it.

What holds:

- Every figure exact and uncharacterised: 640, two, eight weeks, six, two sessions, 31, two against three working days, 4 December 2025.
- The causal line is not crossed. The heading "Mediantiden gäller tilldelning, inte färdigt arbete" heads off the conflation the brief forbids, and the body carries the workload confound and the note's explicit non-attribution, correctly flat where the material is flat ("Perioderna hade olika arbetsbelastning") and correctly attributed where the material attributes ("anteckningen tillskriver uttryckligen inte skillnaden programvaran").
- Assignment time is never turned into completion time.
- Unknown is kept apart from absent: "Någon jämförelse med en annan leverantör redovisas inte", "har inte mätts".
- "Ännu har försöket inte utvidgats. Teamet ska först kontrollera hur kategorierna fungerar för större reparationer och bestämmer därefter." keeps both the pending state and the decision, and the heading above it ("Beslutet om fler hus dröjer") claims nothing more.
- "Teamet höll telefonanmälan öppen för de boende" names the customer as the actor, as the brief requires.

## 3. G2 — **Fail.**

Headline, standfirst, byline, lead, four sections and an ending are all present and in order, and most of them do distinct work: the standfirst's before-state opening is the best lead-in of any draft written from this material, the four sections divide the story cleanly, and the customer's situation, action, results and appraisal are all there with Elm Quay acting throughout.

It fails on one required element: **there is no publisher stance at all.** The criterion asks for a truthful one; a supplier-published case that never says who publishes it does not have one. The reply is aware — it raises "Avsändaren nämns inte" among the open questions for the editor and calls it an editorial decision. It is not: the material supplies the fact, the brief names the publisher, and the ending already has the shape to carry it — one clause beside the checklist link would have done it. As it stands the ending is a bare call to action for the supplier's document, which is the one arrangement that makes the missing disclosure matter most.

Two further weaknesses, not decisive:

- **"## Så kan du väga ett eget försök"** promises the reader a way to weigh their own trial. The section beneath it gives Lind's assessment, her reservation and a link. The heading's job and the section's job are not the same job.
- **"Beslutet om fler hus dröjer"** heads a section that spends its middle on what took time during preparation. The bridge sentence works hard to connect them ("Samma kategorier är det Lind pekar på …") and the seam still shows.

## 4. L1 — **Fail.**

Much of the Swedish is good — the verbless lead-in, V2 throughout, solid compounds (*felanmälningslogg*, *testanteckningen*, *boendenöjdhet*, *implementeringschecklista*), correct double definiteness, and *tillskriver … skillnaden programvaran* with the Swedish double object rather than the English preposition. But three constructions stop a Swedish reader, and two of them are the author's own sentences in load-bearing positions:

- **"Så kan du väga ett eget försök"** (a section heading). Swedish *väga* does not take a trial as its object. You *väger för- och nackdelar*, or you *överväger ett försök*. The reader reaches the end of the heading and has to decide what was meant.
- **"Sitt eget omdöme om testet lämnar Lind med en reservation inbyggd."** The fronted object is fine; *med en reservation inbyggd* is not — *inbyggd* is a thing built into a product, and hung on *lämnar ett omdöme* it reads as a phrase assembled rather than spoken. The sentence introduces the article's most important quotation and is the hardest sentence in the piece to parse.
- **"Samma kategorier är det Lind pekar på när hon beskriver vad som tog tid."** A cleft missing its *som*, with a fronted predicate that Swedish would ordinarily write *Det är samma kategorier Lind pekar på …*. It is a bridge between two sections and it draws attention to itself.

These are not locale mechanics and not synonym preferences; they are places where the syntax does not come from the language the text is in. Three of them in 390 words, two in structural positions, is enough for this criterion.

## 5. Quoted speech

**Q1.** Draft: "– Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var våra; Svale hjälpte oss att få in dem i loggen, skriver Maya Lind, arbetsledare för underhållet." Source: "We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log."
*One pass* — yes. *Meaning, stance, certainty* — intact; the pluperfect with *redan*, the flat ownership claim, Svale subordinate. *få in dem i loggen* for "put them into the log" is a shade more colloquial than *lägga in* and says the same thing. *Voice* — hers. The semicolon is carried across from the English; a punctuation choice, outside this criterion.

**Q2.** Draft: "– Vi lade mer tid på att komma överens om kategorierna än på att mata in de första anmälningarna. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan nästa hus drar i gång, skriver hon." Source: "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts."
*One pass* — yes. **"innan nästa hus drar i gång"** is the right solution to the draft's hardest line: *dra i gång* is a figure Swedish uses for a thing or a stage getting under way, so the source's own ellipsis survives, the reader does not stop to supply a noun, and no noun is supplied for her. *Meaning, certainty, reservation* — comparative exact, *skulle* for *would*, nothing hedged or unhedged. *Voice* — *mata in* for "entering" is the word someone who does the job uses.

**Q3.** Draft: "– Jag skulle välja att göra om testet. Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser, skriver hon." Source: "I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation."
*One pass* — yes. *göra om testet* carries a second, do-it-differently reading in the abstract, but with a completed *test* as its object and *välja att* in front of it the repeat reading is the one a Swedish reader takes. *Meaning, stance, reservation* — the conditional endorsement, the present-tense benefit kept as hers, and the *men* clause last: all three intact.

**Missing quotations:** none; all three permitted quotations appear, none welded, none invented. Attribution is *skriver*, matching "All interviews occurred by email" — the one point on which this draft is stricter than most.

**In one sentence:** every passage of quoted speech in this draft is speech a Swedish reader takes on one pass with the source's meaning, stance, reservation and voice intact — the quotations are the strongest part of the piece.

## 7. Delivery

**A delivery that should not have happened.** Not because of the residue that was disclosed — there was none; the second comparison came back clean, and on the narrow question the comparisons ask, this draft is the tightest of the ones written from this material. It should not have shipped because the one thing the material insists on about the text itself, that it is supplier-published and not independent journalism, is missing from the text, and the run classed that as a matter of editorial taste. The brief names the publisher in its first paragraph; the material states the stance in its last; the draft's ending already carries the supplier's link. Whatever the source-support comparison is or is not for, a case study that reads as independent reporting because a required disclosure was dropped is not a draft that can be handed on.

The reply does name the omission, which is worth something: an editor who reads the "Öppna frågor" section will find it. But it is presented there as a question of whether "publiceringen kräver en tydlig avsändarmarkering", which invites the answer no, and it is the seventh item in a long report, after the byline note and the anatomy measurements. The fix is one clause.
