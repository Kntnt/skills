## 2. Quoted speech

Two passages, in order. Both are judged as a competent reader of **Swedish** reads them.

1. **”– Vi körde parallellt i sex veckor. Sedan gick lagret över, och först då såg vi hur mycket som hade legat i pärmar, säger Ove Ranneby.”**
   - **Wording:** returns identical, dash, punctuation and attribution included.
   - **One pass?** Yes. *Körde parallellt* is the ordinary Swedish way of saying two systems were run side by side, and the bridge above has just described exactly that. *Gick lagret över* is everyday metonymy — the warehouse standing for the people working in it — which Swedish uses of itself and which reads at speed however odd it looks parsed literally. *Hur mycket som hade legat i pärmar* is plain Swedish for stock records kept on paper; a reader supplies nothing from outside the text, because the opening paragraph has already established that the balance was kept outside the system. No stopping point.
   - Unchanged and unobstructed, so neither the reported-change nor the unresolved-obstacle test applies.

2. **”– Jag skulle göra om det. Att se samma saldo för båda lagren hjälper oss, men jag skulle ta en artikelgrupp i taget, säger Ranneby.”**
   - **Wording:** returns identical.
   - **One pass?** Yes. *Jag skulle göra om det* could in isolation be read either as *do it again* or as *redo it*, but the standfirst has already fixed the sense (*skulle göra om den, men börja med färre artikelgrupper*) and the *men*-clause that follows settles it inside the passage itself: he would do it again, differently. *Båda lagren* now has its support inside the body after difference 2. Nothing has to be supplied.
   - Unchanged and unobstructed.
