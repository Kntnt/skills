## 4. Cases tested and set aside

Recorded so the judgement is visible, with the reasoning that disposes of each.

1. **“what its own note records” (l.3).** If “it/its” in the dek resolves to the maintenance team rather than to Elm Quay Housing, the phrase makes the note the team's, where the source says only “Elm Quay's internal trial note”. A compatible case exists: the note could have been written up by a housing manager rather than by the maintenance team, three weeks after the trial ended. Set aside because “own” does the work of the source's “internal” — not the supplier's — rather than asserting authorship, and the body names the document exactly as the source does, “Elm Quay's internal trial note of 4 December 2025”. Defensible as written; no repair proposed.
2. **“two of the company's buildings” (l.7).** The source says only “Elm Quay Housing manages 640 flats”, so a managing agent that holds no title is compatible with it. Set aside because “the company's buildings” names the buildings in its charge, which management establishes, and the draft nowhere claims ownership, freehold or stock.
3. **Heading “The categories took more time than the first reports” (l.25).** The publication's voice states what only Lind asserts, and her time impression is nowhere corroborated by the note, which records counts and medians alone. Set aside because the section attributes it four lines below in her own words, the heading compresses without changing direction, magnitude or the qualifier “first”, and it adds no figure, evaluation or cause. The comparison is the customer's account of the customer's own work, which the brief keeps as the acting party's.
4. **“For eight weeks, in two buildings, they worked from one log.” (l.35).** Emergencies and “work ordered before the trial” are excluded from the note's 31, and a case is compatible in which such work ran outside the log entirely, so that not all work sat in one log. Set aside because the sentence restates the arrangement under trial — “a shared repair log in two buildings” — and claims one shared log to work from, not that every job passed through it; Lind's “Having one view of the reports helps us” supports that the one view existed.
5. **“the appraisal in it is Elm Quay's” (l.39).** The source calls the final quotation “her actual, qualified assessment”, an individual's; the team “will decide after checking how the categories work for larger repairs”, so the company has settled no corporate position. Set aside because the sentence's work is to separate publisher from appraiser — the appraisal comes from the customer side, not the supplier — and Lind gives it as Elm Quay's maintenance supervisor in a customer interview, which the brief keeps as the customer's voice. The draft does not turn it into a company recommendation, and the reservation stands at l.31.
6. **Absences asserted at l.23.** “no cost figures”, “no resident-satisfaction measurement”, “no completion times” are claims of absence and need support; they have it verbatim in “There are no cost, resident-satisfaction or completion-time measurements.”

## 5. Findings

### Finding 1 — l.27: “the order of the work” names something other than what Lind says she would change, and the claim is exhaustive over her views

- **Draft:** “What Maya Lind would do differently concerns the order of the work rather than the software.”
- **Source, in its own words:** “I would set that time aside before the next building starts.” / “I would leave an extra week for preparation.” / “We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports.” / “Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company.”
- **What differs:** the thing named, and whose knowledge it is. Both of her change statements reserve time — set that time aside in advance, leave an extra week — not reorder work. And “rather than the software” makes the sentence exhaustive over what she would change, where the material licenses only what she says: it offers her “complete usable quotations”, which settles what is quotable, not what her full view is.
- **Case where the source holds and the draft fails (order):** the material's own sequence has the team design its categories, then Svale configure and train, then the eight-week trial run — so categories already came first, and “We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports” fits a team that did the agreeing before the first entries. In that case nothing at all would be reordered: her change is that the same preparation gets a week booked for it in the schedule. “I would leave an extra week for preparation” is a quantity of time, not a position in a sequence, on any reading.
- **Case where the source holds and the draft fails (exhaustiveness):** Lind could also want the log to handle larger repairs differently — the team “will decide after checking how the categories work for larger repairs”, and she “did not recommend Svale to every housing company” — with that view simply not among the usable quotations. Then all three quotations stand and “rather than the software” is false. No supplied statement excludes either case: the material says the quotations are complete and usable, never that they exhaust her opinions.
- **Smallest supported repair:** “The change Maya Lind names is the time set aside for preparation, not the software.” This keeps the contrast with the software that the section is built on, scopes it to what she names, and renames the change as the reserving of time.
