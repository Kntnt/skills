### Finding 1 — the workload caveat is moved into the note's voice, and "explicitly" is dropped (l.21) — CONFIRMED

- **Draft:** "The note states that the two periods carried different workloads, and it does not attribute the difference to the software."
- **Source:** "The periods had different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software."
- **What differs:** whose assertion it is. The material states the workload difference in its own voice and attributes only the non-attribution to the note. The draft makes the note the source of both. It also drops "explicitly", so what the material gives as an express refusal becomes, in the draft, consistent with the note simply being silent on causation.
- **Case in which the source holds and the draft fails:** the workload difference is known from the maintenance team's rota and job records, while the two-page internal trial note records the 31 reports, the exclusions and the two medians, and says only "the note does not attribute this difference to the software" without mentioning workload at all. Nothing in the material describes the note's contents beyond the figures, the exclusions and the non-attribution, so nothing excludes this. In that case "The periods had different workloads" is true and "The note states that the two periods carried different workloads" is false.
- **Case in the other direction:** the draft's clause "it does not attribute the difference to the software" is true wherever the source's "explicitly does not attribute" is; it is the weaker of the two and cannot outrun it. The direction that fails is the attribution of the workload fact.
- **Why it matters:** the material requires the customer's reservation to survive, and the draft's version both relocates it and softens the strongest word in it.
- **Smallest supported repair:** "The two periods carried different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software."

### Finding 2 — Lind is given a speech act and an audience the material does not supply (l.27) — CONFIRMED

- **Draft:** "Maya Lind's advice for another maintenance team concerns the order of the work rather than the software."
- **Source:** Lind: "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts." And: "Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company."
- **What differs:** the modality and the addressee. The quotation is a first-person statement about what she herself would do at Elm Quay's own next building. The draft turns it into advice, and into advice directed at a different organisation's maintenance team. "before the next building starts" is Elm Quay's next building — the same 640-flat stock the draft has just described as having more buildings than the two in the trial.
- **Case in which the source holds and the draft fails:** Lind answers the email interview entirely about her own team's sequencing at Elm Quay, declines to generalise (consistent with the material's "Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company"), and offers no counsel to anyone outside the company. Every supplied quotation is compatible with this, and no supplied statement says she addressed or advised other companies. In that case "Lind said she would set that time aside before the next building starts" is true and "Maya Lind's advice for another maintenance team" is false.
- **Case in the other direction:** were the draft's sentence true — she did advise other maintenance teams — the material's "Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company" would still hold, so the two are not equivalent in that direction either; the draft adds, it does not restate.
- **Why it matters:** it presents a customer as counselling the reader's peers on the supplier's page, which is the one direction the material's reservation is there to block.
- **Smallest supported repair:** "What Maya Lind would do differently concerns the order of the work rather than the software." (Alternatively: "Maya Lind's note on preparation concerns the order of the work rather than the software.")

### Finding 3 — "stored separately" is sharpened into two unified stores (l.7) — CONFIRMED

- **Draft:** "Telephone reports were stored in one place and emails in another."
- **Source:** "Previously telephone reports and emails were stored separately."
- **What differs:** the thing described. The source says the two channels were kept apart from each other. The draft additionally says each channel had a single store of its own.
- **Case in which the source holds and the draft fails:** telephone reports are written on pads at each of the two buildings' offices while emails sit in individual staff inboxes — several stores per channel. That is squarely "stored separately", and no supplied statement says either channel had a single store. In that case the source's sentence is true and "in one place… in another" is false.
- **Case in the other direction:** the draft's arrangement is one way of being stored separately, so it does not fail wherever the source holds — the asymmetry is the draft's added specificity.
- **Why it matters:** `claims.md` l.5 counts background as a claim needing support, and the added detail is exactly the sort that makes the "before" state sound tidier than the material establishes.
- **Smallest supported repair:** "Telephone reports and emails were stored separately."

### Finding 4 — a season is supplied that the material does not (l.7) — CONFIRMED, minor

- **Draft:** "…trialled a shared repair log in two of the company's buildings in the autumn of 2025."
- **Source:** "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial…"; "The trial lasted eight weeks"; "Elm Quay's internal trial note, 4 December 2025".
- **What differs:** the material gives a decision month and a note date and never dates the trial itself or locates Elm Quay. "Autumn" is an inference that requires both a start date and a hemisphere.
- **Case in which the source holds and the draft fails:** Elm Quay Housing is in the southern hemisphere — no supplied statement gives its location, and the brief's only geographic content is the target variety of English, which fixes no location. An eight-week trial decided in September 2025 and written up on 4 December 2025 then ran in spring, not autumn. A second, independent case: the trial began in mid-October and ran to the note's date, putting a substantial part of it past the end of autumn on the meteorological reckoning. Neither is excluded by any supplied statement.
- **Case in the other direction:** the supplied dates hold under any season label, so the season adds information rather than restating it.
- **Why it matters:** small, but it is a circumstantial detail with no source, and the draft has already given the reader the supplied date in the summary, so the inference buys nothing.
- **Smallest supported repair:** "…in two of the company's buildings in 2025" (or delete the phrase — "In September 2025" in the summary already dates the work).

## Editorial questions — reported, not repaired

These are places where the line is genuinely unsettled, or where the draft is defensible as written and the alternative is a preference. None is a defect.

1. **Heading l.25, "The categories took more time than the first reports."** This restates in the publication's voice what the material supplies only as Lind's first-person recollection ("We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports"), and no independent record of time spent is supplied. Against that: the heading sits immediately above "Maya Lind's advice… concerns the order of the work" and her verbatim quotation, so an ordinary reader meets it as a preview of her account, and it makes no claim about the software. Reported as a question of how far a heading may carry a speaker's experience unattributed, not as a finding. Were it to be changed, the smallest form is "Lind: the categories took more time than the first reports."
2. **l.39, "the appraisal in it is Elm Quay's."** The appraisal is Maya Lind's, and the material is explicit that the organisation has not settled its position ("the team will decide after checking how the categories work for larger repairs"). Read as an ordinary reader reads a publisher's disclosure, however, the sentence says which *side* the judgement comes from — the customer's, not the supplier's — and that is exactly what the material establishes. Defensible as written; flagged because a reader could take it as a corporate endorsement the material does not record.
3. **l.23, "this account carries no comparison with another supplier."** Supported and safely weaker than the supplied "No comparison with another supplier is available." Noted only because the stronger supplied form preserves the state of knowledge more exactly at no extra length.
4. **l.34/35, "Lind's own summary of the trial."** The material's word is "assessment" ("her actual, qualified assessment"), which is a valuation; "summary" reads as a recap. The quotation carries its own qualification, so nothing is lost in substance. Preference, not a defect.
5. **Length.** The brief asks for approximately 400 words "as material allows"; the draft runs somewhat over. Word count is general editorial review and outside this comparison — recorded here only to confirm that no passage in the draft is padded with unsupported material, which was the claim-side risk.
