## 2. F1 — **fail**

The figures section of this draft is the most careful of anything here: the medians are named as medians, the endpoint is named as assignment, "explicitly" survives, the workload difference is in the material's own voice, the three absences are listed in the material's own modality, the supplied caveat that no comparison with another supplier exists is carried, and the writer adds an explicit guard — "The medians cover the time taken to assign a repair, not the time taken to finish it." Nothing there fails.

The failure is one sentence, and it is the sentence that tells the reader what the customer thinks of the product.

1. **"What Maya Lind would do differently concerns the order of the work rather than the software."** — *Thing named, and scope, both changed.* Source: "I would set that time aside before the next building starts"; "I would leave an extra week for preparation"; and, in the material's own voice, "Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company."
   - *Order of the work.* Neither of her statements reorders anything. Both reserve a quantity of time — a week, time set aside in advance. The material's own sequence already has the categories designed before the trial, so a case is fully compatible with the package in which nothing at all changes position and the same preparation simply gets a week booked for it.
   - *"Rather than the software."* This is the damaging half. It is exhaustive over her views: it tells the reader that the software is not among the things she would change. The material licenses no such statement. It supplies her "complete usable quotations" — what is quotable, not the whole of what she holds — and it records, in its own voice, that she did not recommend Svale to every housing company, and that the team has not yet decided whether the categories will hold up for larger repairs. A supplier-published case that announces its one critical voice has no criticism of the supplier's product, when the material records her withholding a general recommendation of it, has moved the customer's stance in the publisher's direction. That is the movement the material's judging section exists to prevent.
2. **"the appraisal in it is Elm Quay's"** (closing disclosure) — *whose assertion.* The material calls the closing quotation "her actual, qualified assessment" — an individual's, given in a customer interview. The company has settled nothing: "the team will decide after checking how the categories work for larger repairs." Read charitably the sentence only says which *side* the appraisal comes from, and both checkers read it that way. But it sits in the disclosure line, the one sentence in a supplier-published piece a reader reads as a statement of standing, and there it reads as a corporate endorsement the material does not record. Minor beside the first item, and pulling the same way.

Recorded and not counted: "Svale configured the log; Elm Quay chose what went in it" compresses "designed its categories" into choosing the log's contents — the categories structure the log rather than constituting its entries, but the two sentences beneath resolve it. "Maya Lind … describes where the line between the two ran" is answered by the quotation's second sentence though not by its first. "The trial has not expanded" drops the material's "yet", which the following clause restores. The heading "The categories took more time than the first reports" states in the publication's voice a time impression only Lind supplies and the note does not corroborate; her words stand four lines below, direction and magnitude unchanged, so I do not report it, but it is the closest call among the non-findings.

## 3. G2 — **fail**

Every required part is present and in order — headline, standfirst, byline, lead, four sections, ending — and the customer account is complete: situation (separate telephone and email storage, shifts unable to see the same information, 640 flats), action (the team decided, designed its categories, kept telephone reporting open; Svale configured and trained), results (31 reports under exclusions, two days against three with the workload caveat and the note's explicit non-attribution, the named absences), appraisal (the preparation lesson, the withheld general recommendation, the qualified verdict). The publisher stance is present and truthful — "This account is published by Svale Systems" — and placed before the call to action, which is built from the one supplied route and describes it as a document to read.

What fails is the requirement that the parts do **distinct** jobs. The standfirst and the lead do the same job, twice, four lines apart:

> Standfirst: "Elm Quay Housing manages 640 flats, and its maintenance team wanted staff on different shifts to see the same repair information. In September 2025 the team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings."
> Lead: "Telephone reports and emails were stored separately. Against that background, Elm Quay Housing's maintenance team trialled a shared repair log in two of the company's buildings in 2025…"

The standfirst has taken the lead's material — the size of the company, the motive, the date, the decision, the two buildings — and the lead can then only restate it with one fact less. A reader who has read the standfirst gets nothing from the lead but "stored separately" and the supplier choice, and has to read the same clause twice to find them. The standfirst is functioning as a lead and the lead as a weaker second lead.

The motive is then stated a third time, in the last section: "The team's aim was that whoever came on duty should see the same repair information" — which is the standfirst's second clause again, and also what the first quotation says in the speaker's own words. In a piece of roughly 440 words, one idea occupies four separate statements, and the section that should be closing the account opens by restating its premise.

## 4. L1 — **pass**

Assured British English with a distinctive, slightly austere register. "The team's aim was that whoever came on duty should see the same repair information" uses the mandative subjunctive correctly and idiomatically. "For eight weeks, in two buildings, they worked from one log" is a well-judged three-beat sentence that lands the whole account. The framing lines before each quotation ("describes where the line between the two ran", "Lind's own summary of the trial:") let the speech stand as its own paragraph without an attribution verb, which is a legitimate and rather good British feature convention. Nothing reads as imported syntax.

Two seams. "Against that background," is report-writing rather than journalism and slows the lead's first move. And the heading "Elm Quay's own note records 31 reports, and no cost figures" is a zeugma — "records … no cost figures" makes the note the recorder of an absence, and a reader stops on it for a beat before working out that nothing was recorded because nothing was measured.

## 5. Quoted speech

Source language is English and the draft is en_GB, so nothing is translated; all three quotations are reproduced word for word, in double marks, each standing as its own paragraph under a framing sentence.

1. **"We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log."** — Source: the first complete usable quotation, identical. *One pass:* yes. The metonymy of a shift standing for the staff on it is one British English uses of itself, and the semicolon joins two independent clauses in the ordinary way. *Substance:* intact — "helped us" stays help rather than doing, "were ours" keeps the ownership claim. The framing sentence promises a description of the division of labour, which the quotation's second sentence delivers and its first does not; the promise is slightly larger than what arrives, but nothing in the speech is moved.
2. **"We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts."** — Source: the second quotation, identical. *One pass:* yes; "that time" is settled by the sentence before it, and the comparative ellipsis is ordinary English. *Substance:* the words are intact and the conditional "I would" survives — but the bridge above them, "concerns the order of the work rather than the software", tells the reader in advance what the quotation means, and tells them something the quotation does not say. The speech is unmoved; its framing moves it. That is an F1 matter, recorded there, not a quotation defect.
3. **"I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation."** — Source: the third quotation, identical. *One pass:* yes; "one view of the reports" is plain British idiom. *Substance:* intact, including the "but" clause that carries the whole reservation. Introducing it as "Lind's own summary of the trial" keeps it hers and does not derive it from the medians, which the material expressly requires ("It is not an inference from the figures"); the material's own word is "assessment", and "summary" is flatter, but nothing in the speech is softened.

No quotation the material offers is missing: three are supplied as complete and usable, and three are used.

**In one sentence:** every passage of quoted speech in this draft is speech a reader of British English takes on one pass, with the source's meaning, stance, reservation and voice intact — though the second is introduced by a sentence that tells the reader more than the speech supports.
