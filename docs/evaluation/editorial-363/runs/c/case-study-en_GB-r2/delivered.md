# Elm Quay trialled one repair log across shifts

Elm Quay Housing manages 640 flats, and its maintenance team wanted staff on different shifts to see the same repair information. In September 2025 the team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings. Here is how it ran the eight-week trial, what its own note records, and what its supervisor would do differently.

By Thomas Barregren

Telephone reports and emails were stored separately. Against that background, Elm Quay Housing's maintenance team trialled a shared repair log in two of the company's buildings in 2025, having chosen Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair.

## Svale configured the log; Elm Quay chose what went in it

The maintenance team designed its own categories and kept telephone reporting open for residents. Svale Systems configured the log and trained six members of staff over two sessions.

Maya Lind, Elm Quay's maintenance supervisor, describes where the line between the two ran.

"We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log."

## Elm Quay's own note records 31 reports, and no cost figures

The trial ran for eight weeks. Elm Quay's internal trial note of 4 December 2025 records 31 repair reports entered in the log, excluding emergencies and work ordered before the trial.

Median time from report to assignment was two working days during the trial and three working days in the preceding eight-week period. The two periods carried different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software.

There are no cost figures, no resident-satisfaction measurement and no completion times, and this account carries no comparison with another supplier. The medians cover the time taken to assign a repair, not the time taken to finish it.

## The categories took more time than the first reports

What Maya Lind would do differently concerns the order of the work rather than the software.

"We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts."

The trial has not expanded beyond the two buildings. The team will decide after checking how the categories work for larger repairs, and Lind did not recommend Svale Systems to every housing company.

## Lind would run the trial again, with more preparation

The team's aim was that whoever came on duty should see the same repair information. For eight weeks, in two buildings, they worked from one log. Lind's own summary of the trial:

"I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation."
