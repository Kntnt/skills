## 2. F1 — **Fail**

### Cited defects

**1. The bridge misnames what Lind says she would change, and closes off what else she might think.** "What Maya Lind would do differently concerns the order of the work rather than the software." Both of her supplied statements about change reserve time: "I would set that time aside before the next building starts" and "I would leave an extra week for preparation". Neither reorders anything, and the material's own sequence already has the categories agreed before the first entries, so a reading is compatible with the package in which nothing moves and the same preparation simply gets a week booked for it. "Rather than the software" then goes further and makes the sentence exhaustive over her views; the material offers her *complete usable quotations*, which settles what is quotable, not what she thinks. She also "did not recommend Svale to every housing company", which is a view about the supplier the sentence implicitly denies she holds. Delivered knowingly unrepaired, with the defect named and no fidelity asserted for it.

**2. An individual's qualified assessment is handed to the company.** "This account is published by Svale Systems; **the appraisal in it is Elm Quay's**." The material calls the closing quotation "her actual, qualified assessment" — Lind's — and is explicit that the organisation has settled nothing: "the team will decide after checking how the categories work for larger repairs." The sentence's job is to separate publisher from appraiser, which is legitimate, but it does that job by promoting one supervisor's hedged verdict to a corporate one. In the disclosure line of a supplier-published case, that is the shift that costs the reader most: "Elm Quay's appraisal" reads as the customer organisation standing behind the product, which the material declines to say. The second checker tested this and set it aside; I do not.

The sole support is Lind's first-person recollection, and the note — the only record in the package — holds counts and medians and nothing about time spent. The run's own reply concedes the point in its closing notes ("The customer's own time impression stands alone"). The body does attribute it four lines down, which limits the damage; the heading still states it flat.

**4. The absence of cost figures is pinned to the note (minor).** "## Elm Quay's own note records 31 reports, and no cost figures." The material's statement is global — "There are no cost, resident-satisfaction or completion-time measurements" — and says nothing about what the note was or was not trying to record. The heading makes the note the place where cost figures would have been.

**5. Two unrelated facts joined by "and" (minor).** "The team will decide after checking how the categories work for larger repairs, and Lind did not recommend Svale Systems to every housing company." The material keeps these apart. Conjoined in one sentence, the reservation reads as bearing on the expansion decision, which is a connection the package does not make. ## 3. G2 — **Fail**

Every required part is present and in order — headline, standfirst, byline, lead, four sections, ending — and the account requirements are met: the customer's situation (separate telephone and email records, shifts blind to each other), its action (two buildings, own categories, telephone kept open, six staff trained), its results (31 reports, the medians with the workload caveat, the four absences), its appraisal (all three quotations, with the reservation). The customer is the acting party in every sentence that has one; Svale configures, trains and publishes. A publisher stance is present and truthful as far as it goes — "This account is published by Svale Systems" — though it stops short of the material's "it is not independent journalism". The call to action is built from the one supplied offer, at the supplied URL, described correctly as a document to read.

The failure is that the parts do not do distinct jobs at the top of the piece.

**The lead restates the standfirst.** The standfirst's second sentence is "In September 2025 the team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings." The lead's main clause is "Elm Quay Housing's maintenance team trialled a shared repair log in two of the company's buildings in 2025." Same actor, same act, same scope, a vaguer date. The lead is two sentences long, so what it adds over the standfirst is one clause about storage and one about the choice of Svale. The reader effect is a stall exactly where the piece should be gathering speed: having just been told what happened, they are told it again, less precisely, and the sentence they are reading opens with the connector "Against that background", which promises a turn and then delivers a repetition.

**The motive is stated three times.** Standfirst: "wanted staff on different shifts to see the same repair information." Section one, in the quotation: "We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done." Ending: "The team's aim was that whoever came on duty should see the same repair information." The third is meant as a closing callback, and a callback is a legitimate device, but it lands as the third statement of one idea in a 440-word piece, and it is the flattest of the three — the quotation already said it better 300 words earlier.

**The standfirst signposts rather than draws.** "Here is how it ran the eight-week trial, what its own note records, and what its supervisor would do differently" is a table of contents. It is accurate and it is honest about the limits, which is worth something; it does not give the reader a reason to go on that the headline had not already given them.

## 4. L1 — **Pass**

Idiomatic British English throughout, with no imported phrasing and no translationese. "Trialled" as a verb, "six members of staff", "weighing the same step", the singular agreement on "the team", and the handling of the medians ("two working days during the trial and three working days in the preceding eight-week period") are all native. The plainest sentences are the best ones: "The medians cover the time taken to assign a repair, not the time taken to finish it" does real work in fifteen words without hedging, and "The trial has not expanded beyond the two buildings" states an unflattering fact without apology.

Two places where the register goes stiff without breaking. "Against that background" is a report connector rather than a journalistic one, and it sits at the head of the lead, where the prose can least afford to sound administrative. "Svale Systems' implementation checklist is there to read" is understandable but not a phrase a British desk would reach for; "is worth reading" or "is the document to read" are the natural forms. Neither is an error, and neither is a foreign construction — they are flatnesses, not imports.

The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log."** — renders the material's first complete usable quotation, word for word. The introduction — "Maya Lind, Elm Quay's maintenance supervisor, describes where the line between the two ran" — fits the second sentence squarely and the first only loosely, since the shift point is not about the line between Svale and Elm Quay; a small mismatch, not a distortion.
2. **"We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts."** — the second supplied quotation, word for word. One pass: yes; the elliptical comparative is how English does this and no reader stops at it. Her meaning, modality and horizon are intact inside the quotation marks. The framing above it moves her, though: the reader is told her change "concerns the order of the work", then reads a quotation about reserving time, and has to reconcile the two. The quotation is not altered; what it is presented as having said is.
3. **"I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation."** — the third supplied quotation, word for word. One pass: yes. The reservation stays attached where the source attaches it, and the certainty is not raised. It is introduced as "Lind's own summary of the trial", where the material's word is "assessment" — a summary recaps, an assessment judges — which understates what she is doing; the quotation's own hedges survive, so nothing in substance is lost.

**Missing.** None. All three quotations the material offers and permits are used in full, nothing is welded across remarks, no hedge is removed, no fourth quotation appears.

**One sentence:** Every passage of quoted speech in this draft is speech a British-English reader takes on one pass with the source's meaning, stance, reservation and voice intact inside the quotation marks — though the bridge before the second one tells the reader it says something other than what it says.
