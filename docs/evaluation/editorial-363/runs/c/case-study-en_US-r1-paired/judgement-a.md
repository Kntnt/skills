The defect-finding is the best of what I can see in this text, and the account of it is exemplary. Six of the seven changes answer something a reader can see: two subheadings that restated the sentences beneath them, a clause that restated the quotation above it, a standfirst tail that promised nothing, a day-month-year date in an en_US text, and a supplier call to action welded to the customer's own appraisal. All three quotations are verbatim. Every sentence that bounds the text's assertions comes back exactly as it arrived — no supplier comparison recorded, the count's exclusions, the different workloads, the note's refusal to attribute the difference to the software, "Assignment is not completion", the unmeasured completion time, cost and resident satisfaction, and the unexpanded trial.

It fails on the two claims it shipped beyond the text. #5 is the decisive one: the section exists to say that nothing is settled, and its heading — the line a scanning reader takes — now presupposes that the wider rollout is merely waiting. #2 is smaller but the same kind: a duration the text reports as a fact about the trial becomes part of what the team decided at the start. Both survive into the delivered text, which is what a person receives.
