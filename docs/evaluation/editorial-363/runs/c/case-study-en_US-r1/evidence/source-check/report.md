## 3. Considered and set aside (not findings)

**3.1 "says" for quotations gathered by email.** The material states "All interviews occurred by
email", and the quotation guidance asks the text to signal what the material actually is. Here the
material calls the bullets "complete usable quotations", so the words are the speaker's exact
wording, which is what the guidance protects; "says" attributes a statement without asserting a
spoken occasion, and the draft adds no scene, emotion or remembered dialogue. Defensible as
written. Whether to disclose the email channel is an editorial question, not a source-support
defect.

**3.2 "check the categories on larger repairs" vs "checking how the categories work for larger
repairs" (heading, line 25).** A reading of "on" as "applied to actual larger repairs" would differ
from a desk review of how the categories work for them. But "check X on Y" reads naturally in en_US
as "check X with respect to Y", and the body sentence immediately below restates the source
verbatim, so no reader is left with the narrower reading. Defensible as written.

**3.3 Supplier publication not disclosed in the draft.** The material states "The supplier approved
publication of this case as supplier-published material; it is not independent journalism." The
draft makes no claim of independence — it names Svale Systems throughout in the third person, never
uses "we" for the supplier, and the customer remains the acting party. An absent disclosure is an
omission rather than an unsupported or changed claim, so it is outside this comparison; flagged here
only so the decision is visible.

**3.4 "this account records no comparison with another supplier" (line 11).** Self-reference by the
text to its own sourcing. It narrows rather than widens the source and is entailed by it (#14).

**3.5 Quote approval.** The material records supplier approval of publication; it says nothing about
Lind approving her quotations, and the draft neither claims approval nor treats its absence as
resolved. Correctly left outside.

## 4. Findings

### Finding 1 — source support (minor): the checklist is attributed to Svale Systems

- **Draft (line 29):** "Operations managers weighing a similar test can read [Svale Systems'
  implementation checklist](https://example.invalid/svale/checklist)."
- **Source:** "The optional next step is the supplied implementation checklist at
  https://example.invalid/svale/checklist; it is a document to read, not a consultation booking or a
  product trial." The brief elsewhere says only "The publisher is the supplier, Svale Systems."
- **What differs:** whose document it is. The material identifies the checklist by its URL and calls
  it "the supplied implementation checklist"; it never states who wrote or publishes it. The draft's
  possessive asserts Svale Systems' authorship or ownership.
- **Case in which the draft fails and the material holds:** the checklist at that address is an
  implementation checklist written by a housing-sector trade association, merely hosted or
  republished under the supplier's path. Nothing supplied excludes this: the brief's only statements
  about the link are the sentence quoted above and the fact that Svale Systems is the publisher of
  *this case*, not of the checklist.
- **Case in the other direction:** if the checklist is indeed Svale's own, both statements hold —
  the difference is one-directional, which is why this is recorded as minor rather than central.
- **Smallest supported repair:** drop the possessive — "can read the [implementation
  checklist](https://example.invalid/svale/checklist)". The link text still accurately describes the
  destination as a document to read, and the audience, action and URL are unchanged.
