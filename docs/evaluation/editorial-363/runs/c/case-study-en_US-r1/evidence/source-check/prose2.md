# Elm Quay gave two buildings one repair log

Eight weeks, two buildings and 31 repair reports: Elm Quay Housing tested a shared repair log from Svale Systems, with categories its own maintenance team designed. The trial note records a shorter median time from report to assignment, but does not attribute it to the software. Here is what the team learned.

By Thomas Barregren

In September 2025, the maintenance team at Elm Quay Housing, which manages 640 apartments, decided to test a shared repair log in two buildings. Telephone reports and emails had until then been stored separately, and the team wanted staff on different shifts to see the same information.

## The maintenance team designed its own categories

Elm Quay chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair; this account records no comparison with another supplier. Svale configured the log and trained six staff in two sessions.

“We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log,” says maintenance supervisor Maya Lind.

The team designed those categories itself, and kept telephone reporting open for residents. “We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts,” Lind says.

## The note counts 31 reports, and names what it leaves out

Elm Quay’s internal trial note, dated 4 December 2025, records 31 repair reports entered during the trial. It excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial.

Median time from report to assignment was two working days during the trial, against three in the preceding eight-week period. The periods carried different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software.

Assignment is not completion. The trial produced no measurement of how long repairs took to finish, and none of cost or resident satisfaction.

## Elm Quay will check the categories on larger repairs

The trial has not yet expanded. The team will decide after checking how the categories work for larger repairs.

“I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation,” Lind says. Operations managers weighing a similar test can read the [implementation checklist](https://example.invalid/svale/checklist).
