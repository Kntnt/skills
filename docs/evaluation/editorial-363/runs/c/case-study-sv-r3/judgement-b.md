# Judgement — w-f53fcb

## 1. Outcome

**Delivered**, with four disclosed unrepaired findings. **Two** full comparisons ran (`evidence/source-check-1/report.md`, `source-check-2/report.md`); the reply states that the first had to be run again because the report could not be written under `.git/` and the reply carrying it was cut off, and the artefacts bear that out (`source-check-1/report-reply.txt` holds an API error, and the second checker's report ends with the same refused write). The delivered prose is **identical** to the prose the second checker read (`source-check-2/draft.md` matches `delivered.md` from the headline down).

## 2. F1 — **fail**

Four items stand in the delivered text. None is grave alone; together they are four places where the material's carefulness is what got lost.

**(a) "Teamet ska först se efter hur kategorierna fungerar för större reparationer."** The material says "the team **will decide** after checking how the categories work for larger repairs". The draft drops the decision. With *först* in front of it, and with "Försöket har ännu inte utvidgats **till fler hus**" immediately above, a reader takes expansion as scheduled and the check as the last box to tick. The material leaves open that the team decides not to expand at all. This is the item that changes what a reader believes about the story's future, and it is my first-ranked defect.

**(b) "Någon jämförelse med en annan leverantör finns inte."** The material says "No comparison with another supplier **is available**." Unavailable is not non-existent: Elm Quay may well have compared Svale with another supplier and the comparison simply not be in the package. The draft asserts the stronger, cleaner thing. One word — *redovisad* — restores it.

**(c) "Den interna noteringen … redovisar 31 felanmälningar under de åtta veckorna."** The material says the note "says that 31 repair reports **were entered**". Entered into the log, that is, in a trial that kept telephone reporting open alongside. Without that, 31 reads as every fault report Elm Quay received in eight weeks, which is a different and much stranger claim about a 640-flat portfolio, and it makes the exclusion sentence that follows look like an exclusion from all reports rather than from the count.

**(d) "om loggen kunde visa status på varje ärende."** The material says "the status of each **repair**". In property management an *ärende* is wider than a repair — it takes in enquiries, complaints and matters that never become work — so the draft claims a capability over a larger set than the material tested. Minor, and the draft has *reparationer* available: it uses the word correctly two sections later.

**Checked and not counted:**

- "ett bostadsbolag **med** 640 lägenheter" would assert holdings where the material says "manages", but the standfirst two lines above says *förvaltar*, which fixes the relation before the reader reaches it. Cushioned, so not a finding — though the draft says 640 twice and could have said it once, correctly.
- "Utfallet finns i en intern notering" leans a shade towards a result; the section that follows spends three paragraphs denying that any result can be read off the figures, so nothing is left standing.
- "Arbetsbelastningen skilde sig mellan perioderna, och noteringen kopplar uttryckligen inte skillnaden till programvaran" splits the flat statement from the note's refusal exactly as the material does.
- Assignment time is never turned into completion time: "från anmälan till tilldelning" throughout, and "tid till avslutat arbete" only among the unmeasured.
- No causal claim or hedge; no cost, satisfaction or rescue claim; nothing invented; "Hon rekommenderar däremot inte Svale till alla bostadsbolag" keeps both the object and the scope of "did not recommend Svale to every housing company".
- Byline "Av Thomas Barregren" is not in the material; `source-check-1/context.md` records that it comes from the invocation, and the reply says so and says to change it before publication. Not counted.

One thing I would have wanted raised: all three quotations are tagged *säger*, and the material says all interviews were by email. The draft does disclose the channel — but in its last sentence, after every quotation has been read as speech. The second checker treated the closing disclosure as sufficient; I think it arrives too late to do the work, and *skriver* would have cost nothing.

## 3. G2 — **pass**

Every required part is present and doing a job: headline, standfirst, byline, lead, four sections, ending. The customer account is complete — situation (records kept apart), action (the team decided, chose after testing, kept the phone line open, settled what the log was to solve), results (31 reports, two vs three working days, with the caveat and the list of what was not measured), appraisal (her final quotation and the reservation). After the first round of repairs the customer is the acting party in the places that matter: "Teamet valde Svale Systems…", "Telefonanmälan höll teamet öppen för de boende", "Vad loggen skulle lösa hade teamet redan bestämt". Svale configures, trains and helps, always in the third person.

The disclosure is the best-built part of the piece: "Texten publiceras av Svale Systems, som var leverantör i försöket, och intervjun med Maya Lind gjordes via mejl" tells the reader who is publishing, what their stake was, and what kind of interview the quotations come from. It stops short of the material's own "it is not independent journalism", but it gives the reader the facts that phrase is shorthand for.

The call to action is the most accurate of its kind I could ask for here: "är införandechecklistan ett dokument att läsa igenom först" describes the destination as the brief describes it, a document to read, and offers nothing else.

The fault: **the standfirst and the lead start the piece twice.** The standfirst opens "Elm Quay Housing förvaltar 640 lägenheter"; the lead, forty words later, says "ett bostadsbolag med 640 lägenheter". The standfirst says "Under åtta veckor hösten 2025"; the lead says "försöket pågick i åtta veckor". The lead's only new information is that phone reports and emails were stored apart — which is the piece's actual starting point and should have carried the paragraph on its own. Opening a standfirst with a portfolio figure also spends the one line that has to make a busy operations manager read on. The parts are distinct in function; they are not distinct enough in content.

The closing paragraph carries the reservation, the call to action and the disclosure in three consecutive sentences, which is compact rather than crowded, and the order is right: the caveat before the offer.

## 4. L1 — **pass**

This is assured Swedish. V2 holds at every fronting, including the hard ones: "Under åtta veckor hösten 2025 provade underhållsteamet…", "Vad loggen skulle lösa hade teamet redan bestämt", "Telefonanmälan höll teamet öppen för de boende", "Står du inför samma fråga i ditt eget bestånd, är införandechecklistan…". Compounds are closed (felanmälningslogg, underhållsteamet, arbetsbelastningen, införandechecklistan, mediantiden, kvällsskiftet). Double definiteness is right throughout ("den interna noteringen", "de åtta veckorna", "de första anmälningarna", "ditt eget bestånd"). *Koppla … till* is the Swedish construction for the note's refusal, where a translated text reaches for *tillskriva … till*. The vocabulary is the trade's own: *bestånd*, *se efter*, *enas om*, *lägga in*, *hålla ihop felanmälningarna*. Address is *du*, consistently, and every sentence has a real subject.

Two word choices I would change, neither an import from English:

- "notering" for the trial note. *Notering* pulls towards a jotting or a quoted price; *anteckning* is the word for the document the material describes, and the draft uses *notering* three times.
- "tid till avslutat arbete" is a paraphrase where Swedish has a compound (*färdigställandetid*); it is clear but laboured, and it appears twice.

Reader effect: a busy operations manager reads this without noticing the language, which is the test. The prose is noticeably plainer than the material would allow a writer to make it, and that plainness serves the subject.

## 5. Quoted speech

**Q1.** Draft: "– Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var våra, och Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen, säger Maya Lind, arbetsledare för underhållet."
Source: "We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log."
One pass: yes; nothing to supply. The shift compounds are the ordinary ones.
Meaning, stance, certainty, voice: carried, with one small flattening. The source's semicolon juxtaposes the two clauses, and the juxtaposition is where the edge sits — *ours*, and Svale only helped. The draft's *och* makes the second clause additive rather than contrastive. She still owns the categories and Svale still only helps, so no stance has moved, but a little of her emphasis has gone soft. The tag *säger* is the manner problem noted under F1, not a problem inside the quotation.

**Q2.** Draft: "– Vi lade mer tid på att enas om kategorierna än på att lägga in de första anmälningarna. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan nästa hus kommer igång, säger Lind."
Source: "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts."
One pass: yes. *Komma igång* is a figure Swedish reaches for itself for a unit or a stage getting under way, so the source's metonymy carries over instead of having to be unpacked — and, importantly, nothing is supplied: no agent, and no decision about what exactly starts. The material does not settle that, and the draft does not settle it either.
Meaning, stance, certainty, voice: intact. The comparison keeps its Swedish *på att … än på att* frame, and the conditional *skulle* keeps her modality.

**Q3.** Draft: "– Jag skulle välja att göra om försöket. Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser, säger hon."
Source: "I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation."
One pass: yes.
Meaning, stance, certainty, reservation, voice: intact. The marked "I would choose to" survives as "Jag skulle välja att" rather than being smoothed; "hjälper oss" stays present and first-person plural, not generalised to housing companies at large; the *men* clause is the last thing the reader holds, which is where the material puts it. The heading above the section, "Omdömet gäller inte varje bostadsbolag", tells the reader in advance that this is a qualified assessment — the material's own emphasis, built into the structure.

Missing quotations: none. All three permitted quotations appear, each carrying something the narration does not.

**In one sentence:** every passage of quoted speech in this draft is speech a Swedish reader takes on one pass with the source's meaning, stance, reservation and voice intact — with the one qualification that Q1's *och* for the source's semicolon softens her emphasis a shade.

## 7. Delivery

**A delivery that should not have happened.** The disclosure is the best-formed part of this run's reply: four numbered findings, each with the draft's passage, the material's own words, and the smallest repair, plus an honest note that the two comparisons disagreed about two of them. An editor can act on it in three minutes. But that is what it amounts to — three minutes of work handed to the reader because the run would not spend a third comparison on prose it had just been told was wrong in four places. One of those four tells the reader that Elm Quay is on its way to expanding a trial the material says it has not decided to expand. The first round's repairs were all sound, including one genuinely subtle catch about whose position the standfirst was reporting; the rule that froze the text after the final check is what turned a good pass into a delivered draft with four known defects in it.
