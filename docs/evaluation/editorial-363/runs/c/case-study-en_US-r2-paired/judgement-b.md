## 3. R1 — **FAIL**

This is the closest of calls, and the run's diagnostic work is better than its delivery.

What it got right. Three of the four edits are sound. The *Its supervisor* repair fixes a real dangling reference. The *the two steps* repair fixes a real dangling definite. The date comma is a correct en_US mechanical fix. The body of the article — the figures, the four separate refusals to over-claim, the publisher disclosure, the assignment-not-completion caveat, and all three quotations — came back whole, which is the hardest part of reviewing a text like this and is done here without a slip. And the headline finding it acted on is real: *Two Elm Quay buildings shared one repair log* restates the standfirst's opening sentence in the same words (*two of its buildings … in one place*), so a rewrite was warranted.

Why it fails.

- **A changed claim was delivered with its scope widened, by the run's own diagnosis.** *Elm Quay's shifts got one view of repairs* drops the two-buildings bound and attaches the result to the company's shifts as such. The article is 640 apartments wide and the trial was two buildings deep, and the whole character of the piece is scope discipline — no supplier comparison, no attribution of the median difference, no cost or satisfaction or completion figures, no general recommendation. The one line a reader meets first is now the one line that does not observe that discipline. The reply states this plainly and calls it unresolved, but a headline that keeps both the result and the bound was available in the same character budget and in words the text already contains. Declaring the problem is not the same as not shipping it; the delivered text is, on its most visible line, less accurate than the input.
- **The claim account asserts a completeness it does not have.** It says *One claim changed; none was removed*, and that every other claim stands *as it arrived*. Two further body edits are absent from it: the standfirst's *Its* → *The team's* and the bridge's *the two steps* → *two parts of the work*. Both are defensible, both are small, and the second is the kind of edit a reader would want declared because it is at the seam of a quotation. A review whose value is that its account can be trusted line by line cannot leave two of its four edits out of the account while claiming to have listed them all.
- **A visible defect of the same family it elsewhere prosecutes was left unaddressed and unreported.** The lead-in *describes what the team was after and how the work divided* announces to the reader what Lind's quotation then does unaided, immediately after the paragraph that has already stated the division of labour. That is the same pre-echo pattern the run treated as actionable at the headline. It is not reported as a finding left unresolved either, so a reader of the reply would not know it had been considered.

Removals: none — no claim was deleted, which is to the run's credit. Rejected losses: one, the two-buildings bound in the headline. Irreparable findings: none in the brief's sense; the headline could have been settled from the text's own words, without the absent source. Changed claims: one, reported and reported honestly, but shipped anyway. R1 fails on the delivered headline and on the incomplete account.
