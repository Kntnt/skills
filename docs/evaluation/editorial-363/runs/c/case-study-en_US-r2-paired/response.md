# Elm Quay's shifts got one view of repairs

For eight weeks in late 2025, the maintenance team at Elm Quay Housing logged repairs from two of its buildings in one place. Thirty-one reports were entered, and the internal trial note is careful about what they prove. The team's supervisor would do it again, and says what she would give more time to first.

By Thomas Barregren

Elm Quay Housing manages 640 apartments. In September 2025 its maintenance team decided to test a shared repair log in two of the buildings. Phone reports and emails had been stored separately, and the team wanted staff on different shifts to see the same information.

## Elm Quay wrote the categories itself

Elm Quay chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair. No comparison with another supplier is available.

The maintenance team designed the categories and kept phone reporting open for residents. Svale configured the log and trained six staff members in two sessions.

Maya Lind, the maintenance supervisor, describes what the team was after and how the work divided: “We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log.”

## The trial note does not credit the software

Elm Quay's internal note of December 4, 2025, records 31 repair reports entered during the eight weeks. It excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial.

Median time from report to assignment was two working days during the trial and three in the preceding eight-week period. The two periods carried different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software.

No cost, resident-satisfaction, or completion-time measurements were made. What the note records is time to assignment, not time to completion.

## Lind would run it again, with reservations

The supervisor compares two parts of the work: “We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts.”

Lind does not recommend Svale Systems to every housing company. She sums up the eight weeks like this: “I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation.”

## Elm Quay will check the categories on larger repairs

Inside the two buildings, the team has the single view of reports it set out to get. The trial has not yet expanded beyond them; Elm Quay will decide that after checking how its categories work for larger repairs.

Svale Systems publishes this account and approved it for publication; it is not independent reporting. If a similar trial is on your list, the implementation checklist at https://example.invalid/svale/checklist is worth reading before you set a start date.

## Unresolved finding

**The headline claims the result without the bound the text puts on it.** `# Elm Quay's shifts got one view of repairs` names the customer's result, which is what the genre asks a case-study headline for, but it names it unqualified. The ending says *Inside the two buildings, the team has the single view of reports it set out to get*, and the trial covered two buildings of a company managing 640 apartments. A headline is read on its own, with no standfirst to explain it, and read that way this one says the shifts got one view of repairs, with no two-buildings limit on it. In a supplier-published account whose whole character is scope discipline — *No comparison with another supplier is available*, *the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software*, *No cost, resident-satisfaction, or completion-time measurements were made* — a headline that widens the customer's result is out of key with everything under it, and widening a customer's result is the particular risk this genre warns about.

This followed from the repair to the first review's headline finding. The headline it replaced, *Two Elm Quay buildings shared one repair log*, carried the two-buildings bound but stated the arrangement rather than the result and restated the standfirst's first sentence in the same vocabulary. The repair removed the repetition and the bound together. What remains is a headline that keeps both — the result, inside the two buildings — within 20–70 characters. The body needs no change for it.
