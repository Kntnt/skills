# Judgement — x-0be509

Text: English (en_US) case study about Elm Quay Housing's eight-week shared repair log trial.

## 1. Every difference between input and delivered text

Four differences. Frontmatter, byline, every subheading, every figure (640 apartments, 31 reports, December 4 2025, two versus three working days, eight weeks, six staff, two sessions), all three quotations, the disclosure sentence and the closing URL are identical.

1. **Headline.** Before: `# Two Elm Quay buildings shared one repair log`. After: `# Elm Quay's shifts got one view of repairs`. — **Change to what a claim says** (scope, and with it the subject). The old headline bounded the result to two buildings and named the arrangement; the new one names the result and drops the bound, so that on its own it says the company's shifts — all of them, in a company of 640 apartments — got one view of repairs. The text supports the bounded version only: *Inside the two buildings, the team has the single view of reports it set out to get*, and *The trial has not yet expanded beyond them*. This is the largest change in the run and it moves a claim outward.

2. **Standfirst, third sentence.** Before: `Its supervisor would do it again, and says what she would give more time to first.` After: `The team's supervisor would do it again, and says what she would give more time to first.` — **Repair of a defect visible in the text** (a possessive whose nearest antecedent is *the internal trial note*, not a body that has a supervisor), with a small change of subject inside it: the input's *Its* most naturally resolves to Elm Quay Housing, the delivered text pins the supervisor to the maintenance team. The body supports the delivered reading — Maya Lind is introduced as *the maintenance supervisor* of a maintenance team — so the repair is right on the facts. It is nonetheless an edit that changes whose supervisor she is said to be.

3. **First paragraph under *The trial note does not credit the software*.** Before: `Elm Quay's internal note of December 4, 2025 records 31 repair reports`. After: `Elm Quay's internal note of December 4, 2025, records 31 repair reports`. — **Mechanical correction** (US punctuation: a month-day-year date inside a sentence takes a closing comma). Correct, and correct for the declared locale.

4. **First line under *Lind would run it again, with reservations*.** Before: `The supervisor compares the two steps:` After: `The supervisor compares two parts of the work:` — **Between a repair and a change of taste.** *the two steps* is a definite reference with nothing before it to attach to: no pair of steps has been named, and the reader has to look forward into the quotation to find them. The replacement removes the false definiteness. It also loses a little precision, since the quotation does compare two specific activities. Small either way.

No quotation was altered, trimmed, merged, re-punctuated, re-attributed or moved; none gained an event, fact, name or figure; no speaker's stance, certainty, reservation or voice was touched. On the quotations the run is clean.

## 3. R1

**Fail.**

What the run got right should be said first. It left the whole evidentiary spine alone: the note's explicit refusal to attribute the shorter median to the software, the absence of cost, satisfaction and completion measurements, the missing supplier comparison, the distinction between time to assignment and time to completion, Lind's refusal to recommend the supplier to every housing company, and the disclosure that Svale publishes and approved the account. All three quotations survive verbatim. The comma fix is a genuine mechanical correction in the declared locale, and the standfirst pronoun fix answers a real ambiguity. Nothing was invented, and no fact from outside the text was supplied.

It fails on two counts, both about claims.

- **The headline change ships a widened claim.** Reported, and reported accurately — the reply raises it as an unresolved finding, states plainly that the new headline *names it unqualified*, quotes the ending's two-buildings bound against it, and observes that widening a customer's result is exactly the risk in a supplier-published account whose character is scope discipline. The diagnosis is exact. But diagnosis is not delivery: the text handed over carries a headline that says more than the text supports, on the one axis this piece is built to be careful about. Unlike a finding that needs material the run never had, this one is fully repairable from the text itself — the reply says so in as many words (*The body needs no change for it*, and it describes the wanted headline as one that keeps the result and the two-buildings bound inside the length limit). A run that can name the defect, name the fix, and know that no outside material is needed, and still delivers the overclaim, has left the text worse than it found it on the criterion's own terms. The old headline's fault — restating the standfirst's vocabulary, naming the arrangement rather than the result — was the lesser fault of the two.

- **Two of the four differences are not reported at all, under a report that claims completeness.** The reply states *One claim changed; none was removed*, and then *Every other claim in the text stands with its scope, certainty, attribution, chronology, causality and meaning as it arrived*, and it puts the mechanical pass at *One correction*. The standfirst's *Its supervisor* → *The team's supervisor* and the lead-in's *the two steps* → *two parts of the work* fall in neither account. Both are defensible edits, and that is the point: they would have survived disclosure easily. A reader who trusts the account will not know the standfirst was touched, nor that a subject changed there, however lightly. A claim account that asserts nothing else moved, when two things did, is inaccurate about the run's own work.

Taken together — a scope-widening claim shipped after being correctly diagnosed and known to be cheaply fixable, and a claim account that overstates its own completeness — the run does not meet R1, despite a clean record on the quotations and on every figure in the text.
