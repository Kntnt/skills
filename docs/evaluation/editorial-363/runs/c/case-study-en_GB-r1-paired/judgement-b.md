## 3. R1 — **FAIL**

What the run got right. The pre-echo it identified is a real, visible defect: the narrative said *the maintenance team designed its own repair categories* and then handed Lind a quotation whose first business is to say the same thing. Repairing it by deleting the narrative statement rather than by touching the quotation is the correct direction. The reply's accounting is unusually honest — it lists both removed claims by name, states plainly that *Svale Systems configured the shared log* is *not restated anywhere*, correctly identifies the surviving quotation as *a narrower claim*, and raises the orphaned subheading itself as an unresolved finding, saying in terms that settling it needs Elm Quay's material, which the Skill does not have. Every removal and every changed claim in the delivered text is reported, and reported accurately; I found no misstatement in the reply's account of its own work.

Why it fails anyway. R1 is about the text delivered, not the candour of the note attached to it.

- **A claim outside the finding was destroyed.** *Svale Systems configured the shared log* was a distinct fact sharing a sentence with nothing that echoed the quotation. The defect was the categories clause; the repair took out the configuration clause too, for no stated reason beyond sharing a sentence with it. Rewriting the sentence to keep it (*Svale Systems configured the shared log and trained six members of staff in two sessions*, with only the preceding clause dropped) was available and cost nothing. This is a rejected loss, not a legitimate removal.
- **The repair introduced an internal contradiction the input did not have.** The section heading promises *Svale set up the log*; the section beneath it now says only that Svale trained staff. A reader who trusts subheadings is misinformed by the delivered text in a way they were not by the input. The reply frames this as an irreparable finding forced by the budget, but it is not irreparable in the way the brief means — it is not something only the absent source could settle. Restoring the deleted clause resolves it entirely, using nothing but words the input already contained. The Skill treated its own deletion as a fixed point and then reasoned outwards from it.
- **A clean headline was rewritten to taste**, and in the rewriting acquired a tense change and a change of subject. The input headline stated the arrangement the case study is about; the replacement states an outcome in Lind's borrowed phrasing and in the present tense. Neither version is false, so no defect was repaired — which is exactly the rewriting R1 forbids.

Net: two of the three changes are losses, one of them a fact removed outside the finding and one a heading left contradicting its own section, with a third change that had no defect to answer. The quotations, the figures and the article's careful refusals to over-claim all survived untouched, which is the run's real strength; but on the criterion as written — repair defects, preserve what is not a finding, do not rewrite clean prose — this delivery does not pass.
