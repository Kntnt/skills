## 3. R1 — **FAIL**

Much of this review is sound. The headline over-claim is a real, visible defect and the repair is the right one; the pre-echo bridge is a real defect; all three quotations come back verbatim; and every sentence that bounds what the text asserts comes back verbatim — the exclusions on the 31 reports, the differing workloads with the note's refusal to attribute the difference to the software, the assignment-not-completion sentence with its absent measurements, and Lind's willingness as a qualified judgment rather than a recommendation of Svale. Nothing was invented, the missing byline was reported and left unfilled, and the four subheadings the round introduced are declared as its own damage, including the one that flattens a median into a result.

Removals and changed claims, one by one:
- **Removed: "includes a reservation about preparation"** (item 5). Reported and accurate, including that the quotation still carries the reservation and that the attribution was kept in the new bridge.
- **Removed: "is available to read"** (item 6). Reported and accurate.
- **Changed: the headline** (item 1). Reported and accurate; scope and certainty both named.
- **Changed: the closing paragraph's addressee** (item 6). Reported, and honestly described as supplying an object the text itself carries.
- **Changed: the standfirst's report of Lind's appraisal** (item 2). Reported, but **not accurately**. The reply says the clause "moved from a second benefit to the reservation Lind's quoted appraisal states" and that "Attribution to Lind is unchanged". The attribution is indeed unchanged — which is the problem, because what is attributed to her is stronger and different in kind: her quoted words are a conditional preference for more preparation next time, and the standfirst now has her judging that the preparation time was too short. Strength and time-reference moved, and the account says they did not.
- **Not reported at all:** `trial` → `pilot` in the lead (item 3) and `customer case` → `customer story` in the publisher disclosure (item 7). The reply states that the closing mechanical pass found no errors to correct and that nothing else moved, so both edits pass unaccounted for, one of them inside the disclosure line.

A verdict put into a named person's mouth that she did not give, reported as though only the clause's target had moved, plus two silent edits — one of them to the publisher credit — is more than this text's visible defects called for. R1 fails.
