## 3. R1 — FAIL

The review found and fixed the input's one clear claim defect. `Elm Quay brings repair reports into one shared view` asserts as an accomplished, present, whole-company fact what the text reports as an unexpanded two-building trial, and `Elm Quay piloted one log for repair reports` says what the text supports. The reply reports that change under **Changed**, names the movement in scope and certainty, and gives the right reason. The pre-echo bridge was a genuine defect and was removed without losing the attribution, which was rehung as `Lind gave this assessment of the trial:` — reported, accurately. `is available to read` was a real limp ending and the rewritten call to action is better; the shift in addressee is reported. Every quotation came back verbatim, and every sentence that fences what the text asserts came back verbatim — the reply lists them and its list checks out against the input.

Against that, three things go wrong in the delivery and two of them are not reported straight.

**The standfirst now puts a verdict in Lind's mouth.** `but that preparation time was too short` is a retrospective judgement on the trial; what she says is that she would leave an extra week next time and would set the agreeing time aside before the next building starts. Wanting more time is not the same claim as the time having been too short, and in a customer case published by the supplier the difference between a preference and a complaint is not decoration. The reply reports the change but justifies it by calling the input clause `a second benefit` that moved to `the reservation Lind's quoted appraisal states`. The input clause was already the reservation — the sentence reads `says … helps the team, but would allow more preparation time` — so the rationale misdescribes what was there, and the account never says that the claim got stronger. That is the one place where this run's reporting is not accurate.

**Four subheadings arrive where the text had none, and each restates the sentence beneath it.** The run finds this itself, in findings 2, 3 and 4, and the self-criticism is exact: in the two sections that open on a quotation, Lind's words now arrive second with their force spent; `Two working days to assignment, not to completion` hands a scanner a flat median the text is at pains not to assert; three of four open on `Elm Quay`. The diagnosis is right and the delivered text carries the defect anyway.

**Two changes go unreported.** `trial` → `pilot` in the lead, which leaves the text calling the same thing a pilot in the headline and lead and a trial in the other seven places it appears; and `customer case` → `customer story` in the disclosure line. Neither is damaging, but the account claims to be a claim account and says nothing about either.

A clean, unusually well-hedged text came back with one real headline repair, one real bridge repair, one improved call to action — and with a strengthened claim attributed to the customer, four self-defeating subheadings, and two silent edits. Fail.
