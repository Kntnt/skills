## 3. R1 — **Fail**, narrowly

This is a light-handed run with a rigorous account. Only four things changed. All five quoted passages are verbatim. Every sentence that bounds the text's assertions comes back exactly as it arrived — no supplier comparison available, the count's exclusions, the different workloads, the note's express refusal to attribute the difference to the software, "Assignment is not completion", the unmeasured completion time, costs and resident satisfaction, the refusal to recommend Svale to every housing company, the disclosure that Svale publishes the account, and the open decision. One genuine mechanical error, a day-month-year date in an en_US text, was found and fixed correctly. Nothing was rewritten to taste in the body at all.

It fails on the headline. "Two Elm Quay buildings shared one repair log" was working: short, concrete, in the text's own vocabulary, and claiming exactly what the text claims. It was replaced, no defect in it is named, and the replacement is worse by the run's own account — finding 1 says "the log trial" is not what the text calls the thing anywhere else and that the headline is short of self-explaining, and it proposes the wording that would fix what the round itself introduced. That is a working element rewritten and degraded, in the most prominent line of the piece, which is what R1 exists to catch.

**Reporting, item by item.** Uncommonly good, and complete: all four differences are named, and there are no others. There were no removals, and the reply correctly says none. Of the changed claims, #2 is reported with real precision — it states plainly that what moved is the scope of what the note is reported as declining, calls it the wider of the two non-claims, explains why the repair was needed, and points to where a human would carry the distinction back. #1 is reported, and its remaining defect reported against itself, but the account asserts that "no claim left the text with it", which understates the matter: the headline's subject changed from what Elm Quay did to what its supervisor would do. #3 and #4 are reported accurately.
