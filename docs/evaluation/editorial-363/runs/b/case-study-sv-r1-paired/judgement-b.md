## 2. Quoted speech

Three passages, in order, judged as a competent reader of **Swedish** reads them.

1. **”Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var våra. Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen” — Maya Lind.**
   - **Wording:** the quoted words return identical. What changed is outside them: the opening dash (#4) and the attribution tag (#3).
   - **One pass?** Yes. *Kategorierna var våra* is ordinary Swedish for *we defined them ourselves*, and the next clause's *dem* picks the categories straight up. The evening and morning shifts need no introduction; the piece has already said the company keeps a maintenance team and logged reports separately. No stopping point, nothing to supply from outside the text.
   - **Because the tag came back changed:** the reply does **not** name the change — it names none. The change supplies no event, fact, name or figure the text does not carry; name, role and medium simply moved to the preceding sentence, where *hon* now finds its referent one line above rather than in the tag. Meaning, stance, certainty and voice are untouched, the quotation marks' contents being identical.

2. **”Vi lade mer tid på att komma överens om kategorierna än på att registrera de första felanmälningarna. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan nästa hus börjar” — hon.**
   - **Wording:** identical; only the opening dash changed (#4).
   - **One pass?** Yes. *Innan nästa hus börjar* is clipped — *börjar* stands without its complement — but this is the ellipsis Swedish uses of itself when the activity is already on the table, and the text has established a trial in which two buildings started working in the log. A reader carries on without stopping. *Den tiden* refers back inside the passage to the time spent agreeing categories.
   - The tag was already *skriver hon* in the input and is unchanged.

3. **”Jag skulle välja att göra om försöket. Att ha en samlad bild av felanmälningarna hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser” — hon.**
   - **Wording:** identical; only the opening dash changed (#4).
   - **One pass?** Yes. *Göra om försöket* is open in isolation between *do it again* and *redo it*, but the *men*-clause settles it inside the passage: again, with an extra week. No stopping point.

## 3. R1

**Pass.**

The changes are few, small and aimed at things a reader can see with nothing but this text in front of them: definite forms introducing referents the text had not introduced, a source quoted before she was named, and an em dash where Swedish sets a tankstreck. Every quotation comes back word for word. Every figure, date and qualification survives — the excluded acute cases and pre-ordered work, the differing workloads, the note's explicit refusal to credit the software, the unmeasured costs, satisfaction and completion times, the absent supplier comparison, and the closing disclosure that the case is the vendor's own and not independent journalism. Nothing was swept for consistency, no sentence reshaped for rhythm, no subheading rewritten to taste, and nothing was excused by material the review never had.

Removals and changed claims, and whether the reply reports them:

- **No removals.** Correct, and the reply says so accurately: no fact leaves the text, and the words dropped from the attribution tag reappear in full one sentence earlier.
- **No changed claims.** Nothing comes back at a different strength, scope, subject, attribution, chronology or modality. The reply's blanket assurance to that effect is, unusually, true of the delivered text as I find it.
- **One addition** (#2) is not covered by that assurance and is not reported. A whole sentence of new body prose is the kind of change an editor should be told about even when it carries no new fact, and the reply is silent about it.

The weakness of this run is the account rather than the editing. The reply says two findings were raised and both repaired, and then names neither: an editor is told the number and nothing else, and could not check the inserted sentence, the reworked ingress, the shortened tag or the dash pass without diffing the text themselves. One of the changes it made silently corrects nothing at all — *olika arbetsbelastning* was already good Swedish — and one visible roughness is left standing and unmentioned, *Maya Lind rekommenderade inte Svale till alla bostadsbolag*, where the scope of the negation is genuinely unclear, against a declared *Inget fynd kvarstår*. None of this damages the text, and R1 asks first whether the text came through with its voice, arguments, quotations and claims intact. It did.
