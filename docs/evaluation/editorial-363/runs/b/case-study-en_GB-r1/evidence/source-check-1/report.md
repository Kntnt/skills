## 2. Findings

Nine. Each gives the draft passage, the missing or changed support, a concrete case compatible with the supplied material in which the source statement holds and the draft's fails (or the reverse), and the smallest supported repair.

### F1 — The supplier choice is dated to September 2025, and the material does not date it

**Draft:** "In September 2025 the team picked two buildings for the trial, and Svale Systems as the supplier, after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair."
**Source:** "In September 2025 its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings." — and, separately and without a date, "It chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair."
**What differs:** time. The draft's single adverbial "In September 2025" governs both coordinated objects, so it asserts that the supplier was chosen in September 2025. The material dates only the decision to trial.
**Case:** the team decided in September 2025 to run a trial in two buildings, tested Svale's log through late September and early October, and chose Svale on 6 October 2025, the trial's eight weeks then running to the trial note of 4 December 2025. Every element of this is compatible with the supplied material — the dated decision, the undated choice, the eight-week trial, the 4 December note — and no supplied statement excludes it. In that case the source's statements all hold and the draft's does not.
**Repair (smallest):** detach the date from the second object —
> "In September 2025 the team picked two buildings for the trial. It chose Svale Systems as the supplier after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair."

### F2 — The workload difference is presented as the record's claim, not as fact

**Draft:** "It states that the two periods carried different workloads, and it does not attribute the difference to the software."
**Source:** "The periods had different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software."
**What differs:** whose assertion, and certainty. The material asserts the workload difference in its own voice and attributes only the non-attribution to the note. The draft attributes both to the record. This adds a claim about the record's contents and, in the same move, downgrades an established fact to a document's say-so — the fact that carries the article's guard against the forbidden causal inference.
**Case (draft fails where source holds):** the trial note records only counts, medians and the sentence declining to attribute the difference; the workload difference between the two periods is known from the team's rota and the emergency log, which are not part of the note. The material says the periods had different workloads, but never says the note says so, and no supplied statement excludes this. Then "It states that the two periods carried different workloads" is false while the source's statement is true.
**Repair (smallest):** restore the material's own voice for the first clause —
> "The two periods carried different workloads, and the record does not attribute the difference to the software."

### F3 — Lind's remark is reframed as advice to other housing companies

**Draft:** "She has one piece of advice for a housing company planning the same work:" introducing "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts."
**Source:** the quotation itself, whose stated horizon is Elm Quay's own next building; and "Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company."
**What differs:** addressee and intent — circumstantial detail that the contract requires support for. Nothing in the material establishes that Lind addressed, or intended to address, other housing companies. Her words plan her own next building.
**Case:** Lind answered the supplier's email questions about how the trial had gone for Elm Quay, offering the category lesson as an internal one for the team's own next building, and declined — as the material records — to generalise her experience to other housing companies at all. This is compatible with the supplied material and no supplied statement excludes it. Then the draft's framing misstates whose problem she is speaking to, while the quotation itself remains accurate.
**Repair (smallest):** keep the framing inside her own stated horizon —
> "She would change one thing before Elm Quay's next building starts:"

### F4 — The heading gives the note's non-attribution as the organisation's stance

**Draft:** heading "Elm Quay does not credit the software for the difference".
**Source:** "The periods had different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software."
**What differs:** whose knowledge or assertion. The material establishes what one document declines to do. The heading asserts a position held by the company.
**Case:** Elm Quay's trial note declines to attribute the difference, because whoever wrote it judged the workloads too dissimilar to compare — while the maintenance team, Lind included, does think the shared log helped, consistent with her supplied "Having one view of the reports helps us." Nothing supplied excludes an organisational view that differs from what its internal note is willing to put in writing. Then the note's non-attribution holds and "Elm Quay does not credit the software" does not.
**Repair (smallest):** name the document in the heading, which also settles whose record #17 introduces —
> "Elm Quay's own trial note does not credit the software"

### F5 — "explicitly" is dropped from the non-attribution

**Draft:** "and it does not attribute the difference to the software."
**Source:** "the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software."
**What differs:** certainty, in the direction of loss. "Does not attribute" is satisfied by mere silence; "explicitly does not attribute" reports a statement the note makes. The material's forbidden-inferences section makes that explicit disclaimer load-bearing, and the contract requires certainty to be kept where it is warranted.
**Case:** a note that simply gives the two medians side by side and says nothing at all about cause satisfies the draft's sentence but not the material's. The two are therefore different claims, and the weaker one is the draft's.
**Repair (smallest):** restore the modifier —
> "and it expressly does not attribute the difference to the software."

### F6 — "Nothing was measured" asserts more than the absence of measurements (minor)

**Draft:** "Nothing was measured about cost, about how satisfied residents were, or about how long the repairs themselves took to finish."
**Source:** "There are no cost, resident-satisfaction or completion-time measurements."
**What differs:** modality. The material reports that no such measurements exist. The draft reports that no such measuring took place. The draft carries the material's other availability statement — "No comparison with another supplier is available" — verbatim, which shows the distinction is one the draft otherwise observes.
**Case:** Elm Quay runs an annual resident-satisfaction survey across all 640 flats and measured satisfaction in the ordinary way during the autumn; no measurement was made of the trial, and none of it forms part of this material. Then there are no resident-satisfaction measurements here, as the material says, while "nothing was measured about how satisfied residents were" is false. No supplied statement excludes this.
**Repair (smallest):** state the absence rather than the non-event —
> "There are no measurements of cost, of resident satisfaction, or of how long the repairs themselves took to finish."

### F7 — The checklist is described as pre-trial preparation material

**Draft:** heading "Read the checklist before your own trial", and "the implementation checklist sets out what to prepare before a trial begins: https://example.invalid/svale/checklist".
**Source:** "The optional next step is the supplied implementation checklist at https://example.invalid/svale/checklist; it is a document to read, not a consultation booking or a product trial."
**What differs:** the thing described. The brief establishes the destination's name, that it is optional, and that it is a document to read. It establishes nothing about the checklist's contents or about when in a reader's process it applies. The material's judging section asks specifically whether the suggested link accurately describes its destination.
**Case:** Svale's implementation checklist covers implementation after a customer has decided to proceed — data migration from existing telephone and email records, configuration sessions, staff training, category review — and says nothing about what to prepare before a trial begins. Nothing supplied excludes this; "implementation checklist" more naturally suggests it. Then the link is genuinely the optional next step the brief describes, while the draft's description of what it "sets out" is false.
**Repair (smallest):** describe only what the brief establishes, in both places —
> Heading: "Read the implementation checklist"
> Closing sentence: "If you are weighing the same step for your own buildings, Svale's implementation checklist is the document to read next: https://example.invalid/svale/checklist"

### F8 — The title drops the two-building scope (minor)

**Draft:** title "Elm Quay's shifts shared one repair log".
**Source:** "its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings"; "The trial has not yet expanded."
**What differs:** scope. The contract states that the boundary holds "in the title, summary, headings and body alike" and that scope is preserved "wherever a claim appears". Unqualified, the title reads across Elm Quay's 640 flats.
**Case:** the two trial buildings hold 80 of the 640 flats; staff on shifts in the remaining buildings went on working from telephone notes and email for the whole eight weeks, the trial having not yet expanded. This is what the material describes. Then "in two buildings, Elm Quay's shifts shared one repair log" holds and the unqualified title does not.
**Repair (smallest):** restore the limit in the title, where the standfirst already carries it —
> "In two buildings, Elm Quay's shifts shared one repair log"

### F9 — "who supervises the maintenance team" enlarges a supplied job title (minor)

**Draft:** "Maya Lind, who supervises the maintenance team, puts the arrangement this way".
**Source:** "Customer interview with maintenance supervisor Maya Lind".
**What differs:** scope of the role. A job title is supplied; a span of responsibility is asserted.
**Case:** Elm Quay's maintenance team, covering 640 flats across several buildings and at least a morning and an evening shift, is run by a maintenance manager, under whom Lind is one of two maintenance supervisors. She is then "maintenance supervisor Maya Lind" exactly as supplied, while "supervises the maintenance team" is false of her. No supplied statement excludes this. Noted as minor: the reading the draft takes is the natural one, and the standfirst's bare "Supervisor Maya Lind" raises no such question.
**Repair (smallest):** use the supplied title —
> "Maya Lind, Elm Quay's maintenance supervisor, puts the arrangement this way".
