## 2. Findings

### Finding A — the summary credits Lind with commenting on the figures (source support)

**Draft:** "Supervisor Maya Lind says what came of it, **what the figures do and do not show**, and where she would spend more time next time."

**Source:** the material supplies Lind's "complete usable quotations", and all three are about the shifts, the categories and the preparation week; none mentions the count, the medians or the workloads. The non-attribution belongs to the document, not to her: "the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software." And of her closing quotation the material says: "Her final quotation is her actual, qualified assessment. It is not an inference from the figures."

**What differs:** whose assertion it is. The material has the note reading the figures; the draft's summary has Lind reading them.

**Case in which the source holds and the draft fails:** Lind answered the email interview about how the log worked on the floor, and never commented on the measurements at all — the count, the medians and the workload caveat were written up by whoever kept the trial note. Every supplied sentence remains true in that case, and the draft's summary is false in it. No supplied statement excludes it; the material's "complete usable quotations" and "It is not an inference from the figures" point the other way.

The body bears this out: the article's figures section attributes the reading of the figures to the note, and quotes Lind nowhere in it. The summary promises a reader something the article does not deliver and the material does not permit.

**Smallest supported repair:** move the figures to their actual source —

> Supervisor Maya Lind says what came of it and where she would spend more time next time; Elm Quay's own note says what the figures do and do not show.

### Finding B — "decided on its own" (source support)

**Draft:** "Elm Quay Housing's maintenance team wanted staff on different shifts to see the same information, and decided **on its own** to trial a shared repair log for eight weeks."

**Source:** "In September 2025 **its own** maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings."

**What differs:** what "own" attaches to. In the material it modifies the team — Elm Quay's in-house team, as against an outside party. In the draft it modifies the manner of deciding — unprompted, without anyone else's involvement. The material says who decided; it does not say whose idea it was.

**Case in which the source holds and the draft fails:** a Svale representative suggested a two-building trial in August 2025, and Elm Quay's in-house maintenance team decided in September to go ahead. "Its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings" is true in that case; "decided on its own" is not. Nothing supplied excludes it — the material records the supplier choice ("It chose Svale Systems after testing…") but is silent on who first raised the trial.

**Smallest supported repair:** drop the two words, or move "own" back onto the team —

> …and its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log.

The customer remains the acting party in either wording, as the brief requires.

### Finding C — "decided … to trial a shared repair log for eight weeks" (source support)

**Draft:** "…and decided on its own to trial a shared repair log **for eight weeks**."

**Source:** "The trial lasted eight weeks."

**What differs:** a length that was decided at the outset versus a length the trial turned out to run. The material gives only the elapsed duration.

**Case in which the source holds and the draft fails:** the team began the trial without fixing an end date and stopped it after eight weeks to write the note. "The trial lasted eight weeks" is true in that case; "decided … to trial a shared repair log for eight weeks" is not. Nothing supplied fixes a planned length — Lind's "I would leave an extra week for preparation" concerns preparation before a start, not the trial's own term.

**Smallest supported repair:** state the duration as duration —

> …and its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log. The trial ran for eight weeks.

Or drop "for eight weeks" from the summary, where the body already carries "over the eight weeks".

## 3. Borderline passages, reported as editorial questions rather than defects

Each of these differs from the material in some respect, and in each the difference is resolved by the immediate context a reader has in front of them. None is a defect, and none has a repair proposed.

1. **Heading "The team set up the log on its own terms" (row 11).** The material gives the configuring to Svale and the terms to the team. The heading claims only the terms, and the first paragraph under it says plainly that "Svale Systems configured the log". A reader reaching the heading reaches that sentence two lines later; there is no reading of the section in which the team is credited with Svale's configuration work.

2. **Heading "Elm Quay's own note does not credit the software" (row 17).** The material's non-attribution is specific: "does not attribute **the difference** to the software". A note could decline to credit the software for the timing difference while crediting it for cross-shift visibility, and the unqualified heading would then overstate. But the heading sits over a section that presents the count, the medians and the workload caveat and nothing else, and the body sentence restores the qualification verbatim. Read in context the referent is the figures. Worth an editor's eye if the headings are ever read out of context, not a defect in the draft.

3. **"counts 31 repair reports entered over the eight weeks" (row 19).** The material says only "31 repair reports were entered", in an "internal trial note" dated 4 December 2025, with an exclusion that fixes the early boundary ("work ordered before the trial") but not the late one. A count running from the trial's start to the note's date is not excluded by any supplied sentence. Against that, the document is a trial note, the exclusion is framed by the trial, and the trial's length is supplied as eight weeks; the draft's window is the reading the material supports. An editorial question about how tightly to bind the count to the period, not a defect.

4. **Heading "Lind would repeat the trial, but not recommend Svale to all" (row 24).** "did not recommend" in the material is a report of what she did; "would not recommend" is a disposition. Both name the withholding of a universal recommendation, the body sentence restores the material's own tense and its "every housing company", and the reservation survives intact in both. No repair proposed.
