## 2. F1 — **fail**, on one item, in the headline

**The defect.** Headline: "Elm Quay lät två skift dela samma reparationslogg."

The material states the cross-shift arrangement twice, and both times as something wanted, never as something done:

- "The team wanted staff on different shifts to see the same information."
- Lind: "We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done."

What the material records as having happened is narrower: a shared log was trialled in two buildings, Svale configured it and trained six staff in two sessions, 31 reports were entered, and Lind says one view of the reports helps the team. Nothing says the evening shift ever had the log. "Lät … dela" asserts that Elm Quay arranged the sharing and that the sharing took place — the want, reported as accomplished. That is a modality change, and it sits in the headline, the one line a reader takes as the piece's finding.

I weighed the defence that "decided to trial a *shared* repair log" plus the training entails the sharing, and that Lind's present-tense "helps us" shows a single view in use. It is a reasonable argument, and it is why I record this as a narrow fail rather than a gross one. But the material's author wrote "wanted" twice and never once wrote that the shifts shared anything, and a draft is not entitled to close that gap in its headline. The checker's minimal repair ("Elm Quay **ville låta** två skift dela…") costs one word.

**Smaller things, named but not fail-driving:**

- "säger hon" / "säger Maya Lind" (three tags) for answers the material says arrived by email. The draft discloses the channel once before the first quotation ("beskriver i en intervju via e-post"), which takes most of the sting out, but *säger* still asserts a manner the material contradicts. "Skriver" would cost nothing.
- "Hon rekommenderar inte Svale till alla bostadsföretag" puts in the present what the material puts in the past ("Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company"). The material's "Her final quotation is her actual, qualified assessment" supports a held position, so I let this stand. The scope is right: Swedish *inte … alla* negates the universal exactly as "not … every" does.

**Checked both directions on the translated terms:**

- "de boende" for "residents" — correct after repair; *hyresgäster* would have asserted a tenancy the material never states, and would have excluded an owner-occupier or a subletter who phones in a fault.
- "som förvaltar 640 lägenheter" for "manages 640 flats" — correct after repair; *med 640 lägenheter* would have asserted holdings.
- "fastigheter" for "buildings" — in housing-company Swedish *fastighet* is the ordinary word for a block, but strictly a *fastighet* is a property that can carry several buildings, and a building can stand on a shared one. Nothing in this text turns on the difference, so no finding; recorded because the term is not a perfect overlay in either direction.
- "reparationslogg" for "repair log", "testanteckning" for "trial note", "tilldelning" for "assignment" — all exact.

**Clean where it counts:** no causal claim or causal hedge anywhere; assignment time never becomes completion time ("färdigställandetid" appears only in the list of what was not measured); the workload caveat and the note's explicit non-attribution both survive; cost, satisfaction and completion figures are declared absent rather than hinted at; the exclusions are attached to the count, not to the log; "Någon jämförelse med någon annan leverantör redovisas inte" keeps an absence as an absence; nothing is invented to fill the form.

Byline: "Av Thomas Barregren" is not in the material. `check1/context.md` records that the name comes from the invocation rather than the source package, and `response.md` tells the reader so and says to change it before publication. Not counted against F1.

## 3. G2 — **pass**, with two placement faults worth fixing

All required parts are present and in order, and each does its own job. Headline. Standfirst — "Åtta veckor, två fastigheter och en logg som skulle visa status för varje reparation" — sizes the trial rather than repeating the headline, and promises what the piece will settle. Byline. Lead, which opens on the problem in a concrete image ("Telefonanmälningar på ett ställe, e-post på ett annat") and does not restate the standfirst. Three body sections plus a closing one. Ending with the call to action.

The customer account is complete: situation (records kept apart, shifts out of step), action (the team decided, designed its own categories, kept the phone line open, tested status display before choosing), results (31 reports, two vs three working days, with the caveat), appraisal (Lind's final quotation plus the reservation). Elm Quay is the acting party in every sentence where something is done; Svale only configures, trains and publishes. Publisher stance: "Den här kundberättelsen publiceras av Svale Systems" — truthful and third person, though it stops short of the material's own "it is not independent journalism", which was available and would have been stronger. The call to action is built from the one supplied offer and describes it accurately as a checklist to read ("Läs checklistan", "Börja med").

Two faults in placement:

- Lind's second quotation, about spending more time agreeing categories than entering reports, sits under "## Beslutet kommer efter en kontroll av kategorierna", a heading about the pending expansion decision. The heading promises one thing and the section delivers another; the reader has to re-file the quotation as they read it.
- The last heading, "Läs checklistan innan ni sätter i gång", is an imperative where the other three are descriptive, and the section it opens begins with Lind's qualified assessment. A reader carrying the pattern forward can take the heading as her advice, which the material forbids — she recommended Svale to nobody in general. The draft defends itself by putting "Hon rekommenderar inte Svale till alla bostadsföretag" between the quotation and the call to action, which is the right order and was not the order the first checker saw. The residue is a heading that does the ending's job over an appraisal's content.

Neither fault removes a part or duplicates one, so this is a pass, but the last section is doing two jobs under one wrong sign.

## 4. L1 — **pass**, with one import named

The Swedish is confident, and in places better than serviceable. The lead's fragment-then-inversion ("Telefonanmälningar på ett ställe, e-post på ett annat. Så förvarades felanmälningarna…") is a native newspaper opening. V2 holds throughout, including after fronted adverbials ("I september 2025 beslutade det egna underhållsteamet…"). Compounds are closed (felanmälningar, reparationslogg, testanteckning, arbetsbelastning, färdigställandetid, implementeringschecklista, kvällsskiftet). Double definiteness is right ("den interna testanteckningen", "det egna underhållsteamet", "de första anmälningarna"). The s-passive is used where Swedish uses it ("registrerades", "förvarades", "har ännu inte utvidgats", "publiceras"). Idiom is native: "vad teamet var ute efter", "enas om", "avsätta tid", "drar i gång", "sätter i gång", "Vill ni pröva samma sak hos er?". The *ni* address reads as the reader's organisation, which is genuine plural, so it is not the distancing *ni* to a single reader.

The one import: "anteckningen tillskriver uttryckligen inte **skillnaden till** programvaran" is English "attribute X to Y" carried across. Swedish *tillskriva* takes the recipient directly — *tillskriver uttryckligen inte programvaran skillnaden* — or one writes *hänför inte skillnaden till programvaran*. One calque in four hundred words does not make the piece sound translated, so the criterion passes; it is worth naming because it sits in the sentence carrying the piece's most important caveat, where a reader stumbling costs the most.

Reader effect: an operations manager reads a competent trade piece that is specific about what was measured and visibly unwilling to claim more.

## 5. Quoted speech

**Q1.** Draft: "– Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var våra. Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen, säger hon."
Source: "We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log."
One pass: yes; nothing to supply. *Kvällsskiftet*/*morgonskiftet* are the standard terms and the shift-for-staff metonymy is native Swedish.
Meaning, stance, certainty, reservation, voice: intact. The past perfect with *redan* survives, the flat ownership claim keeps its emphasis, Svale stays subordinate. The semicolon becomes a full stop, which loosens the contrast a shade without touching content. The attribution *säger* is the manner problem noted under F1, not a problem inside the quotation.

**Q2.** Draft: "– Vi lade mer tid på att enas om kategorierna än på att registrera de första anmälningarna. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan nästa fastighet drar i gång, säger Maya Lind."
Source: "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts."
One pass: yes. The parallel "mer tid på … än på …" and the fronted "Den tiden" with inversion are ordinary spoken Swedish. "Drar i gång" resolves the source's ellipsis in the way Swedish itself resolves it — the building gets going — without settling what starts, so nothing is supplied that Lind did not.
Meaning, stance, certainty, voice: intact; comparison, conditional *skulle* and the unhedged flatness all preserved. Register is a shade more colloquial than "starts", which is speech, not drift.

**Q3.** Draft: "– Jag skulle välja att göra testet igen. Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser, säger Maya Lind."
Source: "I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation."
One pass: yes.
Meaning, stance, certainty, reservation, voice: intact, and this is the one that matters. The marked "I would choose to" is kept as "Jag skulle välja att" rather than smoothed; the present-tense "hjälper oss" is neither strengthened nor hedged; the reservation after *men* keeps its position and force. Her assessment stays conditional and qualified, which is what the material insists it is.

Missing quotations: none. All three permitted quotations appear, and each adds something the narration does not — the motive and the ownership of the categories, the effort ratio, the qualified verdict.

**In one sentence:** every passage of quoted speech in this draft is speech a Swedish reader takes on one pass, with the source's meaning, stance, reservation and voice intact.

## 7. Delivery

**A delivery that should not have happened as it stands.** The headline is wrong against the material, the second checker said so, the writer agreed in substance, and the piece was published over the top of that with a note. The run's rule — never deliver prose no full comparison has read — is a good rule, and the disclosure is honest, specific and puts the repair in the reader's hand. But the choice it produced here was to ship a false headline rather than fix a word and pay for a third look, and a headline is the worst place in a text to leave a claim the material does not carry. Everything else in this draft is careful, and the four first-round repairs were all real; that makes the outcome more frustrating, not less.
