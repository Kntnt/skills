## 2. F1 — **Fail**, on three counts, one of them the headline.

1. **Headline: "Elm Quay lät två skift dela samma reparationslogg."** The material carries the cross-shift sharing as a want, twice, and never as an outcome: "The team **wanted** staff on different shifts to see the same information", and Lind's "We **wanted** the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done." *Lät … dela* states it as done. What the material does establish is that the log was configured, six staff were trained, 31 reports were entered, and that a consolidated view "helps us" — so something was shared by somebody. It does not establish that it was the two shifts, nor that they were two. A compatible case: through the eight weeks only day staff ever logged in, and the evening shift went on phoning. The second checker found this; it is unrepaired and stands in the piece's most prominent sentence.

2. **"säger hon" / "säger Maya Lind" ×3 for answers given by email.** The material states the channel outright: "All interviews occurred by email." Manner is a claim. The draft mitigates the first instance — "beskriver i en intervju via e-post vad teamet var ute efter" sits directly above it — but the two later attributions stand bare, and a reader meeting "säger Maya Lind" under the last two headings takes her to have spoken. Both checkers noticed it; both filed it as an editorial question rather than a defect, and it was not repaired.

3. **"Telefonanmälningar på ett ställe, e-post på ett annat."** The material says only "telephone reports and emails were **stored separately**." The draft specifies two places. A compatible case: the emails sat in fourteen individual inboxes. The figure is attractive and reads well, and it is more than the sentence it renders.

Smaller, recorded but not decisive:

- **"Hon rekommenderar inte Svale till alla bostadsföretag"** puts the material's past ("Lind **did not** recommend Svale to every housing company") into the present. The negation's scope is right — not-to-all, not not-to-any — and the present is the ordinary Swedish tense for a source's standing position, so this is at the edge; I record it and do not count it.
- **"en logg som skulle visa status för varje reparation"** keeps *reparation* (not narrowed to *felanmälan*) and keeps the requirement as a requirement rather than a finding, which is right.

What holds, and holds well:

- Every figure exact and uncharacterised: 640, two, eight weeks, six, two sessions, 31, two against three working days, 4 December 2025. No count is called high, low or modest, so no comparison is owed.
- The causal line is never crossed, in any layer. The heading "**Siffrorna gäller tid till tilldelning, inte orsak**" puts the reservation where a reader cannot skip it, and the body carries both the workload confound and the note's explicit non-attribution. No causal verb, no causal hedge.
- Assignment time is never turned into completion time: *tid till tilldelning* throughout, *färdigställandetid* only in the list of what was not measured.
- Unknown is kept apart from absent: "Någon jämförelse … **redovisas** inte" is weaker than "is not available", and "Det finns inga mätningar av …" reports missing measurements rather than null results.
- Terms tested both ways: *de boende* ← "residents" asserts no tenure (this was the first checker's Finding 2 and the repair was applied, replacing *hyresgästerna*/*hyresgästnöjdhet* — the right call, since Elm Quay's tenure is nowhere stated); *förvaltar* ← "manages" (the repair replaced *med 640 lägenheter*, which had asserted holding); *reparationslogg* ← "repair log"; *anteckning* ← "note". None comes apart here.
- "Testet har ännu inte utvidgats. Teamet ska **först** kontrollera hur kategorierna fungerar för större reparationer." keeps the sequence and leaves the decision open — the heading above it was repaired away from "Beslutet **hänger på** de större reparationerna", which would have made the check decisive.
- Supplier in the third person throughout; Elm Quay or its team is the subject of every act the material gives them.

## 3. G2 — **Pass**, with one structural blemish.

All required parts are present and in order, and each does its own job: headline (the event and the actor), standfirst (three concrete promises, none of them a repeat of the headline), byline, lead (the before-state as a picture, then the decision), three body sections, ending. The customer account is complete — situation, action, results, appraisal — with Elm Quay acting throughout. The publisher stance is truthful ("Den här kundberättelsen publiceras av Svale Systems"), though it stops short of the material's second half, "it is not independent journalism". The call to action is built from the one supplied route, names it accurately as Svale's implementation checklist and offers it to read.

The blemish: the last subheading, **"Läs checklistan innan ni sätter i gång"**, is an instruction to the reader standing over Lind's personal, qualified assessment. For the length of that quotation the reader is invited to hear her as endorsing their next step, which is exactly what the material forbids ("Lind did not recommend Svale to every housing company", and her final quotation "is her actual, qualified assessment"). The draft catches itself one line later — "Hon rekommenderar inte Svale till alla bostadsföretag" was moved to sit *after* the quotation, which is the right place for it — so the damage is repaired within the section rather than left standing. A heading that named the section's content would have cost nothing.

## 4. L1 — **Pass**, with one clear anglicism.

The Swedish is confident and idiomatic. The lead's verbless opening ("Telefonanmälningar på ett ställe, e-post på ett annat. Så förvarades felanmälningarna …") is a move Swedish feature writing makes on its own, not an import. V2 holds everywhere, including after fronted adverbials and in the conditional without *om* ("Vill ni pröva samma sak hos er?"). Compounds are solid and unsplit (*reparationslogg*, *testanteckningen*, *arbetsbelastning*, *implementeringschecklista*, *färdigställandetid*). *Drar i gång*, *sätter i gång*, *enas om*, *var ute efter* are the phrases a Swedish trade journalist reaches for, and the register is consistent.

The one lapse: **"anteckningen tillskriver uttryckligen inte skillnaden till programvaran."** Swedish *tillskriva* takes a double object — *tillskriver programvaran skillnaden*. The *till* is the English "attribute … **to** the software" carried across intact. It is one preposition, but it sits in the sentence that carries the whole article's reservation, where a Swedish reader is reading closely. Both checkers saw it and both correctly held it outside a source-support comparison; nobody fixed it. Nothing else in the text reads as translated.

Locale mechanics are set aside from this verdict.

## 5. Quoted speech

**Q1.** Draft: "– Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var våra. Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen, säger hon." Source: "We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log."
*One pass* — yes, nothing to supply. *Meaning, stance, certainty* — intact; the pluperfect keeps *redan hade gjort*, the ownership claim keeps its flatness, Svale stays subordinate. Splitting the semicolon into two sentences softens the contrast a shade but leaves both halves adjacent and in order. *Voice* — hers. The attribution verb is the one problem, treated under F1.

**Q2.** Draft: "– Vi lade mer tid på att enas om kategorierna än på att registrera de första anmälningarna. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan nästa fastighet drar i gång, säger Maya Lind." Source: "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts."
*One pass* — yes, and this is the best rendering of the three drafts' hardest line: *drar i gång* is a figure Swedish uses for a thing or a stage getting under way, so the source's own ellipsis (the building standing for the work in it) survives without the reader stopping to supply a noun, and without a noun being supplied for her. *Meaning, certainty, reservation* — the comparative frame is exact, *skulle* carries *would*, no hedge added or removed. *Voice* — the colloquial *drar i gång* is a degree livelier than "starts"; it stays inside what a supervisor speaking plainly would say.

**Q3.** Draft: "– Jag skulle välja att göra testet igen. Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser, säger Maya Lind." Source: "I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation."
*One pass* — yes. *Meaning, stance, reservation* — all three loads survive: the conditional endorsement with *välja* kept rather than smoothed away, the present-tense benefit kept as hers ("hjälper **oss**", not generalised), and the *men* clause last, where the reader holds it. *göra testet igen* is unambiguous in Swedish, more so than *göra om testet*.

**Missing quotations:** none; all three permitted quotations appear, none is welded, none invented.

**In one sentence:** every passage of quoted speech in this draft is speech a Swedish reader takes on one pass with the source's meaning, stance, reservation and voice intact — the only thing moved is outside the quotation marks, in the verb *säger*, which claims she spoke words the material says she typed.
