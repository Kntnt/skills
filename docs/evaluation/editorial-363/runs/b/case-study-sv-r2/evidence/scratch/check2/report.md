## 3. Findings

### Finding 1 — the headline states as accomplished, and counts, what the material carries only as the team's aim

**Draft (line 8):** "Elm Quay lät två skift dela samma reparationslogg"

**Support that exists.** The material carries the shift motive twice, and both times as a want:

> "The team wanted staff on different shifts to see the same information."

> Lind: "We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done."

and the log itself:

> "its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings"

> Lind: "Having one view of the reports helps us"

**Support that is missing.** Nothing in the material says that two shifts did share the log, or how many shifts there are. What the material records as achieved is: a shared log was configured, six staff were trained in two sessions, 31 reports were entered, and one consolidated view of the reports helps the team. The number *two* appears only inside the statement of the aim, and the achievement is *wanted*, not *did*.

**Test, both directions.**

- A case in which the material's statements hold and the headline fails: the six staff Svale trained were all on the morning shift, evening-shift staff were not given the log during the eight weeks, and the 31 reports were entered by day staff. Lind's "Having one view of the reports helps us" still holds — the gain she names is one view of reports that were "previously … stored separately" as telephone reports and emails, which is a gain to the team whether or not the evening shift had access yet. On this case "Elm Quay lät två skift dela samma reparationslogg" is false; the trial was a step towards the want, not its delivery.
- A second such case: Elm Quay runs three shifts and all three had the log in the two trial buildings. "two shifts" then understates the arrangement; the material fixes no number, naming only "staff on different shifts".
- The reverse direction: a case in which the headline holds while the material's statements fail — none; the headline entails the material's statements plus more.

**Nothing supplied excludes the cases.** The only statements about shifts are the two *wanted* sentences above; neither reports an outcome, and neither counts the shifts that had the log.

**Smallest supported repair.** Restore the modality and leave everything else:

> Elm Quay **ville låta** två skift dela samma reparationslogg

With *ville*, the count of two is itself supported, because the want the material records is precisely about the evening shift and the morning shift. An alternative of the same size, if the aim-framing is unwanted in a headline: "Elm Quay prövade en gemensam reparationslogg i två fastigheter".

## 4. Editorial questions (not defects, no repair proposed)

**4.1 The final heading sits above Lind's quotation.** "Läs checklistan innan ni sätter i gång" is the publisher's own call to its supplied next step, which the brief authorises. The draft's three earlier headings are descriptive summaries of their sections; this one is an imperative, and the section it opens begins with Lind's quotation about leaving "an extra week for preparation" and continues with her reservation. A reader carrying the descriptive pattern forward may take the heading as Lind's advice, and nothing in the material connects her to any checklist. Whether that is a misattribution or an ordinary publisher call-to-action heading is genuinely unsettled; I record it as a question for the editor rather than as a defect.

**4.2 "säger" for answers given by email.** The material states "All interviews occurred by email", and the draft discloses the channel before the first quotation ("beskriver i en intervju via e-post"). The later tags at lines 34 and 38 read "säger Maya Lind". After an explicit disclosure this is a conventional Swedish attribution tag rather than an assertion of spoken dialogue, and the material permits the quotations; "skriver" would be tighter. An editorial choice, not a source-support defect.

**4.3 Address form.** The resolved composition guidance makes *du* the default and warns that *ni* to a single reader "is either archaic or a deliberate distancing". Here "ni"/"er" ("hos er") reads as the reader's organisation — an operations manager at a housing company — which is genuine plural, so the guidance's objection does not bite. Noted only because the guidance was supplied.

## 5. Translation check — quotations

Translation is permitted: "Translation of the quotations is permitted, preserving stance and qualification." Each quotation is checked for the same meaning, stance, certainty and distinctive voice in idiomatic Swedish speech, separately from source support.

**Quotation 1 (line 22).**
Source: "We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log."
Draft: "Vi ville att kvällsskiftet skulle se vad morgonskiftet redan hade gjort. Kategorierna var våra. Svale hjälpte oss att lägga in dem i loggen."
Meaning, stance and certainty carried; the past perfect "had already done" → "redan hade gjort"; compounds written as one word ("kvällsskiftet", "morgonskiftet"); nothing added and no hedge removed. The semicolon becomes a full stop, which loosens a faint contrastive link between *ours* and *Svale helped*, but the two clauses keep their content and the limit on Svale's part is intact. No concrete obstruction to the source reading. No finding.

**Quotation 2 (line 34).**
Source: "We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts."
Draft: "Vi lade mer tid på att enas om kategorierna än på att registrera de första anmälningarna. Den tiden skulle jag avsätta innan nästa fastighet drar i gång."
The comparison is parallel in both ("mer tid på … än på …"), the conditional "would" → "skulle" is kept, and the fronted "Den tiden" respects V2. The implicit referent of "that time" is left implicit in Swedish exactly as in English, which the supplied context settles the same way for both. "drar i gång" is a register-level rendering of "starts" that is ordinary workplace Swedish and does not obstruct the source reading; it is a synonym question, not a defect. No finding.

**Quotation 3 (line 38).**
Source: "I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation."
Draft: "Jag skulle välja att göra testet igen. Att ha en samlad bild av anmälningarna hjälper oss, men jag skulle lägga in en extra vecka för förberedelser."
The qualification is what matters here — the material says "Her final quotation is her actual, qualified assessment" — and the draft keeps both conditionals ("skulle … skulle") and the adversative "men", so the endorsement stays conditional and reserved rather than becoming a recommendation. "en samlad bild av anmälningarna" for "one view of the reports" shifts the emphasis from singleness to consolidation; in a text whose opening states that telephone reports and emails were stored apart, that is the same thing, and no concrete obstruction to the source reading follows. No finding.

**Across the three.** Speech dashes rather than quotation marks, as the resolved Swedish guidance prescribes for reported speech; no *man*; double definiteness where required ("de första anmälningarna"); V2 respected inside the quoted speech; no filler added and no self-correction introduced or removed. No translation findings.

**Meaning changed in crossing languages outside quoted speech.** Checked passage by passage in §1; the closest calls are "redovisas inte" for "is not available" (§1.5, weaker in the safe direction), "två av fastigheterna" for "two buildings" (§1.4, partitive) and the s-passive in "har ännu inte utvidgats" (§1.7). None changes a claim. No source-support finding arises from the crossing itself.
