## 2. Quoted speech (English; one direct passage, plus one indirect report)

**Q1 (direct) — "We wanted the rotas settled before the winter. They were, just about. After that the depot stopped ringing round at six in the morning," said depot manager Ruth Calder.**
- Wording: unchanged, word for word, attribution included.
- One pass: yes. "They were, just about" is ordinary English verb-phrase ellipsis (they were [settled]), and the comma-plus-qualifier is how English hedges an affirmative in speech; it reads whole. "ringing round" is everyday British idiom for phoning round the pool of drivers, and the previous paragraphs have established the depot and its drivers. "at six in the morning" needs nothing supplied. A competent reader of English takes it on one pass and stops nowhere.
- The passage did not come back changed, and since a reader does not stop in it there is no obstacle the reply needed to report.

**Indirect report — "Calder said the four services had been straightforward to staff over the trial. She would not say whether that would hold across a full year."**

- The change narrows the scope of what Calder is reported as saying, from the whole trial to the period after the rotas were settled. It supplies no event, fact, name or figure the text does not carry — "once the rotas were settled" is taken straight from her own quotation above. Her stance, her certainty and her refusal to extend the claim to a full year all survive; if anything the narrowed version reports her more faithfully. The reply names this change explicitly and explains why.
