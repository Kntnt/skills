## 2. Findings

Three findings, ranked. All are source-support findings. Each is a changed or missing support, not a preference.

### Finding 1 — "the log held 31 reports" states the log's contents where the material states a note's count under exclusions

**Draft** (line 14): "Eight weeks later the log held 31 reports, and the team had a decision to make."

**Material**: "Elm Quay's internal trial note, 4 December 2025, says that 31 repair reports were entered. **It excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial.**"

**What differs**: the thing measured, and whose assertion it is. The material gives 31 as the figure a note reports, and states that emergencies and pre-trial work stand outside it. The draft asserts, in the narrator's own voice and without the exclusions, that the log contained 31 reports.

**Concrete case, compatible with the material, where the material's statement holds and the draft's fails**: the team entered every report in the shared log, emergencies included, and the trial note counted only the 31 non-emergency reports ordered during the trial. The log then held, say, 36 reports while the note correctly said 31 were entered. Nothing supplied excludes this: "It excludes emergencies" attributes the exclusion to the note (the draft itself renders it that way at line 24), not to the log. The reverse direction also fails to rescue the draft — if the log held exactly 31, the note's statement still holds, so the two are not equivalent.

**Smallest supported repair**: "Eight weeks later the trial note counted 31 reports, and the team had a decision to make." (Or "…31 reports had been entered…".) This keeps the sentence's rhythm and its narrative function while returning the count to the document that asserts it; the exclusions then sit where the draft already places them, at line 24.

### Finding 2 — the trial note is attributed to the maintenance team, where the material attributes it to Elm Quay

**Draft** (line 10): "**The team's** trial note did not claim the software was the reason."
**Draft** (line 24): "**The team's** internal trial note, dated 4 December 2025, records 31 repair reports entered during the eight weeks."

**Material**: "**Elm Quay's** internal trial note, 4 December 2025, says that 31 repair reports were entered."

**What differs**: whose document it is, and therefore whose assertion the disclaimer at line 10 is. The material says the note is the company's; it nowhere says the maintenance team wrote or owns it.

**Concrete case, compatible with the material, where the material's statement holds and the draft's fails**: Elm Quay's central administration compiled the internal trial note from the log's data and the team's input, and it is filed as a company document rather than a team document. "Elm Quay's internal trial note" is then exactly right while "the team's trial note" is not. No supplied statement excludes this; the material names no author for the note, and the only authorship it does establish elsewhere is the team's authorship of the categories.

This matters beyond the possessive, because the draft's central disclaimer — that the note does not attribute the difference to the software — carries more weight as a company-level document than as the trialling team's own note, and less, if the reader takes the team to be vouching for its own trial.

**Smallest supported repair**: replace "The team's" with "Elm Quay's" in both places: "Elm Quay's trial note did not claim the software was the reason." and "Elm Quay's internal trial note, dated 4 December 2025, records…".

### Finding 3 — the summary's definite "the repair reports" implies every report was logged

**Draft** (line 10): "For eight weeks, Elm Quay Housing's maintenance team logged **the** repair reports from two of its buildings in one place."

**Material**: "its own maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log in two buildings"; "31 repair reports were entered. It excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial."

**What differs**: scope. The definite plural reads as the whole of the reports arising in those two buildings during the period. The material establishes only that reports were entered into a shared log, alongside an explicit exclusion.

**Concrete case, compatible with the material, where the material's statement holds and the draft's fails**: emergency call-outs in the two buildings were handled on the existing telephone route and never entered in the shared log, so some repair reports from those buildings were not logged in one place. The material is compatible with this — telephone reporting stayed open for residents, and emergencies are singled out for exclusion — and nothing supplied excludes it.

**Smallest supported repair**: delete the article — "logged repair reports from two of its buildings in one place." One word, and the scope claim goes with it.
