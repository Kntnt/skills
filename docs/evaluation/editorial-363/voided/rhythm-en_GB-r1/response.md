# Kirkwell's night bus trial ended in April

**Kirkwell's council put four late services back on the valley route for a six-month trial. It counted 2,140 boardings excluding concessionary travel, 41 per cent of them on Fridays. A decision on a permanent timetable is due in June.**

By The Newsroom

Four late buses ran between Kirkwell and Ardfern from November to April. They had left the timetable in 2023. The council paid for the trial out of its transport reserve, changed no fares, and rostered the drivers from the existing depot.

## Friday carried 41 per cent of the boardings

The council counted 2,140 boardings on the four services over the six months. Friday accounted for 41 per cent of them. The counts exclude concessionary travel, which the operator records separately.

Passenger numbers on the daytime services did not change over the same period. The council's report does not say whether the late services drew new travellers or moved existing ones.

## In-house rotas covered the trial, and no overtime figure is published

The depot rostered the trial from its own drivers. No overtime figure is published.

“We wanted the rotas settled before the winter. They were, just about. After that the depot stopped ringing round at six in the morning,” said depot manager Ruth Calder.

She would not say whether the staffing would hold across a full year.

## Findings left unresolved

Both are defects the correction round itself introduced, which is why no further round was taken: the next one would have been repairing the last one's work, and that is a judgement to put in front of a person rather than spend a budget on.

**1. The headline no longer states the article's angle.** "Kirkwell's night bus trial ended in April" (41 characters, 7 words) reports that the trial finished, which is the one thing in the piece a reader could have assumed. The angle is what the trial produced and what happens next: 2,140 boardings with 41 per cent of them on Fridays, daytime numbers unchanged, and the council deciding in June. Headlines asks a headline to state "the single most important message, the sentence that would remain if only one could", and sets the test that it "could not head a different text on the same subject" — this one could head any text about this trial. It also opens on the same word as the standfirst, *Kirkwell's*, which Headlines lists among what a headline and a standfirst avoid. A reader judging at a glance whether the piece is for them learns only that something has finished.

This followed from the repair of the headline the text arrived with, "Night buses return to the Kirkwell valley route". That one claimed a present, unqualified return the text does not claim — the services ran November to April and the decision is still to come. The over-claim is gone and the angle went with it.

**2. The second subheading is two clauses joined by *and*, and its second half repeats its own section's sentence word for word.** "In-house rotas covered the trial, and no overtime figure is published" runs to 69 of the 70 characters a subheading may have, and its second half is the section's own second sentence, "No overtime figure is published." Headlines asks a subheading for a simple clause in straight word order, worded from the whole section in words its first sentence does not use, and short enough to sound like something a person would say aloud. The scanning reader meets a heading built like a body sentence and then reads half of it again two lines down, so the heading stops being a way into the section and becomes part of it. Stating one of the two — the in-house rotas, which is the section's news — leaves the unpublished overtime figure where the text already puts it.

This followed from the repair of "Staffing the four services", which named a subject without saying anything about it.

## Claims removed or changed

**Removed.** "Calder said the four services had been straightforward to staff over the trial." The finding named that sentence itself as the defect: it reported Calder as calling the whole trial straightforward directly beneath her quoted account of rotas settled only "just about" and a depot that had been ringing round at six in the morning, and the text reconciled the two nowhere. With it gone, the quotation is the text's only account of those six months. Her refusal to extend anything to a full year is retained.

**Changed.** "She would not say whether that would hold across a full year." became "She would not say whether the staffing would hold across a full year." The pronoun had pointed at the sentence removed above. The limit this places on what the text asserts beyond the trial stands at the same strength.

**Changed.** The standfirst's count now reads "2,140 boardings excluding concessionary travel". Its scope was narrowed to the scope the body already gave it, answering the finding that a standfirst read on its own — which is the use a standfirst is built for — presented the figure as the whole of the trial's boardings.

**Changed.** The headline, from "Night buses return to the Kirkwell valley route" to "Kirkwell's night bus trial ended in April", answering the finding described above. What it claims is now weaker than the text rather than stronger, which is unresolved finding 1.

The first and third subheadings were rewritten from labels into statements of what their sections say. Neither carries a claim the text did not already make.
