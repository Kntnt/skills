**2. Claim changes the account misses**

- **Headline: whose view it is (singular or plural).**
  - Before: "Gemensam ärendebild hjälper, säger Elm Quay om loggförsöket"
  - After: "Elm Quays arbetsledare anser att gemensam ärendebild hjälper"
  - What changed: "Elm Quays arbetsledare" can be singular or plural, so who holds the view is now open. In the plural it becomes the view of all the company's supervisors. In the singular it implies Lind is the company's only supervisor. The body supports only one supervisor, Maya Lind, saying it ("Att ha en gemensam bild av ärendena hjälper oss").
  - What the account says: "Attribueringen har flyttats från bolaget till arbetsledaren." It reports a move to one supervisor and leaves out both the plural reading and the "only supervisor" reading. Finding 1 of the same reply names both readings as unresolved, so the account falls short of the change and also contradicts that finding.

- **Headline: a reported statement became a stated opinion.**
  - Before: "… hjälper, säger Elm Quay …" reported something said, which the reader could match to the quoted remark.
  - After: "… arbetsledare anser att …" states in the article's own voice what the supervisor holds to be true. The headline no longer shows that the claim comes from a remark, which changes how it is framed and how certain it sounds.
  - What the account says: "Tidigare påstod rubriken att Elm Quay … säger …" and "Nu påstår den att Elm Quays arbetsledare anser …", followed by "Attribueringen har flyttats från bolaget till arbetsledaren." It quotes both verbs but names only the change of speaker. It says nothing about the shift from reported statement to stated opinion.
