## Source-support findings

### S1 — Occupational scope in two passages

**Draft:** “underhållsansvariga Maya Lind” in the lead and the introduction to quotation 1.

**Source:** “Customer interview with maintenance supervisor Maya Lind”.

**Difference:** “Supervisor” identifies a supervisory role. “Underhållsansvariga”, read as an occupational title, identifies the person responsible for maintenance. The source provides no statement that Lind holds the customer's general maintenance responsibility; identifying the person does not settle the role's scope. This is an unquoted source-support issue, not a translated-quotation issue and not a gender defect.

**Source true, draft title fails:** Lind could supervise a maintenance shift's staff while a different manager holds the company's maintenance responsibility, budget and overall maintenance decisions. That is compatible with “maintenance supervisor”; the source does not say Lind is the person responsible for maintenance as a whole. The quotation “The maintenance team designed its categories” does not assign that general role to Lind personally. In that case, describing Lind by the broader responsible-person title would misidentify the role.

**Draft title true, source role fails:** A maintenance-responsible technical coordinator could own planning and maintenance decisions while having no staff to supervise; tradespeople might instead report to another manager. Such a person can be “underhållsansvarig” without being a maintenance supervisor. The supplied material has no statement equating these two responsibilities or ruling out that organisational distinction.

**Smallest supported repair:** Use “arbetsledaren för underhåll” in both places: “Här berättar arbetsledaren för underhåll Maya Lind …” and “I en mejlintervju beskriver arbetsledaren för underhåll Maya Lind …”. This preserves the named speaker and supervisory function without adding general responsibility for maintenance.

### S2 — Emergency exclusion broadened to urgent cases

**Draft:** “Akuta ärenden … ingår inte.”

**Source:** “It excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial.”

**Difference:** The source excludes emergencies. “Akuta ärenden” can include pressing repair cases that are urgent without constituting emergencies. The supplied source does not define its exclusion category as all urgent cases. The difference affects the reported set and consequently the qualifications governing the count and timing figures.

**Draft exclusion true, source exclusion does not establish it:** A repair of a room's nonessential fitting could need prompt handling before a scheduled inspection, making it an urgent case, while posing no immediate hazard and not qualifying as an emergency. Such a report could have been among the trial's 31 reports. Nothing supplied excludes it: “It excludes emergencies” does not exclude all urgent nonemergency work. The draft's broader exclusion would remove that case.

**Source exclusion true, draft exclusion fails:** For an ordinary present emergency requiring immediate response, such as a dangerous active water leak, “akut” also applies; no sound opposite-direction counterexample is established on that ordinary reading. This direction therefore does not independently show a defect. The first-direction counterexample remains: the terms can classify a nonemergency urgent report differently, and no supplied statement equates their extension.

**Smallest supported repair:** “Nödfall och arbeten som beställts före försöket ingår inte.” This preserves the source's emergency category rather than substituting the wider urgency category. Keep the pre-trial work exclusion intact.
