# Source-comparison report: draft 1

Compared the complete supplied brief and source package with the complete draft, including its title, introduction, byline, headings and quotations. Target language: British English. Both source and draft are English; no quotation translation is involved. General editing, mechanical review and length assessment are outside this report.

## Findings

### F1 — Source support: the title states a stronger result than the reported ability

**Draft:** “Fenwick staff see which instruments await parts”

**Source:** “The workshop's trial summary, dated 23 February 2026, records 38 jobs entered in the schedule. It says staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts.” The source additionally qualifies the evidential position: “The summary and interview establish the team's reported experience, not a measured improvement in repair speed or a recommendation for every workshop.”

**Difference:** The source attributes an ability to the trial summary: staff *could see* which instruments were waiting for parts. The title makes a direct assertion that staff *see* them. It does not carry the modal qualification or the source of the reported experience. The historical present may be a normal headline convention; this finding does not depend on treating that convention alone as a defect.

**Concrete case, source true but draft assertion false:** The summary reports that the schedule enabled staff to identify instruments awaiting parts, but staff did not actually exercise that ability in ordinary job handling. A reported ability can hold without the observation asserted by “staff see” having occurred. Alternatively, the workshop's summary can report an ability that its writer believed existed, without that report independently establishing the actual result. No supplied statement rules out either case. The final source sentence expressly limits the package to reported experience.

**Reverse direction:** Actual seeing entails the ability to see, so there is no reverse counterexample to that modal relation. Nor can a material-compatible case erase the source report, because the supplied package expressly gives it. The defect is the stronger actuality and unqualified evidential position added to the source's attributed ability, not a mutually exclusive account.

**Smallest supported repair:** “Fenwick summary says staff could see which instruments awaited parts”. This preserves the visibility result while naming its source and retaining its modality.

### F2 — Source support: the introduction supplies an unsupported sequence

**Draft:** “After a six-week scheduling trial, Fenwick Instrument Repairs approved continued use for new jobs but postponed adding its backlog.”

**Source:** “In January 2026 the workshop team began a six-week trial of a shared bench schedule.” Later: “The workshop's trial summary, dated 23 February 2026, records 38 jobs entered in the schedule. It says staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts. It does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction. The manager approved continued use for new jobs but postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions.”

**Difference:** The draft's introductory “After” places the approval and postponement after the six-week trial. The source gives the trial duration, summary date and decision, but supplies no date for the decision and no explicit ordering between that decision and the trial's completion. The subject change from the manager to Fenwick is a defensible institutional attribution in this context and is not a separate finding.

**Concrete case, source true but draft assertion false:** The manager approved continued use for new jobs and postponed adding the backlog in week five; the team completed the six-week trial, and the 23 February summary then recorded the decision. Every supplied statement remains true, but the decision did not occur after the trial. No supplied sentence excludes this case. Its position after the summary description in the material is not a stated chronology of the decision.

**Reverse direction:** A decision made after the trial is compatible with both accounts. There is no converse counterexample within the supplied material: the defect is the draft's narrowing of an unspecified decision date into a specified sequence.

**Smallest supported repair:** Delete “After a six-week scheduling trial,” and begin “Fenwick Instrument Repairs approved continued use …”. The body already gives the six-week duration and preserves the status-review condition.
