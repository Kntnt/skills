## Difference tests and support boundaries

These are examinations of potentially consequential differences, not findings. No defensible passage is proposed for revision.

1. **Title: “identify” versus “see which”.** Both phrases concern finding the waiting-for-parts instruments through the reported schedule. A case in which staff see a waiting-status label for a named instrument but cannot recognise that instrument's physical appearance does not contradict the title: the title claims status identification, not recognition of appearance. Conversely, a case in which they identify waiting instruments through another means would not prove the source's schedule report; the title and ensuing summary attribution do not claim that other means. Neither expression adds accuracy verification, physical inspection or a causal benefit. No concrete unsupported claim survives the ordinary contextual reading.

2. **Institutional approval versus the manager's approval.** A case in which the manager approves continued use while an individual employee disagrees is compatible with “The manager approved continued use for new jobs” and with the standfirst's institutional approval wording. The draft does not assert unanimity. A case in which an employee approves but the manager refuses would change the source's decision-maker, but the supplied statement “The manager approved continued use for new jobs” excludes that case. The body names the manager explicitly. No changed actor is asserted.

3. **Backlog postponement in the standfirst.** A case in which the team has not yet completed the review is compatible with both passages. A case in which the manager has permanently abandoned the backlog would be an additional claim, but the draft asserts postponement and explicitly carries the source condition: “until the team had reviewed its status definitions.” No permanent abandonment is given as fact, and no later addition is presumed.

4. **Passive telephone sentence.** The source entails that customer collection arrangements continued to be discussed by telephone. A hypothetical workshop that only discusses collection internally by telephone could satisfy the passive sentence in isolation; that is a less specific statement, not an added claim that internal-only discussion occurred. Here that hypothetical is also excluded as a replacement account by “The team continued to use telephone calls to discuss collection arrangements with customers.” Nothing in the draft denies or changes those interlocutors. Supported detail has been omitted; no competing factual detail has been substituted.

5. **Interview framing.** A scene involving Vale's facial expression, voice, workshop visit or emotional reaction would exceed the package. The draft supplies no such scene. “Explained”, “reflects” and “said” describe the provided email answers and their subject matter. The source states “The interview took place by email”, and both relevant passages in the draft expressly say email.

6. **Knowledge and causation.** A case in which repair speed improved without being measured is compatible with “It does not measure repair duration” and with the draft. So is a case in which speed did not improve. The draft selects neither as known. A claim that the schedule caused a change in speed, revenue or satisfaction would require further support; no such claim appears. The source's “38 jobs entered in the schedule” does not establish a throughput count, which the draft never asserts.

7. **Vale's proposed extra week.** A case in which no extra week was actually taken remains compatible with the source's “I would allow another week” and the draft's exact quotation. A case in which Vale recommends that every workshop take another week is not asserted: the speaker remains “I”, the setting remains this choice, and the source expressly distinguishes the material from “a recommendation for every workshop”. The draft preserves the conditional personal standpoint.
