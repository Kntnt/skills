## Findings

### F1 — Source support: historical absence exceeds the available measurement statement

**Draft:** “There were no cost, resident-satisfaction, or completion-time measurements.”

**Source:** “There are no cost, resident-satisfaction or completion-time measurements.”

The categories are unchanged. What differs is time and the state of knowledge conveyed by absence. In the draft's past-tense trial narrative, “There were no ... measurements” establishes their historical nonexistence. The complete source supplies no such measurements and states present absence; it does not separately state that none were taken during the trial.

**Source true, draft false — concrete compatible case:** Cost measurements were taken during the trial but the records were later lost, so there are no such measurements now and none are supplied in the source package. The historical claim that there were no measurements would be false. No supplied statement expressly says the customer never took these measurements or rules out this case.

**Draft true, source false — reverse semantic test:** No such measurements existed during the trial, but a retrospective measurement was later produced and exists at the time of the source statement. The draft's historical claim could then be true while the source's present absence would be false. That reverse case is excluded from this package by the supplied statement “There are no cost, resident-satisfaction or completion-time measurements.” Excluding the reverse case does not exclude the first case or establish the stronger historical negative.

**Smallest supported repair:** “The supplied material includes no cost, resident-satisfaction, or completion-time measurements.” This preserves the measurement categories and accurately states the available evidence without asserting historical nonexistence.
