# Fenwick’s manager approves Benchline for new jobs

Fenwick Instrument Repairs postponed adding its backlog to the Benchline schedule pending a review of job status definitions. For workshop managers, its experience shows how the team set up job statuses and what its trial summary could establish.

By Thomas Barregren

In January 2026, the team at Fenwick Instrument Repairs began a six-week trial of a shared bench schedule. The workshop services brass and woodwind instruments; jobs awaiting parts and jobs ready for work had previously appeared on separate paper sheets. The team chose Benchline after checking that it could distinguish those statuses.

## Benchline provides setup and training

The workshop defined the statuses and decided who would update them. Benchline configured those choices and trained four staff in one online session. The team continued to discuss customer collection arrangements by telephone.

In an email interview on 2 March 2026, manager Priya Vale explained the preparation: ‘We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench.’

## The summary documents visibility

The workshop’s trial summary, dated 23 February 2026, records 38 jobs entered in the schedule. It says staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts.

The summary does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction.

## Vale considers the next step

The manager approved continued use for new jobs, but postponed adding the backlog pending a review of the status definitions.

Asked what would change next time, Vale wrote: ‘I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog.’
