## Pronoun accounting

Person, number and natural gender are accounted for separately from referent identity. No natural-gender assertion is introduced anywhere in the draft. Priya Vale's name and managerial role do not establish gender; the draft does not use a natural-gender pronoun for Vale.

- **“which services”**: The relative pronoun refers to Fenwick Instrument Repairs. The singular verb carries singular grammatical agreement with the organisation. It has no grammatical or natural-gender marking. Source agreement is the same: “Fenwick Instrument Repairs services”.
- **“it could distinguish”**: Third-person singular “it” refers to Benchline in the source and draft. It carries grammatical singular number and non-personal reference, with no natural-gender assertion. Exact source support: “The team chose Benchline after checking that it could distinguish these statuses.”
- **“those statuses”**: Plural demonstrative refers to the two named statuses in the draft; the source's “these statuses” is also plural and has the same referent. Neither has natural-gender content.
- **“who would update them”**: “Who” denotes the selected human updater or updaters without resolving number, person beyond third-person reference, or natural gender. “Them” is third-person plural and refers to statuses, with no natural-gender assertion. Source wording is identical.
- **“those choices”**: Plural demonstrative refers to the workshop's status and updater decisions. Source wording is identical; no natural-gender feature.
- **“We had to agree”**: First-person plural includes Vale and the team in the supplied interview context. The source uses the same “We”. The draft preserves first person, plural number and absence of gender marking rather than inferring those features merely from identity.
- **“it had reached the right bench”**: Third-person singular “it” refers to the singular “A part”, both in source and draft. This is non-personal grammatical reference, with no natural-gender information.
- **“It does not measure”**: Third-person singular “It” refers to the singular trial summary. The source uses the same pronoun with the same agreement. No natural-gender information.
- **“its status definitions”**: Third-person singular possessive agrees with the collective singular “team”; the definitions are plural. The source has identical wording. It asserts ownership of the definitions, not a gender feature or singular headcount for the team's members.
- **“I would use” / “I would allow”**: Both are first-person singular pronouns representing Vale's own statements. Exact source wording establishes those features. Neither pronoun marks natural gender.
- **“use it again”**: Third-person singular non-personal pronoun retains the source's reference to the chosen shared bench schedule/Benchline use in the question's context. Both texts leave it in the same exact quotation. It carries no natural-gender feature and is not made more specific by the draft.

The definite phrases “the manager”, “manager Priya Vale” and “Vale” identify the same source-established person without asserting natural gender. The collective “team” is grammatically singular but denotes multiple workshop people as established by the material; the draft does not reduce that group to an individual.
