# Elm Quay brings trial reports into one log

Elm Quay Housing tested a shared repair log in two buildings. This account, published by supplier Svale Systems, follows the team's choices, the limits of its trial figures, and the maintenance supervisor's assessment of what to allow for next time.

By Thomas Barregren

Telephone reports and emails were stored separately at Elm Quay Housing, which manages 640 apartments. In September 2025, its maintenance team decided to trial a shared repair log so staff on different shifts could see the same information.

## The team keeps telephone reporting open

The team chose Svale Systems after testing whether the log could show the status of each repair. Residents could still report repairs by telephone. Svale configured the log and trained six staff during two sessions.

Maintenance supervisor Maya Lind explained the aim in an email interview: “We wanted the evening shift to see what the morning shift had already done. The categories were ours; Svale helped us put them into the log.”

Lind also described the setup work: “We spent more time agreeing on the categories than entering the first reports. I would set that time aside before the next building starts.”

## The trial note limits the comparison

The trial lasted eight weeks. Elm Quay's internal trial note, dated December 4, 2025, says that 31 repair reports were entered. The note excludes emergencies and work ordered before the trial.

Median time from report to assignment was two working days during the trial, compared with three in the preceding eight-week period. The periods had different workloads, and the note explicitly does not attribute the difference to the software.

The supplied material includes no cost, resident-satisfaction, or completion-time measurements. Lind's appraisal was separate from those figures: “I would choose to do the trial again. Having one view of the reports helps us, but I would leave an extra week for preparation.”
