# Fenwick staff could see which instruments awaited parts

Fenwick Instrument Repairs’ trial summary records 38 jobs entered in a shared bench schedule. The manager approved continued use for new jobs, while the backlog awaited a review of status definitions.

By Thomas Barregren

Jobs awaiting parts and jobs ready for work appeared on separate paper sheets at Fenwick Instrument Repairs, which services brass and woodwind instruments. In January 2026, the team began a six-week trial with Benchline after checking that it could distinguish those statuses.

## Benchline configures the workshop’s choices

The team defined the statuses and decided who would update them. Benchline configured those choices and trained four staff in one online session. The team continued to use telephone calls to discuss collection arrangements with customers.

Asked in an email interview on 2 March 2026 what the team had to settle, manager Priya Vale wrote: ‘We had to agree what ready for work meant. A part being in the building did not always mean that it had reached the right bench.’

## The summary does not measure repair duration

The trial summary, dated 23 February 2026, records 38 jobs entered in the schedule and says staff could see which instruments were waiting for parts. It does not measure repair duration, revenue or customer satisfaction.

Vale approved continued use for new jobs but postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions.

## Vale reflects on the next steps

Asked about making the same choice again and what would change, Vale wrote: ‘I would use it again for new jobs. I would allow another week to check the status names before adding the backlog.’
