The plural is supplied by the quotation itself, not merely by resolving a referent. It conveys no natural gender.
- **Draft “I ...” in both quotations** is first-person singular, exactly matching source “I,” with Maya Lind established as speaker. Singular number and person come from the quoted form. It conveys no natural gender; a name and supervisor role do not independently establish gender.

- **Draft “That count”** explicitly points to the 31 entered reports. It is not a gendered personal pronoun; its singular count noun does not alter the plurality of reports.
- **Draft “its categories”** refers to the singular maintenance team. The source says “The maintenance team designed its categories” and later “the team will decide after checking how the categories work.” Possession and singular organizational reference are supported. It conveys no natural gender.

## Findings

### F1 — Source support: unsupported company attribution for the checklist

**Draft passage:** “Svale Systems’ [implementation checklist](https://example.invalid/svale/checklist)”

**Supplied support:** “The publisher is the supplier, Svale Systems.” And: “The optional next step is the supplied implementation checklist at https://example.invalid/svale/checklist; it is a document to read, not a consultation booking or a product trial.”

**Missing or changed support:** The brief establishes who publishes the case and which checklist readers may read. It does not establish who owns, produces, or presents that checklist as its own. The URL contains `svale`, but that string does not by itself establish the document's affiliation. The change concerns attribution, rather than the document's existence, format, or destination.

**Source-true/draft-false case:** Svale publishes the case and the supplied URL leads to an independent housing association's checklist furnished for the case, without the checklist being Svale's. Every supplied statement remains true; calling it “Svale Systems’” checklist would fail. No supplied statement excludes this case.

**Draft-true/source-false direction:** Svale's own checklist could exist at that URL while Svale is not the publisher of the case or the document is not the supplied optional next step. The possessive alone would then be true while those supplied statements would fail. The draft's added attribution is therefore not equivalent to the supplied combination of publisher and destination facts.

**Smallest supported repair:** Delete “Svale Systems’,” leaving: “For operations managers considering a similar trial, the [implementation checklist](https://example.invalid/svale/checklist) is an optional next step to read.”

This keeps the supported audience, optionality, link, document type, and reading action.
