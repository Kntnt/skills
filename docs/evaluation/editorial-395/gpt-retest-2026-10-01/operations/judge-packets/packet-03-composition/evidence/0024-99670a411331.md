Invocation: `/write --genre=casestudy --language=en_GB --output=response source.md`.

## Findings

### F1 — The title presents retention as accomplished

**Draft:** “Fenwick keeps its shared schedule for new jobs”

**Source:** “The manager approved continued use for new jobs but postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions.”

**Difference:** The source establishes the manager’s approval of continued use. The headline’s ordinary factual reading says Fenwick continues to retain/use the schedule for new jobs. Approval and implementation are different states. The source does not establish implementation after approval.

**Concrete test:** The manager could approve continued use, with that approval awaiting an account renewal or another implementation step; no subsequent new job need have used the schedule. This fits the supplied account and makes the approval true without establishing the headline’s accomplished continuation. Conversely, staff could continue using a schedule without managerial approval; the supplied statement “The manager approved continued use for new jobs” excludes that as an account of this case. No supplied statement excludes the first case.

**Smallest supported repair:** “Fenwick’s manager approves continued use of its shared schedule for new jobs”. This preserves the headline’s subject matter and warranted certainty, while identifying the established decision.

Type: source-support finding. No translation is involved.

### F2 — The standfirst adds a sequence not established by the material

**Draft:** “Fenwick Instrument Repairs postponed adding its backlog after a six-week Benchline trial.”

**Source:** “In January 2026 the workshop team began a six-week trial of a shared bench schedule.” Also: “The workshop’s trial summary, dated 23 February 2026, records 38 jobs entered in the schedule.” Also: “The manager approved continued use for new jobs but postponed adding the backlog until the team had reviewed its status definitions.”

**Difference:** The source states when the trial began, its duration, the summary’s date and the postponement’s condition. It does not say that the manager postponed adding the backlog after the six weeks had elapsed. “After” adds a chronological relationship and implies a completed trial before the postponement.

**Concrete test:** A six-week trial beginning on 20 January could run until 3 March. Its 23 February summary could be an interim summary, and the manager could postpone adding the backlog on 23 February while approving continued use for new jobs. All supplied statements can hold, while “postponed … after a six-week … trial” fails. Neither “trial summary” nor its date explicitly rules out an interim summary. Conversely, postponement after some six-week trial does not establish that this trial began in January or that a team review was its condition; the source expressly supplies those facts. No supplied statement excludes the first case.

**Smallest supported repair:** Replace the standfirst’s first sentence with: “Fenwick Instrument Repairs’ manager approved continued use of its Benchline schedule for new jobs but postponed adding the backlog pending the team’s review of its status definitions.”

Type: source-support finding. No translation is involved.
