## Pronoun, person, number and gender accounting

- “det egna underhållsteamet” identifies the customer's team, corresponding to “its own maintenance team”. “Det” marks the grammatical neuter of “teamet”; it makes no natural-gender assertion about staff.
- In the first quotation, “Vi” carries first-person plural, as “We” does. Both report the speaker's collective participation. The draft adds no count, membership list or natural gender. “Jag” carries first-person singular, as “I” does; Lind's identity follows from the quotation attribution, not from the pronoun's gender.
- In the second quotation, “Jag” likewise preserves first-person singular and “oss” preserves first-person plural from “us”. Neither Swedish form conveys natural gender. The source's “Her final quotation” supplies feminine natural-gender information, but the draft adds no gendered pronoun or narrower gender assertion.
- “Sin bedömning” and “sitt samlade omdöme” identify Lind as the possessor through Swedish reflexive possession. Their different endings agree with the grammatical genders of “bedömning” and “omdöme”; they do not assert Lind's natural gender.
- “De boende” is a plural expression for residents in the source's reporting context. It adds neither a resident count nor a natural-gender assertion. It preserves telephone availability for residents without asserting actual use by all of them.
- The relative “som” in the description of Elm Quay Housing correctly refers to the company that manages the flats. It introduces no personal number or natural-gender claim.
- “Du” addresses an individual reader, consistent with an optional next step for the brief's operations-manager audience. It is second-person singular and gender-neutral; addressing one reader does not assert that the entire intended audience consists of one person.
- The draft replaces the source's “It” in “It chose Svale Systems” with “Teamet”. The antecedent is explicitly supplied by the source. Neither form gives the human team a natural gender.
- “Den tiden” refers to time spent agreeing on categories in the immediately preceding sentence, as “that time” does. Its common-gender form is grammatical and does not encode a human characteristic. “Perioderna” and “Anteckningen” likewise retain the source's periods and note, without changing the bearer of an assertion.

## Cross-language boundary checks

The potentially consequential terms were checked in their actual contexts, rather than by dictionary overlap alone: residential “flats”/“lägenheter”; the two housing “buildings”/“hus”; repair “reports”/“anmälningar”; “assignment”/“tilldelning”; excluded “emergencies”/“akuta ärenden”; “trained”/“utbildade”; two “sessions”/“tillfällen”; repair “completion-time”/“tiden till färdig reparation”; and “one view of the reports”/“en samlad bild av anmälningarna”. In these supplied contexts, the draft preserves the things named or measured. None supplies a concrete defensible case in either direction in which the source's contextual claim would hold and the draft's claim would fail, or vice versa. No source-support finding is created by those word changes.

The explicit addition “börjar använda loggen” renders the contextual activity left implicit by “the next building starts”. The surrounding trial and expansion statements settle participation in the shared log. The draft retains its conditional future status rather than asserting that another building has begun.

The following material distinctions were also tested:

- Faster assignment versus faster repair completion: the draft consistently measures assignment, and separately states the absence of completion-time measurements.
- A difference during the software trial versus a software-caused difference: the draft reports the former and expressly preserves the note's refusal to attribute it to the software.
- A comparison missing from the package versus no comparison ever conducted: the draft claims only the former.
- Measurements missing versus benefits absent: the draft claims only the absence of supplied measurements.
- A customer's willingness to repeat its own trial versus recommendation to every housing company: the conditional, qualified assessment and the limit on recommendation both survive.
- A future decision versus an approved expansion: the draft explicitly says expansion has not occurred and gives the check preceding the decision.

These boundaries would admit concrete counterexamples if collapsed—for example, a repair assigned in two days but completed much later, an unavailable supplier comparison that was in fact conducted, or an assignment-time difference produced by workload rather than software. The draft does not collapse them, so those cases are not findings against this text.
