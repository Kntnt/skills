# The frozen objective orders a frontier nothing else can

This record narrows ADR-0083 in one clause: everything it settled about who owns exact frozen routing, and about measured selection staying multidimensional, stands, and only its closing rule — that several non-dominated points inherit unless the frozen profile prices every non-cash dimension — is outrun (issue #216).

A measured frontier with two survivors had no answer. `_resolve_measured_policy()` could take an explicit human budget, a sole Pareto survivor, or a complete set of shadow prices the profile declared — and the shipped profile declares none. So the state this collection is built to reach, a cohort where two configurations have both been measured and both clear the quality floor, resolved to `underdetermined_frontier` and inherited the main seat. Forever, and by design: nothing in the module said which of two good configurations it preferred. The maintainer's objective was never in doubt — the cheapest configuration that holds quality, and the fastest one only when asked — it simply was not written anywhere the code could read.

**The objective is frozen with the context, not decided at each request.** `override_policy` gains a required `objective`, `cost_first` or `time_first`, written by the context derivation from the run's own `runtime.objective` and covered by the snapshot identity like every other frozen fact. A night therefore selects under one objective from its first claim to its last, and a resumed invocation cannot quietly change what the rest of its work is optimised for. `cost_first` is what the shipped defaults freeze.

**Both orders are lexicographic over two measured axes, and neither invents a rate between them.** `cost_first` takes the lowest observed cash and breaks a tie on the lowest Time to Verified Pass; `time_first` reverses the two and breaks a further tie on the Rung order. There is no scalarisation, no knee-finding, and no ratio: converting a minute into a dollar is a statement about what the work is worth, and only a person can make it. Which is why a complete set of shadow prices, where a user declared one, still outranks the objective — it is exactly that statement. Where those prices cannot decide, because one is missing, because a dimension nothing measured, or because two scenarios cost the same, the objective decides rather than the run inheriting: a declaration that fails to separate the frontier is not a reason to stop selecting.

**Measured ordering reads observed means, never the price card.** The Pareto construction, the frontier audit, the shadow-price conversion, and the objective order all read `commercial` off the evidence record — what the configuration was measured to cost — rather than off `mapping.commercial`, which is what it is advertised to cost. The dominance rule itself is untouched; only its input moved. `launch.commercial` still carries the mapping's facts, because a launch instruction is about the point being launched and not about the frontier it was chosen from, and the explicit `request.economics` path still reads them too: a human budget is stated in the units of the price card the human read, and silently repricing it would change what a declared budget means.

**A subscription seat exposes no cash, and that is a fact rather than a gap.** Where no surviving point carries a cash mean at all, `cost_first` orders by the Rung ladder — `(model_capability, portable level)` ascending — whose cheaper end is the cheaper configuration by construction, and names itself `cost_first_rung_order` so the decision says which order chose. Without this a Claude Max seat, which never bills per attempt, would inherit on every frontier it ever measured. Where only *some* points carry cash, nothing is ordered: that is a half-measured ledger, and picking a winner from it would reward the point nobody costed. The same holds for an unmeasured Time to Verified Pass on any survivor. Both are `objective_metrics_missing`, a new audited inheritance reason beside `underdetermined_frontier`, which now means only what its name says — a frontier the applicable order genuinely cannot separate.

**Time to Verified Pass is the policy's own clock.** A chain is one task's routed attempts inside one run, grouped by `(run_identity, task_id)`, ordered by `attempt_index`, with the new `prior_attempt_id` validating the links where the ledger holds a whole one. It is assembled from the whole ledger rather than from inside one frontier, because Orchestrate's own escalation crosses one: a build that failed and the amend that then passed are different strata, and a chain built inside a single Cohort would never see the escalation it exists to price. It is charged inside one, though — the Cohort the passing row belongs to — so a fingerprint measured in two Cohorts still keeps them apart (ADR-0145). A chain that reached a passing verdict charges the configuration that verdict landed on twice over: the cash of every attempt up to and including the pass, and the seconds from the chain's first launch to that verdict's completion instant. A failing row still contributes its `fail` to its own point's quality — it is evidence about that point — but its cost goes to the chain that later passed and never to its own point's means, because a cheap start that needed two escalations did not save anybody anything. A chain no verdict passed contributes neither, a censored attempt being neither fast nor free, and a mean is reported only where every contributing chain carried the measurement. Both identities now ride in the ledger, so a chain can be read back out of it by a reader that saw none of the batch it was written in.

**`--fast` is a run lock, not a per-wave preference.** It sits beside `--model` and `--deliberation` in `Routing`, in `locks_refusal()`, in `described_locks()`, in the plan, in the report, and in the resume check, and an invocation that adds or drops it is refused before anything is claimed. The engine also refuses a route whose frozen context selects for the other objective: the flag makes a promise about how the night selects, the selection happens inside the snapshot, and a flag saying one thing while the routing does another for a whole night is worse than a refusal at the first request.

**The report says how long, and which silence.** Every ticket carries `time_to_verified_pass_seconds` and `time_to_verified_pass_status`, decided by engine instants alone: `verified_pass` with a number, `not_started` where the run launched no attempt for it, `incomplete` where a launched attempt has no completion, and `not_passed` where every finished attempt failed, parked, was blocked, or collided. Four cases rather than a nullable number, because an absent measurement and a zero are not the same fact and a reader must never have to guess which one a blank was.

**What this costs.** The objective now decides where a person used to be asked, and it decides on two axes out of five: a configuration that is cheapest and fastest while burning quota nobody priced will win, and the quota dimensions ride along unweighted unless somebody declares a shadow price for them. Making the evidence record the input to dominance means a ledger-derived frontier, whose three quota dimensions are `null` by construction, never triggers dominance at all — the objective decides every measured frontier the loop produces, and the sole-survivor branch survives only for hand-written records. `objective_metrics_missing` will fire on any ledger written before this change, since no row of it carries a projected `commercial`, so the first runs after this land go on inheriting until the loop has measured a chain to a pass. And the Rung fallback trusts `model_capability` as a price proxy on an unpriced seat, which is true of the ladder as shipped and would stop being true of a snapshot whose capability numbers and prices disagreed.
