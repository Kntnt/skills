# A fresh amended verdict buys one continuation amend

Orchestrate's first verifier-informed amend ends in a fresh verifier that deliberately sees neither the first verdict nor either builder's report. That independence can reveal a different blind spot or a regression introduced by the amend. Recent tickets demonstrated both shapes: one amended ticket passed every repository gate but retained two small contract discrepancies, while another retained several concrete specification and standards defects already settled by its ticket. Both were recorded failed even though the new verdict was a complete bounded brief and the later manual repair required no maintainer decision.

**A fresh failed verdict after amend one buys exactly one continuation amend.** A ticket may therefore receive at most two verifier-informed amends after its initial build. The continuation uses the same amend template and ticket allocation, receives the immediately preceding verdict verbatim, and runs in a fresh building session. Another fresh verdict session then receives the ordinary full verification brief, sees no earlier verdict, amend history, or builder report, runs the fixed gate, and checks every acceptance criterion. A pass after either amend integrates normally; a failed final verdict after amend two is terminal.

**The tracker carries the append-only phase before dispatch.** Amend markers distinguish attempt one and attempt two, and the engine projects zero, one, or two spent attempts into Plan and Report. The caller names which attempt its verdict spends, so replaying that transition after an uncertain tracker response is idempotent instead of consuming the next opportunity. The historical unnumbered marker means attempt one. An interruption before, during, or after either builder or verifier resumes the recorded attempt instead of appending a duplicate or consuming the next opportunity on the same verdict.

**The orchestrator applies the bound mechanically rather than asking a verifier to grade its own findings.** A verifier still passes only when every gate command and acceptance criterion passes. Mechanical hinders, genuine decisions, discovered dependencies, waiting subagents, and partial work retain their existing triage; concrete findings do not become a partial pass, while a real decision does not become an automatic continuation.

**The continuation amend and the collision rebuild remain independent bounds.** A ticket may spend two verifier-informed amends and one collision rebuild because they answer different changed conditions. At a concurrency ceiling of one, no later ticket is built on the run branch until the continuation has a passing final verdict or a terminal outcome. With isolated worktrees, both amends retain the ticket's working tree, scratch directory, reservations, run-owned-file rules, and notes, while unrelated completed tickets may still be accounted for.

**This decision narrows ADR-0069.** Its premise that a verifier's concrete verdict changes an amender's conditions still stands; the same premise now applies once more when the deliberately fresh amended verifier supplies new information. Its one-amend bound becomes a two-amend bound, while its independent-verifier architecture and the separately bounded collision rebuild stand unchanged.

**What this costs.** A genuine terminal verification failure may now consume three builds and three ticket verifications before it is recorded, excluding the collision rebuild. Tracker and report state gain one numbered phase, and a resumed run must distinguish a recorded attempt from a new verdict that may spend the next one. In return, a complete fresh verdict receives the same single bounded opportunity as the original verdict, without turning repair into an open-ended retry loop.
