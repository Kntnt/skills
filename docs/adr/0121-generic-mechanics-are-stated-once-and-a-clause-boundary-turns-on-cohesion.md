# Generic mechanics are stated once, and a clause boundary turns on whether the clauses cohere

The Claude-family evaluation of Proofread found four independent runs correcting every planted error in the American English fixture and leaving one comma, each naming it in the reply as a deliberate retention: the contrastive comma in *The bug were not in the parsing at all, it was in a silent retry*. That was filed as a defect in the Skill. It was not one. Garner's Modern English Usage accepts the joint where the clauses are short, closely related, and carry no risk of a miscue, and names the negative-positive contrast as an acceptable type; the Chicago Manual of Style's own Q&A calls a near-identical construction borderline and perfectly acceptable for most writing; Språkrådet's *Svenska skrivregler* holds satsradning correct so long as the second main clause explains, specifies, or modifies the first. The Skill was right, and building the ticket as filed would have made it worse.

What survived was the reason it was right by accident. No shipped mechanics scope named sentence-boundary punctuation at all, so what kept the comma was the closing preference rule each scope stated separately, applied by a model's own reading of register — and the next text would have been decided the same way, in either direction.

**The rules of objective correctness that do not depend on the language are stated once, in the Collection Library.** `references/editorial/mechanics.md` sits beside the contract a first draft is written against and the catalogue of machine-sounding prose, and it is written the way they are: outcomes rather than procedure, no Skill and no option named, English examples applied semantically in the target language. The two authorities above draw one line and differ only in how strictly they default, which is what makes the rule generic; a scope was the wrong home for it, because a Language Resource takes a rule only where the language genuinely differs or where the generic rule was observed to fail there, and a rule written three times is a rule that can disagree with itself. Where a language does default differently, its own scope says so and is the more specific instruction.

**Proofread admits that one document by name, and its exclusion is otherwise untouched.** It loads the resolved language's mechanics scope and this contract, and the two together are the run's rules; no base contract, no genre, no technique, no anti-slop catalogue, and no other scope. The Skill that reviews needs no change of its own, because it reaches the contract through the proofreading pass it already delegates. Nothing else about Proofread widens: what it corrects, what it refuses to touch, how it delivers, and its refusal to become a rewrite are what they were, and it gains one document to read rather than a licence.

**A comma joining two independent clauses is an error where the second does not explain, specify, restate, or contrast with the first, and is not an error where it does.** Stating it as a conditional is the whole point: stated flatly as an error, a mechanical pass would start correcting a construction both shipped languages accept, and stated nowhere it is decided by whoever is reading. The correction is mechanical and bounded to the joint — the comma becomes a period or a semicolon, and the sentence is not reordered, merged, split, tightened, or given a conjunction, because *and*, *but*, and *so* each assert a relation the writer did not. Where the reading is genuinely open, the comma stands: this rule reaches the boundary that misleads a reader, not the boundary that is merely informal.

**The preference rule moves with it, worded so that it cannot reach an error the contract names.** Where a language establishes more than one correct form the text's own choice stands, and only inconsistency within one text is corrected — once, for every language, instead of three times in three scopes and a fourth time in Proofread's own body. Beside it stands the limit that was missing: a construction the rules in hand name as wrong is corrected however consistently the text commits to it, because consistency is what an established variant is judged by rather than what makes something established.

**The evaluation's finding was the corpus's premise, not the Skill's behaviour.** The record's observation stands as history — the comma was left, on every run, for the stated reason — but the corpus documentation that listed it among the fixture's planted errors was wrong about the sentence, and the record's note that it was a defect was wrong with it. Both are corrected rather than left to be read as an outstanding fault, and the fixture gains a comma joining two main clauses that do not cohere, because a conditional rule that only ever meets the accepted case has not been tested in the direction that matters. That is a deliberate exception to leaving the corpus alone when an evaluation finds something: the protocol's rule exists so that a corpus is never softened to match what was built, and here the corpus asserted an error that neither language's authorities recognise.

What this costs is a second document every mechanical run loads, and one more place to look when a mechanical question has no obvious home — a rule that is nearly generic can now be put in the wrong one of two places rather than the wrong one of three languages. It also puts a semantic judgement in a mechanical Skill's hands: whether two clauses cohere is a reading, and a reading can go wrong in both directions, which is why the contract says plainly that an open case is left alone. Whether that holds is visible only in a run, which is what the corpus and its provider-native evaluations are for (ADR-0093).
