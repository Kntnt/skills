# The genre orders the document and the technique orders its sections

The report genre puts the answer at the top: its reader may act on the first paragraph and read the rest only if they doubt it. The PAC technique tests a claim and draws its conclusion where the test is finished, and a run resolving both — an ordinary pairing, and the default the arrangement this Collection replaced gave every report — was told to put a conclusion in two places. What either resource offered it was a precedence: the arc is written inside the genre, and the genre's convention stands where the two meet. That says which rule wins without saying what either rule is about. A run applying a precedence it cannot scope either buries the answer to protect the arc or flattens every section to protect the answer, and nothing in the Collection named the collision.

The arrangement this Collection replaced held the opposite principle, and held it as a rule of style rather than a rule of one genre: evidence before conclusion, applied strictly in reports, whose conclusions sat at the end of each major section and whose list of characteristic defects included a conclusion delivered before its basis. The reasoning was not aesthetic. A conclusion that arrives before its evidence asks the reader to trust the writer, because they cannot weigh what they have not yet been shown; a conclusion that arrives after it lets the reader reach it themselves. That arrangement judged the second to be the only variant that works in a text published by a party with an interest in the conclusion, which described most of what it was written to produce. This Collection inverted the document-level half of that principle when it wrote the report genre, and said nowhere that it had.

**The two rules govern different scopes.** The genre orders the document: the report's own answer to the question it was asked stands at the top, where the reader who may act on it and stop meets it. The technique orders the section: its arc's unit is a section, and the conclusion it places is that section's, standing where that section's test is finished. Both hold in one report because they place different things, so neither is a softening of the other and neither is a tie-break applied to the other.

**Evidence still precedes the conclusion wherever a conclusion is drawn.** What the inversion moved is the report's answer, not the order of argument. The reader who doubts what they were told at the top reads down into the sections, and there they meet the material before the result it produced, exactly as the older arrangement required. What they no longer have to do is read a document to the bottom to learn what it found, which is what a principle stated for every text at once had required of them.

**The inversion is deliberate, and what it trades is on the record.** It buys the thing this genre's reader actually needs: a known reader with a decision waiting, who reads a report once and returns to it for a figure, is served by a verdict they can act on and evidence they can check, and is not served by being walked to a conclusion they commissioned. What it spends is the older principle's protection at the one place a self-interested writer would most like to spend it. The genre answers that with its other requirements rather than with its order — every figure carries its basis, the strongest objection is put at full strength and then answered, what the report cannot settle is written down, and a recommendation claims no more than the material supports. Where those hold, an answer at the top can be checked by the reader who doubts it; where they fail, no ordering would have saved the report.

**Each resource states its own scope and neither states the other's.** The genre's base half says where the report's answer stands and that the rule fixes the document; the technique's base half says that what a genre fixes for the document is not its arc's conclusion moved forward, and that a document built as one arc has misread the scale. A rule written in both places would be one rule free to disagree with itself, and the review halves diagnose only the misreadings of the rules their own base halves state (ADR-0095). This record carries the reasoning neither of them holds, because a genre resource arguing with a technique resource is where a reader would have to read both to learn what one of them requires.

The road not taken was to say the precedence harder: a longer sentence in each technique declaring that the genre wins wherever the two meet. It would have left the collision unnamed and made every later one another exception to write, and it would have kept teaching that one of the two rules yields — which is the reading that produced the buried answer in the first place. A table of per-genre exceptions inside the technique was refused for the same reason it is refused everywhere here: it makes one resource the owner of rules its peers own.

**What this costs** is a scope line held by prose alone. The suite can assert that both resources ship with their review halves beside them; whether a run read the scope and applied it is visible only in a draft, which is what the corpus and its provider-native evaluations are for (ADR-0093). It also settles one pair. The shape generalises — a genre that fixes a document-level order, a technique whose arc is a section's — but the next genre with a fixed order, and the next technique added beside it, meet the same question in prose somebody has to write.
