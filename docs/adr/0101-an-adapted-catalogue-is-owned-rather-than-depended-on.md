# An adapted catalogue is owned rather than depended on

The patterns that mark machine-sounding prose are well catalogued outside this collection, and Peter Yang's `no-ai-slop` Skill is the best-known statement of them. Reusing it would mean either declaring an external Skill as a Dependency or vendoring its workflow whole. The first makes an editorial review of this collection's fail wherever that Skill is not installed, and hands a third party the power to change what a review of ours finds. The second imports a standalone Skill's two jobs, its English word lists, and roughly two thousand words of instruction into a collection whose whole reason for splitting drafting from reviewing was that rules were competing with the text for context.

**The collection owns a condensed adaptation and declares no external Skill.** The transferable part of such a catalogue is the patterns and their repairs, not the workflow around them. Ours states each pattern once, with one example and the minimum change that removes it, and it names nothing about how a Skill is invoked. No external Skill becomes a required or an optional Dependency, so what a review finds is settled by files this repository ships and can be diffed.

**Where wording or a substantial portion of an upstream catalogue is carried over, the upstream notice ships with the adaptation.** MIT terms permit local adaptation and require the notice to travel with substantial portions. The notice therefore sits in the adapted file itself rather than in a repository-level licence file a reader of an installed Skill never receives.

**The catalogue is language-independent and lives in the Collection Library.** Its examples are English because it is, and they are applied as semantic patterns in the target language rather than matched as strings; a word, a phrase, a punctuation convention, or a construction that only exists in one language belongs to that language's own anti-slop scope. More than one Skill applies this pass, so the Library is where it lives — a copy under the Skill that needed it first would make that Skill the implementation owner of its peer's rules.

The cost is that improvements upstream do not arrive on their own, and a pattern named there and not here is one this collection does not catch until somebody writes it down. In return the review has no external failure mode, no second workflow competing with the one this collection ships, and one catalogue that a second consumer can read without depending on the first.
