# How this repository records decisions and writes tickets

This record settles how the repository writes down what it has decided and what it intends to build: which document answers *what applies now* and which one answers *why it became that*, what a ticket may claim about a tree that keeps moving under it while it waits, when a ticket has to ride a wave alone, why the archive no longer annotates itself when a later decision outruns an earlier one, and what standing an external validator that rejects every Skill here is given. It is a consolidation — it supersedes the four records named below and carries forward what those records were for, which is the reasoning. It is also this reform's own record, and the place a reader who finds the archive changed shape should look to learn why. What the rules are is stated in [`docs/rules/docs.md`](../rules/docs.md) for where a rule goes and what earns a record, in [`docs/rules/tickets.md`](../rules/tickets.md) for what a ticket may assert and declare, and in [`docs/rules/skills.md`](../rules/skills.md) for the frontmatter fields the validator objects to. The archive's own ingress is [`docs/adr/README.md`](README.md), which states what a number means and what *next free* is; the commands the validator is run with, and the standing this record gives them, are in [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](../../CONTRIBUTING.md); the capitalised terms are defined in [`CONTEXT.md`](../../CONTEXT.md). This record argues rather than legislates, and where it needs a rule it cites the file that carries it rather than repeating it. A reader who wants today's law should be reading one of those and not this.

## The records folded into this one

- ADR-0066 — The reference validator is a baseline, not a gate
- ADR-0067 — A ticket asserts only what stays true until it is built
- ADR-0075 — A later record that outruns an earlier one leaves a pointer in it
- ADR-0099 — A ticket that rewrites an invariant declares that it builds alone

**Three of the four are field findings, and the fourth is a stance towards somebody else's tool.** Two of them are what an unattended run cost when a ticket said something a sibling had made false — once in five collisions over a shared number, once in a branch that contradicted itself with no file conflict anywhere in it. The third is what the archive had to do to itself to stay honest while it was being read as current law, and it is the one this reform retires rather than carries. The fourth is the smallest and has the longest reach: it is why a green run against the specification's own validator is not something this collection has, or wants. Everything below is reasoning a reader would otherwise have to reconstruct from evidence that no longer exists anywhere but in these records.

## Why the archive stopped answering what applies now

**Two incompatible functions had been living in one format, and the format could only serve one of them.** A record describes the world at its own date and answers why a rule became what it is; that is what makes it worth keeping and what makes it immutable. But the pile was consumed as current law — an agent starting work here derived what binds it by reading forward through ninety-odd records and applying supersessions by hand, some of them written in prose no scan and no reader could reliably pair. The derivation was expensive, it was different every time it was done, and it was wrong often enough that the collection kept paying for it. Nothing in the archive was at fault: each record was true as of its date, which is precisely the property that makes a pile of them a bad answer to a question about today.

**The dilution was the second half of the cost.** Every instruction loaded into a session that the session cannot act on weakens the binding force of the ones it can, so an archive read as law does not merely cost the reading — it makes the rules that matter compete with the rules that no longer apply for the same finite attention. That is why the split is a rules document *and* an archive rather than a better-indexed archive: an index would have made the derivation cheaper without making it unnecessary, and the derivation is the thing that had to go.

**So the two questions were given two documents, and only one of them is authoritative about the present.** What each is and how they relate is stated in [`docs/rules/docs.md`](../rules/docs.md), together with the bar a decision now has to clear before it earns a place here at all. That bar is the other half of the same reform: an archive that admits everything fills with how-texts wearing a why-document's clothes, and the reader who has to skip them pays the same attention as the reader of a record that had to be written.

**The immediate consequence is that a machine can now be told what binds it in one cheap read.** A plan compiled for an executor has to inline the law that governs the work; against ninety-odd records and a hand-applied supersession chain that is not something any tool can do honestly, and against a module per subject it is a file read. That is the one forward-looking claim this record makes, and it is deliberately narrow: three of the rules below are ones the new pipeline is expected to carry as machinery rather than as convention — the record-number reservation, the durable-ticket-versus-compiled-plan boundary, and the Solo Ticket's wave — and each is named where it comes up.

## The pointer duty, and why retiring it is this reform paying itself back

**The duty existed because a record that had gone stale was still the best answer anybody had** (ADR-0075). The collection paid for that twice. ADR-0055 went on claiming, in its title and its opening, that the rebuild is the only rerun the Skill performs, after ADR-0069 gave a failed verification a rerun of its own; ADR-0058 went on opening on *done* being the one outcome that takes a ticket out of the open scope, after the report's closed reader had been widened to every recorded outcome (issue #81). Both decisions were untouched and still correct — it was the framing around them that went false, silently, for as long as nobody happened to reread them (issue #86). A reader looking for how something worked found a confident wrong answer in the one body of prose this repository pointed at for architecture.

**The fix was one sanctioned edit to a body of documents whose whole discipline is not being edited, and the author of the later record owed it** (ADR-0075). One sentence beside the claim it corrects, naming the later record and what of the earlier one it takes over, written in a vocabulary the suite could read from both ends: a record declaring a takeover had to be answered by a pointer back, and every pointer had to name a record that existed and came later. Making it the later author's duty was the load-bearing part — the author of the later record is the only session that knows, at the moment the ground moves, both what moved and where it used to stand, and a convention that waits for a reader depends on somebody returning to a file they have no other reason to open, which is exactly what let the two records go stale.

**What the duty was buying is precisely what the rules document now provides, and provides better.** The pointer kept one shelf of prose true for a reader who had nowhere better to look. A module rewritten whenever its rule changes keeps that same reader right without any record being touched at all, and it keeps them right about rules whose supersession nobody wrote in the declared vocabulary — the residual ADR-0075 named and could not close. Retiring the duty therefore gives back everything that record priced: the breach of immutability, a relation vocabulary that had become load-bearing prose, and a floor of known relation pairs in the suite that grew by hand, one pair per pointer, because a scan that has drifted from the prose passes by finding nothing.

**Retiring it also removes the archive's obligation to be self-consistent about a question nobody asks it.** An archive of records that describe many different dates has no coherent answer to *what is true today*, and the pointer discipline was an attempt to manufacture one inside a format that cannot hold it. Nothing now reads the archive for that, so nothing has to be maintained for it. What a reader owes in exchange — read a record as of its own date, and read nothing into the presence or absence of a pointer sentence — is stated in [`docs/rules/docs.md`](../rules/docs.md) and in the archive's ingress, and pointer sentences written while the duty stood stay exactly where they are, because a record is not rewritten to agree with a later arrangement any more than it is rewritten to agree with a later decision.

## What a ticket may assert, and the eleven instances behind it

**One `/orchestrate` run hit eleven instances of a single failure: a ticket asserted something true when it was written and false when an agent built it** (ADR-0067, issues #45–#55 and #61–#62). Every instance was caught — by a builder stopping to flag it, by a verifier, or by hand mid-run — and none reached a release. Each cost real work anyway, and one class of it recurred five times. The tickets were not careless; the gap is a property of the workflow. Filing and building are separated by hours or days, sibling tickets land in between, and an unattended run builds whatever the ticket said with nobody in the loop to notice that the ground moved.

**The five collisions came from three tickets, over the one shared resource nobody had reserved.**

| Ticket | Filed | Named | Landed as | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| #47 | 2026-08-19 09:32 UTC | ADR-0051, three times | 0059 | `44477bd` landed ADR-0051 after the ticket was filed |
| #51 | 2026-08-19 11:00 UTC | ADR-0051 | 0060 | the same commit |
| #54 | 2026-08-19 12:36 UTC | ADR-0051, then 0052, then 0061, then 0062 | 0063 | 0052 taken by `2d6e7d2`; 0061 and 0062 taken by the builds of #52 and #53 |

**#54 is the instructive one, because it was corrected three times and none of the corrections stuck** (ADR-0067). Triage had already spotted its first collision and reassigned the ticket to a number that was also taken, and it was corrected twice more mid-run as sibling tickets consumed the next two numbers in turn. Every correction named a number, and the wave kept consuming numbers. A ticket that names one is racing every other ticket in its wave, and losing that race is invisible until integration — which is why the rule the finding produced is to name none at all and let the commit say which number was taken.

**The other two classes cost less and cost it in the good case.** Three tickets cited line numbers a sibling's build had moved: #48 gave three line numbers in the Manager for two functions, #52 gave two ranges whose functions its builder found some fifty lines lower because #51 had landed in between, and #45 cited a line in the test suite (ADR-0067). Nothing broke — every agent found the symbol anyway — but each one had to notice the discrepancy, decide whether it mattered, and say so, and the bad case is an agent that trusts the line and edits whatever happens to be there. Three more tickets enumerated the collection and omitted `orchestrate`, which had shipped in 0.9.0 shortly before they were filed: #50 named five Skills, #53 tabulated six while its own reasoning said all seven, and #54 stated flatly that `orchestrate` had no flat sidecars when it had five. Three builders independently widened the work to the Skill the ticket had missed and three verifiers independently upheld them, which is luck plus good agents rather than a property of the tickets.

**The fourth rule is the cheapest and it repairs the other three when they fail** (ADR-0067). One line naming the commit the ticket was written against lets a builder tell drift from error instead of guessing which of the two they are looking at, and how much else in the ticket to distrust. It is the rule that costs an author a single hash and returns the ability to read everything else in the body as of a known moment.

**Nothing enforces any of this, and that is the decision rather than an omission** (ADR-0067). Only the first rule is mechanically checkable, and the check would mean the suite reaching the tracker over the network — which nothing else here does — for one rule out of four. What the suite refuses instead is the harm that reached the tree: a number two records claim, and a citation no record answers. What stays uncaught is exactly what the eleven instances were, and it is stated plainly rather than hidden: a ticket that went stale between being filed and being built, found by whoever happened to read it against the tree, hours later and at the cost of a correction or a rebuild.

**The rules bind the durable layer and not the compiled one, and the reform is what made that distinction real.** A ticket waits; a plan is produced against the tip the work starts from and consumed by the run that produced it, so nothing can land between its reading of the tree and its writing. The drift these rules exist against needs time a plan never has, which is why exact paths, line numbers and code excerpts — the very things a ticket may not hold — are what let an executor work from a plan alone. Where the boundary is drawn is stated in [`docs/rules/tickets.md`](../rules/tickets.md); what it means for this record is that the first rule stops depending on an author's discipline once numbers are reserved by the new pipeline that compiles the plans, and the other three go on being convention.

**Two things ADR-0067 said about itself are worth keeping.** It was written against `9fc3af0`, and it and the record that became ADR-0066 were both told to take the next free number; both would have taken the same one had they been built in the same wave, and they were built in different waves only because triage had made one a blocker of the other — the fix that a ticket naming no number would not have needed. And no closed ticket was rewritten to comply. The drift recorded in those bodies is the evidence these rules rest on; retrofitting them would have left the rules reading as taste, with nothing left to show they were findings.

## The wave that contradicted itself

**An overnight run stopped on a branch that contradicted itself, and no file conflict was behind it** (ADR-0099, issues #99–#103 and #113). One ticket in the wave rewrote a repository-wide invariant — how a valued flag attaches its value (ADR-0096), a spelling every shipped file is under — while two siblings in the same wave added brand-new Skills, written in the form that ticket was outlawing. Each branch was correct alone. Only the merge could show the contradiction, and by then the new instances existed.

**No blocking edge could have prevented it, which is the whole reason a separate declaration was needed.** An edge names a ticket, and the instances a concurrent sibling would add did not exist when the tickets were written. Both the invariant ticket and one sibling saw the risk and wrote it in prose — *not to be built concurrently* — which bound the one sibling they could name and nothing else. What an author of such a ticket reliably knows is not which tickets conflict with theirs but that their rule reaches everything, and the declaration asks for exactly that.

**The road not taken was a tracker relation of the shape *exclusive over surface X***, the ticket naming the surface it rewrites and the planner keeping two tickets over one surface apart (ADR-0099). It was rejected twice over. A surface taxonomy asks the author to enumerate what does not exist yet — the new instances a concurrent sibling will add being precisely what cannot be named in advance, which is the same wall the blocking edge hit. And a registry of surfaces is a hand-maintained list of exactly the kind this repository had just watched decay, and a list nobody updates is a planner confidently reporting that two tickets are independent.

**Serialising is priced from the losing side, and the error is deliberately asymmetric.** One wave of parallelism — minutes in an unattended night — against a branch-level contradiction every time the class recurs. Making the marker a declaration rather than an inference from the ticket's text follows from the same asymmetry: an inference wrong in the safe direction serialises a wave for nothing, and wrong in the other direction is the night that was lost. Nothing verifies that a Solo Ticket's subject really is repository-wide, so an over-cautious author buys silence with the run's parallelism, and that is accepted. The expensive direction is left to whatever reads the merged branch for coherence, at the cost of one correction round; the declaration keeps the common case off that branch and the check catches what the declaration missed, and neither has to be perfect for the pair to hold. What the rule is, and how the line is written, is stated in [`docs/rules/tickets.md`](../rules/tickets.md); the term itself is defined in [`CONTEXT.md`](../../CONTEXT.md). It is the third of the rules the new pipeline is expected to carry as machinery, the wave being something a scheduler computes rather than something an author remembers.

## A validator that is a baseline rather than a gate

**`skills-ref validate`, the reference implementation of the Agent Skills specification, rejects every Skill this collection ships, and has for as long as anyone has looked** (ADR-0066, issue #63). The complaints are `argument-hint` and `disable-model-invocation`, against a closed allowlist of the six fields the specification tabulates. Both fields stay, the rejection is accepted as a knowing deviation, and the tool is read as a baseline that must not regress rather than a gate that must pass.

**The specification states no rule these fields break.** It tabulates its six fields and then says nothing whatever about an unknown top-level one — no *MUST NOT*, no instruction that a client reject it, not even that it is ignored. The closed allowlist is the reference implementation's own strictness, stricter than the prose it implements, so what the two fields deviate from is a tool's inference and not the specification's text. That distinction is the whole of why the deviation is defensible rather than merely convenient: silence is not permission, but it is not prohibition either, and refusing the fields would mean choosing a validator's inference over a Harness's documented behaviour.

**The specification's own escape hatch cannot carry them** (ADR-0066). `metadata` exists so that clients can store additional properties the specification does not define, and this collection already uses it correctly. But Claude Code reads `argument-hint` and `disable-model-invocation` at the top level only, so relocating them does not preserve the behaviour behind a conformant spelling — it discards the behaviour silently and leaves a validator-green Skill that no longer does what it did. A hatch the reader cannot see through is not a hatch.

**What the fields buy is worth more than a green run.** `argument-hint` is what puts a Skill's grammar in front of the user at the moment they type the slash command, and every Skill here refuses on a grammar — a hint withdrawn does not simplify the Skill, it hides what the Skill will reject. `disable-model-invocation` is the difference between a verb the user asks for and a verb a session may decide to run on its own, and neither field has a specification-blessed equivalent. The choice is the field or the behaviour, and it is the behaviour these Skills are for.

**The confusion this record exists to end is between *no new complaint* and *no complaint*.** A criterion asking that the baseline be unchanged is one a change can meet; a criterion asking that the validator pass is unsatisfiable for as long as the two fields stay, which is why the standing the tool is given is written where a contributor meets the command, in [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](../../CONTRIBUTING.md). The two are easy to confuse, and were: the criteria of issues #52, #53 and #54 asked for *no new complaint* and were verified honestly, but a reader of those closed tickets would conclude the collection validates, because nothing said which baseline the complaint count was counted against.

**The deviation is bounded by a test rather than by intent, and that is what makes it a stance and not an exception for nine files.** A Skill added later may carry the same two fields on the same reasoning, and a third one cannot arrive quietly: the field set is held by the suite rather than by an author's intent, so widening it is a deliberate change read against the record that accepted the deviation — which is now this one. What the set is remains [`docs/rules/skills.md`](../rules/skills.md)'s to state. So the collection's distance from the reference implementation is a number this repository knows rather than one somebody rediscovers by running the tool.

**The baseline has already moved once, by a change that carried no record of its own.** ADR-0066 was written when eight of the nine Skills carried `disable-model-invocation` and `agents-md` did not; every Skill now writes it explicitly, `false` included, so that the Codex sidecar has something to agree with. The baseline is therefore both fields on every Skill, and the exception stated in the original record is history rather than description — which is exactly the kind of drift the archive is no longer asked to keep track of, and exactly why the count that matters is the one a reader re-derives by running the two commands `CONTRIBUTING.md` gives.

## What this costs

**A reform that moves the authority out of the archive bets everything on the archive's replacement being maintained.** While the pile was law, a stale module could be corrected by reading forward; now a module that goes stale is simply wrong, with nothing behind it to reconstruct the answer from, because reconstructing the answer is the work this arrangement exists to abolish. The duty that keeps the bet good is on the author of every change that falsifies a rule, and it is a duty no scan can conjure — the same shape as the pointer duty being retired here, moved to a document where one edit fixes it for every reader instead of one edit per record.

**The archive loses its ability to warn a reader from the inside.** A record whose premise has moved now says so nowhere in its own file, and a reader who opens it out of context and reads it as current gets the confident wrong answer ADR-0075 was written against. What protects them is a convention — read a record as of its own date — and the ingress that states it, rather than a sentence placed where they are already looking.

**All four ticket rules stay unenforced, and three of them are not checkable at all.** The residual ADR-0067 priced is carried forward unchanged rather than reduced, and the reform does not touch it: what catches a ticket that went stale is still somebody reading it. The Solo Ticket declaration is uncheckable in both directions for the same kind of reason — a ticket that means it and spells its line differently schedules like any other, and one declared out of caution costs a wave of parallelism nobody gets back.

**And the validator baseline is a fact about somebody else's tool that this repository has to re-derive by hand.** The reference implementation may loosen its allowlist, tighten it, or change what it says about a field, and nothing here will notice; what the suite holds is this collection's own field set, which is the half of the comparison it owns. The other half is a comparison a contributor runs, reads against this record, and judges — which is why it is deliberately absent from the four checks and from CI, a check red on every run being no gate at all.
