# `/tldr` addresses its mode through a command path and carries no free-text operand

`/tldr` was the only Skill in this collection whose formal grammar accepted free text. Anything after the Skill name that was not `--`-prefixed was an instruction in any language, obeyed as written, and that is why its standing mode was reached by `--on`, `--off`, and `--status` where every other Skill with a closed vocabulary uses the bare words. The reasoning was sound when it was written: `on` as a bare word would shadow a legitimate instruction, and `/tldr on the security part` has no reading that is both strict and useful. What has moved is the premise underneath it.

**The collection now has a channel for exactly this, and `/tldr` is the case its own record named.** ADR-0078 gave every Skill a reserved separator and a Contextual Instruction behind it, and its considered options name trailing prose as ambiguous precisely for a Skill whose formal grammar already accepts free text. `/tldr -- only the security part` is a legal shape today, before any of this work, and it does everything the operand did through the channel every other Skill uses for it. The operand is therefore a second unseparated channel for one thing, and it is the second channel that makes the first one ambiguous.

**So the operand goes, and the mode is addressed through a command path.** The Skill accepts four forms: a bare invocation, which is unchanged in meaning and treats the invocation as feedback that the preceding answer missed the useful level, focus, or density; `on` and `off`, each taking the scope flag and the confirmation flag; and `status`, taking neither. Every form still admits the optional separator and its Contextual Instruction. With no free text in the grammar there is no prose for a bare word to shadow, and the ambiguity that forced the flags is gone with the thing that caused it.

**The flag spellings go rather than becoming aliases.** Two spellings for one form is the ambiguity this record exists to remove, and keeping the old ones would leave the Skill carrying it in a smaller place. This collection has one user, so the migration lands in one change rather than in a compatibility period. `--user` and `--yes` remain flags, keeping their spellings and their meanings, and sit where ADR-0097 puts them: after the command path.

**Each command path becomes addressable.** Under ADR-0077 `on`, `off`, and `status` each get a page under the Skill's `help/` directory with a direct `--help` route, and the root page gains the `COMMANDS` section a command with subcommands carries. That is not bookkeeping: it is what lets an invalid form be refused with the `## SYNOPSIS` of the most specific recognized page, which the Skill's single flat page could not address. `/tldr on --nonsense` is now answered with the grammar of `on` rather than with the whole Skill's.

**What the operand did is not lost, and the Skill body says where it went.** Widening the range, naming a language, narrowing the subject, and constraining the output are each a choice the Skill's contract leaves open, which is what a Contextual Instruction is permitted to settle. The step that settles the range and the step that writes the replacement answer read that instruction rather than a free-form tail, and an instruction that would widen the Skill's responsibility takes the context refusal ADR-0078 already defines rather than a syntax refusal.

This withdraws from ADR-0048 the closing argument that the mode must be reached by flags rather than by bare words; that record's portable standing-instruction mechanism, its two scopes, the absence of a project scope, and its persistence decisions are untouched. It amends ADR-0080, whose closing paragraph states that it leaves ADR-0048's grammar intact — a sentence that stops being true here, while everything ADR-0080 decided about perspective and content selection stands.

**What this costs.** A user who typed `/tldr bara säkerhetsdelen` now types `/tldr -- bara säkerhetsdelen`, and the three characters are not free: the shortest invocation of the Skill's most-used form grows, and a user who forgets the reserved separator meets a syntax refusal where they previously got what they asked for. That is the price of the grammar being closed, and it is paid once, in muscle memory, against a channel that reads the same in every Skill of the collection. What is bought is that the Skill's grammar can be stated exhaustively, that `on` means what it means everywhere else here, and that a refusal can quote the page belonging to the form it refused.
