# What an editorial Skill is held to

This record settles the editorial contract in one place: where a language's guidance lives and how a Skill selects it, what a first draft is answerable to and how a review extends that without adding to it, how three editorial Skills compose without any of them owning another, where a result goes and when the file that supplied it may be replaced, whose catalogue the anti-slop pass is and what carrying it obliges, and how any of this is checked at the one seam that has a model on the other side of it. It is a consolidation — it supersedes the six records named below, and carries forward what those records were for, which is the reasoning. What the rules are is stated in [`docs/rules/skills.md`](../rules/skills.md) for the shape of the resources and the boundary between peer Skills, and in the contracts themselves, which are Collection Library references: [`editorial/README.md`](../../skills/kntnt/library/references/editorial/README.md) is the format page for the base contract, the genres, the techniques and the anti-slop catalogue, [`languages/README.md`](../../skills/kntnt/library/references/languages/README.md) is the format page for a Language Resource, and [`delivery.md`](../../skills/kntnt/library/references/delivery.md) is the complete delivery contract. How a run of the fixture corpus is judged is [`docs/evaluation/protocol.md`](../evaluation/protocol.md). The capitalised terms are defined in [`CONTEXT.md`](../../CONTEXT.md). This record argues rather than legislates, and where it needs a rule it cites the file that carries it rather than repeating it. A reader who wants today's law should be reading one of those and not this.

## The records folded into this one

- ADR-0087 — A Language Resource co-locates selectors and scoped guidance
- ADR-0088 — Editorial Skills compose without requiring Write provenance
- ADR-0091 — Delivery is the response by default, and a source file is replaced only on request
- ADR-0093 — One corpus, evaluated separately inside each provider family
- ADR-0095 — A first draft's requirements are stated once, and review guidance extends them
- ADR-0101 — An adapted catalogue is owned rather than depended on

**All six carry an argument, and four of them are the same argument applied to four different materials.** A language, a draft's requirements, a genre and a catalogue of surface patterns are each guidance more than one Skill needs and no Skill owns, and each of the four records answers the same question about its own material: where does it live so that one consumer does not become the implementation owner of another's rules, and how little of it can a Skill load and still be bound by all of it. The other two answer what happens at the two ends — where the result goes, and how anybody finds out whether the contract held. Everything below is reasoning a reader would otherwise have to reconstruct from evidence that no longer exists anywhere but in these records.

## One file per language, and the scopes named for the guidance

**Three Skills need the same language identity and different language-specific guidance, and both ways of arranging that are worse than the file** (ADR-0087). Splitting the aliases from the scopes, or letting each consumer own the part it reads, makes every new locale a coordinated change across a registry, an alias table and three Skills — the kind of change that is done wrongly once and then imitated. Loading one monolithic document instead spends the model's context on rules the active Skill cannot act on, which is the cost this whole wave exists to remove.

**Naming a scope for its guidance rather than for the Skill that reads it is what keeps it shared** (ADR-0087). Composition, review, anti-slop and mechanics are four kinds of guidance; a scope called after the Skill that reached for it first reads as that Skill's property, and the second consumer either copies it or asks permission for it. The frontmatter carries the identity and the body carries the scopes, so the resolver can inventory what is installed without opening a body, and a caller asking for one scope pays for one scope.

**Selection is semantic first and deterministic second, and the order is the point** (ADR-0087). A person names a language in whatever words they have, so an unlisted description is interpreted by the agent and only then verified against what is actually installed. That is why the alias list is curated and short rather than exhaustive: a multilingual registry would be trying to do, by enumeration, the one part of the job the model is better at, and it would go stale in every language nobody thought of.

**Generic guidance stays outside a Language Resource, and a language-specific rule has to earn its place** (ADR-0087). The shared rules and their English examples are language-independent and applied semantically in the target language; a Language Resource gains a rule only where the language genuinely differs, or where observed behaviour has shown the generic rule insufficient there. Without that bar every resource would slowly become a translation of the shared contract, which is the monolith again with one copy per language.

**What it costs is a schema and a resolver somebody maintains** (ADR-0087). In exchange, locale administration is one directory, adding a language is writing one file, and nothing loads a locale, an alias or an inactive scope it will not use.

## One statement of what a draft has to meet

**A Skill that writes and a Skill that reviews have to agree about what a good draft is, and the two obvious ways to arrange that both fail** (ADR-0095). Written into each Skill, the agreement is two documents free to drift until a requirement and its review disagree about what was required — and the disagreement surfaces as a review finding nobody can act on. Written as one document holding everything either Skill could want, it makes a Skill that only writes pay context for diagnostics it cannot use.

**So the base contract holds every normative outcome, once, and the diagnostics sit in a separate file beside it** (ADR-0095). A section inside one file would have been the smaller change and would not have been separable, and separability is the whole point: a Skill that only writes loads the base half and stops. What the extension may hold, and the two things it may not — an independent target, and a restatement of a base rule in other words — are stated on the format page, [`editorial/README.md`](../../skills/kntnt/library/references/editorial/README.md). Both prohibitions come from the same place as the split itself. Anything a draft has to meet is a requirement, and a requirement the writing Skill never sees is one it cannot meet; and a rule written twice can disagree with itself about what was required, which is the failure the single statement was adopted to end.

**The contract names no Skill and no option, and that is what lets a Skill be added under it later** (ADR-0095, ADR-0091). What differs between drafting and reviewing is what a Skill does about a failure, never what counts as one. A contract mentioning a flag would bind a grammar the consuming Skill owns, and the next Skill would inherit a spelling it never chose.

**The register baseline is stated together with the fact that it is overridden, because stated alone it is wrong most of the time** (ADR-0095). Idiomatic contemporary prose with the restraint, concreteness, factual precision and cadence of a well-edited general-interest newspaper or quality magazine is a good default and a bad law: genre, audience and purpose override it, and a baseline given without that clause turns a letter into a news article.

**A genre or a technique is one file, and the directory is the only list** (ADR-0095). Nothing enumerates the installed set anywhere else, so adding one is writing one file rather than a coordinated edit across a registry and its consumers, and a value with no file is refused rather than resolved to a default — a fallback here would deliver a contract the caller did not ask for and could not see. The asymmetry between the two kinds is deliberate: an unspecified content type deserves a contract rather than the absence of one, which is why the default genre ships as a complete contract, while a technique applies because somebody selected it and nothing gives a Skill grounds to infer one from a draft's shape.

**Fixing the filename as the canonical value is a real cost and was taken knowingly** (ADR-0095). The filename without its extension is what a Skill resolves and what Handoff Metadata records, whatever the user typed, so renaming a genre renames the value and the Handoff Metadata written before the rename stops resolving. The alternative was a registry mapping names to files, which is the coordinated edit the directory-as-list arrangement exists to avoid.

**Most of this has no executable check behind it** (ADR-0095). The suite can hold the placement, the silence about flag spellings, the separability of the review half and which resources a Skill reaches for. Whether a draft actually meets the contract is visible only in the draft, which is what the corpus and its provider-native evaluations are for.

## Three Skills, and deliberately not three stages

**Handoff Metadata makes a later review more precise, and requiring it would have made two Skills into stages** (ADR-0088). A Skill that writes can attach the resolved genre, technique and language; a Skill that reviews is better off knowing them. But a review that demanded that metadata, or the original source material, would be usable only on texts this collection had itself produced — and the ordinary case is a text somebody else wrote. So the metadata is an optimisation and never provenance: formal values override it, and context or the Text Artifact supplies what is missing.

**Composition happens through public Skill interfaces and stops there** (ADR-0088). A reviewing Skill may depend on a peer Skill and follow its `SKILL.md`; it reads none of that peer's private references and runs none of its scripts. The general form of that boundary, and the price the collection pays for it, is the **Peer internals are not an interface** rule in [`docs/rules/skills.md`](../rules/skills.md). Here it has a specific consequence worth naming: the mechanical pass is a Skill invocation rather than a shared subroutine, which is why the reviewing Skill can be told exactly what to forward and what not to.

**Source Fidelity belongs to the Skill that writes, and to no other** (ADR-0088, ADR-0095). Representing the supplied material truthfully — facts, attribution, uncertainty, scope, chronology, causality, the meaning of an edited quotation — is a promise only a Skill holding that material can make. A reviewing Skill judges the Text Artifact against the editorial contract without comparing it with source material and without reporting that source verification was unavailable, because a caveat repeated on every review is a caveat nobody reads. That is also why fidelity stays out of the shared base contract: a shared document asserting it would ask a reviewing Skill for a check it cannot make, and then invite exactly that caveat.

**The cost is a real gap and it is not covered elsewhere** (ADR-0088). A source-faithfulness failure — by the writing Skill or by a human author — is not something the review will catch. What is bought is that each Skill stays independently usable on a text with no history, which is the case the collection actually meets.

## Where a result goes

**Every Skill producing a Text Artifact meets the same three questions, and answering them per Skill gets answers that differ in ways nobody chose** (ADR-0091). Where does the result go, may the file that supplied the text be replaced by it, and what happens when the run finds nothing to change. A user who has learnt one Skill's habits then guesses wrong about the next, and neither Skill is wrong on its own.

**The answer is a document with several consumers, not a runtime** (ADR-0091). A shared output engine would buy an executable check by adding a component to a decision whose whole difficulty is deciding: the effect is a single write, and what varies is everything before it. So the contract states behaviour and never a flag spelling, each Skill exercises it through its own seam, and the options exposing it stay each Skill's own — which is the same reason the base contract names no option.

**Delivery is non-mutating until the caller says otherwise, and naming a path is the whole of the authorization** (ADR-0091). The default Output Target is the response, and supplying a local file as source material selects no destination. An explicit path naming an existing file overwrites that exact file with no second confirmation: demanding a force flag on top of a named destination would teach that naming a destination means less than it says, which is a worse lesson than the overwrite is a risk.

**A directory is a place rather than a file, so nothing derived is ever written over** (ADR-0091). Naming a directory selects where the result goes and not what it replaces, and the run derives a filename from what it has — the source basename, a referenced title, the Skill's own working title. What it does at a collision is the part worth arguing: repeated delivery grows one numbered sequence from one stem, because a directory where each collision forked a new stem is one whose history nobody can read.

**Replacing a source has exactly one name, and an equal path is refused rather than honoured** (ADR-0091). In-place Editing belongs to the Skills that review an existing Text Artifact; a Skill that creates one never edits the material its brief and sources came from, there being nothing it was asked to improve. The conditions on it all follow from there being exactly one thing to replace, which is why inline text, a URL and a read-only or uploaded source have no in-place form at all. An explicit output path equal to the input path expresses the same effect by coincidence and is refused in favour of the named form, so that a reader of an invocation can see that a source is about to be replaced without comparing two paths to find out.

**A request the contract cannot honour is refused before anything is written** (ADR-0091). Contradictory, meaningless and unusable output requests are all decidable from the invocation alone, so the moment to refuse is the one where nothing has happened yet and there is no partial effect to describe. Deciding any later would leave a report having to account for half a delivery, which costs the user more than the refusal it replaced and is the harder thing for the Skill to get right.

**An unchanged result is reported rather than re-delivered, except where delivering it was the request** (ADR-0091). A response-targeted or in-place run that changed nothing writes nothing and returns a short status in the Text Artifact's own language: repeating the text spends output on what the caller already holds, and rewriting the file makes its timestamp claim work that did not happen. An explicitly selected different file or directory still receives the complete artifact, because creating that artifact there was the request and not a side effect of changing it.

**What this costs is a contract the suite can only partly see** (ADR-0091). Placement, the silence about flag spellings and the presence of the rules a consumer depends on are checkable; whether a Skill obeys the contract is visible only in that Skill's behaviour, which is the corpus's job again.

## The catalogue is ours, and the licence travels inside the file

**The patterns that mark machine-sounding prose are well catalogued outside this collection — Peter Yang's `no-ai-slop` Skill is the best-known statement of them — and both ways of reusing one directly were worse than adapting it** (ADR-0101). Declaring an external Skill as a Dependency would make an editorial review of ours fail wherever that Skill is not installed, and would hand a third party the power to change what our review finds. Vendoring one whole would import a standalone Skill's two jobs, its English word lists and roughly two thousand words of instruction into a collection whose whole reason for splitting drafting from reviewing was that rules were competing with the text for context.

**What transfers is the patterns and their repairs, not the workflow around them** (ADR-0101). The adaptation states each pattern once, with one example and the minimum change that removes it, and says nothing about how a Skill is invoked. No external Skill is a required or an optional Dependency, so what a review finds is settled by files this repository ships and can be diffed.

**Carrying somebody else's wording obliges the notice, and the notice has to reach the reader who receives the file** (ADR-0101). MIT terms permit local adaptation and require the notice to travel with substantial portions. A repository-level licence file does not travel: a reader of an installed Skill receives the Skill's files and never this repository, so the notice sits inside the adapted file itself. That is the consequence the licence actually has here, and it is why the file carries a paragraph that looks out of place in a guidance document.

**The catalogue is language-independent, and it lives in the Library because more than one Skill applies that pass** (ADR-0101). Its examples are English because it is, and they are applied as semantic patterns in the target language rather than matched as strings; a word, a phrase, a punctuation convention or a construction that exists in one language only belongs to that language's own anti-slop scope. A copy under the Skill that needed it first would have made that Skill the implementation owner of its peer's rules — the same failure the base contract and the Language Resources are arranged against.

**The cost is that upstream improvements do not arrive on their own** (ADR-0101). A pattern named there and not here is one this collection does not catch until somebody writes it down. In return the review has no external failure mode, no second workflow competing with the one this collection ships, and one catalogue a second consumer can read without depending on the first.

## The only check that can see the promise

**These Skills promise things no unit test can look at** (ADR-0093). That a draft invents nothing the source did not carry, that a mechanical pass does not quietly improve a sentence, that a directory-targeted result steps past an occupant rather than over it. The seam where those promises are kept or broken is the Skill's real output in an actual supported Harness, and that seam has a model on the other side of it. Two provider families will run the same Skills, and the point of running both is to find out where they differ.

**The corpus is built from the specification rather than from an implementation, which is the only way it tests the contract** (ADR-0093). It could therefore exist before the Skills did. The same property makes it usable as a check on a later change: material chosen from the specification does not quietly acquire the shape of the code that grew beside it. Where a run exposes a real defect the defect becomes its own ticket against the Skill, and neither the corpus nor the criteria absorb it — a criterion softened to accommodate an implementation has stopped testing anything.

**It is fixture material and not a test suite, and the distinction is what lets the suite stay cheap** (ADR-0093). Nothing in it asserts an exact sentence and nothing claims a model writes perfectly or finds every error. Each fixture says what the material is, how it reaches a Skill, and what a correct run must never do with it, so an evaluator uses a fixture without first reading the Skill that consumes it. What runs under `tests/` therefore checks the corpus rather than the models — coverage of every kind the specification names, documentation in both directions, the protocol's rejections and record fields — and runs no model, which is exactly why it can run on every commit.

**A criterion is semantic, blinded and floored, and the floor is the part that does the work** (ADR-0093). It is answerable from the delivered artifact and the recorded side effects alone, against criteria fixed before the run, without knowing which model produced the output and without a reference text to compare against; several valid texts pass, and a criterion separating two correct drafts on wording is written wrongly. Five things fail regardless of how well the output reads — an unsupported fact, wrong locale behaviour, a substantive edit from a contract limited to mechanics, an unresolved mandatory finding, an incorrect side effect — and no criterion may be phrased so that one of them passes. Without that floor a semantic criterion decays into approval of anything fluent, which is the failure mode a model judging prose has by default.

**The families are isolated in both directions, and it is a rule about sessions rather than about tooling** (ADR-0093). A session of one family runs only that family's evaluations and never starts, controls or invokes the other's Harness or model. Neither half is a statement about which provider is trusted; both follow from wanting a comparison no cross-provider agent control could have contaminated, and from the plain operational fact that a session driving another vendor's harness is a session nobody can account for afterwards.

**Because the families cannot meet, the record is the only place they do — so the comparison is a read** (ADR-0093). The recording format is fixed in the protocol and materialised as a template: which model in which Harness against which corpus commit, and per fixture what was typed, what came back, what the filesystem shows and how each criterion was judged. Side effects are read from disk rather than from the Skill's account of itself, because a Skill reporting its own writes is the one witness the output contract exists to doubt. A re-run is a new record rather than an update: what a configuration did on a given day is history.

**What this costs is deliberate and is not small** (ADR-0093). Two runs are needed for every comparison and the second may happen weeks after the first, on material that must not have moved in between — hence the corpus commit in every record, and the immutable reference rather than a live page. Evaluations are hand-run and unautomatable by design, so the corpus grows only when somebody adds to it. And a corpus written from the specification contains fixtures no Skill turns out to need, which is a gap worth keeping: a fixture nothing consumes is a question about the contract rather than dead weight.
