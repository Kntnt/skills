# A correction is delegated fresh and verified by review

Redline's review half returns findings. Repairing them in the reviewing context is the obvious next step and the wrong one: the session that wrote the findings also wrote the reasoning behind them, the passages it weighed and dismissed, and its own account of what the text is trying to do. A repair made there is made by the reader least able to see the text as it now stands, and a second look from the same context confirms what the first look already believed. Both halves of the loop are shaped by that.

**Each correction is attempted by a subagent started fresh for it.** Fresh means it carries no earlier findings, no earlier attempt, and no part of the reviewing session's reading — only the complete current Text Artifact, the complete findings of the most recent review, the genre, technique and language the run resolved, and the requirement to leave alone whatever the findings do not concern. Each of those arrives whole rather than summarised, because a summary is the dispatching session's reading of the text and that reading is exactly what a fresh subagent exists to be without. What reaches it is a filled-in brief and nothing else, which is also what makes the round reproducible: the same brief is the same input, whatever the session that produced it had been thinking.

**A correction is verified by review, never accepted on the report of whoever made it.** The returned text is reviewed again from the top, against the same documents the first review was read against, and it is that review's findings — not the correcting subagent's account of what it fixed — that decide whether the loop continues. An agent reporting on its own work is the one reader who cannot check it, and a loop that trusted the report would terminate on confidence rather than on the text.

**The budget is a ceiling, and the loop has three exits.** It stops when no findings remain, with the rest of the budget unspent, so a text that is already clean is not rewritten to use up a number. It stops when a correction makes no relevant progress — the findings it was given still stand and nothing they named has changed — because the next round would be the same round, and saying what remains is worth more than repeating it. And it stops when the budget is spent, delivering the text with the findings that are left for a person to finish. The default is one correction, so an ordinary review buys one repair and one chance to verify it; zero is a review that reports rather than corrects, and every non-negative integer above that is a bound the caller states. In every case the closing mechanical pass runs exactly once after the loop, whichever exit was taken, and nothing substantive follows it.

**Subagents stay a hard Capability whatever budget is in force.** A declaration that changed with the invocation would be an availability contract a caller could only discover by running, and the default invocation delegates. This is ADR-0062's rule applied rather than an exception to it.

The costs are real. A fresh subagent per correction pays for the isolation twice over — it reads the contract documents again each round, and it cannot learn from the attempt before it, so two rounds may repair the same finding two different ways. Re-reviewing after every correction means the review runs one more time than there were corrections. And the no-progress stop is a judgement rather than a comparison: a round that changed prose without resolving a finding is progress by one reading and none by another, so a stop is occasionally taken one round early. In return, no round is biased by the round before it, no correction is believed because it said so, and the number a caller writes on `--max` bounds the work rather than describing it.
