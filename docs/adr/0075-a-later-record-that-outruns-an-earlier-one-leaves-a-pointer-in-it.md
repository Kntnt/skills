# A later record that outruns an earlier one leaves a pointer in it

A decision record describes the world at its own date and is never rewritten, which is what makes this directory worth reading at all. That discipline has a failure mode the collection has now paid for twice: a later record narrows or replaces an earlier record's premise, the earlier record goes on asserting it, and a reader looking for how something works finds a confident wrong answer in the one place the repository points at for architecture. ADR-0055 kept claiming in its title and opening that the rebuild is the only rerun after ADR-0069 gave a failed verification a rerun of its own, and ADR-0058 kept opening on done being the one outcome that leaves the open scope after the report's closed reader had been widened to every recorded outcome (issue #81). The decisions both records carry are untouched and still correct; it is the framing around them that went stale, silently, for as long as nobody happened to reread them (issue #86). One pointer existed before this record — the closing sentence of ADR-0017, written by hand — and nothing asked the author of the next later record to do the same.

**A later record that narrows or replaces what an earlier record asserts leaves a pointer in the earlier record: one sentence, beside the claim it corrects, naming the later record and saying what of the earlier one it narrows.** The earlier record's body is otherwise left exactly as it stands, because the record's value is that it describes the world at its own date — the pointer does not soften the old claim or rewrite it, it tells the reader where the world moved. The sentence carries the relation in a form the suite can read: the relation word beside the citation, as in *narrowed by ADR-NNNN*, *superseded by ADR-NNNN*, *withdrawn by ADR-NNNN*, *amended by ADR-NNNN*.

**Writing the pointer belongs to the author of the later record, not to whoever next reads the earlier one.** The author of the later record is the only session that knows, at the moment the ground moves, both what moved and where it used to stand. A convention that waits for a reader depends on somebody returning to a file they have no other reason to open, which is exactly what let two records go on asserting a replaced premise until a reader happened to notice. The same duty already binds every other body of prose here — the refactoring-completeness rule in `docs/coding-standard/general.md` asks the author of a change to sweep every claim the change falsified — and the records were the one body of prose it was not applied to.

**The suite checks the relation in both directions.** Where a record declares in its own text that it takes over another's ground — the verb beside the citation, as the withdrawal paragraph of ADR-0059 writes it — the record it names must carry the pointer back to it. And every pointer must name a record that exists and comes later than the one carrying it, so a pointer at a number nothing answers to, or at a record that could not have done the outrunning, fails the same way a stale citation already does. The relation is written once, by one author, and read from both ends, so the omission that produced this record fails a test rather than waiting for a reader to notice.

What the check cannot see is stated rather than implied. A later record that never writes the relation in the declared vocabulary leaves nothing for the scan to key on — ADR-0069 narrowed its predecessor's exclusivity claim in prose that names no relation at all, and whether such prose claims a takeover is a reading, not a comparison, the same boundary the suite's citation-aptness check states for itself. And a premise can be outrun by a change that carries no record, as ADR-0058's was by a bug fix: the pointer is still owed, it names the change and the issue that produced it, and the mechanical check has nothing to hold it to. Both boundaries are why the pointer is a duty of authorship with a check behind it, rather than a thing the suite could conjure on its own.

**What this costs.** A record that outruns an earlier one now costs one more edit, to a file whose whole discipline is not being edited — bounded to a single appended sentence, which is the narrowest breach that keeps the collection true. The relation vocabulary becomes load-bearing: an author who declares a takeover in fresh words the scan does not know gets no red test, only this record to cite when a reader finds the gap. And the floor of known relations in the suite grows by hand, one pair per pointer, which is the price of a scan that can tell an empty result from a broken one.
