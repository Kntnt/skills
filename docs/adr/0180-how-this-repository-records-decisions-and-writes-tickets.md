# How this repository records decisions and writes tickets

This record settles how this repository writes down what it decides: why what applies now and why it became so are two documents rather than one, why a later record no longer reaches back into the record it outran, how a claim this collection makes about an outside tool has to be phrased so that a closed ticket does not read as a conformance it never had, and what a ticket's readiness review is actually measuring when it asks whether somebody could build from the ticket alone. It is a consolidation — it supersedes the three records named below, and carries forward what those records were for, which is the reasoning. It also records the reform that produced it and every record beside it, which is written down nowhere else: the rules modules are the authority on the present, `docs/adr/` is history, and the pointer duty is retired along with the reader it existed for. What the rules are is stated in [`docs/rules/docs.md`](../rules/docs.md), and for the two frontmatter fields a Skill carries knowingly in [`docs/rules/skills.md`](../rules/skills.md), and for what a ticket may claim and how a ticket is read in [`docs/rules/tickets.md`](../rules/tickets.md); those modules are the authority on them. This record argues rather than legislates, and where it needs a rule it cites the module rather than repeating it. A reader who wants today's law should be reading the module and not this. None of the three below is annotated to say that it was folded: the duty that once had a later record reach back into an earlier one is retired, and this record is where that retirement is argued.

## The records folded into this one

- ADR-0066 — The reference validator is a baseline, not a gate
- ADR-0075 — A later record that outruns an earlier one leaves a pointer in it
- ADR-0155 — The review brief names the builder and logs what the reviewer supplied

**The two records about ticket-writing a reader would expect here are not folded, and they are still standing.** ADR-0067, the four assertions a ticket may make about a repository that moves under it, and ADR-0099, the declaration an invariant ticket makes to get a wave of its own, are both cited by `/orchestrate`'s engine and its suite; that citation is what keeps a record out of this reform entirely, so both stay where they are and [`docs/rules/tickets.md`](../rules/tickets.md) cites them directly rather than through this record. What is written below about the ticket layer is what is left once those two are taken out of it: not what a ticket may assert, but what the review that reads a ticket before a builder is handed it is measuring.

**The reform's own decision is recorded here because it was taken nowhere else.** It was taken across the tickets that carried it out — the triage of the archive, the modules under `docs/rules/`, the six consolidations of which this is the last, and the sweep that deletes what they absorbed — and none of those wrote a record, each of them having been the doing rather than the deciding. A choice that hard to reverse, that surprising to a reader who arrives after it, and that squarely the result of a trade is one the bar in [`docs/rules/docs.md`](../rules/docs.md) says earns a record; the C6 bin is where the triage put it, and this is it.

## Two questions in one format, and only one of them answered

**The archive was being read as current law, and it had grown to the size where that stopped working.** When this reform triaged it, `docs/adr/` held 153 records, 904 KB across some 2 100 physical lines, and it was the directory this repository pointed an agent at to learn how anything worked. That is not a document answering *what applies now*. It is 153 answers to *what was decided on 153 different dates*, with nothing in any of them saying which of the 153 survived, so the only honest way to derive today's law from it was to read the whole thing forward and apply every supersession by hand — in a session that had already spent its context getting there. The cost was paid on every read, by every agent, for a question the archive was never shaped to answer.

**The two questions want opposite things from a document, which is why one format could not carry both.** *What applies now* wants a document that is edited: one statement per subject, rewritten in place the day the rule changes, so that the reader who finds it has found the current answer and needs nothing else. *Why it became so* wants a document that is never edited: the alternatives as they looked at the time, what they were thought to cost, the evidence that forced the choice — all of which stop being evidence of anything the moment they are updated to agree with today. A single format has to give up one of the two, and what this repository had given up was the first, silently, by letting the immutable one be read as though it were the mutable one.

**So the split is by audience and by question, and the archive keeps the half it is good at.** The modules under `docs/rules/` are current law, one module per subject, and nothing else claims to be. `docs/adr/` keeps what no module can hold: the reasoning, which is what a reader needs before deciding whether a rule is a finding or a taste. Deleting the archive outright was the cheaper-looking option and it was rejected — a rule with its evidence removed reads as a preference, and the next author to find it inconvenient has nothing to weigh against their own judgement. What is deleted instead is the *duplication*: a record whose whole content is a rule now stated in a module carries nothing the module does not, so the six consolidations lift the reasoning out and the sweep takes the rest.

**Consolidating rather than annotating is what makes the archive smaller instead of longer.** The alternative that keeps every file is to leave all 153 standing and mark each one with where its rule now lives. That preserves history perfectly and fixes nothing: the directory stays the size it was, the reader still meets 153 files, and every one of them now carries a line that has to be kept true as the modules are edited. Folding a bin into one record trades the per-file provenance — which git keeps anyway, in the commit that deletes the file — for a directory a reader can actually finish.

## The pointer that kept the wrong document true

**The duty existed because the archive was load-bearing, and the failure it answered was real and had happened twice.** A record's premise gets replaced by a later record; the earlier one goes on asserting it, because a record is never rewritten; and the reader who opens it finds a confident wrong answer in the one place this repository pointed at for architecture. ADR-0055 went on claiming, in its title and its opening, that a rebuild is the only rerun, after ADR-0069 had given a failed verification a rerun of its own. ADR-0058 went on opening on *done* being the one outcome that leaves the open scope, after the report's closed reader had been widened to every recorded outcome. Both decisions were untouched and both were still correct; it was the framing around them that had gone stale, silently, for as long as nobody happened to reread the file (issues #81 and #86). One pointer existed before the duty was written — the closing sentence of ADR-0017, put there by hand — and nothing asked the author of the next later record to do the same.

**What the duty bought was one sanctioned edit, in the narrowest form that could work.** A later record that narrowed or replaced what an earlier one asserted left a single sentence in the earlier one, beside the claim it corrected, naming the later record and saying what of the earlier one it took. The body was otherwise untouched: the pointer did not soften the old claim or rewrite it, it told the reader where the world had moved. And the sentence carried the relation in a vocabulary the suite could read — the participle beside the citation, *narrowed by*, *superseded by*, *withdrawn by*, *amended by* — so that the omission that produced the duty in the first place failed a test rather than waiting for a reader to notice. The check ran from both ends: a record declaring that it took over another's ground had to be answered by the pointer in the record it named, and every pointer had to name a record that existed and came later than the one carrying it.

**The duty also drew a boundary around itself, and the boundary is the part worth keeping.** A later record that never writes the relation in the declared vocabulary leaves the scan nothing to key on: ADR-0069 narrowed its predecessor's exclusivity claim in prose that names no relation at all, and whether such prose claims a takeover is a reading rather than a comparison. And a premise can be outrun by a change that carries no record — ADR-0058's was, by a bug fix — where the pointer was still owed and the mechanical check had nothing to hold it to. Both are why the arrangement was a duty of authorship with a check behind it, rather than something the suite could ever have derived on its own. The floor of known relations grew by hand, one pair per pointer, which was the price of a scan that could tell an empty result from a broken one.

**What it cost was an edit to a body of documents whose entire discipline is not being edited.** Bounded to one appended sentence, which was the narrowest breach that kept the collection true — but a breach, and a standing invitation to a slightly larger one. It made the relation vocabulary load-bearing: an author who declared a takeover in fresh words the scan did not know got no red test, only ADR-0075 to cite when a reader later found the gap. And it fell on the author of the later record, at the moment the ground moved, which was the only session that knew both what had moved and where it used to stand — correct, and one more thing that had to be remembered by whoever was least likely to be thinking about the old file.

## Retiring the duty is the same decision as the split

**The duty's whole justification was a reader who no longer exists.** Every sentence of the argument above runs through one figure: somebody looking in the archive for how something works today. Once the rules modules are the authority on the present, nobody is asked that question of a record any more. The archive is not consulted about what applies, so it does not have to be kept self-consistent about what applies — a body of documents each describing its own date is exactly what it is now for, and a reader meeting a record that asserts a replaced premise is meeting a true statement about a past date rather than a wrong answer about the present. The pointer was a repair to the archive for a job the archive should never have had.

**Retiring it gives back precisely what ADR-0075 recorded it costing, item for item.** No record is edited any more, so the discipline of never editing a record is unbroken rather than bounded. No vocabulary is load-bearing, so an author who describes a takeover in their own words is not silently outside a scan. No floor of relations grows by hand. And no author owes an edit to a file they have no other reason to open, at the moment they are least likely to think of it. Against that stands the loss of the one thing the pointer gave a reader — that a record carrying no pointer had, at least, not been overtaken in the vocabulary — and the loss is smaller than it looks, because that guarantee never held for the two boundaries the record itself named.

**What replaces it is not a weaker pointer but a different document.** A module rewritten whenever its rule changes keeps the same reader right without any record being touched at all, and keeps them right about the boundary cases too: the takeover written in prose the scan could not read, and the premise outrun by a change that carried no record, both reach the module, because the module states what is true rather than tracking what changed. So there is no residue of the duty to preserve in a weakened form.

**What a reader owes in exchange is to read a record as of its own date, and the absence of a pointer now says nothing.** A record written while the duty stood may carry its pointer sentence; those sentences stay exactly where they are, because a record is not edited to remove them either. Nothing distinguishes a record that carries one from a record that does not, other than when it was written. That asymmetry is the accepted cost of retiring a convention rather than reversing it, and it is why the archive's ingress page and `docs/rules/docs.md` both say so in as many words: what tells a reader whether a decision still holds is the module, in every case, and never the presence or absence of a sentence in the file they happen to be looking at.

## A validator this collection is measured against and does not pass

**`skills-ref validate` rejects every Skill this collection ships, and always has** (ADR-0066, issue #63). The reference implementation of the Agent Skills specification holds a `SKILL.md`'s frontmatter to a closed allowlist of the six fields the specification tabulates — `allowed-tools`, `compatibility`, `description`, `license`, `metadata`, `name` — and every Skill here carries `argument-hint`, and every Skill here now carries `disable-model-invocation` explicitly, `false` included, so that the sidecar Codex reads has something to agree with. Both fields stay. The rejection is accepted as a knowing deviation, and the tool is read as a baseline that must not regress rather than as a gate that must pass.

**The specification states no rule these fields break.** It tabulates the six and then says nothing whatever about an unknown top-level field — no prohibition, no instruction that a client reject one, not even that it is ignored. The closed allowlist is the reference implementation's own strictness, stricter than the prose it implements, so what the two fields deviate from is a tool's inference rather than the specification's text. That distinction is the whole of why the deviation is defensible rather than merely convenient: silence is not permission, but it is not prohibition either, and refusing the fields would mean choosing a validator's inference over a Harness's documented behaviour.

**`metadata` is the specification's own escape hatch and it cannot carry these two.** The field exists so that a client can store properties the specification does not define, and this collection already uses it correctly, in the flat prefixed namespace the record of what a Skill ships carries (ADR-0177). But Claude Code reads `argument-hint` and `disable-model-invocation` at the top level only. Relocating them under `metadata` does not preserve the behaviour behind a conformant spelling; it discards the behaviour silently and leaves a validator-green Skill that no longer does what it did. A hatch the reader cannot see through is not a hatch.

**What the fields buy is worth more than a green run.** `argument-hint` is what puts a Skill's grammar in front of the user at the moment they type the command, and every Skill here has a grammar it refuses on — a hint withdrawn does not simplify the Skill, it hides what the Skill will reject. `disable-model-invocation` is what keeps a Skill from being started by the model on its own, which for most of the collection is the difference between a verb the user asked for and a verb a session decided to run: `/release` or `/orchestrate` starting itself is not a trade any conformance argument pays for. Neither field has a specification-blessed equivalent, so the choice is the field or the behaviour, and it is the behaviour these Skills are for.

**The reason this needed a record at all is what a ticket is allowed to say about the tool.** An acceptance criterion may require that the known state is unchanged — no new field, no new complaint, no Skill failing on anything else. It may not require that the validator passes, and one phrased that way is unsatisfiable while this stands. The two are easy to confuse and were confused: the criteria of issues #52, #53 and #54 asked for *no new complaint* and were verified honestly, but a reader of those closed tickets would conclude that the collection validates, because nothing said which baseline the complaint count was counted against. A closed ticket is the account of what was believed when it was worked and is not rewritten to comply, so the correction cannot live there; it has to live in a document a later reader will meet. That is what makes this a decision rather than a note — the claim a closed ticket makes about an outside tool outlives the ticket, and the only defence is that the baseline it was counted against is written down somewhere permanent.

**The deviation is bounded by a test rather than by intent.** A Skill added later may carry the same two fields on the same reasoning — which is what makes this a stance and not an exception for the Skills that existed when it was taken. It may not quietly carry a third: the suite holds every shipped `SKILL.md`'s top-level fields to the specification's six plus these two, and a ninth field fails the run until the rule accepts it. So the collection's distance from the reference implementation is a number this repository knows, rather than one somebody rediscovers by running the tool. The rule itself is in [`docs/rules/skills.md`](../rules/skills.md), beside the rest of what a Skill's frontmatter may carry.

## What a ticket's readiness review is measuring

**The review asks one question — could somebody who has only this ticket build it — and for a long time it never said who that somebody is** (ADR-0155, issue #229). Two of its criteria then quietly measured against the reviewer's own ease: one counted a fact as present where *a builder could obtain it from the repository in a minute*, another asked whether *a builder could find out which branch it is on* without asking a person, and the note on what is not a finding repeated the same test. A reviewer answers all three by trying. What it finds in a minute is what *it* finds in a minute.

**The gap that made this a decision rather than a wording slip is a capability gap the routing chain creates on purpose.** The reviewer is a subagent started with no model of its own, so it runs on whatever a Harness gives a subagent by default, which is the Main Seat. The builder it is imagining is routed under a cost-first objective, in the Cohort an initial build takes, under a Standing Policy whose shipped ceiling is that same Seat (ADR-0147, ADR-0149) — so the builder sits at or below the reviewer, and the routing exists in order to put it below. Running the check from a strong Seat is the sensible choice, a check being cheap next to a wave that stops, and every rung of that choice widened the gap the brief was silent about. A test phrased as the reviewer's own ease therefore gets *easier to pass* exactly as the run gets more aggressive about routing the builder down.

**So the brief names the builder, and every test phrased as ease is rewritten against that builder.** The reviewer is told, before it reads a criterion, that the builder is a Seat no more capable than itself and possibly several Rungs below, with less deliberation, a smaller model, or both, and that its own ease is therefore the measure of nothing. The criteria ask whether *that* builder could obtain the fact, or determine the branch, without knowing where to look — so a fact the reviewer found by knowing where to look is a fact the ticket has to locate. The phrase *in a minute* leaves the brief entirely, because a minute is the reviewer's minute and no brief has a way to say whose.

**Naming the builder fixes the tests that named it wrongly and does nothing about the rest of the read.** A capable reviewer resolves an ambiguity, infers an intent, or establishes an unlocated fact without noticing that it did, and the rule that an uncertain verdict is a *no* does not catch that: a reviewer that filled a gap silently is not uncertain. So the brief has it log, as it reads, every intent it inferred, every choice it made between readings the ticket allows, and every fact it established that the ticket neither carries nor locates — at the moment each happens, a list reconstructed at the end being a list of what the reviewer still notices having done. Each entry is a cost at minimum, and a stop where the described builder could not have made the same inference.

**Why not run the check from a weaker Seat and let the measurement be literal.** That is the obvious alternative and it is worse. A weak reviewer misses gaps as readily as it fails to fill them, and a miss is invisible — it leaves no trace in the report at all, where a strong reviewer's fill is at least visible once logged, and lands on the maintainer as a sentence to fix. The isolation the review already enforces is not an answer either: it removes the author's knowledge of what the ticket meant, which is a different leak from the reviewer's own capability, and capability leaks through any test phrased as the reviewer's own ease no matter how isolated the context is.

**What this costs is a longer read and some reports that get a shrug.** The brief grew, and it now asks the reviewer to keep a running log while it reads a whole ticket and a repository. Reports get longer, and some of what they gain is a finding on a sentence a maintainer will read and shrug at, an inference the described builder would have made just as easily. That is the intended trade: the shrug is cheap and costs a maintainer a moment, where the same inference unreported is a ticket that passed the check and stops a builder in a run with nobody watching.
