# A forecast is pooled from a group's routed rows where it has any

A measurement of the maintainer's own store on 2026-09-20 found that the two halves of Model Selector's answer for `claude-opus-5` × `implement` rested on largely disjoint rows. Of 213 rows for that point, 75 were routed builds carrying a grade and no price at all, and 132 were non-routed spans of the maintainer's own session — median 608 seconds and 7.01 USD — carrying the price. The chance of success came from the first set and the appetite and the runtime from the second. The figure the ranking then compared against a Codex build was the price of a span inside somebody's working session, where a Codex row is one whole build of twenty-five to fifty minutes. That comparison sent a day's work to a model two to four times dearer than the alternative, and it was never like for like.

Why the two sets were disjoint is a separate defect, repaired in the same ticket: a routed Claude attempt was filed without its token counts, because the read of the builder's own transcript matched the attempt identity against the first line of the subagent's first message and a dispatching session hands a builder a pointer to a brief file rather than the brief. That repair fills the routed rows in. It does not settle what a forecast is made of once they are full, because the spans do not go away: the maintainer works in the same Harness all day, and every long stretch of that work is a Unit like any other.

**So the token and elapsed-time forecast pools a group's routed rows wherever that group holds any, and all of its rows only where it holds none.** `Estimator.tokens` and `Estimator.seconds` have no exact cell; they back off through the kind-and-model group and then the model-wide group, and each of those is narrowed before it is pooled. Appetite and runtime are properties of the size of a job. A routed attempt is a whole job somebody dispatched and waited on; a span of a Main Seat session is a stretch of a conversation. Averaging the two produces a number that describes neither, and the direction of the error is not random — the spans are the cheaper and shorter of the two, so the pooled figure understates what the dear model would actually spend on a build and understates it most where the store holds the most of somebody's own work.

**The choice is made once per group, and never per category.** The alternative reading — take each category from the routed rows where they carry it — was rejected because it reassembles exactly the average this record exists to end: a cache-read count taken from a session span while the routed rows beside it supply the output count is one forecast built out of two sizes of job. What the whole-group rule costs is that a point whose routed rows carry no counts at all falls through to the model-wide group and then to the kind's shipped prior. That consequence is intended. A store that has never measured what a routed build of a point spends should say so and answer from its prior, rather than answer confidently with the price of something else.

**The narrowing stops at the forecast and is deliberately not extended to the chance of success.** There the level and the model are the conditions being estimated rather than factors every row is read back through, the hierarchy already backs off through three tiers of its own with each parent fitted on the rows its child does not hold, and narrowing its rows would interact with what counts as measured — `ENOUGH` rows at a point is what makes a point measured, and a point measured on seven rows would become unmeasured on three. How well work went is a reading of the work; how much it cost and how long it took are measurements of its size. The two are not symmetric, and the reason is written into the module beside the code so that the next author does not extend it there out of symmetry.

**Nothing already stored is reinterpreted, and no row gains a second shape.** The rows the store holds stay exactly as they are; what changes is which of them a forecast is pooled from. The routed fact those rows carry was already part of a Measurement's own vocabulary (ADR-0182), and the same ticket makes both sides of a routed attempt file the fact they hold rather than leaving one of them asserting a constant `false`. Where two sides file one attempt, that fact travels with the grade of the higher authority, as the kind and the label already do, so a caller's own reading of whether it chose the seat outranks a transcript's — a transcript records the seat that ran and never whether anybody chose it. The boundary ADR-0179 draws stands untouched: a Main-Seat spawn nobody routed is delegated and is not routed.
