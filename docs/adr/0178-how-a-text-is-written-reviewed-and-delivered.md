# How a text is written, reviewed, and delivered

This record settles the editorial contract in one place: what a first draft is answerable to, where the resources that say so live, how a language reaches the Skill that needs it, what a review may report and what it must read past, how a correction is attempted and what stops the loop, where the finished text goes, and how any of it is judged when no assertion can see it. It is a consolidation — it supersedes the eighteen records named below, and carries forward what those records were for, which is the reasoning. What the rules are is stated in [`docs/rules/skills.md`](../rules/skills.md), which is the authority on the shape the editorial resources have and on the licences a Skill resolving one is under; the contract itself is stated once in the Collection Library, across [`editorial/base.md`](../../skills/kntnt/library/references/editorial/base.md) and the genre, technique, anti-slop and mechanics files beside it, with [`editorial/README.md`](../../skills/kntnt/library/references/editorial/README.md) as their format page, [`delivery.md`](../../skills/kntnt/library/references/delivery.md) as the shared output contract, and [`languages/README.md`](../../skills/kntnt/library/references/languages/README.md) as the Language Resource format page; how a Skill is evaluated against the fixture corpus is stated in [`docs/evaluation/protocol.md`](../evaluation/protocol.md). This record argues rather than legislates, and where it needs a rule it cites the file that carries it rather than repeating it. A reader who wants today's law should be reading one of those and not this. None of the eighteen is annotated to say so: the duty that once had a later record reach back into an earlier one is retired, for the reason [`docs/rules/docs.md`](../rules/docs.md) gives, and each of them is read as of its own date until the sweep that removes it.

**Half of the eighteen were written from evidence, and the evidence is most of what they carry.** Nine of them were forced by something an evaluation run or a live session actually did — four runs leaving one comma, two runs of one Skill reporting in two languages, seven runs where one echoed the artifact it had just written to a file, two runs disagreeing about whether a docstring is prose, two subagents reading each other's brief off one path, a loop that emptied a text over three rounds and reported the emptiness as a finding, eleven runs opening a genre resource nobody had told them to open, a model invocation loading a whole Language Resource the resolver had just narrowed, and a report burying its own answer to protect an arc. Those observations are the part that cannot be reconstructed from anything else in this repository, and they are written out below rather than summarised, because a rule with its evidence removed reads as a preference.

**Four of the eighteen extend or correct another rather than carry a subject of their own, and are argued inside the section of the record they extend.** ADR-0118 settles how the brief ADR-0107 specified actually reaches its subagent; ADR-0120 adds a fourth exit to that loop's three; ADR-0123 and ADR-0124 each answer a question the delivery contract had left open, on the same document and about the same response. ADR-0164 is a fifth of that kind at one remove — it changes where a technique selection may come from, in one clause of ADR-0095 — but it also makes ADR-0163's scoping the ordinary case, so it is argued beside both.

**Two why-chains below continue in records that are not folded here.** That shared implementation belongs to the Collection Library rather than to whichever Skill needed it first is ADR-0076's, which is the C3 bin's and is argued in *What a Skill ships*; nearly every placement decision in this record leans on it and none of them re-argues it. That a Harness capability is a Dependency a Skill declares rather than a thing it discovers at run time is ADR-0062's, from the same bin, and the subagent Capability below is that rule applied. Where a paragraph needs either it names it and stops.

## One contract, and the halves a Skill actually loads

**Write produces a first draft and Redline reviews one, and they had to agree on what a good draft is** (ADR-0095, ADR-0076). Written into each Skill, that agreement is two documents free to drift until a requirement and its review disagree about what was required. Written as one document holding everything either could want, it makes a Skill that only writes pay context for diagnostics it cannot act on — which is the cost the whole arrangement exists to remove. So every normative outcome a first draft has to meet is stated once, in English, in a Library reference that names no Skill and no option, with examples written to be applied semantically in the target language rather than duplicated per language.

**The register baseline ships with its own override attached** (ADR-0095). Idiomatic contemporary prose with the restraint, concreteness, factual precision and cadence of a well-edited general-interest newspaper or quality magazine is stated together with the fact that genre, audience and purpose outrank it, because a baseline stated alone turns a letter into a news article.

**The review half is a separate file rather than a section** (ADR-0095). `general.review.md` sits beside `general.md`, so a Skill that only writes loads the base half and stops. The extension carries diagnostics, examples, edge cases, ambiguity resolution and minimum-safe-correction guidance for requirements the base half already states; it introduces no independent target, because anything a draft has to meet is a requirement and belongs where the writing Skill will see it, and it restates no base rule, because a rule written twice can disagree with itself.

**Source Fidelity stays out of the shared contract on purpose** (ADR-0095, ADR-0088). It is Write's invariant, and the reviewing Skill reads a Text Artifact without its source material, so a shared document asserting it would ask a reviewer for a check it cannot make and invite the caveat that it could not make it.

**What the contract costs is a document with almost no executable check behind it** (ADR-0095). The suite can hold the placement, the silence about flag spellings, the separability of the review half and which resources a Skill reaches for; whether a draft actually meets the contract is visible only in the draft. It also fixes a filename as an interface, which the next section is about.

## The directory is the list

**A genre or a technique is one file beside the base contract, and nothing enumerates the installed set anywhere else** (ADR-0095). The filename without its extension is the canonical value, and it is the value recorded in Handoff Metadata whatever the user typed. Adding a genre is therefore writing one file rather than a coordinated edit across a registry and its consumers, and a value with no file is refused rather than resolved to something near it. `general` is the default genre and ships as a complete contract, because an unspecified content type deserves a contract rather than the absence of one.

**Renaming a resource renames the value, and that is the price of the property** (ADR-0095). Handoff Metadata written before a rename no longer resolves. The alternative is a registry mapping stable identifiers onto files, which is the second statement of what is installed that the paragraph above exists to avoid.

**A technique applies because something selected it and never because a text resembles one, and that half has never moved** (ADR-0095, ADR-0164). What did move is where a selection may come from. Until ADR-0164 the only sources were the invocation, a recognized map in the material's frontmatter, and an instruction naming one, with no technique at all below them — so most of what this collection writes was written with no spine unless the person invoking knew to ask for one. The installed genres are not neutral about that: an article, a column, an opinion piece, a case study, a teaser and a page of web copy are ordinarily written with a narrative arc, and a report with the analytical one. The techniques were installed, the practice was settled, and the two met only for somebody who already knew both.

**A genre's base half therefore names the technique that genre is ordinarily written with, or states that it has none** (ADR-0164). It is a selection and not evidence: an installed genre names its ordinary arc whatever text is in front of the run, so nothing about the material has been read to reach it, and a genre naming none leaves the level below it standing. What makes the coverage exhaustive is the directory at the time of writing and no list written anywhere else — the arrangement this collection replaced kept the mapping in a hand-maintained index beside the genres, which reads better than nine statements until somebody adds a genre and does not update it, and then there are two answers to what is installed and no way to tell which is stale.

**The statement sits below the resource's requirements rather than in its opening, for a reason the next section supplies** (ADR-0164, ADR-0115). A technique named in the opening would enter the reading a run does while it is still deciding which genre applies, which is reading an arc off the material by a longer route. Below the requirements it is out of reach of inference and in reach of the run that has already settled the genre, which is the only run that needs it.

**A collection-wide default technique was the road not taken** (ADR-0164). One arc applied wherever nothing named another would have given every text a spine at the cost of the only thing a genre knows that a collection-wide default cannot: that a report is not written as a story and a press release is not written as an arc at all. What the chosen shape costs is a text under a genre that names an arc, wanted without one, needing a sentence to say so — the invocation's technique flag names installed resources, so it cannot name none, and refusing the genre's arc is an instruction or a map carrying `none`, which is a gesture a user has to know about. That is also why `none` in a recognized map is read back as a value rather than as a gap: with a genre able to supply an arc, the difference is between a draft written without one being reviewed without one and being reviewed against an arc nobody chose.

## Inferring a genre, and how far a run may read

**Inference is the one level of the genre precedence that is handed no name, and the run answered the question the way that was left** (ADR-0115, issues #139, #140). The levels above it — the invocation, a recognized map, the current instruction, conversation context — each supply a name that is verified against the directory that installs it. Inference supplies nothing, and the only reading the resolution step authorised was that same listing, which carries filenames. So runs opened a candidate resource to find out what that genre asks for. The evaluations of Write and of Redline against the fixture corpus caught it in eleven runs between them, every one on an invocation that named no genre and never otherwise, which is what a gap in an instruction looks like rather than a Skill disobeying one.

**The licence is the opening and stops there** (ADR-0115). The `# <Name>` heading and the paragraph under it say what the genre is; what the genre *asks for* is not evidence about whether it applies, so a resource read past its opening has been loaded rather than considered. That is why the opening became a requirement on every genre and technique rather than a courtesy — a genre whose opening does not say what it is leaves a run choosing between installed genres nothing but a filename, and the reading it would fall back on is exactly the one the licence removes.

**The licence sits in the resolution step, and the loading step is untouched by it** (ADR-0115). Each Skill loads the contract and nothing besides it, admitting the genre inference settled on and no other, whatever inference read to settle it. Both Skills state the licence in identical words, because one precedence with one question cannot have two answers.

**An index would have answered the same question and cost the property that makes adding a genre one file** (ADR-0115). It is the registry refused above, arriving by another door. The openings are already written, already required, and already where a run is looking.

**What the licence costs is a read the loading rule cannot see the end of** (ADR-0115, ADR-0093). Nothing measures how far into a file a run went, so the bound is held by the instruction and by the provider-native evaluations that found the gap. The suite can hold that both Skills carry the licence, that they carry the same one, that it sits in the resolution step, and that every installed resource opens the way inference needs; whether a run stopped where it was told is visible only in the run's own trace.

## What a genre orders and what a technique orders

**One report was told to put its conclusion in two places** (ADR-0163). The report genre puts the answer at the top, because its reader may act on the first paragraph and read the rest only if they doubt it; the PAC technique draws its conclusion where the test is finished. A run resolving both — an ordinary pairing, and the default the arrangement this collection replaced gave every report — was offered a precedence and nothing else: the arc is written inside the genre, and the genre's convention stands where the two meet. That says which rule wins without saying what either rule is about, and a run applying a precedence it cannot scope either buries the answer to protect the arc or flattens every section to protect the answer.

**The two rules govern different scopes, so neither is a softening of the other** (ADR-0163). The genre orders the document: the report's own answer stands where the reader who may act on it and stop will meet it. The technique orders the section: its arc's unit is a section, and the conclusion it places is that section's. Both hold in one report because they place different things.

**Evidence still precedes the conclusion wherever a conclusion is drawn** (ADR-0163). The arrangement this collection replaced held the opposite principle as a rule of style rather than of one genre — evidence before conclusion, applied strictly in reports, whose list of characteristic defects included a conclusion delivered before its basis — and the reasoning was not aesthetic: a conclusion arriving before its evidence asks the reader to trust the writer, because they cannot weigh what they have not been shown. This collection inverted the document-level half of that when it wrote the report genre, and said nowhere that it had. What the inversion moved is the report's answer and not the order of argument; the reader who doubts the top reads down into the sections and meets the material before the result it produced.

**What the inversion trades is on the record rather than argued away** (ADR-0163). It buys what this genre's reader needs — a known reader with a decision waiting, served by a verdict they can act on and evidence they can check, and not served by being walked to a conclusion they commissioned. It spends the older principle's protection at the one place a self-interested writer would most like to spend it, and the genre answers that with its other requirements rather than with its order: every figure carries its basis, the strongest objection is put at full strength and then answered, what the report cannot settle is written down, and a recommendation claims no more than the material supports. Where those hold, an answer at the top can be checked; where they fail, no ordering would have saved the report.

**Each resource states its own scope and neither states the other's, which is why the argument is here** (ADR-0163, ADR-0095). A genre resource arguing with a technique resource is where a reader would have to read both to learn what one of them requires. Saying the precedence harder was the road not taken: a longer sentence in each technique declaring that the genre wins would have left the collision unnamed, made every later one another exception to write, and gone on teaching that one of the two rules yields — the reading that produced the buried answer. A table of per-genre exceptions inside the technique fails the way every such table fails here, by making one resource the owner of rules its peers own.

**What this costs is a scope line held by prose alone, and it settles one pair** (ADR-0163, ADR-0093). The shape generalises — a genre that fixes a document-level order, a technique whose arc is a section's — but the next genre with a fixed order meets the same question in prose somebody has to write. And since a genre now names its ordinary technique, the pairing is the ordinary case rather than an occasional one, so the line is exercised constantly and still held by prose.

## A language is one resource, and a scope is what a Skill gets back

**Three Skills needed the same language identity and different language-specific guidance** (ADR-0087). Splitting aliases and scopes across consumer-owned files would make every new locale a coordinated multi-file change; loading one monolithic file would spend context on rules the active Skill cannot use. So each language or locale is one resource whose frontmatter carries the canonical code, a bounded set of aliases, inheritance metadata and enough to select it without loading its body, and whose body carries separate composition, review, anti-slop and mechanics scopes — each named for the guidance it holds rather than for the Skill that reads it, so that a scope more than one Skill consumes does not read as one Skill's property. A deterministic resolver inventories frontmatter and extracts only the requested scopes; semantic interpretation handles an unlisted human language description before the resolver verifies that the derived language is available.

**Generic editorial guidance stays outside the Language Resources** (ADR-0087). Shared rules and English examples stay language-independent and are applied semantically in the target language, and a Language Resource gains a language-specific rule only where that language genuinely differs or observed behaviour shows the generic rule insufficient. That bar is what keeps the same rule from being written once per installed locale, and the mechanics section below is the case where it was tested.

**The resolver's own response carried the address that defeated it** (ADR-0129, issue #170). The GPT-family evaluation of model-invoked Proofread produced the right mechanical corrections through both branches of its trigger and still failed its loading contract: the Harness trace showed the complete `en_US` resource opened *after* the resolver had returned its mechanics scope, putting composition, review and anti-slop guidance into context before the pass. Invocation by name had followed the resolver boundary; model invocation had treated the resource itself as further reference. The delivered artifact could not reveal the difference, which is why the trace and not the text exposed it — and the body already said exactly which command to run and exactly what was admitted, so the failure was not an absent rule. What the successful response carried was a `path` to the file it had just filtered: unnecessary to use the response, and enough to make the resolver's private input look like the next thing to read. A caller could obey the selection and defeat it in consecutive actions.

**A successful scoped result is a terminal result rather than a pointer** (ADR-0129). The path is an implementation detail used inside the resolver, and it stays available inside diagnostics, where a malformed installed file has to be repaired and the response stops the editorial run rather than supplying rules for it. An editorial Skill acts on returned content directly and does not need to know whether it came from the selected locale or an inherited base, because the response already names the scope's source code. The resolver stays the one public seam at which a resource is selected and narrowed, and a caller cannot step around that seam using an address the seam supplied.

**Repeating the prohibition was the rejected alternative, and a filtering flag was the other** (ADR-0129). The loading step already excluded every inadmissible scope and document in direct language, and one trigger branch followed it while the other opened the path anyway; another sentence would have spent more context on the same instruction and left the affordance intact. Making opacity optional would have preserved an implementation detail solely so that callers could bypass the boundary that defines the seam. What it costs is a convenient file address gone from successful output — a maintainer diagnosing a malformed resource still gets its path in the refusal, and somebody administering the installed directory already knows where the resources live.

**Model invocation and invocation by name converge before resource loading** (ADR-0129, ADR-0094). The trigger decides whether the Skill starts and supplies the current turn's Text Artifact as the omitted operand; after that both branches and a named invocation enter the same numbered steps. That matters even with the path removed: the response boundary prevents one observed bypass, and convergence prevents the trigger wording from becoming a second execution contract that could invent another.

## The catalogue this collection owns

**The patterns that mark machine-sounding prose are well catalogued outside this collection, and both ways of reusing one were worse than adapting it** (ADR-0101). Declaring an external Skill as a Dependency makes an editorial review of ours fail wherever that Skill is not installed, and hands a third party the power to change what a review of ours finds. Vendoring one whole imports a standalone Skill's two jobs, its English word lists, and roughly two thousand words of instruction into a collection whose whole reason for splitting drafting from reviewing was that rules were competing with the text for context.

**The transferable part of such a catalogue is the patterns and their repairs, not the workflow around them** (ADR-0101). Ours states each pattern once, with one example and the minimum change that removes it, and names nothing about how a Skill is invoked. No external Skill becomes a required or an optional Dependency, so what a review finds is settled by files this repository ships and can be diffed.

**The upstream notice travels inside the adapted file** (ADR-0101). MIT terms permit local adaptation and require the notice to accompany substantial portions, and a repository-level licence file is not something a reader of an installed Skill ever receives.

**The catalogue is language-independent and lives in the Library** (ADR-0101, ADR-0076). Its examples are English because it is, and they are applied as semantic patterns in the target language rather than matched as strings; a word, phrase, punctuation convention or construction that exists in only one language belongs to that language's own anti-slop scope. What the ownership costs is that improvements upstream do not arrive on their own, and a pattern named there and not here is one this collection does not catch until somebody writes it down.

## Mechanics that do not depend on the language

**A defect report against Proofread turned out to be a defect in the corpus** (ADR-0121). The Claude-family evaluation found four independent runs correcting every planted error in the American English fixture and leaving one comma, each naming it in the reply as a deliberate retention: the contrastive comma in *The bug were not in the parsing at all, it was in a silent retry*. Garner's Modern English Usage accepts the joint where the clauses are short, closely related and carry no risk of a miscue, and names the negative-positive contrast as an acceptable type; the Chicago Manual of Style's own Q&A calls a near-identical construction borderline and perfectly acceptable for most writing; Språkrådet's *Svenska skrivregler* holds satsradning correct so long as the second main clause explains, specifies or modifies the first. The Skill was right, and building the ticket as filed would have made it worse.

**What survived was the reason it was right by accident** (ADR-0121). No shipped mechanics scope named sentence-boundary punctuation at all, so what kept the comma was the closing preference rule each scope stated separately, applied by a model's own reading of register — and the next text would have been decided the same way, in either direction.

**The rules of objective correctness that do not depend on the language are therefore stated once, in the Library** (ADR-0121, ADR-0087, ADR-0076). The two authorities draw one line and differ only in how strictly they default, which is what makes the rule generic; a scope was the wrong home for it, because a Language Resource takes a rule only where the language genuinely differs, and a rule written three times is a rule that can disagree with itself. Where a language does default differently, its own scope says so and is the more specific instruction.

**The clause-boundary rule is stated as a conditional because both flat statements are wrong** (ADR-0121). Stated flatly as an error, a mechanical pass would start correcting a construction both shipped languages accept; stated nowhere, it is decided by whoever is reading. The correction is bounded to the joint — the comma becomes a period or a semicolon, and the sentence is not reordered, merged, split, tightened or given a conjunction, because *and*, *but* and *so* each assert a relation the writer did not — and where the reading is genuinely open the comma stands. The rule reaches the boundary that misleads a reader, not the boundary that is merely informal.

**The preference rule moved with it, and gained the limit that was missing** (ADR-0121). Where a language establishes more than one correct form the text's own choice stands and only inconsistency within one text is corrected — once, for every language, instead of three times in three scopes and a fourth time in one Skill's body. Beside it: a construction the rules in hand name as wrong is corrected however consistently the text commits to it, because consistency is what an established variant is judged by rather than what makes something established.

**The corpus was corrected rather than left standing, which is a deliberate exception to the rule about corpora below** (ADR-0121, ADR-0093). The protocol's rule exists so that a corpus is never softened to match what was built; here the corpus asserted an error that neither language's authorities recognise, so the documentation that listed the comma among the planted errors was wrong about the sentence, and the fixture gained a comma joining two main clauses that do not cohere — a conditional rule that only ever meets the accepted case has not been tested in the direction that matters.

**What this costs is a semantic judgement in a mechanical Skill's hands** (ADR-0121). Whether two clauses cohere is a reading, and a reading can go wrong in both directions, which is why the contract says plainly that an open case is left alone. There is also one more place to look when a mechanical question has no obvious home: a rule that is nearly generic can now be put in the wrong one of two places rather than the wrong one of three languages.

## Where a result goes

**Several Skills produce a Text Artifact, and each of them had to answer the same three questions** (ADR-0091, ADR-0076). Where the result goes, whether the file that supplied the text may be replaced by it, and what happens when the run finds nothing to change. Answered per Skill, those get answers that differ in ways nobody chose, and a user who has learnt one Skill's habits guesses wrong about the next. Answered by a shared output engine, they would introduce a runtime none of the Skills needs — the effect is a single write, and what varies is the decision before it. So the contract is a document rather than a component: it states behaviour and never a flag spelling, and the Formal Invocation options that expose it stay each Skill's own, which is what lets a Skill added later be bound by the contract without inheriting a grammar it did not choose.

**Delivery is non-mutating until the caller says otherwise** (ADR-0091). The default Output Target is the agent response and a run that keeps it changes nothing on disk; supplying a local file as source material selects no destination. Naming an existing path is the authorization and there is no second confirmation, because demanding a force flag on top of a named path teaches that naming a destination means less than it says. A directory derives a filename and never overwrites what it derives onto, keeping one stable stem and taking the first free numbered candidate from `-2` upward, so repeated delivery grows one sequence rather than forking a new stem at every collision.

**Replacing a source has exactly one name** (ADR-0091). In-place Editing belongs to the Skills that review an existing Text Artifact, and a Skill that creates one never edits the material its brief and sources came from. An explicit output path equal to the input path is refused in favour of it rather than honoured, so a reader of an invocation can see that a source is about to be replaced without comparing two paths to find out.

**An unchanged result is reported and not re-delivered** (ADR-0091). Repeating the text spends output on what the caller holds, and rewriting the file makes its timestamp claim work that did not happen. An explicitly selected different file or directory still receives the complete artifact, because creating that artifact was the request.

**A run that names a destination does not repeat the artifact in the response either, and it took an observation to settle that** (ADR-0124, issues #148, #111). Seven runs of `/unslop` named a file or a directory on one day; six reported where the artifact went and left the response to the findings, and the seventh named its destination and then printed all four paragraphs into the response as well. That seventh run is one of the two whose pass found nothing, so the text it echoed was byte-identical to the source the caller was already holding — and the other such run wrote its file and printed nothing. Neither disobeyed anything: the contract settled where the artifact goes and what a response-targeted run does, and said nothing about what a file-targeted run may put in the response beyond the findings.

**The reasoning was already one section above, written for the run that found nothing to change** (ADR-0124, ADR-0091). Repeating a text into the response spends output on what the caller already holds, doubly so when the file just written holds it too. The response is left for what the file cannot carry: the findings, which of them are unresolved, and where the run put the file, the derived filename included — that last being the one part of the outcome the caller cannot read off their own invocation. Printing only on the unchanged run was the shape the observed runs almost formed, and it inverts the rule exactly where it costs most: the run with the least to report would be the only one to spend a whole artifact reporting it, and the caller who has just been told nothing changed would be handed the text they supplied.

**Nothing in the destination rules was falsified by either extension** (ADR-0124, ADR-0091). Path handling, overwrite semantics, filename derivation and collision numbering are what they were; the response-targeted default still delivers the complete artifact in the response. What was added is what the response holds once the text has gone somewhere else.

**What the contract as a whole costs is a document with no executable check behind most of it** (ADR-0091). The suite can hold its placement, its silence about flag spellings, and the presence of the rules a consumer depends on; whether a Skill obeys it is visible only through that Skill's behaviour. The alternative — an output runtime the Skills call — would buy that check by adding a component to a decision whose whole difficulty is deciding, and would still leave each Skill to state which options reach it.

## What language the report is written in

**Two runs of one Skill over one Swedish text reported in two languages, a day apart** (ADR-0123, issues #145, #111, #109). The review-only run wrote all fifteen findings in Swedish; the run with one correction to spend delivered the Swedish text and wrote its findings in English. `/unslop` reproduced the split with the polarity reversed — twenty-nine English findings on a review-only run, six Swedish ones on the run that corrected. Neither Skill disobeyed anything: the delivery contract settled the language of exactly one response element, the short no-change status, and both obeyed that on every Swedish fixture that produced one. The findings beside a delivered artifact were left for each run to decide, and each run decided differently.

**A report about a text is written in the language of that text, never in the language of the invocation** (ADR-0123). This is the answer the no-change status already had, for the same reason: a report about a text is read by the reader of that text — here, the person who has to finish the work the correction budget did not. The invocation's language records how the run was asked for; the artifact's language records what the run is about.

**The invocation's language has one real argument and it loses on who the findings are for** (ADR-0123). The operator who typed the command is waiting for the answer, and a report they cannot read helps nobody. But a findings report is not a status line about the run: it is a list of unfinished editorial work in a specific text, quoting that text and addressed to whoever opens it next. Answering in the invocation's language would also make one response bilingual whenever a Swedish artifact needed no work in one place and further work in another, or else force the status rule open to keep the two halves together — and that rule is not wrong. The cost falls on the operator who does not read the artifact's language and is offered no override: they may have to translate fifteen findings rather than one status line, which is the same trade the status rule made, at greater length.

**A sentence in each Skill's steps was the smaller diff, and the second Skill is the evidence against it** (ADR-0123, ADR-0124). Unslop had the identical gap before anything was written, and it was found only because a second evaluation happened to run. A rule written where the defect was reported is a rule the next Skill has to be caught before it learns; a Skill that delivers through the shared contract inherits the answer without an edit and without knowing the rule exists. The same argument settled the destination question a day later, on the same document.

**Leaving it to the run is what was in force in both cases, and it is not neutrality** (ADR-0123, ADR-0124). A question a contract leaves open is not open in practice; it is answered per run, invisibly, by whatever the surrounding context happened to make salient. Two answers to one question in one day's evaluation of one Skill is what that produces.

## What an editorial pass reads past

**Two runs over one code-carrying article disagreed about whether the prose inside its fenced Python block was the text** (ADR-0125, issues #149, #111). The review-only run located three of its fifteen findings inside the block — a docstring carrying four catalogue patterns, and two comments carrying one each — and drew its own boundary through the sample, repairing the prose and leaving the identifiers and the message string as the program. The correcting run reported ten findings, none inside the code, and gave the opposite reason: the block is source that ships in the real world, so it is verbatim material. Both preserved every byte of code, so nothing here was a preservation failure; what differed was what counted as a finding, and a finding is what the caller acts on.

**A code sample is quoted material rather than the text's own prose, and nothing inside one is a finding** (ADR-0125). The document quotes a sample; it does not assert it. A fenced block, an indented block and an inline code span are all read past rather than read against the rules, so a docstring, a comment or a string literal inside one is neither reported nor repaired however much it sounds like the prose around it. This sits beside the clause the correction brief already carried for quotations, and it keeps a pass that cannot run the program from rewriting the comments that explain it — a comment is answerable to the code beneath it, and an editorial pass holds none of what would say whether the rewrite is still true. Prose *about* code is untouched by any of this: a paragraph naming a function's arguments is ordinary prose.

**The boundary is the sample and not the prose inside it, because the inner boundary has no answer** (ADR-0125). Report-but-never-correct was the shape the review-only run improvised, and it leaves the same ambiguity one layer down: the docstring is prose, the exception message is arguably prose, the log format string is arguably both, and the identifier is not. Every run would draw that line itself. It also splits the two Skills that correct from the one that only corrects, since a mechanical pass has no findings to report separately and the option gives it no answer at all. Drawing the line at the sample leaves nothing to settle, at the price of the occasional real defect going unreported in a comment — which is the same trade the preservation clause already made for quotations, where the material's own wording outranks the pass's opinion of it.

**A flag was not seriously weighed** (ADR-0125). It offers the caller a choice between two answers where one of them is wrong, and it would have to be carried by three Skills and every one of their grammar surfaces to settle a question a sentence settles.

**The mechanical Skill is included deliberately** (ADR-0125). A misspelling inside a code sample is a misspelling in somebody's program, and correcting it is the one change a mechanical pass must not make.

## Three Skills that compose without a pipeline

**Handoff Metadata is an optimisation and never provenance** (ADR-0088). Write can attach metadata that makes a later review more precise, but requiring it — or requiring the original source — would make the reviewing Skills usable only as stages of one pipeline. So the three editorial Skills have independent input contracts and compose through public Skill interfaces: formal values override the metadata, and context or the Text Artifact supplies what is missing.

**The reviewing Skill depends on the mechanical one and reads none of its internals** (ADR-0088). It invokes it once after editorial correction, through the public Skill, which is the Collection's Dependency boundary applied rather than an arrangement of its own.

**What that boundary costs is a source-faithfulness failure nobody catches** (ADR-0088). Source Fidelity belongs to Write, which must represent its supplied material truthfully; the review reads a Text Artifact against its editorial contract without comparing it with source material and without reporting that source verification was unavailable, and the mechanical pass changes only mechanical language errors. So a fabrication made by Write or by a human author survives the review. In return each Skill stays independently usable on a text nobody here produced, which is most of the texts there are.

## One lens, shipped narrower than the contract

**Sometimes the only thing wrong with a finished text is that it sounds like a machine wrote it** (ADR-0112). The full review reads a text against the base contract, the selected genre, the optional technique, the language's own guidance and the shared catalogue. Typing all of that to get one pass of it loads five documents to apply one, and invites corrections to four things nobody complained about.

**A single lens gets a Skill of its own, and that Skill is narrower than the review rather than parallel to it** (ADR-0112). It selects no genre, no technique and no editorial contract; it loads the shared catalogue and the resolved language's anti-slop scope and nothing else; and it neither replaces the review nor grows into one. Substantive correction keeps one workflow because this Skill cannot reach the contract that workflow applies — the narrowness is what keeps the two from becoming two public interfaces onto one job. A Skill added this way extends the editorial set rather than being enumerated by it: the next lens that earns one is another file and no coordinated edit anywhere else.

**It reads no more than it may act on** (ADR-0112). A Skill holding the review scope it is contracted not to apply is a Skill one round away from applying it, and a correction made against guidance the review never used is a change nobody asked for. So the language's wider review guidance stays with the Skill that applies the whole contract, and the correction brief this one dispatches carries the resolved language and no other editorial parameter.

**One pass was asked for and one pass is delivered** (ADR-0112). The full review closes with a mechanical pass because it has just applied an entire editorial contract and mechanical errors must be removed last; a single-lens Skill has done nothing of the kind, so it proofreads nothing, declares no Dependency on a proofreading peer, and names the separate gesture on its own page. Closing every editorial Skill with a mechanical pass for symmetry would make a user asking for one thing receive two, and make a Skill Unsatisfied whenever the peer it never needed is absent.

**It is user-invoked** (ADR-0112). *This text reads like AI* is a judgement about how prose sounds, and a model reaching for a rewrite on that judgement unasked is deciding something the author is entitled to decide.

**What it costs is a second Skill applying rules the review also applies** (ADR-0112). The two loop contracts stay in step by reuse rather than restatement — the Correction Budget's semantics are the Collection's and not this Skill's — and the duplication of the catalogue pass is the price of the frugality: a pass that loads one catalogue is a pass somebody will actually run on a finished text.

## The correction loop, and the four ways it stops

**Repairing findings in the reviewing context is the obvious next step and the wrong one** (ADR-0107). The session that wrote the findings also wrote the reasoning behind them, the passages it weighed and dismissed, and its own account of what the text is trying to do. A repair made there is made by the reader least able to see the text as it now stands, and a second look from the same context confirms what the first look already believed.

**Each correction is attempted by a subagent started fresh, and everything it gets arrives whole** (ADR-0107). Fresh means it carries no earlier findings, no earlier attempt and no part of the reviewing session's reading — only the complete current Text Artifact, the complete findings of the most recent review, the genre, technique and language the run resolved, and the requirement to leave alone whatever the findings do not concern. A summary would be the dispatching session's reading of the text, which is exactly what a fresh subagent exists to be without. What reaches it is a filled-in brief and nothing else, which is also what makes a round reproducible.

**A correction is verified by review and never accepted on the report of whoever made it** (ADR-0107). The returned text is reviewed again from the top against the same documents, and it is that review's findings — not the correcting subagent's account of what it fixed — that decide whether the loop continues. An agent reporting on its own work is the one reader who cannot check it, and a loop that trusted the report would terminate on confidence rather than on the text.

**The budget is a ceiling and not a quota** (ADR-0107). The loop stops when no findings remain, with the rest of the budget unspent, so a clean text is not rewritten to use up a number. It stops when a correction makes no relevant progress, because the next round would be the same round and saying what remains is worth more than repeating it. And it stops when the budget is spent, delivering the text with what is left for a person to finish. The default is one correction — an ordinary review buys one repair and one chance to verify it — zero is a review that reports rather than corrects, and the closing mechanical pass runs exactly once after the loop whichever exit was taken.

**Subagents stay a hard Capability whatever budget is in force** (ADR-0107, ADR-0062). A declaration that changed with the invocation would be an availability contract a caller could only discover by running, and the default invocation delegates.

**How the brief reached the subagent was left open, and every run took the invitation differently** (ADR-0118). The briefs said only that writing the filled-in brief to a file and pointing the subagent at it was fine wherever it cost less than repeating it. Across a handful of runs of one Skill in one Harness, some passed a path under a freshly made temporary directory, one put the process id in the name, and four independently chose the same fixed path. Two of those four overlapped in time: one run wrote its brief, dispatched its subagent, and had the file deleted underneath it, and the subagent read a longer brief left behind by the other run, holding differently worded findings against an unrelated text. That run recovered because its subagent noticed the text was not the one it had been sent to repair, which is a property of that subagent and not of the Skill.

**The brief is handed to the subagent directly, as the whole of its instruction** (ADR-0118). The brief is text and a subagent's instruction is text, so the file was never buying anything the direct handover does not give. Handing it over removes the exposure rather than managing it: no path to make unique, no directory to create per round, and no window in which a second process can read, replace or delete what the round is answerable to. Length is not a reason to depart from it, because passing every element whole is what the loop already requires.

**It is stated once, in the brief itself** (ADR-0118). The delegating step says the correction is delegated from the brief filled in *and delivered* as that brief says, so there is one document to read and one rule in it. A second statement beside the first is how the improvisation started: two instructions where one was needed, and a run picking whichever it happened to weigh more. The rule belongs to the Collection rather than to the Skill it was observed in, because both Skills that spend a Correction Budget dispatch from a brief carrying the user's text under the same words.

**What the direct handover costs is a bound met at dispatch instead of in the repair** (ADR-0118). The brief is repeated in full into every round's instruction where a path would have been a line, so a Harness that bounds how much instruction a subagent may be given bounds how long a text this loop can correct. That is worth meeting honestly: a run that cannot pass the whole text is a run that cannot make the correction the loop describes, and discovering it at dispatch beats discovering it in a repair made against half a text.

**The loop then emptied a text and reported the emptiness as a finding** (ADR-0120). On the corpus fixture built out of the anti-slop patterns in concentration, all three exits let a longer loop reach a worse text than a shorter one: three rounds handed the correcting subagent 333 words, then 84, then 63, and delivered two sentences with three findings still standing, where one round on the same fixture delivered about fifty-five words that still carried the draft's claim about distributed teams working across time zones. Every round was correct in its own terms — an empty opening cut, a generic conclusion deleted, an unattributed claim removed rather than furnished with a source it does not have — and every round made relevant progress, so the no-progress exit never fired. The sharper evidence is inside the same run: a later round cut *respecting focus* from the body as a repetition of the heading, and the next review reported that the title names a claim the text under it never makes.

**Nothing was disobeyed, and that is the diagnosis** (ADR-0120). The brief already asked for the smallest change that removes a finding, and the catalogue already says to leave a strong sentence alone; both were honoured locally in each round. What was missing is anything holding *across* rounds — each round gets the whole text and the whole current findings with no memory by design, so no round can see that the text is a third of what it was two rounds ago, and no exit can see it either, because deleting the passage a finding names is progress by every test they apply.

**A repair removes what the finding names and leaves the claim the passage carries beside it** (ADR-0120). Where the defect is the whole of the passage — an opening that would sit in front of any text, a closing that restates what the reader has just read, a claim credited to nobody that the text cannot source — cutting it takes no claim with it, and that stays the repair the catalogue prescribes. Where the passage carries a fact, an observation or the point it exists to make beside the defect, the smallest change is the one that leaves that standing; and where the two cannot be separated, the passage is left and reported, exactly as a finding that cannot be repaired without inventing a fact already is. The asymmetry is the whole of the gap: the brief told a subagent not to invent and said nothing about not emptying, so the one failure it could not commit was the one it was asked to stop for.

**The fourth exit fires when a re-review raises a finding an earlier round's own repair created** (ADR-0120, ADR-0107). It is the single claim of the three-exit loop that changed; the fresh delegation, the verification by review, the budget as a ceiling and each of the three named exits are untouched. The condition is stated in the delegating step because that is where it can be read: the dispatching run holds every state the text has passed through and is the only party that does, the subagent being fresh by design and each review reading the text in front of it. Where it fires, the outstanding findings are carried forward as the other exits carry them, with the repair the new finding followed from named beside them, because that is what a person has to settle.

**A shrink threshold was the road not taken, and the fixture's own numbers refute it** (ADR-0120). The good result is about fifty-five words and the bad one about fifty, so no measure of the text tells them apart, and the same corpus supplies the counter-case from the other side: a 213-word fixture survived four rounds intact and came back slightly longer than it went in. What separates a repair from an emptying is which claim survived, not how much prose did, so both guards are written about the passage in front of the reader and neither counts anything. Neither adds a flag either, and both hold at every budget — the brief's rule is applied by a subagent that cannot see the budget at all, and a guard only a long loop could meet would leave the defect where it was found, since the run that behaved well was the short one.

**What the loop costs is paid twice for the isolation, and the judgements are judgements** (ADR-0107, ADR-0120). A fresh subagent reads the contract documents again each round and cannot learn from the attempt before it, so two rounds may repair the same finding two different ways, and re-reviewing after every correction means the review runs one more time than there were corrections. The no-progress stop is a judgement rather than a comparison, so a stop is occasionally taken one round early; the fourth exit is a harder judgement still, since a review can report a defect an earlier round created and a defect the draft always had in the same words. A correction round can also come back having repaired less than it could have. Every one of those costs falls on the side of leaving a text as it was, which is the side the defect was on.

## What no assertion can see

**The editorial Skills promise things no unit test can see** (ADR-0093). That a draft invents nothing the source did not carry, that a mechanical pass does not quietly improve a sentence, that a directory-targeted result steps past an occupant rather than over it. The seam where those promises are kept or broken is the Skill's real output in an actual supported Harness, and that seam has a model on the other side of it. Two provider families run the same Skills, and the point of running both is to find out where they differ.

**The corpus is built from the specification and not from an implementation** (ADR-0093). It could therefore exist before the Skills did, which is the only way it tests the contract rather than describing whatever was built, and it is the same property that makes it usable as a check on a later change. Where a run exposes a real defect, the defect becomes its own ticket against the Skill; the corpus and the criteria are not the place that absorbs it, because a criterion softened to accommodate an implementation has stopped testing anything. The mechanics section above records the one sanctioned exception and why it is one.

**It is fixture material and it is not a test suite** (ADR-0093). Nothing in it asserts an exact sentence and nothing claims a model writes perfectly. Each fixture says what the material is, how it reaches a Skill, and what a correct run must never do with it, so an evaluator uses a fixture without first reading the Skill that consumes it. What the suite under `tests/` checks is the corpus rather than the models — that every kind the specification names is covered, that every file is documented and every documented file exists, that the protocol states its rejections and defines its record fields — which is exactly why it can run on every commit.

**A criterion is semantic, blinded, and floored** (ADR-0093). It is answerable from the delivered artifact and the recorded side effects alone, against criteria fixed before the run, without knowing which model produced the output and without a reference text to compare against; several valid texts pass, and a criterion that would separate two correct drafts on wording is written wrongly. Five things fail regardless of how well the output reads — an unsupported fact, wrong locale behaviour, a substantive edit from a contract limited to mechanics, an unresolved mandatory finding, an incorrect side effect — and no criterion may be phrased so that one of them passes. Without that floor a semantic criterion decays into approval of anything fluent.

**The artifact-and-side-effects-only claim has one exception, and the resolver defect is why** (ADR-0093, ADR-0129). A mechanically correct artifact proves only that wider guidance did not visibly change that output, so an obligation that is otherwise unobservable — what a run loaded — is judged from the provider-native Harness trace instead. Both model-trigger branches are re-run, and each verdict names the scoped resolver call, the returned scope set, the shared mechanics contract, and the absence of any Language Resource file or other editorial guidance from the trace. Static tests hold the convergence and opaque-output contracts; the provider-native record holds what the model actually loaded.

**The families are isolated in both directions, as a rule about sessions rather than about tooling** (ADR-0093). A Codex session runs only GPT-family evaluations and never starts, controls or invokes a Claude Harness or model; a Claude session runs only Claude-family evaluations and never touches a Codex Harness or GPT model. Neither is a statement about which provider is trusted: both follow from wanting a comparison no cross-provider agent control could have contaminated, and from the plain operational fact that a session driving another vendor's harness is a session nobody can account for afterwards.

**The comparison is a read, not a run** (ADR-0093). Because the families cannot meet, the record is the only place they do, so the recording format is fixed in the protocol and materialised as a template: identity fields saying which model in which Harness against which corpus commit, and per-fixture fields saying what was typed, what came back, what the filesystem shows, and how each criterion was judged. Side effects are read from disk rather than from the Skill's account of itself, because a Skill reporting its own writes is the one witness the output contract exists to doubt. A re-run is a new record, in the same sense a run outcome is history.

**What this costs** (ADR-0093). Two runs are needed for every comparison, and the second may happen weeks after the first on material that must not have moved in between — hence the corpus commit in every record and the immutable URL rather than a live page. Evaluations are hand-run and unautomatable by design, so the corpus grows only when somebody adds to it deliberately. And a corpus written from the specification contains fixtures no Skill turns out to need, which is a gap worth keeping: a fixture nothing consumes is a question about the contract, not dead weight.
