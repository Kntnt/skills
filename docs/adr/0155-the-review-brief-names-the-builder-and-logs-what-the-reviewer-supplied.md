# The review brief names the builder and logs what the reviewer supplied

`ready-for-agent-check` asks one question — could somebody who has only this ticket build it — and the brief never said who that somebody is. Two of its seven criteria then measured against the reviewer's own ease: criterion 5 counted a fact as present where "a builder could obtain it from the repository in a minute", *What is not a finding* repeated the test as "a fact the repository would give up in a minute", and criterion 2 asked whether "a builder could find out which branch it is on" without asking a person. A reviewer answers all three by trying, and what it finds in a minute is what *it* finds in a minute. The reviewer is a subagent the Skill starts with no model of its own, so it runs on whatever a Harness gives a subagent by default, which is the Main Seat; the builder is routed under a `cost_first` objective in the `orchestrate/initial_build` Cohort (ADR-0147), under a Standing Policy whose shipped ceiling is that same Seat (ADR-0149), so it sits at or below the reviewer and the routing exists to put it below. The stronger the Seat the check runs from — the sensible choice, a check being cheap next to a wave that stops — the wider the gap the brief was silent about (issue #229).

**The brief names the builder, and the tests phrased as ease are rewritten against it.** The paragraph before the seven criteria states that the builder is a Seat no more capable than the reviewer and possibly several Rungs below it, with less deliberation, a smaller model, or both, and that the reviewer's own ease is therefore the measure of nothing in the brief. Criteria 2 and 5 and the sentence under *What is not a finding* now ask whether that builder could obtain the fact, or determine the branch, without knowing where to look — so a fact the reviewer found by knowing where to look is a fact the ticket has to locate. The phrase "in a minute" leaves the brief entirely, because a minute is the reviewer's minute and the brief has no way to say whose.

**Every inference the reviewer had to make is a finding.** Naming the builder fixes the three tests that named it wrongly; it does nothing about the rest of the read, where a capable reviewer resolves an ambiguity, infers an intent, or locates an unlocated fact without noticing that it did. The brief's rule that an uncertain verdict is a no does not catch that: a reviewer that filled a gap silently is not uncertain. So the brief has the reviewer log, as it reads and as it checks the repository, every intent it inferred, every choice it made between readings the ticket allows, and every fact it established that the ticket neither carries nor locates — at the moment each happens, a list reconstructed at the end being a list of what the reviewer still notices having done. Each entry is a Cost at minimum and a Stop where the described builder could not have made the same inference, and it quotes the sentence it hangs on like every other finding. The seven criteria stay seven and in their order; the log is what catches what they let a strong reviewer pass silently.

**The report keeps its two classes.** A logged inference is reported under Stops or Costs as any other finding is, and no third heading is added, because the maintainer is fixing a sentence in a ticket either way and a class that sorted findings by how the reviewer arrived at them would sort them by nothing the fix depends on. What changes is what the report carries: the gap the ticket has rather than the gap the reviewer happened not to have.

**Why not run the check from a weaker Seat instead.** The obvious alternative is to route the reviewer at the builder's own Seat and let the measurement be literal. It is worse. A weaker reviewer misses gaps as readily as it fails to fill them, and a miss is invisible — it leaves no trace in the report at all, where a strong reviewer's fill is at least visible once logged, and lands on the maintainer as a sentence to fix. The isolation the Skill already enforces is not an answer to this either: it removes the author's knowledge of what the ticket meant, which is a different leak from the reviewer's own capability, and capability leaks through any test phrased as the reviewer's own ease no matter how isolated the context is.

**What this costs.** The brief grew by a section and a paragraph, and it now asks the reviewer to keep a running log while it reads, which is work in a context that already reads a whole ticket and a repository. Reports get longer, and some of what they gain is a Cost on a sentence a maintainer will read and shrug at, an inference the builder would have made just as easily. That is the intended trade: the shrug is cheap and reading it costs a maintainer a moment, where the same inference unreported is a ticket that passed this check and stops a builder at three in the morning.
