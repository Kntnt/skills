# How work is routed, and what it leaves behind

This record settles routing and evidence in one place: why the decision which model does a piece of delegated work has exactly one owner, what a frozen decision binds and why it is frozen at all, the one launch destination that names no flag, what an attempt must have been judged by before it counts as evidence of anything, who may put that evidence into a ledger, and what a Skill owes once it writes into a Harness's own configuration. It is a consolidation — it supersedes the four records named below, and carries forward what those records were for, which is the reasoning. What the rules are is stated in [`docs/rules/collection.md`](../rules/collection.md), and, for the frontmatter key by which a Skill declares what it installed outside its own directory, in [`docs/rules/skills.md`](../rules/skills.md). The routing vocabulary itself — the adapter, its destinations, the attestation, the refusal reasons — is model-selector's own, stated in that Skill's [`references/model-routing.md`](../../skills/models/model-selector/references/model-routing.md) beside the shipped request schema the module validates against. This record argues rather than legislates, and where it needs a rule it cites the file that carries it rather than repeating it. A reader who wants today's law should be reading one of those and not this.

## The records folded into this one

- ADR-0083 — Model-selector owns exact frozen routing
- ADR-0089 — Routed work reports the evidence it never imports
- ADR-0090 — A Skill owns the Harness integrations it installs
- ADR-0104 — A control carried by inheritance completes the point

**The four are one chain and are best read as one.** The first gives the decision an owner and a shape; the second says what a run that used it may hand back, and under what conditions that hand-back is worth anything; the third closes the gap the second leaves, that ordinary unrouted work produces no evidence at all, and pays for it by writing outside the collection's own directories for the first time; the fourth is the only one written from field evidence rather than from design, and it is the record of the day the whole arrangement was found to be inert in the one Harness the maintainer works in. Everything below is reasoning a reader would otherwise have to reconstruct from evidence that no longer exists anywhere but in these records.

## Why the decision has one owner

**The recommendation a person reads and the decision a Skill launches from are two different artifacts, and the mistake would have been to make one out of the other** (ADR-0083). A recommendation is deliberately detailed, carries frontier neighbours, banners and an experiment brief, and may begin first-use setup. A caller launching unattended work needs something compact and exact. Letting each caller translate the report would have duplicated selection policy in every caller, concealed native controls the Harness does not actually support, and let unattended work drift as profiles, aliases, prices, evidence and Harness capabilities change underneath it.

**So there is one public route Interface beside human recommend, and it returns a decision that is one of three things** (ADR-0083). A selection carries an exact Harness-native launch instruction; an inheritance audits why no override was safe; a refusal carries a stable reason. Both surfaces share the same filtering, evidence classes, costs, uncertainty and escalation semantics, and recommend adds only what a person needs on top. One executable core, two presentations — the alternative being two implementations of one policy, which is the drift the ownership decision exists to prevent.

**The context is frozen because reuse must not silently pick up a changed world** (ADR-0083). A snapshot binds the profile revision, the evidence identity and vintage, the active Harness inventory, the main-seat identity, the verified native-control mappings, the commercial facts, the workload-capability facts and the override policy. Reusing it therefore excludes every later environmental change rather than quietly incorporating it. The public adapter derives the current context locally from the selected data directory and the active Harness before calling the pure module; the module freezes those controlled inputs and then performs no setup, no network access, no evaluation, no research and no persistent write. That split is what makes a decision reproducible: everything environmental happens before the freeze, and nothing after it can reach outside.

**A frozen Harness adapter is data rather than a channel label, and this is the load-bearing part** (ADR-0083). Each adapter declares the exact models, channel, Harness, surface, serving modes, native controls, tool sets, policies and flag mappings it can launch. The core accepts a point only when one adapter matches every field and translates every launch-relevant fact, and that adapter and those facts go into the point fingerprint. A label saying *this is Claude Code* would have been cheaper and would have let the module claim launches nobody could perform; making the adapter carry the translation is what makes *exact* mean something.

**The main seat is the authority ceiling and it owns verdicts** (ADR-0083). Execution points, explicit overrides included, cannot exceed its complete capability, and work that judges other work inherits the exact main seat and is never price-optimised — judging is authority rather than a decision that happened to come back inheriting. Unknown ceilings and unrepresentable inheritance are refusals rather than optimistic fallbacks, because a fallback here is a launch nobody authorised.

**Portable deliberation is narrow, exact, and makes no claim it cannot support** (ADR-0083). Five public values, with native names and numeric budgets kept inside verified mappings for an exact model, channel, surface and Harness; a missing mapping is unavailable and never approximated. The scale establishes adjacency — which rung is next to which — and deliberately makes no monotonic quality claim, so nothing may infer that a higher value is a better answer.

**Overrides are field-level and evidence stays honest, which are the same discipline seen twice** (ADR-0083). A supplied model locks the model alone and a supplied deliberation locks the deliberation alone; hard filters and the ceiling still apply, so an override narrows the search rather than escaping it. Measurement-based selection requires representative matched evidence, and where there is none the answer is unknown rather than a guess dressed as a measurement. Cheap-first escalation is admitted only for reversible, objectively checked work with an external signal, consumes an existing retry rather than inventing one, and ordinarily advances a single supported adjacent level on the same model.

**Selection stays multidimensional, and an undetermined frontier is reported rather than broken by fiat** (ADR-0083). Conservative quality and cash, rolling quota, weekly quota, allocated subscription cost and latency form a Pareto frontier; a unique non-dominated point wins, and several non-dominated points inherit with an audited reason unless the frozen profile explicitly prices every non-cash dimension. Picking one arbitrarily would produce a decision with a rationale nobody could reconstruct, which is the opposite of what the frozen decision is for.

**The shipped request schema is the runtime structural authority** (ADR-0083). The core applies the deep nested schema before interpreting a request or a snapshot, then applies only the cross-field invariants a schema cannot express — canonical snapshot identity, control-map equality, verified `max`. Malformed process input refuses as a machine-readable artifact refusal, while one invalid request inside a batch remains an ordered request-level refusal beside its valid peers, so a bad entry cannot take the batch down with it.

**What it costs is stated plainly** (ADR-0083). Callers must supply structured workload and Harness facts and retain a potentially detailed snapshot, and exact mappings make some configured points unavailable until the Harness can prove how to launch them. In return, unattended decisions are reproducible, auditable and executable without any dependent Skill owning model-selection policy.

## The destination that is not a flag

**A night of real work found the whole arrangement working exactly as designed and producing nothing** (ADR-0104). Twelve execution decisions were routed and every one of them inherited, each for the same reason: no selection control was available. The module admits a candidate only through a concrete adapter carrying a complete flag translation, and the Harness in play — Claude Code spawning through its Agent tool — selects a model family while exposing no deliberation control on a subagent at all. No honest adapter could exist, so every candidate was excluded as unreachable and the audit trail came back complete, honest and inert. Model selection was unavailable in exactly the Harness the maintainer works in, and had been since routing shipped.

**That framing hid a fact, and the fix was a vocabulary rather than a mechanism** (ADR-0104). On a Harness that applies the spawning session's own deliberation value to the subagent it starts, the control is not unknown: it is the frozen main seat's exact value, which the snapshot already carries and which every verdict already inherits. The point was complete all along; what was missing was a way to say so. So the adapter schema learned one destination that names no flag — a native control mapped to inheritance completes the translation and emits no launch argument — and the distinction from an absent mapping is the whole point. An absent mapping means nobody knows what the subagent will deliberate at, and goes on being refused; a carried one means somebody checked, and the answer is the seat's own value.

**The claim cannot be computed, so the schema refuses to accept it implicitly** (ADR-0104). Such a destination is valid only beside the declaring session's attestation that this Harness applies that value to subagents by default, verified against what the Harness actually does before the adapter is declared. No script knows which Harness invoked it — which is why this collection's Harness Capabilities are answered by the running agent rather than tested, as [`docs/rules/collection.md`](../rules/collection.md) states — and it is why the adapter is declared data rather than something the module derives. Requiring the attestation is the most a schema can do about a fact it cannot check.

**A carried control is not a dimension route selects over, and every consequence of that follows from the fingerprint** (ADR-0104). It resolves to the frozen main seat's exact portable and native value, so the point yields one variant rather than one per mapped level; only the model dimension of the ceiling can exclude it, a subagent inheriting the seat's deliberation being by definition at the seat's deliberation; and the fingerprint carries the effective seat value, so recorded evidence binds and accrues to the configuration inheritance actually launches rather than to a level nothing ran at. An explicit deliberation lock is refused rather than quietly satisfied, which is the field-lock rule applied where it bites. And bounded escalation moves only along a dimension the adapter can address: a carried control has no reachable adjacency, so on such a seam no escalation is emitted at all, rather than a rung being invented in the model dimension out of an ordering nobody has verified.

**The cold-start promise was settled in the same change, because this is where it first bites** (ADR-0104). The contract promises that reversible, objectively checked work begins at the weakest plausibly sufficient configuration and escalates only after verified failure; the code took that branch under a different guard, so a caller that declined unmeasured starts got inheritance for work whose failure its own checker catches, and the two surfaces disagreed about when a heuristic start happens. Reversible work with an external checker or a declared failure signal now takes the heuristic whatever the policy says, and the policy keeps deciding for everything else.

**Four roads were not taken, and the reasons are worth keeping** (ADR-0104). A genuine partial-selection concept — a point selected in one dimension and unknown in the other — was rejected because an unknown control cannot be fingerprinted, and evidence that cannot bind to a configuration is not evidence about anything: the ledger would fill with records of runs nobody could reproduce. Mapping an existing portable value onto the seam's observed behaviour was rejected as a calibration claim nobody has verified, saying that this Harness's inherited thinking is `high` asserting a correspondence no measurement supports. Moving selection out of the module and into the calling Skill was rejected because a second implementation of adapter reachability living in a caller is exactly the drift single ownership exists to prevent. And the Harness's other seam was named rather than built: pre-declared agent-type definitions do expose a per-agent effort control, behind a user-gated tool, and the honest road there is a real adapter with a real flag translation, addressable rather than carried — a larger piece of work with a consent step in the middle of it, and nothing here forecloses it.

**What it costs is a hole held open by one thing only** (ADR-0104). The attestation is a claim the module cannot check: a session that declares it without looking gets exact-looking decisions resting on a Harness fact nobody verified, and the fingerprint will then bind evidence to a configuration that never ran. What holds it is that the declaring session is the one place with standing to answer. Selection on such a seam is also one-dimensional in practice — the model moves, the deliberation does not — so the frontier a run explores is narrower than the vocabulary suggests and a failure has no cheap next rung to buy. And reversible checked work can no longer be held back from an unmeasured start by policy, which is the deliberate half of the cold-start change: the checker is what makes the start safe, and a caller that wants no unmeasured start has the choice of not calling.

## What makes an attempt evidence

**Routing had an owner and callers that used it, and both left the same thing unsaid: what happened next** (ADR-0089). A run selected an exact point, something judged the work it produced, and the only record of the pair was prose in a report nobody could import. Selection that cannot learn from its own results stays at the evidence class it started in, however many nights it is run.

**An observation adds one thing to a decision that already carries everything else** (ADR-0089). The decision is immutable and already holds the exact point, the native control, the fingerprint, the evidence class and the provenance. What the attempt adds is the outcome, and whatever the environment actually exposed about what the attempt cost.

**The bar for what counts as judged is set outside the attempt, and it is set high on purpose** (ADR-0089). The alternative on offer was a wider bar and more rows: take the builder's own report, take a subagent's confidence, keep an unchecked success as a weaker kind of measurement. That builds a frontier out of self-assessment, and a configuration that is bad at the work but confident about it then wins. The whole value of the ledger is that a number in it was checked by something that could have said no, so the qualifying kinds of check — and the exclusion of everything else — are the rule stated in [`docs/rules/collection.md`](../rules/collection.md).

**A route decision by itself is audit data and not an observation** (ADR-0089). Nothing is serialized for an attempt that was interrupted, that launched nothing because its decision refused, or that no verdict reached. An account of what a run decided is worth keeping and is kept, in the run's own account of its routing; the difference is what stops an unfinished night from reading later as a successful one.

**The workflow's own failures are not the model's** (ADR-0089). A mechanical hinder, an open product decision, a newly discovered dependency, a tracker failure and a merge collision each end an attempt without saying anything about the configuration that met them. They are retained as infrastructure errors and abstentions, they carry the condition that caused them, and they are excluded from the conservative success rate a frontier is built from. Counting them as failures would make the cheapest point look worst exactly where the environment was worst, which is the one inference the evidence must not support.

**Building the artifact by allow-list rather than by stripping is the entire safety argument** (ADR-0089). A denylist is a claim to have thought of everything, and it fails silently and late: the field nobody anticipated is in the file, and the file has already been written into somebody's scratch. Copying named fields fails in the safe direction instead — what nobody thought of is simply absent, and a caller adding a field gets nothing rather than a leak. The list of what is copied and what is therefore absent is in [`docs/rules/collection.md`](../rules/collection.md). Two consequences of the same discipline are worth naming: a value that reads as material rather than as an identity refuses its attempt instead of being trimmed into one, and a measurement the environment did not expose stays null, because an absence read as zero would make an unmeasured configuration the cheapest one on the frontier.

**Emission is the caller's act and import is the user's** (ADR-0089). The artifact is written into caller-owned scratch, its path is named in the report the caller was already giving, and nothing else happens — no ledger, no derived frontier, no profile, no repository file, no tracker state and no lifecycle hook is touched by a night's work. A person runs the import. Identical observations are idempotent at both seams, and an identity offered twice with different content is surfaced as a conflict that overwrites neither side, because two disagreeing accounts of one attempt are a question for a human rather than a race for the last write.

**One contract rather than two** (ADR-0089). Model-selector owns emission as well as import: the same module builds the observation, validates it with exactly the validation an explicit import applies, and appends it. A caller reaches that through the public Interface and reimplements no sanitization, validation or key derivation — a second normalized representation of routed work would have been a second thing to keep true, and the two would have disagreed first about the fields nobody looks at.

**What it costs** (ADR-0089). A caller must reach an explicit completion boundary for every attempt it wants recorded, and an attempt whose outcome nobody establishes is simply lost to the evidence. Runs on a Harness exposing no usage, cost or quota facts produce observations that are mostly empty and support little more than a success rate. In return, the evidence a later routing decision could be made from is produced by the work itself, in the shape the ledger already accepts, without any part of ordinary delegated work acquiring an undisclosed persistent side effect.

## Measuring the ordinary day, and the price of reaching outside

**The gap the previous record left is the ordinary case** (ADR-0090). A user works all day in a Harness and nothing is measured, because measuring is a thing they have to remember to do. Closing that means the Harness calling us rather than the user invoking us, which means writing into the Harness's own configuration — the first thing this collection installs that is neither a Skill file nor removed by deleting one.

**Capture is an opt-in of an Enabled Skill and never a consequence of enabling one** (ADR-0090). Select places files and nothing else, and that is the promise being kept: enabling a Skill installs a Skill. Persistent lifecycle integration and the handling of local session metadata are a second decision, made with the first opt-in, which states what is installed, what is retained, how it is cleaned up and how to stop it before anything reaches disk. A Skill that started capturing because it was Enabled would make every future Enable a question about what else it might turn on.

**The owner travels inside what is written, which is what makes removal surgical without anything having to be remembered** (ADR-0090). Every entry carries a stable ownership identity inside the command it runs, rather than beside it in a key the Harness's own schema does not define — a key the Harness does not know about is a key the Harness is free to drop. The disk is then read, our entries are found by their owner, and everybody else's survive untouched. Nothing keeps a private ledger of what was installed, so a hand-edited, externally repaired or half-removed Harness is a state the next run converges from rather than one nothing can account for, and install, repair, update and remove are all the same operation applied to whatever is actually there.

**The Manager asks the Skill to remove them while the Skill can still answer** (ADR-0090). Deleting a Skill's files is the one moment its integrations become unreachable — after it, nothing on the machine knows what was installed or where. So removal runs first, on the one seam every removal path already passes through, and it is the Skill's own script that runs, declared through the frontmatter key [`docs/rules/skills.md`](../rules/skills.md) states; the Manager says one word to it and learns no Harness's hook format in the process. A teardown that fails is reported per Skill and never raised, because external state this collection does not own must not hold up the removal of files it does own. Making the Skill Disabled is therefore sufficient to stop capture, and there is no second shutdown step to forget.

**Harness-specific mechanics belong to the Collection Library, and an unsupported Harness says so** (ADR-0090). Three Harnesses, three shapes: two owned hook tables and one plugin file named for its owner. That knowledge is not one Skill's private business. Which Harness is running is still the agent's to say and never a script's to sniff, and a Harness whose supported lifecycle cannot carry the contract reports an Unsatisfied Capability rather than being quietly skipped — a feature that believes it is installed where it is not is worse than one that knows it is not.

**Captured ordinary work is the same observation under the stratum it belongs to** (ADR-0090). Nothing routed it, so it is recorded as the inheritance that actually happened: the exact main seat the session ran on, fingerprinted from that seat, still reading as an inheritance so that no part of it reads as a choice somebody made, and with provenance naming the captured environment because there was no routing snapshot to freeze. It is a further stratum rather than a second representation, so the envelope, the validation, the refusals, the idempotency and the conflict rules answer for it unchanged.

**Nothing inside the work may grade the work, and here there is less to lean on** (ADR-0090). There is no independent verifier in an ordinary session, so an objective checker, a frozen rubric, a declared failure signal or the user's own confirmation establishes an outcome, and nothing else does. Unjudged work waits in bounded pending review for a human rather than becoming weak evidence, an abandoned session is reconciled into that same waiting state instead of into a result, and an infrastructure error keeps its own outcome so that a bad environment cannot make a configuration look bad.

**The hook path is fail-open, bounded and local, and the reason is behavioural rather than technical** (ADR-0090). It runs inside somebody's session, so it does local metadata I/O and nothing else — no network request, no model call, no test run, no repository-wide hashing, no transcript scan, no long-lived work — and every failure in it is swallowed. Capture that breaks a session is worse than no capture, because a user who has to weigh measurement against their Harness working will turn measurement off. Notifications are debounced and user-facing for the same reason in reverse: a measurement reminder placed where the model reads it would change the configuration being measured.

**What it costs** (ADR-0090). The collection now writes outside its own directories, and a machine whose Harness config was hand-edited between an install and a removal can be left holding an entry we no longer recognise — reported, but the user's to delete. Retention is a bound rather than a promise: pending captures past an age, a count or a size are removed oldest-first, so evidence nobody reviewed is evidence that eventually goes. And sessions in an unsupported Harness, or with no checker and no answer from the user, produce nothing at all. In return the evidence that should replace public priors is produced by the work itself, in the shape the ledger already accepts, with an off switch that is the same switch as the Skill's own.
