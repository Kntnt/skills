# Evidence reset takes a ratcheted Cohort's Standing Policy down with it

The maintainer's evidence ledger held forty-seven judged routed observations naming no Cohort, made permanently inert by ADR-0145's own Cohort-scoped comparability, and there was no way to remove them: `config reset` touches only `config.json`, `config policy reset` restores a Standing Policy and keeps its history, and a purge of captured data was a stated act nothing implemented. Issue #227 gives `config reset --evidence` to the maintainer as the way to throw inert measurement away and start clean. Doing that surfaces a question ADR-0149 and ADR-0150 answered once each, on the assumption that nothing else would ever answer it again: a Standing Policy override moves up only, and the one thing that moves it down is `config policy reset`.

**A Standing Policy override is measurement, not configuration.** ADR-0150 ratchets a Cohort's starting Rung only on verified failures a threshold trip counted from the ledger; the override the store then holds is a claim about what those failures showed, and the history beside it is the record of which runs showed it. Discarding the failures and leaving the ratchet in place would leave a Cohort started above its own cold start on a claim with nothing behind it — a Rung a threshold bought that the evidence no longer pays for. So `config reset --evidence` removes `standing-policy.json` and `standing-policy-history.jsonl` outright, exactly as it removes the ledger, the derived frontiers, and the quota store: not restored to the shipped default the way `reset` leaves them, but gone, the same as every other measurement this act discards.

**`config policy reset` is unchanged, and it is the narrower act.** It still restores one Cohort, or every overridden Cohort, to the shipped default while keeping the history that explains how each one got there — a user who wants to know why a Cohort started where it did before the reset can still read it. `config reset --evidence` is not a second way to ask that same narrow question; it is the wider act ADR-0159 gives the maintainer, and it happens to reach the Standing Policy store because that store is measurement like everything else the wider act removes.

**The price is a second downward path where ADR-0149 wanted exactly one.** ADR-0149 settled that a Standing Policy has one moving part — a threshold trip writes it, `config policy reset` is the only way to remove it — and ADR-0150 repeated that closing clause as settled. Both statements are now false: `config reset --evidence` is a second write that takes a Cohort's override down, and it does so without appending a row, because the history it would explain is leaving with it. Both records are corrected in place with a pointer to this one rather than rewritten, per this repository's own convention (ADR-0075) — narrows ADR-0149 and narrows ADR-0150.

**What this costs.** A user who runs `config reset --evidence` for the ledger's sake also loses every Cohort's learned starting point, whether or not they meant to touch routing policy at all; there is no way to keep the Standing Policy override while discarding the evidence that justified it, because keeping it would be exactly the claim-with-nothing-behind-it this record refuses. `config policy reset` and `config reset --evidence` are now two acts that can each leave a Cohort at its shipped default, which is one more thing a reader of this collection's routing behavior has to hold in mind than ADR-0149 promised. In return, the maintainer's answer to inert data — throw it away and start clean — is not narrowed by a Standing Policy quietly outliving the evidence it was built on, and every shipped statement that named `config policy reset` as the only way down now says what is actually true.
