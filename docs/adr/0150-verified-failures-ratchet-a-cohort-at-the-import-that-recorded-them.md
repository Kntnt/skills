# Verified failures ratchet a Cohort at the import that recorded them

ADR-0149 gave every workload Cohort a Standing Policy and one field anybody could store in it, and then left the only two writers a person and a reset. Nothing moved a Cohort up. The SeaTwirl Fast Track night made exactly that decision — builders from `high` to `xhigh` — in prose, mid-run, by asking the user, which is the one thing an unattended run is supposed not to need. Evidence flows in automatically at every verdict (ADR-0137) and the policy is frozen into every route (ADR-0149); the missing piece was the sentence that reads the first and writes the second (issue #217).

**The evaluation lives inside `record`, and there is only one `record`.** It runs once per Cohort the import touched, after the ledger append, in the Collection Library module that owns the append. Orchestrate's automatic import at a verdict and the user's own `record` verb reach it through the same call, because both change the same ledger and a threshold that tripped for one and not the other would be two thresholds. Nothing under either Skill's `scripts/` is imported to get there: the ledger module loads the Library's policy store from beside itself, which is the same rule ADR-0149 wrote the store into the Library for.

**A row is judged on the ladder its own run froze.** Every observation now carries `provenance.standing_policy` — the Cohort, its `policy_revision`, and the resolved `starting_rung`, `current_rung`, `floor`, `ceiling` and `next_rung_up` — copied by the emission allow-list out of the audit block ADR-0149 already put on every execution decision. The evaluation reads that block and never the current store, so a policy that has moved since cannot reinterpret an attempt made under the one before it, and a movement is always to a Rung some frozen decision actually named as the next one. A verdict inherits by authority rather than by policy and carries no block, so it teaches the policy nothing.

**Eligibility is what makes `N of M` mean anything, and a pass is as eligible as a failure.** A row enters the window when it names the Cohort, is decisive, was judged by an independent verifier, an objective checker, or a declared failure signal, carries no exploration tag, carries the Cohort's current revision, ran on exactly its own carried `starting_rung`, and completed inside the current epoch. The window is the last M such rows ordered by `completed_at` ascending and then by `run_key`, and it need not be full — a Cohort with three eligible rows is judged on three. The policy moves when at least N of them failed. Under the shipped 2 of 4, `fail, pass, pass, pass, fail` does not trip, because the window holds one failure; an earlier draft that counted only failures made M inert and was the reason this rule is written out here rather than left to the code.

**The Rung clause is the one that stops a retry from voting twice.** A row counts only where the Rung it ran on equals the start its own decision resolved, so the escalated retry that follows a verified failure is not counted against the start it escalated from. Without it a cheap-first chain of one failure and one escalated failure reads as two failures at the start, and every Cohort ratchets on its first bad night.

**Every movement begins an epoch, and evidence from before one has already been answered.** A threshold trip and a reset alike stamp the Cohort with their `effective_at`, and only rows completing after it are eligible. That is what keeps the same historical failures from escalating a Cohort twice in a row, and what keeps them from immediately undoing a reset a person just asked for. The triggering run keys ride along in the history row's cause, with the `source_run_identity` of the run that closed the window, so the movement says what it was made of.

**Staleness is compared on the revision alone.** A Cohort's revision changes whenever its Rung changes, so equal revisions imply an equal stored start, and the symbolic shipped default never has to be compared with a resolved concrete Rung. An in-flight run frozen before another run's movement therefore keeps its evidence in the ledger and loses only its authority over the policy, which is reported as `stale_policy_context` rather than silently dropped.

**Up only, and the ceiling is a fact the frozen decision already carried.** Nothing is appended when the triggering failure's `next_rung_up` is `null`, which is what ADR-0146's adjacency returns at the top of a Cohort's resolved ladder; that case reports `standing_policy_ceiling_reached` and the evidence still lands. There is no automatic move down. `config policy reset` is the only way back and appends its own row, exactly as ADR-0149 settled.

The claim that `config policy reset` is the only way back is narrowed by ADR-0159, which gives `config reset --evidence` a second, wider downward write that discards the ratchet's own evidence together with the store — a Cohort held up by failures that no longer exist being a claim with nothing behind it.

**The run reports what it moved, from its own import rather than from the store.** `record` answers with a per-Cohort outcome — `moved` with the appended row, `standing_policy_ceiling_reached`, `stale_policy_context`, or `below_threshold`, each carrying the failures, the rows in its window, and the threshold behind them — Orchestrate retains that beside the attempt that produced it, and the report renders one line per moved Cohort with the reset command. Selecting the history rows by timestamp, or by the run identity that caused them, was the earlier design and was dropped: the import is idempotent, so what a run moved is a fact the run already holds, and a report that re-reads a shared store to find out what it did can be made to disagree with itself by a second session writing between two renderings.

**What this costs.** A Cohort now escalates without anyone approving it, and the ticket that asked for this asked for exactly that; the safeguard is that every step is paid for by externally verified failures at the Cohort's own start and that one command undoes it. A Cohort whose work is not homogeneous averages its lesson across everything in it and ratchets on failures that another kind of work in the same Cohort caused. The window is per Cohort and not per benchmark or stage, so two unrelated checks inside one Cohort share a threshold. And a threshold trip changes the store while a concurrent run is frozen against the old one, which the revision check turns into a reported `stale_policy_context` rather than a silent second escalation — correct, and still a run whose evidence bought nothing. In return, the escalation the maintainer made by hand in the middle of a night is now made by the policy, reported, and reversible in one line.
