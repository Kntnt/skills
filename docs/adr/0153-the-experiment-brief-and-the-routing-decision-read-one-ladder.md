# The experiment brief and the routing decision read one ladder

`recommend` answers a low-confidence decision with a frozen experiment brief: the exact configurations a human is told to go and measure. The brief froze those points by its own private adjacency — one position up the portable scale inside the launch point's own mapping, admitted on a main-seat ceiling and a launchable adapter and nothing else. The routing decision beside it, in the same module, resolves its next escalation through the pool the request actually reaches and the shared Rung resolver. Two readings of one ladder gave one request two answers: under a deliberation lock the decision emitted no escalation at all while the brief named a point at the level the lock forbids, and on a Harness carrying the deliberation control the decision climbed into the next model while the brief, whose private adjacency has no second dimension, named nothing and still instructed the reader to run "its permitted adjacent escalation" (issue #221).

**The brief resolves its comparable point through the same bounded pool and the same Rung resolver the routing decision uses.** A brief is a promise that the points it lists can be launched, and the only construction in this module that knows what a request can launch is the candidate pool as the Cohort's Standing Policy left it — the hard filters, the explicit locks and the variant exclusions applied by the pool, then the resolved inclusive floor and ceiling applied over that (ADR-0149). The step between two listed points is `_adjacent_rung` on that same pool, which is the two-dimensional ladder the escalation and the Exploration Attempt already climb (ADR-0146). Both of the brief's paths read it: the inherited path keeps its measured frontier first and its cap, and only which pool it appends from changes.

**A request that pins the deliberation gets a brief of one point, by the record rather than by the pool.** A lock is not a Rung, and the pool does not encode that: with the deliberation pinned to the weakest level, the bounded pool still holds every other model at that level and the shared resolver returns one of them — a point inside the pool, which no reachability check can object to. What forbids it is the decision ADR-0146 already made, so the brief refuses under the lock exactly where the routing escalation refuses, before adjacency is asked for at all. This is the one place the two readings are kept in agreement by a guard rather than by a shared call, and it is written that way in both.

**Reaching no comparable point removes the comparison, not the section.** Where the lock above holds, or the ladder is exhausted, or the pool offers no further Rung, the section stays, lists the one point the request can launch, and both of its plans say plainly that there is nothing to compare it against. Measuring that single point is exactly what a heuristic starting point needs — it converts a guess into evidence, and `record` accepts the resulting observation as it accepts any other — so a section that vanished would leave a reader with a low-confidence banner and no route to measurement at all. The run bound keeps its derivation, one run per listed point and a second where the caller owns a retry: it authorises runs and has never claimed how many points there are.

**What this costs.** The brief re-derives the bounded pool rather than receiving it, so the pool is built twice for a recommended request — once for the decision and once for its brief — and a future change to how the Standing Policy narrows must land in one function that two callers reach rather than in a value one caller threads to the other. The alternative, widening the fingerprint function's parameters to take the pool the decision already resolved, buys one construction at the price of a seam whose correctness depends on every caller passing the right pool; the shared derivation cannot be passed the wrong one. The uncertainty summary beside the brief keeps its own unbounded read deliberately: it answers how well measured the configurations a request reaches are, which is a question about the hard-filtered ladder rather than about what a human should go and run.
