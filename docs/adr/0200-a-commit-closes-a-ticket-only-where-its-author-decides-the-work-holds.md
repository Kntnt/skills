# A commit closes a ticket only where its author decides the work holds

On 2026-09-20 a push to `main` in `Kntnt/briefsmith` closed issue #13. The branch carried three commits for that ticket, the last of them trailing `Closes #13`, and GitHub acted on the trailer as it is designed to. The work had been failed by three independent verifiers in a row; Orchestrate had recorded the outcome as `failed` and had deliberately left the ticket open, its `record --outcome=failed` answering `"closed": false`. The ticket's timeline shows it closed by commit `d221733` at 06:50 and reopened by hand at 07:14. That commit has a single parent directly on `main`, so the run had `worktrees` false: the builder commits straight onto the run's branch, a failing verdict leaves those commits where they stand, and the trailer closes the ticket at the next push. With working trees, failed work is never integrated, so the hole is only open in that mode — but the claim the trailer makes is unfounded in both.

The trailer was not wrong about GitHub. It was indiscriminate: nothing between the builder and the tracker asked whether the work held. Orchestrate's own architecture already says it should have. A builder never decides its own work here — a separate verifier does, and the engine's `record --outcome=done` is the single thing that closes a finished ticket, in either mode, marking it `orchestrated` as it goes. The engine states the rule in the comment it files at `record`: there is no path from a builder's own report to a closed ticket. The `Closes` trailer was precisely that path, running beside the sanctioned one and answerable to nothing.

So the rule is about authority rather than about pushing: a commit may claim to close a ticket only where whoever writes the commit is also whoever decides that the work holds. Where somebody else decides it, the commit refers to the ticket and closes nothing, and the closing stays with whoever records the outcome. Orchestrate's briefs therefore instruct `Refs #<number>` and forbid GitHub's closing keywords outright — in every brief under the Skill's `references/` that grants a subagent the right to commit, not only the one that carried the trailer, because a builder handed a brief that says nothing writes `Closes` out of habit. Nothing in the engine's closing path changes, and nothing is lost by the trailer's removal: the engine already closes every passing ticket in both modes.

Work taken outside Orchestrate is untouched. An agent or a person who picks up a ticket directly is both the author of the commit and the judge of the work; there is one authority there, and `Closes #<number>` remains the ordinary and wanted way to close. That is this repository's third option from the ticket — the trailer stays for hand work and leaves Orchestrate's briefs — and its stated cost, two conventions in one repository, is answered rather than accepted: it is one rule, not two. The reference follows the authority, which is exactly what makes the history legible. `Refs` says somebody else decided; `Closes` says the author did. Reconciliation depends on that legibility: `completion_candidates` in `skills/code/orchestrate/scripts/run.py` finds the commit that finished a parked ticket by hand through its closing reference, and hand work is the case where the reference is still correct. `CLOSING_REFERENCE` therefore stands unchanged.

Two directions were rejected. The first is a tracker check in `/push`: read every ticket the pushed commits would close and refuse, or warn, where the ticket is not settled. It costs push a network call and a tracker dependency it has never had, in order to guard a claim that should not be made — and once the briefs stop making it, the check has nothing left to catch. The second is the ticket's first direction read literally: `Refs` everywhere, hand work included. It costs work done outside Orchestrate the automation it wants, with nothing put in its place, and it misdescribes that case, where the author really is the judge.

Nothing in `/push` changes. The fault lay in what a commit was allowed to say, not in the act of carrying it to the default branch. `/push` says nothing about ticket references today and goes on saying nothing; silence does not contradict the rule. The rule's home is a new module, `docs/rules/commits.md`, because it binds everybody who commits in this repository rather than one Skill's own body — which is what `docs/rules/docs.md` says a subject none of the existing modules covers becomes. The suite refuses the regression from the direction it actually arrived from: a check over the reference directory itself, rather than over a list of the briefs there are today, so a brief written later is held to the rule from the moment it exists.
