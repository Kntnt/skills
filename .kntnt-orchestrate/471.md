- `/brief` now follows the maintainer's rewritten template of twelve questions, *Questions to answer before you write*, and treats a brief as a direction rather than a finished dossier. The template separates the sender's message (question 3), which may stay unsaid, from the text's conclusion (question 9), which carries it and is what the structure lands in; a brief that made them one thing got either a conclusion stating the message outright or a message the structure never reached. Question 1 now gathers the working title, genre, channel, the source language and how other versions are produced, a provisional length and the search phrase; question 7 asks for the whole text in one sentence, in a form the template states for ABT and PAC with their sources named, and a sketch step by step, with an ending that ties back to the hook; question 11 holds RIV and a written chain check; question 12 lists the sources already known and what still has to be found out. The per-question quality criteria are gone, and so are the `[WEAK: …]` marker and the two follow-ups per point: an interview asks at most one follow-up per answer, only where the angle, the message or the conclusion leaves the direction unclear, and a common mistake the template names is pointed out once, as a suggestion. No brief has to be substantiated; that requirement binds the finished text. The Skill says a brief is enough to write once its direction is settled and questions 1–11 have answers, open research questions or not. The `kntnt` map's `language` is question 1's source language, the genre's own form leaves the technique out, and free structure is `none`. Nothing that describes the brief names a question count any more, Redline's brief review included (#471).

## A brief is a direction, and follows the template of twelve questions — #471

The Claude-family evaluation of [#471](https://github.com/Kntnt/skills/issues/471), which rewrote the writing-brief template to the maintainer's twelve questions and made `/brief` stop guarding a brief as a finished dossier. Read it with [`../editorial-471/plan.md`](../editorial-471/plan.md) and [`../editorial-471/results.md`](../editorial-471/results.md). There is no pre-change arm, as the template ships whatever the measurement shows. Every run was a top-level session made with the trace runner, extended here to stage Brief and to continue an interview turn by turn.

- [`brief-claude-2026-09-30-471.md`](brief-claude-2026-09-30-471.md) — eight `Brief` runs in Claude Code 2.1.285 on `claude-opus-5-5` at high deliberation, two blind judges each: a Swedish and an English interview of fourteen turns, a draft from notes with no sources, a review of a brief written to the new template and one written to the old thirteen-question template, and a revise round of the three runs that missed. On the first candidate both interviews asked again about a chain-check inconsistency after the one follow-up was spent, and the draft left a condition of its own unmarked. After the revised wording every criterion was met on both judges, and every run's statement on whether the brief was enough to write agreed with the judges' own reading.
- [`write-claude-2026-09-30-471.md`](write-claude-2026-09-30-471.md) — three `Write` runs in Claude Code 2.1.285 on `claude-opus-5-5` at high deliberation, each given a brief delivered by one of the three modes, two blind judges each. Every run took the genre, technique and language from the brief's map and wrote in question 1's language. The draft from the Swedish interview's brief delivered two source-comparison findings disclosed rather than repaired, which is recorded under #376.
