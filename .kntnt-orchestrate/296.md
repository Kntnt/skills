### Fixed

- The judge is bought as the review work it is, reads the whole unit, and says why it failed. Asking the engine for `converse` at high stakes had priced the call honestly and then let the easiest kind in the vocabulary set the bar, so the cheapest model on the machine cleared it: on the maintainer's own store that judge graded twelve `analyze` units of Opus 5 at `xhigh` at 0.24 on average and a build the verifier had passed at 1.0 at 0.18, and those numbers entered the hierarchy above the shipped priors and were carried into other kinds. Grading is `review` — judging work somebody else finished against a stated standard — and that is now what the engine is asked for, at high stakes as before, the bar deciding who is strong enough rather than the kind deciding who is cheap. The pricing was never the question: one grading is one bounded exchange of a few thousand tokens on the channel the profile names, whichever kind it is asked as. The unit reaches the judge whole, capture keeping four thousand characters of an instruction and twelve thousand of a result where it kept eight hundred of each — a delegation brief and its report, rather than the opening paragraph of both — and both still living in `pending.jsonl` only until the unit is graded. The grader asks as what it is with `--harness=process`, a script being unable to spawn a subagent, so it is answered with a command it can start rather than a subagent definition it cannot name, and asks with `--read-only`, which is what puts `-s read-only` on the Codex bridge and an empty `--tools` on the Claude one; every other caller keeps its tools and its writable workspace. A call that produced no verdict now leaves the word for which of four things happened — `no-judge`, `timed-out`, `exited-nonzero`, `unparsable` — on the pending row, and `status` counts the queue by it, so a machine where every call fails the same way says which way instead of reporting units patiently waiting. The per-pass budget, the daily cap and the three-attempt rule are unchanged.
