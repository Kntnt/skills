### Fixed
- The recorded cost of a session-end invocation, kept in the comment above `HARNESS_TIMEOUT_CEILINGS` for whoever next raises the unattended refresh's budget, now covers what has actually been observed and says what kind of figure it is. It read as 1.1 to 1.7 seconds across repeated runs, with no sample stated, so it invited being read as a bound — and an independent re-measurement of five whole session-end invocations, launcher included, every source forced due and fetched over the real network, saw one run at 1.98 seconds, above that upper figure. Nothing about the decision changes, the three-second clamp Codex honours being a second clear of even the slowest run, but the number the next author sizes a budget against was narrower than two independent sets of runs had seen. The comment now records the spread across all ten runs — 1.22 to 1.98 seconds, timed in two sets of five by two sessions on one machine, under the conditions those runs ran in — and says plainly that this is an observed spread rather than a bound, so a budget sized against it is sized against variance rather than against the quick end of it. The timeouts themselves are untouched: `HARNESS_TIMEOUT_CEILINGS` is unchanged and every installed entry carries the timeout it carried before (issue #280).
