### Added

- `/model-selector status` says when the judge is at its daily cap and how long the oldest Unit of Work has waited to be graded. `grade.py` buys at most fifty judgings in a rolling twenty-four hours and leaves everything beyond that waiting in the pending store, and a queue that sits at its cap every day falls further behind every day. The Status section counted the waiting Units and grouped their failures, but it never said the judge was capped or how long the queue had stood: on 2026-09-11 the grader's last pass reported `capped: true` and `graded: 0` with 57 Units waiting, the oldest finished the previous morning, and nothing the user read said so. `capture.py status` now reports `judge_capped`, `judged_in_window`, `judge_frees_at`, `oldest_waiting_at` and `now`, the instant they were computed at. The cap is worked out from the measurement store when `status` runs, by the same test the grader applies. It is not read from `grader.json`, whose `capped` is always false because every session end runs the free half. The cap frees a day after the (count − limit + 1)-th oldest judge-graded row in the window rather than a day after the oldest, because a pass that starts one short of the limit may still buy twenty. That arithmetic lives once, in `grade.judge_cap`, and the grader reads it as well. The Status section says a capped judge is capped, gives when the cap frees, says judging resumes at the next session end after that, and gives the oldest Unit's wait as an age. `help.md`, `help/status.md` and `references/measurement.md` say so too. Three statements that said a capped pass stops entirely are corrected, because the free half of the pass still runs: `references/measurement.md`, the comment on `DAILY_JUDGE_LIMIT`, and the name of the test that pins the cap.
