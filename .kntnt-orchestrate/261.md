### Changed

- The Invocation Envelope is stated once, in the Collection Library, and every Skill and every manpage points at it. Sixteen bodies carried an `## Invocation Envelope` section and a `## Help` section between the Skill's own description and its grammar, each of them argued in the same words why a flag with no work to do is refused rather than ignored, and forty-seven manpages carried the same 3.3 KB `## INVOCATION ENVELOPE` section byte for byte — one contract in as many editable places as the collection has Skills and pages, and a third of the smallest bodies. It now lives at `skills/kntnt/library/references/invocation-envelope.md`: the reserved separator and its quoted and attached forms, what a Contextual Instruction may settle and what it may not, both refusals with the narrowing that guidance a documented precedence has settled against is suppressed rather than refused, the page a refusal addresses and the `--help` route it closes with, selective propagation to a nested Skill, and the seven worked split cases. Each body replaces its two sections with one `## Invocation` that points at that file and names this Skill's own help routes — which pages exist being the one part of the contract a shared reference cannot know — and each `## Arguments` refuses an invalid form as the reference says, adding only which page a refusal addresses where the Skill has several and what the Skill leaves undone when it stops. Each manpage's section becomes a pointer that names the separator a reader meets in that page's own `SYNOPSIS` forms and then gives the contract's plain path, a manpage being printed to a person who has no `$LIBRARY`; `DIAGNOSTICS` still documents that command's own refusal in that command's terms. Behaviour is identical: the same separator is honoured, the same invocations are refused with the same outcome, and help routes where it routed before. One promise is withdrawn rather than left to be discovered — a Skill installed without the Manager no longer reads the contract from its own manpage but stops with `/kntnt update`, `tldr` and `brief` now reaching the Library for it like every other Skill, which is the price of the contract being written down once. The coding standard carries the new shape and, with it, the rule that a flag's presence follows its function: there is no collection-wide flag set every Skill is expected to offer, and a Skill carrying no such flag documents no exception for it, which is why `ready-for-agent-check` no longer explains the absence of `--yes` (ADR-0059, ADR-0076, ADR-0078, ADR-0122).
