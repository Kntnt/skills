### Added
- `/mirror` finds pages through sitemaps and feeds as well as links. Before the crawl goes on from the start page it reads every sitemap the `Sitemap:` lines of the host's `robots.txt` name, under `--ignore-robots` too, and tries `/sitemap.xml`, `/sitemap_index.xml` and `/wp-sitemap.xml` at the host's root on every run; indexes are followed recursively and read once each, gzip-compressed sitemaps are read, and every `loc` is a candidate. Feeds named by a page's `link rel="alternate"` of type `application/rss+xml` or `application/atom+xml`, and `/feed/` at the host's root and under the root prefix, are read once each, and every item's link is a candidate; a feed `alternate` is no longer taken as a page. Candidates are treated exactly as links — normalised, held to the scope, `robots.txt` and `--max-pages` at admission, recorded as `out-of-scope` otherwise — with source `sitemap` or `feed` and the document that named them as their discovering URL. Sitemaps and feeds, on whatever host, are fetched with the run's identity and headers, obey `robots.txt`, get a manifest row of kind `sitemap` or `feed`, and are never saved in the tree; a well-known location that answers `404` or `410` is `missing` and fails nothing. `--no-sitemap` and `--no-feeds` turn each source off, `--no-links` now leaves sitemaps and feeds on, and the report counts the sitemaps and feeds read and the candidates each source contributed (issue #318).
