- `/redline` no longer reads an opinion's reference to a pending decision as the author's forecast of its outcome. Reviewing the clean debate article in the evaluation corpus, it read the standfirst's *pröva båda bokningsvägarna ett halvår till innan den ena stängs* as the author conceding that a booking route closes. The headline and the lead make that closure the decision the author asks to postpone, but it deleted the clause or hedged it into *innan det avgörs om den ena ska stängas*. The opinion genre's review half now says what such a reference takes for granted: that the change it names is on the table, which the text says. It says that the reference is not the text stating the outcome as settled, and that it needs no rewording to say whether the change will come. A passage that says the change is already decided, or will come whatever the step shows, while another passage leaves it open is still a finding. No word and no part of the text is exempted. Measured on `claude-opus-5-5` at high deliberation. Three of six runs changed that clause before the change. A first wording, which said such a passage neither forecasts nor concedes the outcome, still changed it in two of ten runs, which cited that wording as the requirement the clause failed. The wording that ships changed it in none of six. A variant stating the closure as settled was caught in every run before the change and with the first wording. It was not run against the wording that ships. The controls held. One run added a clause naming the telephone route to the lead, and both judges failed it; that is filed as #492. A lead sentence removed from the clean article in one run, on instructions the change does not touch, is filed as #486 (#479).
