- `/redline` now reviews a text against a brief written to the new template of twelve questions, and treats the brief as a direction rather than a finished dossier. The sender's message and the text's conclusion are two requirements: the message is fulfilled when the text carries it, by saying it or by where it leads the reader, and falls short only when the text leads elsewhere or contradicts it, and a repair never states it outright; the conclusion is judged by whether the text lands in it. The structure is judged by whether the text realises the brief's one sentence for the whole text, resolves the hook and lands in the conclusion, not section by section against the sketch, and an indirect call to action by what the text leaves the reader with. Open research questions are named as open and an unsupported claim in the sketch is never demanded of the text, while a claim in the text the material does not support is still reported and left for the writer. The report goes question by question under the template's number and heading word for word, and gives the status a text arrived with beside the delivered one where a correction changed it. A brief written to the earlier template is mapped onto the new one: its *message* answer becomes the conclusion, and the sender's message counts as unanswered unless the brief keeps the two apart (#472).
