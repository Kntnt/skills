### Fixed

- Every surface naming what `/kntnt select --on` and `--off` take now names it by one word, and it is the word that is true of them. Both flags take a Catalog entry: the engine resolves the name against the Skills and the Features together, and the manpages have documented that all along, writing the metavariable *ENTRY* and glossing it as a Skill or a Feature. The Manager's `argument-hint`, its `select` steps, the engine's own usage line and the README's command table each named a Skill instead, so a harness advertised `--on=<skill>` for a flag that accepts a Feature, and a reader who met the same option under two names had to work out whether it was one option. The lowercase surfaces now write `--on=<entry>` and `--off=<entry>` — the angle-bracket form every `argument-hint` uses and the one `docs/rules/collection.md` had already settled on — the engine prints `ENTRY` in its usage line, and the prose around them says entry wherever the sentence is about what the flags name: an entry nobody named keeps the state it had, a named entry already on disk and deviating is what the overwrite question is about, and `--yes` alone Enables no entry that was not Enabled already. What stays about Skills is what only a Skill has, and it now says so in the same breath — a named Skill still brings its Dependencies along, a Feature having none. Two messages a user can meet change with it: a name in neither group is refused as an `unknown entry` rather than an unknown skill, and giving both answer forms at once is refused with *entry names are the whole answer*. The manpages are untouched, having been right, and the suite now holds the three prose surfaces and the engine to the word the manpages use rather than to a second word written beside them.
