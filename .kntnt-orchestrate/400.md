Under `## [Unreleased]`, in `### Fixed`:

- `/redline` now reports a headline, a standfirst or a subheading it changed with the defect that licensed the change, so that a reader of its account can tell a repaired part from a rewritten one. Replies had replaced a column's headline and a case study's two subheadings on sound findings without naming what was wrong with them, and described a headline that lost its place name as *shorter*. Step 6 now records a finding against one of those parts with the defect in it, and step 11 reports every such part the delivered text changed with that finding. Both `/redline` and `/unslop` now report a changed claim by what it now asserts rather than by how far its wording moved, and a headline, a standfirst and a subheading carry claims like any other passage. Where a repair takes a limit, a connective or a bounding clause out of the text, they now say what a reader of that text no longer has, not only which passage went. Both correction briefs and both help pages say the same, and `headlines.review.md` and `base.review.md` agree. Measured in Claude Code on `claude-opus-5-5` over the four #362 drafts and the ten *Redline controls*, the in-session pre-change arm did not reproduce the defect where the ticket measured it, so Thomas's ruling ships on its own. None of the four #392 rows reproduced its removal in three runs. Two remaining misses are filed as #436 and #437 (#400).
