- The press release now has the shape of the maintainer's instruction for one. Its headline is its level-1 heading, states the news on one line and is at most 70 characters, spaces included, written as the headline guidance says, which the genre now links. Its summary is the first paragraph after the headline, names the sending organisation, restates the headline's news in its own words and is at most 60 words. A first quotation follows the summary and comments on the news; the body is one or two short paragraphs a journalist can lift; a second quotation after the body carries one of the sender's most important messages and no new fact. Then come the background, the links and attachments, a contact with name, title, telephone and email as far as the material gives them, and a neutral description of the organisation. A publication time stands above the headline, and no notes block stands below the release. A quotation is still written only where the material has one: with one, it stands after the summary, and with none, the gap is reported. The review half diagnoses each new requirement. The two limits are counted ones, so the Library's measuring script now takes `--genre=pressrelease` and measures them alone, and `/write`, `/redline` and Redline's correction agent run it as they do for the four article genres. A Claude-family evaluation of ten runs on synthetic material, with two blind judges each, met every criterion after one revise round, which added the rule against a notes block. (#473)
