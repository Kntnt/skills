- The PAC technique now says what each of its three parts owes the reader, and no longer only that the three are there and hang together. The premise raises a real question a thinking reader could have and shows why its answer matters; a topic, or a question set up only to be knocked down, is not one, while facts from which the question is evident still make a valid descriptive premise. The direction shows early, and the sharpest statement may wait for the conclusion, so a report's opening answer and an article's standfirst stay valid. The analysis is usually most of the text and the premise often a paragraph, with no ratio prescribed. Each step of the analysis advances the reasoning rather than listing more of the same point or recounting facts in place of reading them, and a reader can see the facts, the interpretation and what binds them. A long analysis may divide into sub-questions that do not overlap and together cover the question. The conclusion answers the premise's question, brings together rather than repeats, keeps the limits the analysis found and introduces no new support, replacing a sentence that could be read as its own opposite. `pac.review.md` gains a diagnosis for each of these but the proportion, which sets nothing to diagnose, and says that a missing link between facts and claim is reported to the author and never written into the text. No falsifiable thesis, invented objection, fixed section pattern or ratio comes back (#470).
