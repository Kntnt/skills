### Changed

- `/tldr` now addresses its standing mode through a command path and accepts no free text at all: `/tldr on`, `/tldr off`, and `/tldr status` stand where `--on`, `--off`, and `--status` were, and the free-form tail that made those flags necessary is gone. It was the only Skill in the collection whose formal grammar accepted free text — anything after the name that was not `--`-prefixed was an instruction in any language, obeyed as written — which is precisely why its mode could not use the bare words every other closed vocabulary here uses: `on` would have shadowed a legitimate instruction, and `/tldr on the security part` had no reading that was both strict and useful. That premise moved when ADR-0078 gave the whole collection a reserved separator with a Contextual Instruction behind it and named this very case among its considered options. `/tldr -- bara säkerhetsdelen` already did everything the tail did, through the channel every other Skill uses for it, so the tail was a second unseparated channel for one thing — and it is the second channel that makes the first ambiguous. Widening the range, naming a language, narrowing the subject, and constraining the output all arrive that way now: the step that settles the range and the step that writes the replacement answer read the instruction, and guidance that would widen the Skill's responsibility takes the Envelope's context refusal rather than a syntax refusal. The flag spellings go rather than becoming aliases, two spellings for one form being the ambiguity this removes, so `/tldr bara säkerhetsdelen` stops working and becomes `/tldr -- bara säkerhetsdelen`; that is the cost, and it is stated rather than discovered. `--user` and `--yes` keep their spellings and their meanings and sit after the command path, where the collection's invocation order puts them. Each path is addressable as well: `on`, `off`, and `status` each ship a manpage under the Skill's `help/` with its own `--help` and `-h` route, the root page gains the `COMMANDS` section a command with subcommands carries and loses `POSITIONAL ARGUMENTS`, and an invalid form is now refused with the synopsis of the most specific recognized page rather than the whole Skill's. Nothing the Skill does changes: the perspective, the range rules, the two scopes, the managed block, the staleness report, and the confirmation gate are all as they were. ADR-0103 is the record; it withdraws ADR-0048's closing argument for flags over bare words and amends ADR-0080's claim that it left that grammar intact (issue #115).
