`CHANGELOG.md` — under `## [Unreleased]`, in `### Added`:

- `/write` turns a brief and its material into one truthful first draft and stops there: it reviews nothing, proofreads nothing, and invokes no peer Skill, so running an editorial pipeline stays a separate choice. Material may be text in the invocation, local files, URLs, an instruction after the reserved separator, or what the conversation already holds, and several sources may feed one draft. Genre, technique, language, and the output options are each resolved independently — the Formal Invocation, a recognised Kntnt map in supplied frontmatter, the current instruction, applicable conversation context, inference, then the default — so an explicit genre coexists with a language taken from context; genre defaults to `general`, no technique applies unless one was selected, and a materially mixed or ambiguous language is asked about rather than guessed. Source Fidelity is the invariant: no invented facts, and attribution, uncertainty, scope, chronology, and causality preserved, with spoken quotations repaired only inside a boundary that leaves meaning, stance, certainty, distinctive wording, and a speaker's own self-correction untouched, and paraphrase where fidelity is doubtful. By default the artifact carries Handoff Metadata — a reserved `kntnt` map in leading YAML frontmatter holding only the normalised genre, technique, and language, merged into frontmatter the artifact already needs — and turning it off suppresses that map alone. Delivery follows the Collection's shared output contract, In-place Editing is not offered, and the material that supplied the brief is never edited in place.
- The Collection Library now carries the editorial contract those Skills are written and reviewed against: `references/editorial/base.md` holds every normative outcome a first draft has to meet, stated once and in English with examples meant to be applied semantically in the target language, including the newspaper-or-quality-magazine register baseline and the rule that genre, audience, and purpose override it. Beside it, `references/editorial/genres/general.md` ships the default genre as a complete contract rather than the absence of one. A genre or technique is one file whose name is its canonical value, the directory is the only list of what is installed, and each resource's review half is a separate `<name>.review.md` beside it, so a Skill that only writes never loads guidance it cannot act on. ADR-0095 records the reasoning (issue #103).
