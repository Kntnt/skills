- `/redline` no longer gives a conforming ending a subheading of its own. The article anatomy's test for when the last section is the ending said what closes a piece, but not whether stating the proposal in full for the first time, or doubting it, opens a new line of argument. So a column's last section that states its proposal, admits how it sounds and asks the reader to try it was read as argument with a closing line appended, and its call was split off under a new subheading that made claims of its own. `article-anatomy.md`, which Write and Redline both load, now says that stating the recommendation or proposal the text has built towards is closing content, even where the last section is the first place it is stated in full, and so are the writer's own reservation about it and the call to try or adopt it. A section carries the argument only where it brings reasons or evidence the conclusion rests on that no earlier section gave. The rule that the ending is a section of its own, and the review half's repair of closing content standing inside a section that does carry the argument, are unchanged. Measured on `claude-opus-5-5` at high deliberation: two of six pre-change `column-clean` runs split the ending under a new subheading and none of six after the change did, and `opinion-flawed`'s closing exhortation got its own section in every run of both arms (#464).
