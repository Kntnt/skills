- The ABT technique now carries what Randy Olson calls its second level, which `/write` reads and `/redline` reviews against. Each part moves from the general to the specific: the situation, then what it puts at stake; the complication, briefly, then its causes; what follows, then how. The reader knows why the situation matters before the complication arrives, with the stakes taken from the material as facts, never a claim that the subject is important, and none invented where the material has none. The And holds only what the complication needs, and the complication comes early. An arc has one complication, with other problems ordered under it as its causes. The consequence answers the causes the text has given, so a list of actions is sound when each action answers one, and a complication stated as an open question is answered by taking up the question. In medias res works where the scene itself carries the situation. Where a genre compresses, withholds or reorders part of the arc, such as a teaser, web copy or an opinion, the genre governs. The review half adds Trey Parker's test, a transition that only adds where a turn or a consequence belongs, and a diagnosis for each of these requirements, read from the visible text only: a missing stake or cause is reported and never written in. No template, labels, word thresholds or fabricated drama come back, and PAC, the genres and the writing brief are unchanged (#469).
