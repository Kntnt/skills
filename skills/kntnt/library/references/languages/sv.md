# Swedish

Base Swedish. A regional variety — Finland Swedish, for instance — is added as its own resource inheriting from this one and overriding only the scopes that genuinely differ.

## Composition

Swedish is a V2 language: the finite verb is the second constituent of a main clause, so fronting anything at all pushes the subject behind the verb. Write *I morgon åker vi till Malmö*, never *I morgon vi åker till Malmö*. This is the single most common structural tell of a text drafted in English and rendered into Swedish.

Compounds are written as one word. *Kvalitetssäkring*, *kundtjänstmedarbetare*, *miljökonsekvensbeskrivning*. Splitting them is not a stylistic choice in Swedish and often changes the meaning outright — *en rökfri miljö* and *en rök fri miljö* are different claims. Where a compound grows unreadably long, rewrite it into a prepositional phrase rather than splitting it.

Definiteness is marked twice. An adjective before a definite noun takes both the front article and the noun's suffix: *den nya rapporten*, *det stora huset*, *de svenska kunderna*. English has one article and drafts translated from it routinely lose one of the two Swedish markers.

The s-passive is the ordinary Swedish passive and is shorter than its periphrastic alternative: *rapporten skickas på fredag*, not *rapporten blir skickad på fredag*. Both exist; the s-form is the unmarked one, and the *bli*-form carries a change-of-state reading that is often not intended.

*Du* is the default address in Swedish prose, including business and journalism. *Ni* to a single reader is either archaic or a deliberate distancing, and reads as a translation from a language that has a formal pronoun. *Man* is the ordinary generic pronoun and is idiomatic where English would use *you*, *we*, or a passive.

Genitive takes a bare *s* with no apostrophe: *Kntnts kunder*, *Sveriges riksdag*. A name already ending in *s*, *x*, or *z* takes nothing at all: *Anders bok*, *Marx teori*.

Swedish tolerates a shorter sentence than English does and punishes subordination harder. Prefer a main clause with a fronted adverbial to a long relative construction, and prefer a verb to a nominalisation: *när kommunen beslutade* over *vid kommunens beslutsfattande*.

Reach for the established Swedish term where one exists and the field actually uses it — *återkoppling*, *underlag*, *avstämning* — but do not purge the loanwords a professional reader expects. A text that writes *dator* and *webbplats* naturally is Swedish; a text that hunts down every English term and replaces it with a coinage nobody says is not.

## Review

Read for särskrivning first. A split compound is the error a Swedish reader notices before anything else, and generated or dictated text produces them in bulk.

Check the word order of every clause that opens with something other than the subject. A fronted adverbial, object, or subordinate clause must be followed immediately by the finite verb.

Nominal style is the characteristic failure of Swedish institutional prose. *Genomförande av en utvärdering av verksamheten* is three nouns doing the work of one verb; *utvärdera verksamheten* says it. Look for *-ande*, *-ning*, and *-else* nouns paired with a colourless verb — *ske*, *utföra*, *genomföra*, *möjliggöra* — and give the sentence its real verb back.

Watch the heavy pre-modifier: *den av kommunen beslutade planen*, *de i rapporten redovisade siffrorna*. It is grammatical and it is bureaucratic. Unpack it into a relative clause: *planen som kommunen beslutade om*.

Prepositional filler multiplies in Swedish official prose: *i syfte att* for *för att*, *avseende* and *gällande* for *om*, *utifrån* for *enligt* or *efter*, *i samband med* for *när*. Each costs words and none adds meaning.

Check that the register is one register. Swedish shifts sharply between *ni*-formality, *du*-directness, and youth-informal, and a text that mixes them reads as assembled rather than written.

Look for the English participle rendered mechanically as an *-ande* form. *Ökande kostnader* is Swedish; a paragraph in which every subject carries an *-ande* modifier is an English draft in Swedish clothes.

Finally, check the sentence rhythm the way a Swedish reader hears it: known information before new, one main idea per sentence, and no clause postponed behind three others.

## Anti-slop

Openings that mark generated Swedish: *I dagens snabbrörliga värld*, *I en tid då*, *I en värld där*, *Det är viktigt att notera att*, *Låt oss dyka ner i*, *Låt oss titta närmare på*. Cut them; the sentence after them is the real opening.

Closings of the same kind: *Sammanfattningsvis*, *Avslutningsvis kan vi konstatera att*, *I slutändan handlar det om*, *Framtiden får utvisa*. A Swedish text that has made its point stops.

The imported false contrast: *Det handlar inte bara om X — det handlar om Y*, *Inte bara ... utan också ...*, *Det är inte X. Det är Y.* as a paragraph of its own. The construction exists in Swedish, but its density in generated text is the tell.

Metaphor stock: *en resa*, *navigera i landskapet*, *nyckeln till framgång*, *hörnstenen i*, *ta det till nästa nivå*, *game changer*, *att verkligen förstå kraften i*. These arrive as a set rather than because the writer meant any of them.

Superlative inflation: *revolutionerande*, *banbrytande*, *otroligt kraftfull*, *helt avgörande*, *oöverträffad*. Swedish general-interest prose is understated, and a text that reaches for these in every paragraph reads as advertising copy whatever it was meant to be.

Connective adverbs at the head of every paragraph — *Dessutom*, *Vidare*, *Följaktligen*, *Därtill*, *Sammantaget* — are a generation tell when they become a template for the text's progression.

A concentration of the objective punctuation errors identified in Mechanics is also evidence of English punctuation carried into generated Swedish. Refer to that scope for the forms; this scope does not restate them.

The triad reflex: *snabbt, enkelt och säkert*, *tydligt, tryggt och tillgängligt*. Three adjectives where the writer had one thing to say.

Hedging translated wholesale: *Det är värt att notera*, *Det bör understrykas*, *Det är viktigt att komma ihåg*, *Som alltid gäller att*. Swedish has the constructions; a text that opens four paragraphs with them is not hedging, it is padding.

Empty attribution: *studier visar*, *experter menar*, *det är allmänt känt att*, with nobody named. In Swedish as in any language, an unattributed authority is a claim the writer has not made.

## Mechanics

Särskrivning is a spelling error. Compounds are written together, and where a compound is split the correction is to join it, not to rephrase around it.

*De* and *dem*: *de* is the subject form, *dem* the object form. *Dom* is spoken Swedish and correct only where the text deliberately renders speech.

*Var* and *vart*: *var* is location, *vart* is direction. Similarly *ligga* and *lägga*, *sitta* and *sätta*, *stå* and *ställa* — the first of each pair is intransitive.

*Att* after a modal verb is an error, not a variation: *kan göra*, not *kan att göra*. After *börja*, *sluta*, and *behöva* the bare infinitive is the ordinary form.

Verbs take no person or number marking: *jag är*, *vi är*, *de är*. Adjectives do agree: *ett stort hus*, *stora hus*, *den stora bilen*.

Numbers use a decimal comma and a space as the thousands separator: *3,14*, *1 000 000*. A percentage takes a space before the sign: *50 %*. A range takes an unspaced en dash: *2020–2024*, *sidorna 12–18*.

Dates are written *2026-08-24* or *24 augusti 2026*; month names and weekdays are lowercase, as are nationalities and languages — *svenska*, *måndag*, *en tysk forskare*. Clock times are written *kl. 14.30* or *14:30*; both are current.

Currency follows the amount: *1 250 kr*, *250 000 SEK*, with a space before the unit.

A sentence-initial connective adverb takes no comma before the finite verb: *Dessutom är det viktigt*, not *Dessutom, är det viktigt*.

A sentence-breaking dash is a spaced en dash: *snabbare – det*, not the unspaced em dash in *snabbare—det*. Ranges instead take an unspaced en dash, as stated above.

Quotation marks are *”…”* — the same right-pointing mark at both ends — so English marks in *“kundresa”* are an error. Quotation inside quotation uses *’…’*. Dialogue may instead be set with an em dash at the start of the line.

A simple list takes no serial comma: *teknik, processer och kultur*, not *teknik, processer, och kultur*.

In running prose, *och* is written out: *Service och support*, not *Service & support*. Preserve an ampersand that belongs to a proper name or quoted matter.

Abbreviations take periods: *t.ex.*, *bl.a.*, *dvs.*, *m.m.*, *osv.*, *ca* without one. An initialism compounded with a word takes a hyphen: *VD-brev*, *USA-baserad*, *EU-direktiv*.

Genitive takes *s* without an apostrophe, and nothing at all after *s*, *x*, or *z*: *företagets resultat*, *Lars hus*.

Only the first word of a heading or title is capitalised, along with any proper noun in it. Title case is English and is an error in Swedish.

Two forms both listed in *Svenska Akademiens ordlista* are both established Swedish, neither of them the error: *paket* and *paketet*, *-are* and *-er* plurals in the cases that allow both, *ska* and *skall*.
