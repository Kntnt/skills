# The mechanics contract

Objective correctness, in the half of it that does not depend on which language a text is in. A Skill contracted to mechanical correction reads this document together with the resolved language's own mechanics guidance, and the two together are that run's rules. What is true of every language the Collection installs is stated here and stated once, so that it is not written again in each language, where the copies would be free to disagree with one another about what is an error.

Nothing here is a preference. A rule belongs in this document only where the text is wrong whatever the writer intended, and only where it would be wrong in the same way in any language. Spelling, inflection, agreement, the language's own punctuation, and its conventions for dates, numbers, currency, and quotation all turn on the language and belong to that language's resource. Where a language defaults more strictly or more loosely than a rule below, its own guidance says so, and what it says about itself wins.

The examples are English because this document is, and they are patterns rather than strings. Apply each by what it does in the target language rather than by the words it happens to use.

## Clause boundaries

Two independent clauses joined by a comma alone are an error where the second does not explain, specify, restate, or stand in direct contrast to the first. *The project will be expensive, it starts in the spring* puts two statements in one sentence that have nothing to do with each other beyond sitting there together: the reader takes the comma as a signal that the sentence continues, and meets a new subject and a new verb instead. Two clauses run together with no punctuation at the joint at all are the same error, and take the same correction.

The same joint is not an error where the second clause does explain, specify, restate, or contrast with the first. *The bug was not in the parser, it was in a silent retry* is the negative-positive contrast, where the comma is what holds the two halves together as one statement, and separating them would break a single thought into two. *We shipped it anyway, nobody had a better idea* specifies the first clause with the reason behind it. Both are established usage rather than tolerated looseness, and a pass that corrects them has changed how the sentence reads without being asked to.

Judge a joint by whether the clauses cohere, and not by their length, by how formal the text is, or by how the text punctuates elsewhere. Where the reading is genuinely open — where a careful reader could defend the joint — the comma stands, because what this rule reaches is the boundary that misleads and not the boundary that is merely informal.

Correct an erroneous joint at the joint and nowhere else: the comma becomes a period or a semicolon, and nothing else about the sentence moves. Do not reorder the clauses, do not merge them into one, and do not supply a conjunction — *and*, *but*, and *so* each assert a relation between the two clauses, and the writer asserted none. Neither half is rewritten, split, or tightened on the way past. Where more than one of a period and a semicolon would serve, the period is the safer of the two, since it claims nothing about how closely the statements are related.

## Established variants

Where a language establishes more than one correct form, the text's own choice stands. A form is not an error because another form is more common, more modern, or the one the reader would have written, and the correction is never to normalise the text towards one of them. Only inconsistency within one text is corrected, and it is corrected towards whichever of the established forms that text already uses more.

That preserves nothing the rules in hand name as an error. A construction this contract or the resolved language's own mechanics guidance identifies as wrong is corrected however consistently the text commits to it, because consistency is what an established variant is judged by and never what makes something established. A text that is wrong the same way throughout is wrong throughout.
