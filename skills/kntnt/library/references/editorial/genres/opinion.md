# Opinion

A piece published in public to argue a position: an op-ed, a debate article, a trade publication's opinion pages, a post on the writer's own platform whose point is the case it makes. The author is visible as the source of the position and the text pushes: it says where it stands at the top and spends its length making that stand hold, so a reader who gets no further than the opening already knows what is being claimed and by whom.

## What this genre asks for

**A reader meets the parts in the order an argument puts them.** Somebody on an opinion page decides within a few lines whether to argue back, to agree, or to move on, and none of those is open to them until they know what is being claimed. So the title states the claim rather than naming the subject; the position stands in the opening, ahead of the case for it; the case follows in the order that makes it, with the grounds before what is drawn from them, and with the objection the reader is already forming met where it arises rather than collected at the end where it reads as an afterthought; and the piece closes on what should happen. These are parts a reader recognises rather than sections to be labelled, and a piece that withholds its position until the case is complete has found the one order in which a reader who disagrees never reaches it.

**The position is somebody's, and the somebody is in the text.** An opinion piece asks a reader to change their mind, and a reader is entitled to know who is asking. The writer appears as the holder of the position rather than as a neutral narrator of it, and appears sparingly enough that the argument and not the person stays in front of the reader. How a language realises that first-person presence is that language's business; what the genre requires is that the position never floats free of somebody prepared to be held to it.

**The headings carry the argument.** Each heading says what its section establishes or what it asks for, so that a reader taking the headings alone has the case in outline and every section announces where it is going before it goes there. A label — *Background*, *The situation*, *Analysis* — sorts the piece without advancing it, and a heading written to intrigue makes the reader enter the section to find out what it was for, which is a column's move made in a text that has already declared its hand. The visibility is the point: an opinion piece is meant to be seen working, and a reader who can trace the case from outside it is a reader who can weigh it.

**The same freedoms, on a shorter rein.** The devices a column uses for pace are available here and are spent for a different purpose: a fragment or a sentence begun on a conjunction lands a point the case has just earned, and a phrase returning after each part of the argument gathers force from the argument rather than from the pleasure of the return. What this genre will not carry is any of them used for texture. The piece is not entertainment, and a sentence in which the reader notices the writing is a sentence in which they are not weighing what is being claimed.

**The argument stands on what a reader can check.** Its reader may well be the person it is arguing against, and such a reader extends no credit, so a claim doing real work in the case names where it comes from in the text, at the point the reader meets it, rather than in an apparatus at the end or nowhere at all. Provenance is part of the push here rather than a scholarly courtesy: an unattributed load-bearing claim asks to be believed by somebody who has just been told they are wrong, and a reference that does not resolve is found in this genre sooner than in any other, by a reader with a motive to look.

**The personal material is the material's.** An opinion piece grounds its case in things that happened — a project that went wrong, a conversation, what somebody in the field actually said — and those come from the material or they are not in the text. Nothing is invented to make an argument concrete: no scene the writer was not in, no exchange nobody had, no illustrative case assembled from what such a case would plausibly look like. Where the material carries none, the argument is made from what it does carry. This holds exactly as strictly for a piece published over somebody else's name, which is the case the rule exists for: the writer reaches that person's experience only through the material, and a plausible invention in a piece its named author will be answerable for is a liability handed to somebody who cannot see it coming.

**It ends on what should happen.** An opinion piece exists because something ought to change, so its close names the change and whoever has to make it: a decision to take, a practice to stop, a thing somebody has to start doing differently. What it names is particular — *it is time to act* is an ending shaped like an ask that asks for nothing — and it is addressed to people who are actually in a position to act on it. A piece that argues well and closes without an ask leaves its reader convinced and idle, and a piece that cannot say what it wants has not finished working out what it thinks.

## What this genre does not do

It does not draw. A column takes the reader along and lets the point arrive; an opinion piece declares the point and then defends it. The scene, the digression, and the reflective passage that give a column its pull drain an argument of drive, and a draft that reaches its position by arriving at it has written a column on a debate page's subject.

It does not weigh from outside. An article reports and lets the material carry what it carries; a report analyses from a position it has not taken; an opinion piece argues from one it has declared and declares it at the top. A draft that sets out the considerations without committing to any of them is an article on the same subject, and the reader who came to the opinion page still does not know what anybody thinks.

It adds no requirement the base contract already holds. Register, structure, sentences, words, claims, openings, endings, formatting, and consistency are stated there once, and a rule repeated here would be one rule made into two things to keep true.

## The technique this genre is ordinarily written with

ABT ([`abt.md`](../techniques/abt.md)), and here it may show. The case is a complication and what follows from it — this is how things stand, this is what cuts against it, this is therefore what should happen — and the close the genre asks for is that consequence put as an ask. A reader deciding whether to argue back is entitled to see the steps, which is the same arc a column is at pains to hide.
