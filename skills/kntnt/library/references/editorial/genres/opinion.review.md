# Opinion — review guidance

Diagnostics for the requirements the opinion genre's base half states, for a Skill reviewing a draft written against them. Nothing here is a requirement: every target a draft has to meet is in the base half, where the Skill that wrote the draft could see it, and a finding raised here is raised against a rule stated there. The examples are English and are patterns — apply what one does, in the language of the draft, rather than looking for its words.

## The shape

**Test.** Read the draft for its parts alone, ignoring how well each is written: what is claimed, where the claim stands, what supports it and in what order, where the objection is met, what is asked at the end. Check that sequence against the one the base half states.

**Failure.** A title naming the subject where the piece has a claim to make; a position that arrives only after the case for it, so a reader who disagrees has been given the evidence for a conclusion nobody told them was coming; conclusions drawn before the grounds that carry them; every counter-argument gathered into one paragraph near the end, where it reads as a concession made after the fact; a piece that ends when the case ends and asks for nothing.

**Edge.** The parts are what a reader recognises rather than sections to be labelled, and a part is not missing because it runs to a clause. Where a piece makes a single point, the case may be one movement and there is no order to get wrong.

**Minimum safe correction.** Move the part whole and change nothing inside it — a part in the wrong place is rarely also badly written. Where the objection sits in a block at the end, move each objection to the section it belongs against; that is usually the whole repair.

## The declared position

**Test.** Read the title and the opening and write down what the piece thinks, in one sentence. If you cannot, or if what you wrote is a subject rather than a claim, the position is not declared.

**Failure.** An opening that establishes why the subject matters and stops there; a thesis appearing at the halfway mark, which is concealed polemic; a position stated so hedged that nobody could disagree with it; a piece in which the position is stated but nobody holds it, the case arriving as though the field had reached it by itself.

**Edge.** Declared is not strident. A position stated quietly is declared, and a piece whose claim is modest is not failing for lack of force. The writer's presence is sparing by requirement, so a piece in which they appear rarely is not thereby a piece in which they are absent.

**Minimum safe correction.** Find the sentence in the draft that does state the position — it is usually already there, further down — and move it into the opening. Where no sentence states it, the position is missing rather than misplaced, and that is a finding against the draft's thinking rather than its arrangement.

## The headings

**Test.** Read the headings alone, in order, and ask whether somebody who read nothing else would have the argument.

**Failure.** *Background*, *The situation today*, *Analysis*, *Conclusion* — a filing system rather than a case; a heading that teases what the section will reveal; a heading naming the section's topic where the section establishes something about it — *Pricing* over a section arguing that the pricing is what fails; headings whose sequence would read the same in any order, which means the case is not in them.

**Edge.** A heading may be an imperative — *Broaden the portfolio* — and that carries the argument as well as a declarative does. A piece short enough to carry no headings at all is complete, and nothing here applies to it.

**Minimum safe correction.** Take the sentence in each section that states what it establishes and compress that into the heading. Where a section has no such sentence, the heading is not the problem and the finding belongs to the section.

## The narrower freedom

**Test.** Mark every fragment, sentence begun on a conjunction, and deliberately repeated phrase, and ask of each what point it lands. In this genre a device that lands nothing is a device spent on texture.

**Failure.** Fragments used for rhythm through a passage that is making its case; a repeated phrase returning on a schedule rather than after each part of the argument; a passage where the sentences are enjoying themselves and the argument has stopped moving; wordplay in a heading that has an argument to carry.

**Edge.** The devices are permitted here, and their presence is never itself a finding — only their use for pace alone. A single fragment landing the sharpest point in the piece is exactly what the rein allows.

**Minimum safe correction.** Restore the surplus departures to ordinary sentences and keep the one that lands. Never repair by removing all of them; a piece stripped of every device argues no better and reads as though it were afraid to.

## The references

**Test.** Take each claim the case actually rests on — remove it and the argument falls — and ask where the draft says it comes from, and whether a hostile reader could get to the source from what the text gives them.

**Failure.** *Studies show*, *the industry agrees*, *everybody in this field knows*; a figure with no origin doing the heaviest work in the piece; a source named only in a list at the end, so the reader meets the claim with nothing attached; a reference precise enough to look checkable and absent from the material.

**Edge.** Not every claim needs a reference — only the ones the case rests on. A claim about the writer's own practice is attributed by being theirs. Where the material carries a source the piece does not need, adding it is not a repair.

**Minimum safe correction.** Move the attribution the material carries into the sentence that makes the claim. Where the material carries none, weaken the claim to what the piece can support, or drop it and let the case stand on its remaining grounds — never supply a source to fill the gap.

## The personal material

**Test.** Take every scene, anecdote, exchange, and named case in the draft and find it in the material. Anything you cannot find is a finding, however small and however well it argues.

**Failure.** An opening example of a project, a client, or a meeting the material never records; a composite case built from several the material does carry; a quoted remark assembled from a paraphrase; circumstantial detail added around a real event to make it land — what somebody said next, how long it took, what nobody thought to ask.

**Edge.** A piece published over somebody else's name is held to this exactly as a first-person one is, and the writer's distance from that person's experience makes invention more tempting rather than more permissible. A hypothetical offered plainly as a hypothetical is not an invented case; what fails is one offered as something that happened.

**Minimum safe correction.** Cut the invented case and make the point from the grounds the material does carry; an argument with one fewer illustration is intact. Never repair it by blurring the particulars, which removes the reader's ability to check the claim while leaving the claim in place.

## The close

**Test.** Read the last paragraph and write down what the piece wants, and from whom. If either answer is missing, or the answer to the first is *to think about this*, the close has not landed.

**Failure.** *It is time to act*; *the industry must take responsibility*, addressed to nobody who could; a close summarising the case just made; an ask aimed at people with no power to act on it while the people who have it are never addressed; a piece that argues to a clear position and then stops.

**Edge.** The ask need not be large. A single concrete change is a complete close, and where the piece argues that something should stop, saying so plainly is the ask. A piece whose whole point is that a decision should be deferred asks for that.

**Minimum safe correction.** Name what should happen using the case's own terms, taking the words from the section that argued for it. Where the draft genuinely does not know what it wants, the finding is against the argument rather than the paragraph, and a manufactured ask conceals it.
