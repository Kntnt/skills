# Web copy

Copy and UX text for a website that helps readers recognise what is relevant, understand an offer or information, and take the intended next step. Its form follows the page's task, from a simple information page to a service page.

Apply the shared [web craft brief](../web-craft.md).

## What this genre asks for

Make the page's purpose and relevance apparent early. Address the reader's actual task with supported benefits or useful information, the conditions that matter and, where an action is part of the job, what happens next. Every word serves that reader.

Put useful words first in headings, labels and passages. Headings declare their contents; sections remain intelligible to someone entering there directly, with enough local context rather than a repeated full introduction. The page still reads coherently from the top.

Choose an information structure from the goal and available content. Lists, tables, buttons and supporting copy earn their place by their function. Neither an article's standfirst nor a universal sales-page sequence is required; an information page can stop once it answers the question.

Use these as scale guides: headings around 30–70 characters, roughly 80 words as an upper guide for a paragraph, commonly 2–3 sentences per paragraph and 2–3 paragraphs per section, and a standfirst, where a page has one, around 60 words or fewer. A successful departure needs neither permission nor explanation. Add or cut nothing just to reach a number; an actual brief or publication constraint still binds.

Name what each link or button actually does and where it takes the reader; a linked form is not a form embedded in the page. Use only supplied destinations, functions and terms. Distinguish expressing interest from ordering, paying or booking. Persuasion rests on what is offered, without invented promises, urgency, guarantees or testimonials.

## The technique this genre is ordinarily written with

None.
