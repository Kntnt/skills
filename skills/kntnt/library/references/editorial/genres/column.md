# Column

A personal journalistic reflection that opens a perspective through a particular author's observation and thought. It may argue, but its main job is to explore that perspective rather than drive an opinion piece's declared demand.

Apply the shared [web craft brief](../web-craft.md).

## What this genre asks for

Begin with a concrete observation the material carries, develop the reflection through meaningful connections, and leave the reader with a resonant thought. The point may be visible early and the ending may retain honest uncertainty. Choose the movement rather than filling a narrative formula.

Let the author's way of seeing and thinking carry the personal voice. Humour, irony, images, fragments and rhetorical returns are available when they earn their effect; no device or pronoun is compulsory.

In ghostwriting, the author's supplied observations, stance and experience set the limits of the personal voice. Do not turn an observation about others or a practice into the author's own conduct. Scenes, memories, conversations and feelings need the same support as any other fact. An observation can be enough.

Give the piece a relevant title, evocative or informative. Use paragraphing and, when the length and reflection benefit, subheadings to orient the web reader without flattening the rhythm. No commercial close is required.

## The technique this genre is ordinarily written with

None.
