# Article

A piece written to be published and read voluntarily — in a newspaper, a magazine, a trade publication, or a site that publishes such things. Its reader chose it, owes it nothing, and may stop at any sentence, and every requirement below follows from that.

## What this genre asks for

**A reader meets the parts where an article puts them.** An article is published into a form that has carried articles before, and its reader arrives knowing where each thing will be: the headline, then a standfirst set off above the body, then the byline, then the body itself, opening on its lead and running on through its sections. Somebody who has read one article has read the form, and a draft that arrives as prose under a title and nothing else leaves that reader looking for parts that are not there and the publication filling the places itself. The standfirst is the part most often missing and the one the form depends on most: the same words stand at the door of the article and are lifted from there to serve as its teaser wherever the article is featured — a newsletter, a front page, a search result — so what a standfirst owes as a text is the teaser genre's to state ([`teaser.md`](teaser.md)) and is not repeated here. The byline carries the name the request gives and no other, and where the request gives none the article carries none rather than a name written to fill the place. These are parts a reader recognises rather than sections to be labelled, and what the shape settles is which of them are there and in what order, never how much of the article any of them takes.

**One angle, and the reader has it early.** An article is not about a subject; it is about one thing said about a subject. *Why a support team gave up its shared inbox* is an angle, *our support team* is a subject, and a draft that never narrows from the second to the first is a survey with a headline on it. The angle is settled before the first sentence is written, is unmistakable within the opening paragraphs, and decides what else belongs: material that is true of the subject but says nothing about the angle is cut, however much of it there is.

**The reader is held rather than assumed.** Nothing obliges anybody to reach the fourth paragraph, so each one gives a reason to want the next: a fact not yet explained, a tension not yet resolved, a consequence not yet drawn. Where a passage exists because the writer knew it rather than because the reader needs it here, it goes, moves, or shrinks to the clause it deserves.

**It reports rather than asserts.** A reader with no stake in the subject reads to find out what happened, not to be told what to conclude, so the material is at the surface of the text: the incident, the count, the date, the thing that was tried and what it did. Conclusions the article draws stand on material the reader has already been shown, and a paragraph of judgement whose evidence stayed in the notes is asking to be taken on trust the reader has no reason to extend.

**People are in it where the material has them.** An article about a change is an article about somebody who changed something or had something changed, and where the material names those people, says what they did, or quotes them, they belong in the text with the role that makes them relevant. Where it names nobody, none is invented and no composite stands in for one.

**The title carries the angle.** Where the artifact has a title, it says the one thing the article says rather than naming the subject, and it promises exactly what the article delivers. A question the article never answers, and a promise it cannot keep, are both spent in the first line a reader reads.

**The parts at the top do not say the same thing.** The headline, the standfirst, the lead and the first section lie close together and are read in one movement by somebody still deciding whether to stay, and each has a job the others do not: the headline carries the angle, the standfirst gives a reader who is not here yet what the article holds, the lead is the article's own first move on a reader who has decided to read, and the first section puts the material in front of them. The lead therefore takes a different angle from the standfirst rather than continuing it — where the standfirst says what is at stake, the lead can open on the case, the scene, or the habit the article is about to cut against — and it opens on its own words rather than on the standfirst's. The failure survives a writer's own reading because every part is sound read by itself and it is only the sequence that repeats. Strike the headline, the standfirst and the lead in turn: where the reader loses nothing they had not already been given, the part struck was repeating another. The first section is not struck but read from its opening sentence, since it cannot leave: where it begins by saying again what the lead has just said, the lead has been spent and the section starts from a standstill.

**The article is written at a scale its reader can hold.** Nobody is obliged to be here, and the reading happens in glances and often on a screen, so the units are ones a reader takes in whole: a paragraph whose point is visible without reading it twice, and a stretch of body text a reader can enter at its subheading and see the end of. Two or three sentences, and two or three paragraphs between one subheading and the next, is what that ordinarily comes to — an indication of the scale rather than the rule the draft is held to, since a paragraph carrying one step of a hard argument earns more room than one carrying a scene, and a section that ends where its point is finished is not divided to reach a number. What settles it is what the reader can hold: a screen of unbroken text fails that whether or not any paragraph in it is long, and an argument broken by a subheading every second paragraph fails it from the other side.

**The article ends by paying what its opening promised.** The headline the reader chose it by, the standfirst that brought them and the lead that told them what this would be are together a promise about what they will have at the end, and the last section is where they are given it. It names something the reader can do with what they have just read — the question to put to their own case, the thing to check, the change worth making — plainly enough to act on and within what the article actually showed them. Where the request gives somewhere the reader can go to do it, the closing points there; where it gives none, the closing names the action and stops rather than sending the reader somewhere invented for the purpose.

**A stated length is a constraint.** Where the request or the publication gives a length, the article is written to it within a small margin, and cutting to it is part of writing rather than an operation afterwards. Where none is given, the article runs as long as its angle takes and stops there.

## What this genre does not do

It does not make every text a news story. An article may be an argument, a profile, an explanation, or an account of what happened, and the angle and the audience decide which; what none of them becomes is a document written to be filed. Where the material is a question a named group has to decide, that is a report, and where it is an announcement addressed to editors, that is a press release.

It adds no requirement the base contract already holds. Register, structure, sentences, words, claims, openings, endings, formatting, and consistency are stated there once, and a rule repeated here would be one rule made into two things to keep true.

## The technique this genre is ordinarily written with

ABT ([`abt.md`](../techniques/abt.md)). An article's reader may stop at any sentence, and what holds them is a complication they are following: the situation as it stood, the thing that cut against it, and what came of that. The arc runs underneath the parts above rather than over them, and a reader carried by it never has to see it.
