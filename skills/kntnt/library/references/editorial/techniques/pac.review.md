# PAC — review guidance

Locate the factual starting point or question, the interpretive work and the warranted conclusion. A descriptive premise is sufficient; do not demand a falsifiable thesis or manufacture a counterargument. For a hypothesis, examine whether the text actually tests it rather than merely restating it. A premise that only names a topic, or poses a question the text's own material shows no reader would hold, is a finding; judge it from the text alone.

Check the inferential step. *Repairs took a third of volunteer time, so doubling opening hours will clear every repair* claims an effect the figures do not establish. Clarify the existing distinction between an observation and its implications; do not add evidence to rescue the conclusion. Where nothing the reader can see or supply binds the facts to the claim, report the gap; never write a warrant, premise or assumption into the text.

Test whether each step advances: *busy in March, busy in June, busy in October, so busy* lists one point three times. Where the analysis is divided, parts that cover the same ground or leave part of the question uncovered are a finding.

An answer at the top is not a failure when the subsequent reasoning supports it. Where a reader cannot tell which way the analysis goes until the conclusion, and the genre does not withhold it, move the text's existing answer or pointer forward rather than writing one. A conclusion that repeats the analysis rather than answering the question, drops a limit the analysis found, or brings in support first met there is a finding. Diagnose missing analysis, an unsupported conclusion or a connection the reader cannot follow, rather than moving a successful summary or forcing a preferred section pattern. Use existing support; report what the text cannot settle.
