# PAC

*Premise, Analysis, Conclusion*: an analytical structure moving from a question, stated or raised by the facts, through what the evidence means, to the conclusion that answers it. Testing a hypothesis is one use; explaining an observed result is another.

## What this technique asks for

**The premise raises a real question and shows why it matters.** Establish the facts, situation or question, moving quickly from what the reader already accepts to a question a thinking reader could have and why its answer matters. A topic is not yet a question: *the repair session* names a subject; *whether it uses volunteer time well* gives the analysis something to settle. Nor is a question no reader would hold, set up to be knocked down. The question may be stated, or evident from the facts a descriptive premise sets out.

**The direction shows early.** The answer, or a clear pointer to it, comes early enough that a reader knows which way the analysis goes; its sharpest statement may wait for the conclusion. A report's opening answer or an article's informative standfirst may precede the detailed analysis.

**The analysis is usually most of the text and the premise often a paragraph**, but no ratio is prescribed.

**Each step of the analysis advances the reasoning.** Analyse the facts' meaning and limits, weighing alternative explanations or objections where the material gives reason. Every step adds something: more examples of one point make a list, and recounting the facts does not stand in for interpreting them.

**Facts, interpretation and what binds them are visible.** A reader can tell what is observed, what the text makes of it, and why one supports the other. The link needs no sentence of its own where the reader can supply it from the text.

**A long analysis may divide into sub-questions.** Where it does, the parts do not overlap and together cover the question, and each may carry a small arc of its own. PAC can organise a whole short text or useful units within a longer one; connected sections may share parts where the reader can follow them. No fixed labels or compulsory nesting applies.

**The conclusion answers the premise's question.** Draw the conclusion that analysis warrants, including uncertainty or a need for further investigation where that is the result. It brings together what the analysis showed rather than repeating it, and keeps the limits the analysis found. It introduces no support the analysis did not present.

For example: a tool library asks whether its repair session uses volunteer time well; repairs used 40 of 120 volunteer hours, a third of recorded time, while missing delay data prevents an efficiency judgement; so the answer waits until delays are recorded, before any decision to extend the session. Facts, interpretation and implication are distinct, without a controversial thesis or an invented opposing view.

Make the reasoning traceable in the genre's own form. Where a genre's own rules compress, withhold or reorder part of the arc, the genre governs there.
