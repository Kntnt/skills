# PAC

*Premise, Analysis, Conclusion*: a three-part analytical arc in which a claim that could be wrong is tested against what is actually known, and a conclusion is drawn from what the test showed. It is a requirement on what the text establishes and in what relation, never a template of three sections waiting to be filled.

## What this technique asks for

**The Premise is a claim that could be false.** It is stated early and plainly, in a form somebody could contradict. *Whether the second monitoring service earns what it costs* is a premise; *monitoring matters* is not one, because nothing could disturb it and an analysis of it has nothing to do. Where the material supports several readings of the question, the text says which one it is taking up.

**The Analysis tests the premise rather than illustrating it.** What supports the premise and what resists it both appear, and the strongest thing that can be said against it is said in its own terms. A text that accumulates confirmations has assumed its conclusion and is showing its workings for a result it never risked; a reader who can see which evidence was left out will not believe the rest.

**The Conclusion is what the analysis earned.** It follows from what the text showed and says something a reader could not have written from the premise alone. That the premise holds, that it holds under conditions the text names, and that the material cannot settle it are all conclusions — the last of them a result rather than a failure to reach one.

**Nothing enters at the end.** A fact, a consideration, or an objection appearing for the first time in the conclusion has escaped the test the text exists to perform. It belongs in the analysis, where it can be weighed, or nowhere.

**The relations have to hold; the labels need not appear.** Nothing here requires the words *premise*, *analysis*, or *conclusion*, in any language, and nothing requires them as headings. What a reader has to be able to see is the claim being tested, the test, and what the test settled.

**This arc is allowed to show.** A reader who came for the reasoning is following the path from what is known to what it settles, and being able to see where they are on it is part of why they believe the conclusion when they reach it; a text that hides its reasoning spends exactly the credibility the reasoning was buying. What is not allowed is labelling. A section headed *Analysis* names the part the section is; a section headed *The second service raised no first alert in twelve months* names what it found, and a reader given the findings can follow the arc without being told the parts at all.

**The arc's unit is the section, not the whole of a long document.** A text short enough to be read at a sitting carries one arc and is finished. A long analytical document ordinarily carries one per large section, each taking up a claim of its own, testing it against the material that section holds, and saying what the test settled. A long document built instead around a single premise tests it once, and every section before the test is evidence for a claim the reader has had no occasion to weigh.

**A section may carry part of the arc where the rest of it is elsewhere.** Premise and analysis, with the conclusion gathered later among the others; or analysis and conclusion, where the premise was established earlier in the document and the section refers back to it. What makes either complete rather than unfinished is that the missing part is somewhere the reader reaches and the section leaves no doubt which part that is. A conclusion deferred to a section that never gathers it, and an analysis resting on a premise nothing in the document states, are unfinished sections wearing the shape of a partial arc.

**Proportion follows the material.** The analysis is usually most of the text and the premise is often a paragraph, but no ratio is prescribed. Any part may be short, so long as each is present where the arc completes and the reader can tell which is which.

**The arc is written inside the genre, not over it.** Where the selected genre fixes something — where a report puts its answer, what a press release leads with — that convention stands and the arc is built to accommodate it. A technique shapes the spine of a text; it does not release the text from being the kind of thing it is.

**What the genre fixes for the document is not this arc's conclusion moved forward.** A report's answer stands where the genre puts it and answers for the whole document; the conclusion this technique places belongs to the unit named above and stands where that unit's test is finished. Neither is a softening of the other, and neither is a tie-break applied to the other: the genre orders the document and the arc orders the section. A draft that reads the opening answer as the arc's conclusion has built the whole document as one arc; a draft that withholds that answer to keep the arc intact has let a technique decide what kind of document it is.
