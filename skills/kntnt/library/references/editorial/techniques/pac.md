# PAC

*Premise, Analysis, Conclusion*: an analytical structure moving from a factual starting point or question, through what the evidence means, to what follows. Testing a hypothesis is one use; explaining an observed result is another.

## What this technique asks for

Establish the relevant facts, situation or question. Analyse their meaning and limits, weighing alternative explanations or objections where the material gives reason. Draw the conclusion that analysis warrants, including uncertainty or a need for further investigation where that is the result. Add no new supporting evidence only at the conclusion.

For example: repairs used 40 of 120 volunteer hours; they account for a third of recorded time, while missing delay data prevents an efficiency judgement; record delays before deciding whether to extend the repair session. Facts, interpretation and implication are distinct, without a controversial thesis or an invented opposing view.

Make the reasoning traceable in the genre's own form. A report's opening answer or an article's informative ingress may precede the detailed analysis. PAC can organise a whole short text or useful units within a longer one; connected sections may share parts where the reader can follow them. No ratio, fixed labels or compulsory nesting applies.
