# ABT — review guidance

Identify the situation and its stakes, the complication or question, and what follows. Read the visible text alone, never asking whether the material had stakes or causes the text does not state. Test the connections rather than the presence of three words, equal parts or one global arc.

**Parker's test.** A transition between parts or sections that only adds, *and then*, where a *but* or *therefore* belongs shows the relation missing at that point: *we opened an office, but we also hired staff* supplies only an addition. The test complements the check that the relations hold without the words and does not replace it.

**Stakes.** A complication that arrives before a reader can tell why the situation matters is a finding. It is none where the situation already shows what depends on it, where an early result in the standfirst or lead precedes the arc, or where an opening scene carries the stakes.

**One complication.** Two problems of equal rank competing for one consequence are a finding. Independent arcs, such as sections answering their own reader questions, a teaser's arc, or a nested or revisited arc, are none.

**The consequence.** An action or conclusion answering a problem the text never raised is a finding. A But whose causes the text does not give, answered by a consequence that takes up its question, is none.

**In medias res.** An And laid out before the scene leaves an ordinary opening with an anecdote in front of it. An And left out altogether pushes the facts into the first sentences after the scene, where they spend the tempo the scene bought.

**Correction.** Move or clarify existing content: bring stakes the text already states ahead of the complication, order a cause under the complication it explains, and keep qualifications in the consequence. A missing stake, cause, link or outcome is reported and never written in; a fabricated crisis cannot repair a missing relation. Leave another coherent application alone, including an early result followed by its explanation.
