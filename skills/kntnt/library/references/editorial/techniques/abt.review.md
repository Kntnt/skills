# ABT — review guidance

Diagnostics for the requirements the ABT technique's base half states, for a Skill reviewing a draft written against them. Nothing here is a requirement: every target a draft has to meet is in the base half, where the Skill that wrote the draft could see it, and a finding raised here is raised against a rule stated there. The examples are English and are patterns — apply what one does, in the language of the draft, rather than looking for its words.

A review reaches this document because ABT was the resolved technique for the draft. A text that happens to fall into this shape with no technique selected is not reviewed against it, and its resemblance is never a reason to raise a finding from this page.

## The And

**Test.** Write down what the draft establishes as settled before anything goes wrong, then ask whether the complication needs all of it. What the complication does not need is surplus.

**Failure.** A setup that gives the reader the organisation's history, its tooling, its team structure, and its plans, of which the complication touches one. A setup so thin that the complication cuts against nothing the reader has been told — a text that opens on the problem has no And.

**Edge.** The And may be a sentence where the reader already knows the situation, and it may be most of the text where the complication depends on an arrangement nobody would guess. Its length is not the test; what the complication needs is.

**Ambiguity.** Where the material offers two settled situations and the complication could cut against either, the arc is not yet determined, and the choice belongs to whoever owns the text rather than to the reviewer.

**Minimum safe correction.** Cut from the setup what the complication does not touch. Where the setup is thin, take what is missing from the material and add it before the complication, rather than moving the complication later.

## The But

**Test.** State the middle of the text as a sentence beginning *but*. If *and then* would serve as well, the relation is sequence rather than contradiction.

**Failure.** *We moved to per-agent assignment, but we also added an unassigned queue* — an addition wearing the connective of a contradiction. A middle that raises a difficulty the text never lets matter. A complication asserted rather than shown, where the material carries what would show it.

**Edge.** A complication may be quiet — a cost, an assumption that failed, a result that went the wrong way — and need not be a crisis. What it may not be is the next event.

**Ambiguity.** Where the draft holds both a genuine contradiction and a sequence dressed as one, the finding is against the dressing, not against the arc: the text has its But and has confused the reader about which one it is.

**Minimum safe correction.** Where a real contradiction is present elsewhere in the draft, make it the middle and demote the sequence. Where the material carries no contradiction at all, the finding is that this material does not support this technique, and that belongs in the report rather than in an invented complication.

## The Therefore

**Test.** Ask whether the last part could have been written from the setup alone. If it could, it is a summary rather than a consequence.

**Failure.** A closing that recaps the text; a lesson addressed to the reader about what organisations should learn; a resolution stated with a confidence the material does not carry — *the change fixed the team's responsiveness* where the material reports nine weeks of one measure and says the result may not generalise.

**Edge.** An unresolved complication yields a Therefore that says what follows so far and what is still open. That is the arc completed, not abandoned.

**Minimum safe correction.** Weaken the consequence to what the material supports, or replace the recap with the consequence the draft states elsewhere. Never supply an outcome the material does not hold in order to finish the arc.

## Connectives without relations

**Test.** Remove every *and*, *but*, and *therefore* from the reading and ask whether the three parts are still visible. If they vanish with the words, they were never there.

**Failure.** Three paragraphs opening with the three connectives over a text that is a chronology; *therefore* introducing something that merely happened afterwards.

**Edge.** A text that uses the three words naturally and also has the relations is fine. The words are neither required nor forbidden.

**Minimum safe correction.** Correct the connective to the relation that actually holds, and raise the missing relation as its own finding. Removing a false *therefore* is safe; leaving it while repairing the paragraph under it is not.

## One arc

**Test.** Name the complication. If naming it takes two, the text has two arcs competing for the middle.

**Failure.** A draft whose first half turns on unanswered messages and whose second turns on the cost of the tooling, with no relation drawn between them.

**Edge.** A subordinate arc inside a section is not a second arc where it serves the governing one — an anecdote with its own turn, ending back on the main complication.

**Minimum safe correction.** Subordinate one complication to the other where the material relates them, or cut the one the angle does not need. Do not merge two complications into a single sentence that asserts a relation the material does not carry.
