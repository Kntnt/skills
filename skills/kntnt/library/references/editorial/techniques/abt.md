# ABT

*And, But, Therefore*: a three-part arc in which a settled situation is disturbed by something that genuinely cuts against it, and a consequence follows. It is a requirement on what the text establishes and in what relation, never a template of three paragraphs waiting to be filled.

## What this technique asks for

**The And is what the reader can accept without argument.** The situation as it stood: what was true, what worked, what everybody assumed. It holds what the complication needs in order to bite and nothing else, because a setup that surveys the whole subject has spent the reader's attention before the text has a problem to spend it on.

**The But is a real contradiction.** Something that cuts against the And — a fact that does not fit, a cost nobody counted, an assumption that failed, a result that went the other way. What it is not is the next thing that happened: *we did this, and then we did that* is a sequence with a connective laid over it, and a text whose middle is a sequence does not have this arc however its sentences are punctuated.

**The Therefore is what the complication forces.** The consequence, the resolution, the decision, or the change that follows from the two parts before it and could not have been stated without them. It is neither a summary of the text nor a lesson drawn from it. Where the material carries no such consequence — where the complication stands unresolved or its outcome is not yet known — that is what this part says, plainly; a Therefore assembled from what the material does not hold is a fabrication in the shape of a structure.

**The relations have to hold; the words need not appear.** Nothing here requires *and*, *but*, or *therefore* to be written anywhere, in any language. What a reader has to be able to see is the situation, the thing that cuts against it, and what followed. Writing the three connectives over a text that lacks the relations is this technique's characteristic failure, and the connectives make it harder to notice rather than easier.

**One arc governs the text.** Whatever else the text carries, one complication is the one the reader is following, and everything else stands in some relation to it. Where two complications compete for the middle, the reader stops knowing which one they are following, and the text has to choose.

**An arc may be nested.** A section, an anecdote, or a passage inside any of the three parts may carry a complete arc of its own. What makes it subordinate rather than a second arc is where it leaves the reader: it turns on something the governing complication has already raised, and its own consequence hands the reader back to the part it sits in. Size decides nothing here — a nested arc may run for pages — and an inner arc whose consequence points somewhere the text never goes is a second arc however briefly it was told.

**An arc may be iterated.** The same arc told twice: once simplified, so the reader has the shape, and once in full, so the reader has the substance. Both passes turn on the same complication and reach the same consequence — a full pass turning on a different one has given the reader a shape that does not fit what follows — and the second owes what the first left out: the evidence, the mechanism, the qualifications, the detail the shape was standing in for. A second pass that says the first again at greater length is repetition in the shape of a structure, and the reader who already had the shape pays for it twice.

**One arc stops serving where the material outgrows it.** A single arc carries a text for as long as one complication holds everything between the situation and the consequence. Where the material has several complications that each need a situation of their own, or a consequence the reader cannot be brought to in one step, the text is built from nested or iterated arcs rather than from one arc stretched over the whole distance. Length indicates rather than decides: past roughly a thousand to fifteen hundred words a single arc ordinarily stops holding, and what the material takes is still what settles it. An arc stretched to fill a length grows an And nobody needed and postpones the Therefore until the reader has stopped waiting for it.

**In medias res is an outer arc enclosing the whole text.** The opening scene is the complication; the consequence begins at the bridge that reveals what the scene actually was, and continues as the rest of the text, which carries an arc of its own inside it. The And is what decides whether the device works, and the scene carries it: the same sentences that move the scene establish where the reader is, who is involved, and what is at stake. An And laid out before the scene has lost the device — exposition and then a dramatic opening is an ordinary opening with an anecdote in front of it, whatever the scene is worth. An And left out altogether loses it the other way: the bridge becomes the first place the facts can go, and that fact dump spends exactly the tempo the scene had bought. The device asks for material with real drama in it; over a situation that has none it is a mannerism, and the reader notices the manner instead of the subject.

**Proportion follows the material.** Nothing here says three paragraphs, three sections, or equal thirds. Any part may be a sentence and any part may be most of the text, so long as each is present and the reader can tell which is which.

**The arc is written inside the genre, not over it.** Where the selected genre fixes something — what a press release puts in its first sentence, where a report puts its answer — that convention stands and the arc is built to accommodate it. A technique shapes the spine of a text; it does not release the text from being the kind of thing it is.
