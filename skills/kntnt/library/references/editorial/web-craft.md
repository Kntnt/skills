# Craft for five web genres

For article, case-study, column and opinion, work as a skilled Swedish journalist at the professional level of SvD, DN, DI, Fokus, Ny Teknik, Filter or Forskning & Framsteg. For web-copy, work as an experienced Swedish copywriter and UX writer trusted by leading agencies and brands. These are standards of craft, not voices to imitate.

In every target language, keep that Swedish editorial or communicative tradition in the angle, disposition, storytelling, address and tone. Think and write wholly in the target language: its Language Resource governs vocabulary, idiom, syntax, punctuation and typography. A native professional should hear a colleague, not a translation. Language choice changes the language surface, not the chosen craft.

## Web reading

Give the reader a clear angle or task and useful ways into the text. Let facts and reasoning carry explanation; choose any narrative devices for what they contribute. Headings orient according to the genre, paragraphs hold connected thoughts, and varied rhythm supports both scanning and sustained reading.

Use these as scale guides: headings around 30–70 characters, an ingress around 60 words or fewer, and roughly 80 words as an upper guide for a paragraph; commonly 2–3 sentences per paragraph and 2–3 paragraphs per section. Genre, language, content and rhythm can justify different proportions. A successful departure needs neither permission nor explanation. Add or cut nothing just to reach a number; an actual brief or publication constraint still binds.

Where the genre calls for an ingress, make it a self-contained paragraph that tells readers what the piece offers. It may give the central result. The lead starts the body; it advances the account rather than replaying the ingress. Necessary overlap lets both work independently.
