# Reviewing against a Writing Brief

Use this branch only when `## Resolution` selects a brief. The brief is a standard for the Text Artifact, never a second Text Artifact. Read it without editing, proofreading or delivering it. If its path or URL cannot be read, report that obstacle and stop before review or writing; never silently substitute a contract-only review. Resolve relative material references from the brief's location.

## Read and map

Read `$LIBRARY/references/editorial/writing-brief.md`, the authoritative English template. Map the selected brief onto every numbered question by meaning, in any language or layout. Retain the answer and its provenance for each question; preserve gaps instead of supplying answers. The template's guidance explains what each question means; it is not an instruction to review or repair the brief.

An answer consisting only of `[MISSING: …]` is unanswered. Review an answer marked `[SUGGESTED: …]` as written and label its assessment **unconfirmed**; review an answer marked `[WEAK: …]` as written and label its assessment **weak**. When a question has both supplied content and missing parts, assess only the supplied content and name the missing parts. An unmarked brief has the same mapping: an absent answer remains unanswered.

Read the material the brief points at where it bears on an answered requirement, including source evidence and a referenced brand platform. Record what it actually supports; the brief's demand for a fact or a quotation is not evidence for that fact or quotation. An inaccessible or missing source is a reported limitation, not permission to fill its contents from memory or external research. Review what is available and leave shortfalls needing the unavailable material for the writer.

## Assess and correct

At step 6 and each re-review, assess each answered question against the current text: **fulfilled**, **partly fulfilled** or **not fulfilled**. Give a passage that demonstrates the judgement, or explicitly say which needed passage is absent. For either shortfall status, state what the reader loses. Cover the requirements the answer actually gives: assignment and channel, reader, RIV, sender and voice, angle, message, intended effect, structure, hook, use of sources, exclusions, working title and frame. A working title identifies the assignment; it does not require a verbatim final headline. An intended effect is judged by how the text works towards it, not by claiming to know real reader reactions or measured results.

Record every shortfall as an ordinary finding with its question number, supplied requirement, passage or absence, and reader loss. Mark findings requiring missing facts, sources or quotations as left for the writer. Keep the original metadata conflicts as findings in their own right, naming both values and the resolved value, even after step 8 synchronizes the text's map. Formal options still settle configuration: report the mismatch with the brief without correcting the text against an overridden genre, technique or language.

Repairable brief findings join the same list, fresh correction rounds, Correction Budget, difference comparison and rejection rules as editorial findings. There is no separate budget or parent-authored substantive repair. With each repairable brief finding, include the answer it is measured against and the exact supporting passages from the text or the material read through the brief, identified by path or URL. This support travels inside the complete finding sent to the correction agent; do not send that agent an extra interpretation of the brief.

Source Fidelity binds these repairs: every fact, source, quotation, attribution, uncertainty, scope, chronology and causal relation must stay supported by the text or the material the brief points at. The available material may supply a missing fact, but an inference, demand or gap in the brief cannot. Where neither contains what fulfilment would require, report the shortfall and leave it for the writer. Check source support as part of every candidate's re-review; invented support is a repair-created defect, so the existing rejection rule applies. Keep the normal preservation rules for claims beside a defect, quotation meaning and code samples.

## Report

Deliver a **Brief fulfilment** section in the artifact's language, separate from the Text Artifact and present even when no correction was needed. Name the selected brief and any suppressed selection of a different brief. List each answered question by template number and name, with its status, evidence in the delivered text (or explicit absence), and reader loss where it falls short. Flag every status measured against an unconfirmed or weak answer. Name all unanswered questions by number and name, including MISSING-only answers, and any missing parts of otherwise answered questions.

The section assesses what is delivered: refresh its evidence after accepted corrections and the final mechanical pass without making a further edit. Keep the normal finding and claim account so the writer can distinguish repaired shortfalls, unresolved ones and rejected attempts; report an original map conflict as such, never as a conflict still present after synchronization. Reporting the section does not authorize returning the brief itself or rewriting an unchanged artifact.
